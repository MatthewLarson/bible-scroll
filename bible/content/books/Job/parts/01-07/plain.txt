## A man in the land of Uz
1:1 A man lived in the land of Uz, and his name was Job. The man was wholehearted and upright. He feared God and kept away from evil.
1:2 Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.
1:3 His flocks and herds came to seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred pairs of oxen and five hundred female donkeys, and his household of servants was very large. Of all the people of the East, he was the greatest.
1:4 His sons would take turns giving a feast, each in his own house on his day. They sent word to their three sisters and asked them to come and eat and drink with them.
1:5 Whenever the days of feasting had come full circle, Job would send for his children and make them holy. He got up early in the morning and offered burnt offerings, one for each of them. For Job thought, "Maybe my children sinned. Maybe they cursed God in their hearts." This is what Job always did.
## The first test
1:6 Then the day came when the sons of God came and took their stand before the LORD. The Accuser came too, among them.
1:7 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Where are you coming from?" The Accuser answered the LORD, "From ranging across the earth, and walking all around it."
1:8 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Have you set your mind on my servant Job? Not one on earth is like him. He is a wholehearted and upright man. He fears God and keeps away from evil."
1:9 The Accuser answered the LORD, "Is it for no reason that Job fears God?
1:10 Haven't you yourself put a fence around him, around his house and all he owns, on every side? Whatever his hands do, you bless, and his livestock has burst out over the land.
1:11 But just put out your hand and touch everything he has, and see if he does not curse you to your face."
1:12 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Look, all he has is in your hand. Only do not put out your hand against the man himself." Then the Accuser left the LORD's presence.
## Four messengers
1:13 The day came when his sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their firstborn brother.
1:14 A messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were at the plow, with the donkeys grazing next to them,
1:15 and raiders from Sheba fell on them and took them. They cut down the young men with the sword. Only I got away, just me, to tell you."
1:16 This one was still talking when another came and said, "God's fire fell from the sky. It blazed among the sheep and the young men and devoured them. Only I got away, just me, to tell you."
1:17 This one was still talking when another came and said, "The Chaldeans made up three bands. They charged the camels and carried them off. They cut down the young men with the sword. Only I got away, just me, to tell you."
1:18 This one was still talking when another came and said, "Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their firstborn brother,
1:19 and look, a great wind came from across the desert. It touched the house at its four corners, and the house fell on the young ones. They are dead. Only I got away, just me, to tell you."
1:20 Then Job rose. He ripped his cloak and shaved his head. He fell to the earth and bowed low.
1:21 He said: / "Naked I came out from my mother's womb, / and naked I will return there. / The LORD gave, and the LORD took. / Let the LORD's name be blessed."
1:22 Through all this Job did not sin. He did not say that God had done anything unfitting.
## The second test
2:1 Then the day came when the sons of God came and took their stand before the LORD. The Accuser came too, among them, to take his stand before the LORD.
2:2 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Where are you coming from?" The Accuser answered the LORD, "From ranging across the earth, and walking all around it."
2:3 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Have you set your mind on my servant Job? Not one on earth is like him. He is a wholehearted and upright man. He fears God and keeps away from evil. He still holds on to his integrity. Yet you lured me against him, to swallow him up for no reason."
2:4 The Accuser answered the LORD, "Skin for skin! Whatever a man owns, he will hand it over for his life.
2:5 But just put out your hand and touch his bone and his flesh, and see if he does not curse you to your face."
2:6 The LORD said to the Accuser, "Look, he is in your hand. But keep his life safe."
2:7 The Accuser left the LORD's presence. He struck Job with bad boils from the soles of his feet to his scalp.
2:8 Job took a clay shard to scratch himself with as he sat right in the ashes.
## Job's wife
2:9 His wife said to him, "Still holding on to your integrity? Curse God, and die."
2:10 He said to her, "You are talking the way a godless woman talks. Should we take the good from God, and not take the bad too?" Through all this Job did not sin with his lips.
## Three friends come
2:11 Now three friends of Job heard of all this harm that had come to him. They set out, each from his home: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite. They arranged to meet, so they could come and shake their heads with him and comfort him.
2:12 They looked up from far away and did not know him. They cried out and wept. Each tore his cloak, and they tossed dust toward the sky, onto their heads.
2:13 Seven days and seven nights they sat with him on the ground. Not one of them said anything to him, for they could see how terrible his pain was.
## Job curses his day
3:1 Afterward, Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.
3:2 Job spoke up:
3:3 "Let it be lost, the day I was born, / and the night that said, 'A male has been conceived.'
3:4 That day: let there be darkness! / Let God on high not ask after it, / and let no daylight gleam on it.
3:5 Let darkness and deep shadow reclaim it. / Let a cloud make its home on it. / Let whatever blackens the day terrify it.
3:6 As for that night, let gloom carry it off. / Let it have no joy among the days of the year. / Let it never come into the count of the months.
3:7 Look, let that night be barren as stone. / Let no glad shout come into it.
3:8 Let those who put curses on days curse it, / those who are ready to wake Leviathan.
3:9 Let its twilight stars go dark. / Let it hope for light, and find none, / and never see the eyelids of dawn.
3:10 For it did not close the doors of the womb I lay in, / and so hide misery from my eyes.
## Why did I not die?
3:11 Why did I not die as I left the womb, / and breathe my last as I came from the belly?
3:12 Why did knees meet me, / and why were there breasts for me to drink from?
3:13 For by now I would lie quiet. / I would sleep, and there would be rest for me,
3:14 beside kings and the advisers of the earth, / who raised up ruins for themselves,
3:15 or with nobles rich in gold, / who stuffed their houses with silver.
3:16 Or I would be like a stillbirth tucked away, / like babies who have never seen the light.
3:17 There the wicked are done with turmoil, / and there the ones whose strength is spent can rest.
3:18 The prisoners all lie at ease; / they no longer hear the driver's shout.
3:19 Small and great are all the same there, / and the slave is free of his master.
## Why give light to the miserable?
3:20 Why does he give light to the sufferer, / and life to those whose souls are bitter,
3:21 who wait for death, and it is not there, / and dig for it harder than for buried treasure,
3:22 who would be glad to the point of shouting, / and thrilled to come upon a grave?
3:23 Why give light to a man whose road is hidden from him, / whom God has fenced in?
3:24 Sighs come to me in place of bread, / and my roars spill out like water.
3:25 For I dreaded a dread, and it has reached me, / and what I feared is coming to me.
3:26 I am not at ease, I am not still, / I am not at rest, and turmoil comes."
## Eliphaz speaks
4:1 Then Eliphaz the Temanite spoke up:
4:2 "Will it wear you out if we try a word on you? / But who can hold in what he has to say?
4:3 Look, you have set many people straight; / you put strength back into tired hands.
4:4 When someone stumbled, your words stood him up; / when knees were buckling, you braced them.
4:5 But now it comes to you, and you are worn out; / it touches you, and you fall apart.
4:6 Should not your fear of God be what you lean on, / and your wholehearted ways be your hope?
4:7 Remember, please: has an innocent man ever been lost? / Where have upright people ever been wiped away?
4:8 What I have seen is this: / whoever plows wickedness and plants misery gets a harvest of it.
4:9 God breathes, and they are gone; / the wind of his nostrils finishes them off.
4:10 The roar of the lion, the snarl of the prowling lion, / the teeth of the young lions: all are knocked out.
4:11 With no prey, the mighty lion dies, / and the lioness's whelps run off in all directions.
## A voice in the night
4:12 Now a message stole up to me, / and my ear caught a murmur of it.
4:13 Among the troubled thoughts of night visions, / at the hour deep sleep falls on mortals,
4:14 dread met me, and shaking, / and it set my bones, every one, quaking.
4:15 A breath of wind slid past my face, / and the hair of my skin bristled.
4:16 It halted. I could not tell what it looked like. / A figure was there in front of my eyes. / A hush, and a voice, I heard:
4:17 'Is any mortal in the right in God's sight? / Is any man pure next to the one who made him?
4:18 Look, he will not rely on his servants, / and even in his messengers he finds error.
4:19 Then what of those who live in houses of clay, / whose footings are in the dust? / They are crushed as easily as a moth.
4:20 From morning to evening they are shattered. / With no one paying attention, they are gone for good.
4:21 When the rope of their tent is pulled up, / don't they die, and with no wisdom at all?'
## No one to answer
5:1 "Go ahead, cry out. Is there someone to answer you? / Which of the holy ones will you look to?
5:2 For bitterness slays the fool, / and passion is death to the naive.
5:3 I watched a fool sending down roots, / and then, in a moment, I cursed his homestead.
5:4 His sons are a long way from rescue. / They are trampled in the town gate, and no one saves them.
5:5 Whatever he harvests, the hungry eat. / They snatch it even out of the thorns, / and a snare gapes open for their wealth.
5:6 Wickedness does not grow up out of the dirt, / and misery is no crop of the soil.
5:7 No, human beings are born for misery, / and the sons of flame fly up high.
## Turn to God
5:8 If it were me, I would turn to God, / and leave my case with God.
5:9 He does great things, beyond searching out, / marvels that no one can count.
5:10 He pours rain over the land / and sends water across the open country.
5:11 He puts the humble in high places, / and the grieving are set high and safe.
5:12 He breaks up the schemes of clever people, / and their hands get nothing done that lasts.
5:13 He snares the wise in their own cunning, / and the scheming of the twisted is rushed and fails.
5:14 By day they bump into darkness, / and at midday they feel their way as though it were night.
5:15 He rescues the needy from the sword, from their mouths, / and from the strong man's hand.
5:16 So there is hope for the weak, / and wrongdoing must shut its mouth.
## The one God corrects
5:17 Think how fortunate a man is when God corrects him! / So do not spurn the Almighty's discipline.
5:18 He is the one who causes pain, and he bandages; / he smashes, and his hands make whole.
5:19 Through six troubles he will save you, / and in seven, harm will not even touch you.
5:20 When famine comes, he will buy you back from death, / and in battle, from the sword's grip.
5:21 When tongues whip, you will be kept out of sight; / when havoc comes, you will not be afraid of it.
5:22 Havoc and hunger will make you laugh, / and the beasts of the land will hold no fear for you.
5:23 The stones in the field will be bound to you by covenant, / and the creatures of the field will make peace with you.
5:24 You will know your tent is at peace; / you will look over your homestead and miss nothing.
5:25 You will know that your descendants will be many, / your offspring as plentiful as the plants of the earth.
5:26 Still strong, you will go down to your tomb, / as a stack of sheaves goes up at harvest time.
5:27 There: we have studied it, and that is how it is. / Listen to it, and take it to heart for yourself."
## Job speaks
6:1 Then Job spoke up:
6:2 "If only my bitterness could really be weighed, / and my ruin lifted onto the scales beside it!
6:3 Now it would weigh more than all the sand of the seas. / This is why my words have run wild.
6:4 The Almighty's arrows are stuck in me, / and my spirit is drinking their venom. / God's terrors stand in battle lines against me.
6:5 Will a wild donkey bray if it is standing in grass? / Will an ox bellow beside a full trough?
6:6 Who eats bland food with no salt? / Is there any taste in the slime of purslane?
6:7 My appetite will not go near them; / they are like disease in my bread.
6:8 Who will see to it that my request comes true, / and that God grants my hope:
6:9 that God would be pleased to crush me, / that he would free his hand and cut me off?
6:10 That would still be my comfort; / I would spring up in pain that does not spare, / for I have not hidden the sayings of the Holy One.
6:11 How strong am I, that I could hold on? / What is my end, that I should stretch my life out?
6:12 Am I as strong as rock? / Is my body made of bronze?
6:13 Is there no help in me at all, / now that every resource has been driven off?
## Friends like a dry stream
6:14 A man who is melting away should have loyal love from his friend, / even if he gives up the fear of the Almighty.
6:15 My brothers have betrayed me like a wadi, / like the beds of seasonal streams that pass away,
6:16 murky with ice, / with snow buried in them.
6:17 In the season they dry up, they fall silent; / in the heat they are gone from their beds.
6:18 Their courses twist and turn; / they run up into the wasteland and are gone.
6:19 The caravans from Tema stare out for them; / the trading bands of Sheba count on them.
6:20 They are put to shame for trusting; / they arrive there, and their faces fall.
6:21 That is what you have turned into now. / You see a horror, and you shrink back in fear.
6:22 Have I ever told you, 'Give me a present'? / Or, 'Use your money to pay someone off for me'?
6:23 Or, 'Get me free from my enemy's grip'? / Or, 'Buy me back from the hands of tyrants'?
## Teach me
6:24 Instruct me, and I will hold my tongue. / Make me see what mistake I have made.
6:25 How sharp the words of honesty are! / But when you scold, what is it you are scolding?
6:26 Are you planning to scold mere words, / when a hopeless man's talk is only wind?
6:27 You would cast lots over a fatherless child, / and strike a bargain over your own friend.
6:28 Now please, turn your eyes on me; / I swear I will not lie to your faces.
6:29 Think again, please, and do no injustice. / Think again: my being in the right still hangs on this.
6:30 Is anything crooked on my tongue? / Does my palate not know the taste of disaster?
## Life is hard service
7:1 "Is not human life on earth a term of hard duty? / Are its days not like the days of a day laborer?
7:2 He is like a slave panting for the shade, / like a worker hoping for his wages.
7:3 So I have inherited months of nothing, / and nights of trouble are counted out for me.
7:4 I lie down and say, 'When can I get up?' / The evening stretches on, / and I have my fill of tossing around until first light.
7:5 Maggots and clods of dirt cover my body like clothes. / My skin cracks, and then it runs.
7:6 My days fly by faster than a weaver's shuttle; / they run out, with no hope left.
## Job speaks to God
7:7 Keep in mind that my life is wind. / My eye will not come back to see anything good.
7:8 Whoever sees me now will not set eyes on me again. / Your eyes will be on me, and I will not be there.
7:9 A cloud thins out and goes away; / just so, the one who goes down to Sheol never climbs back up.
7:10 He does not come home again, / and his place will not recognize him anymore.
7:11 So I for my part will not muzzle my mouth. / In my spirit's distress I will speak; / in my soul's bitterness I will pour out my complaint.
7:12 Am I the Sea, or am I the Dragon, / that you station a guard over me?
7:13 If I tell myself, 'My bed will console me, / my couch will help carry my complaint,'
7:14 then you break me down with dreams, / and send visions to terrify me,
7:15 until I would rather be strangled, / and would pick death over my bones.
7:16 I give up: I will not live forever. / Back off from me, for my days are a puff of air.
## What is a human being?
7:17 What is a mortal, that you treat him as so great, / that you set your mind on him,
7:18 that you check on him each morning / and put him to the test minute by minute?
7:19 How long until you take your eyes off me, / and let me be for the time it takes to swallow my spit?
7:20 Suppose I sinned: what do I do to you, / you watcher of human beings? / Why have you set me up as a thing to strike at, / so that I am a burden to myself?
7:21 And why not lift off my offense / and let my guilt pass from me? / For now I am going to lie down in the dust. / You will search hard for me, and I will not be there."
