## Bildad speaks
8:1 Then Bildad the Shuhite spoke up:
8:2 "How long will you keep talking like this? / How long will your words be a mighty wind?
8:3 Does God twist justice? / Does the Almighty twist what is right?
8:4 If your children sinned against him, / he sent them away into the grip of their own rebellion.
8:5 But you, if you go looking for God / and beg the Almighty for mercy,
8:6 if you are pure and upright, / he will wake up for you this very moment / and make your rightful home whole again.
8:7 Your early years will seem a small thing; / your later years will grow very large.
## Ask the generation before
8:8 Just ask the generation of long ago; / set your mind on what their fathers learned by searching.
8:9 We are yesterday, and we do not know; / a shadow is all our days on earth.
8:10 Won't they teach you and talk to you? / Won't words come out of their hearts?
## Papyrus without a marsh
8:11 Without a marsh, can papyrus rise high? / Without water, can the reed grow?
8:12 Still in its first green, before anyone picks it, / it withers ahead of every other plant.
8:13 That is the road of everyone who forgets God, / and the godless man's hope will be lost.
8:14 His confidence snaps, / and what he trusts is a spider's house.
8:15 He leans his weight on that house; it will not stand. / He grips it tight; it will not last.
8:16 Look at him in the sunshine, green and full of sap; / his young shoots run out across his garden.
8:17 His roots wrap around a pile of stones; / he looks out on a house of rocks.
8:18 Yet if someone rips him out of his place, / the place denies him: 'I have never seen you.'
8:19 Look, that is the joy his road leads to; / and out of the dust others spring up.
## God will not reject the wholehearted
8:20 Look: God does not reject the wholehearted; / he will not grasp the hand of wrongdoers.
8:21 He will still fill your mouth with laughing, / your lips with a joyful shout.
8:22 Your haters will wear shame like clothes, / and the tent of the wicked will not be there."
## Job answers Bildad
9:1 Then Job spoke up:
9:2 "Yes, I know it is so. / But how can a mortal be in the right with God?
9:3 Suppose a man wanted to argue his case with him: / not one time in a thousand would he answer.
9:4 Wise of heart and mighty in power: / who has stood stubborn against him and come out whole?
## The one who moves mountains
9:5 He picks up mountains and moves them, and they do not know, / when in his anger he turns them upside down.
9:6 He jolts the earth out of its place; / the pillars under it shudder.
9:7 He gives the sun an order, and it stays dark; / he shuts the stars away under a seal.
9:8 Alone he spreads out the skies / and tramples on the back of the Sea.
9:9 The Bear is his work, and Orion, and the Pleiades, / and the hidden rooms of the south.
9:10 He does great things, beyond searching out, / and marvels that no one can count.
9:11 He could walk right past me and I would not see; / he sweeps on by, and I would never grasp it was him.
9:12 He grabs, and who will make him return it? / Who will ask him, 'What are you doing?'
9:13 God will not call off his anger; / Rahab's helpers crouched down under him.
## Who could take him to court?
9:14 Then how could I, of all people, answer him, / and pick out my words to face him?
9:15 Though I had right on my side, I could not reply; / I could only beg the one who judges me to be kind.
9:16 If I cried out and he answered me, / I still would not trust that he had lent my voice his ear.
9:17 He is the one who bruises me in a windstorm / and piles up my wounds for no reason.
9:18 He does not allow me to take a breath again; / instead he stuffs me full of bitter things.
9:19 Is it strength? Here he is, the mighty one. / Is it justice? Who will name me a day in court?
9:20 Say I am innocent: my own mouth would pronounce me guilty. / Say I am wholehearted: it would twist me into a crooked man.
9:21 I am wholehearted; I no longer know myself. / I reject my life.
9:22 It makes no difference. So I say it out loud: / wholehearted or wicked, he finishes them off.
9:23 If the whip kills without warning, / the innocent despair, and he scoffs at them.
9:24 A land is put into a wicked man's power, / and he hides the faces of its judges. / If he is not the one, then who is?
## Faster than a runner
9:25 My days have outrun a runner; / they flee without seeing any good.
9:26 They glide away with the papyrus boats, / like an eagle that drops onto its food.
9:27 If I tell myself, 'I will put my complaint out of mind, / I will drop this face and brighten up,'
9:28 then all my pains fill me with dread; / I know you will not clear me.
9:29 I am going to be the guilty one. / So why wear myself out for a puff of air?
9:30 Even if I scrubbed myself in snow water / and made my hands clean with lye,
9:31 then you would dip me down into the pit, / and my own clothes would be disgusted with me.
## Someone to stand between us
9:32 He is no human like me, someone I could answer back, / so that the two of us could walk into court together.
9:33 If only someone stood between us to judge the case, / to put his hand on each of us.
9:34 Let him lift his rod off me, / and let his terror stop frightening me.
9:35 Then I would speak, and not be afraid of him, / because inside myself I am not that way.
## Job speaks to God
10:1 "My whole being is sick of living. / I will turn my complaint loose on myself; / I will talk out of the bitterness inside me.
10:2 I will say to God: Do not condemn me. / Let me know what your case against me is about.
10:3 Is it good for you to crush people, / to toss aside what your own hands worked so hard to make, / and to beam your light on what the wicked plan?
10:4 Are your eyes made of flesh? / Is your seeing like a mortal's seeing?
10:5 Are your days the days of a mortal? / Are your years the span of a man's days,
10:6 that you go looking for my wrongdoing / and dig for my sin,
10:7 even though you know I am not a wicked man, / and there is no one to pull me out of your hand?
## Your hands made me
10:8 Your hands molded me and made me, / every part of me, and now you swallow me up.
10:9 Please remember: you shaped me as from clay. / And now you will send me back to the dust?
10:10 Like milk you poured me out, did you not? / Like cheese you made me thicken.
10:11 With skin and flesh you clothed me; / with bones and sinews you wove me together.
10:12 You worked life and loyal love for me; / your watchful care guarded my breath.
10:13 But all along you kept these things hidden in your heart. / I know that this is what you meant to do.
10:14 If I sinned, you kept your eye on me, / and you would never clear me of my guilt.
10:15 If I am guilty, oh, woe is me! / If I am innocent, I still cannot raise my head; / I have had my fill of shame, and I look at my misery.
10:16 If my head goes up, you track me down like a lion, / and you turn around to show off your wonders at my expense.
10:17 You keep calling new witnesses to face me; / your anger at me grows and grows; / one shift of soldiers after another is against me.
## Why did you bring me out?
10:18 So why did you bring me out of the womb? / I would have breathed my last, and no eye would have seen me.
10:19 I would have been like someone who never existed, / brought from the belly to the grave.
10:20 Are my days not few? Stop, turn away from me, / so I can brighten up a little,
10:21 before I go off and do not come back, / to the land of darkness and deep shadow,
10:22 a land of dimness like the gloom, / of deep shadow and no order, / and when it shines, it shines like gloom."
## Zophar speaks
11:1 Then Zophar the Naamathite spoke up:
11:2 "Is no one going to answer all these words? / Will a man who is all talk win his case?
11:3 Are grown men supposed to sit silent at your bragging? / You scoff, and no one puts you to shame?
11:4 You said, 'What I teach is spotless; / I have been clean in your eyes.'
11:5 How I wish God himself would speak, / and open his lips to argue with you,
11:6 to show you wisdom's hidden things, / since sound wisdom is twofold. / Understand, then, that God is letting part of your guilt be forgotten.
## Higher than the heavens
11:7 Will you get to the bottom of God by searching? / Will you arrive at where the Almighty ends?
11:8 Heights of the heavens! What can you do? / Deeper than Sheol! What can you know?
11:9 To measure it, you must go past the length of the earth / and beyond the breadth of the sea.
11:10 When he passes through, puts someone under arrest, / and calls the assembly, who will make him turn back?
11:11 He knows the men who are false; / he sees evil, and he need not look twice.
11:12 A hollow man will get a heart / on the day a wild donkey's foal is born human.
## If you set your heart right
11:13 As for you, if you will make your heart steady / and spread your palms out to him,
11:14 if your hand holds wickedness, throw it far off, / and give injustice no room in your tents.
11:15 Then you could raise your face without a flaw on it, / set solid as cast metal, and afraid of nothing.
11:16 Your misery will slip from your mind; / you will think of it as floodwater long gone.
11:17 Your lifetime will stand up clearer than midday; / if it grows dark, it will turn into morning.
11:18 There will be hope, and so you will trust; / you will search your place and sleep without a care.
11:19 You will stretch out to rest, and no one will startle you; / many people will come seeking your favor.
11:20 As for the wicked, their eyes will wear out; / there will be nowhere left for them to run; / all they can hope for is to breathe out their life."
## Job answers Zophar
12:1 Then Job spoke up:
12:2 "Truly, you are the people! / Wisdom will die when you do.
12:3 I have a heart too, just like you; / I do not fall short of you. / Who is there who does not know such things?
12:4 My own friend laughs at me, / at one who called to God and he answered him; / people laugh at a righteous man, a wholehearted one.
12:5 The comfortable man looks down on bad luck; / a shove waits, he thinks, for feet that are already slipping.
12:6 Raiders live in quiet tents, / and people who make God angry are safe, / people who carry their god in their own hand.
## Ask the animals
12:7 Just go and question the cattle, and let them teach you; / the birds of the air can explain it to you.
12:8 Or have a talk with the ground and let it teach you, / and let the fish of the sea recount it to you.
12:9 Among all these, who does not know / that it was the LORD's hand that did this?
12:10 He holds in his hand the life of every creature that lives, / and the breath in every human body.
12:11 The ear puts words to the test, does it not, / just as the mouth tastes food to eat?
12:12 The gray-haired have wisdom; / the long-lived have understanding.
## The one who overturns
12:13 But wisdom and strength live with him; / good advice and understanding belong to him.
12:14 He knocks down, and it cannot be built again; / he locks a man in, and there is no opening it.
12:15 He shuts off the waters, and everything dries; / he sends them out, and they overturn the earth.
12:16 Power is with him, and sound wisdom; / the one who strays and the one who leads him astray are both his.
12:17 He marches advisers off barefoot; / he turns judges into fools.
12:18 He breaks the grip of kings / and binds a plain cloth around their waists.
12:19 He sends priests off barefoot / and brings down those who seemed lasting.
12:20 He strips the lips of the trusted, / and he takes the good sense away from old men.
12:21 He pours scorn on the nobles / and unbuckles the belt of the strong.
12:22 He lays bare things hidden deep in the dark; / he drags deep shadow out into the light.
12:23 Nations grow great because of him, and he wipes them out; / he lets nations spread wide, and then he leads them off.
12:24 He strips the heads of the earth's peoples of their sense / and sends them wandering in an empty waste with no road.
12:25 They fumble in the dark, with no light; / he sends them weaving about like a drunk.
## Lies on God's behalf
13:1 "I have seen every bit of it with my own eye; / my ear has heard it and made sense of it.
13:2 I know as much as you know; / I do not fall short of you.
13:3 Yet it is the Almighty I mean to speak to; / I want to argue it out with God.
13:4 As for you, you are plasterers of lies, / healers of nothing, all of you.
13:5 I wish you would just shut your mouths entirely! / For you, silence would count as wisdom.
13:6 Please hear the charge I bring; / give your attention to the disputes on my lips.
13:7 Is it for God that you speak what is unjust? / Is it for him that you talk deceit?
13:8 Will you take his side because of who he is? / Is it God you are defending in court?
13:9 Would it be good for you if he searched you through? / Or will you trick him as people trick a man?
13:10 He will rebuke you, and rebuke you hard, / if you play favorites in secret.
13:11 Should not his greatness frighten you, / and his terror come down on you?
13:12 The things you keep quoting are proverbs made of ash; / the walls you hide behind are clay walls.
## Come what may
13:13 Stop talking and let me be, so that I can speak, / and whatever happens to me, let it happen.
13:14 Why do I grip my own flesh in my teeth / and hold my life in my open palm?
13:15 He is going to kill me; I have nothing left to hope for. / But I will argue for the way I have lived, right to his face.
13:16 Even this will turn into my rescue, / for the godless do not come into his presence.
13:17 Listen, really listen, to what I say, / and let my statement reach your ears.
13:18 Here it is: I have set my case in order. / I know that I am the one who is right.
13:19 Who would take me to court? / If someone could, I would hold my tongue and breathe my last.
## Job turns to God
13:20 Just two things, do not do them to me, / and then I will not hide from your presence:
13:21 pull your hand back, far off me, / and let your terror stop frightening me.
13:22 Then you call me, and I will answer. / Or I will speak, and you give me a reply.
13:23 How many guilty acts and sins do I have? / Make my rebellion and my sin known to me.
13:24 Why are you hiding your face from me? / Why do you treat me as your enemy?
13:25 Do you want to scare a leaf that is being blown about, / and hunt down dry stubble?
13:26 You draw up bitter charges against me; / you hand me the guilt of my young days as my inheritance.
13:27 You clamp my feet in the stocks; / you keep an eye on every path I take; / you scratch a mark on the bottoms of my feet.
13:28 And he wears out like rot, / like cloth a moth has eaten.
## Born of a woman
14:1 "A human, born of a woman, / is short of days and has his fill of turmoil.
14:2 Like a blossom he comes out, and then he droops; / like a shadow he runs off, and he does not stay.
14:3 Even on such a one you open your eyes? / Will you bring me into court with you?
14:4 Who can make what is clean come out of what is unclean? / No one can.
14:5 If his days are cut to measure, / if you hold the count of his months, / if you have set him bounds he will not cross,
14:6 then take your eyes off him, and let him have a rest, / so that like a hired man he can be content with his day.
## Hope for a tree
14:7 A tree, at least, has hope. / Chop it down, and it will come back with new growth; / its young shoots will keep on coming.
14:8 Though its roots age underground / and its stump lies dead in the dust,
14:9 one whiff of water, and it breaks into bud; / it sends out new shoots as if it were just planted.
14:10 A strong man, though, dies and lies limp; / a human gives up his breath, and where is he then?
14:11 Water runs out of the sea; / the river shrinks and goes dry:
14:12 so a man lies down and does not rise. / Until the heavens are gone, they do not wake up, / and no one rouses them out of their sleep.
## If a man dies
14:13 I wish you would tuck me away in Sheol, / keep me out of sight until your anger turns back, / fix a date for me, and then remember me!
14:14 When a man dies, can he come back to life? / Through every day of my hard duty I would wait / for my relief to arrive.
14:15 You would call me, and I would answer you; / you would yearn for the thing your hands had made.
14:16 Then you would count each step I take, / but you would not be lying in wait for my sin.
14:17 You would tie up my rebellion in a pouch with a seal on it, / and coat my guilt over with plaster.
## Water wears away stone
14:18 Yet even a mountain falls and wears away, / and a cliff is shifted out of its place;
14:19 water rubs stones smooth, / its downpours carry off the dust of the land; / that is how you wipe out a human's hope.
14:20 You beat him down for good, and off he goes; / you alter his face and send him away.
14:21 If his sons rise to honor, he never knows; / if they sink small, he does not notice.
14:22 All he has is the ache of his own flesh / and the grief of his own soul."
