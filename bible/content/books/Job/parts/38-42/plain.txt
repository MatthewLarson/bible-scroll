## The LORD answers from the storm
38:1 Then the LORD answered Job from the windstorm. He said:
38:2 "Who is this who blurs the plan / with words that have no knowledge in them?
38:3 Get your belt tight, like a man. / I will ask you, and you tell me what you know.
## The earth and the sea
38:4 Where were you when I founded the earth? / Speak, if you have the insight.
38:5 Who decided its size? You must know! / Or who ran a builder's line over it?
38:6 Into what were its bases sunk? / Who set down its corner stone,
38:7 when the stars of morning sang as one, / and all the sons of God raised a shout?
38:8 Who closed doors to hold back the sea / when it broke out and came from the womb,
38:9 when I gave it the clouds to wear / and wrapped it like a baby in deep darkness,
38:10 when I carved out my boundary for it / and put up a bolt and doors,
38:11 and I said, 'Up to here you may come, but not one step more. / Here a stop is set for your proud waves'?
## Dawn, death and light
38:12 In all your days, have you commanded the morning? / Have you taught the dawn where it belongs,
38:13 so that it takes hold of the earth by its corners, / and the wicked are shaken loose from it?
38:14 The earth changes like clay under a seal; / things stand forth like clothing.
38:15 The wicked lose their light, / and the arm they lift high is snapped.
38:16 Have you come to the sources of the sea? / Have you walked about where the deep is deepest?
38:17 Has anyone opened death's gates to you? / Have you laid eyes on the gates of deep shadow?
38:18 Have you grasped how broad the earth is? / Speak up, if you know all of it.
38:19 Which way leads to the place where light lives? / And darkness: where does it stay,
38:20 so you could lead it back to its own country / and recognize the roads to its house?
38:21 Of course you know: you were born by then! / You have so many days behind you!
## Snow, rain and stars
38:22 Have you walked into the places where snow is stored? / Have you looked where hail is kept,
38:23 which I save up for a time of distress, / for a day of fighting and war?
38:24 By what road is the light parceled out, / or the east wind spread across the land?
38:25 Who digs a ditch for the downpour / and opens a road for the thundercloud,
38:26 to send rain on ground with nobody on it, / on a desert where no person lives,
38:27 to give the ruined, barren places their fill / and make fresh green shoots come up?
38:28 Is there a father for the rain? / Who became father to the drops of dew?
38:29 Whose womb does the ice come out of? / And the white frost of the sky, who bore it,
38:30 when water stiffens like a stone / and the face of the deep locks tight?
38:31 Can you tie the knots that hold the Pleiades, / or untie the ropes of Orion?
38:32 Will you bring the star-groups out, each at its time? / Will you steer the Bear and her young?
38:33 Are the rules of the heavens known to you? / Can you make them govern the earth?
38:34 Will you shout up to the clouds / and be drenched by a mass of water?
38:35 Will you send off the lightning, and off it goes, / saying to you, 'Here we are'?
38:36 Who has put wisdom in what lies hidden? / Who gave insight to the mind?
38:37 Who is wise enough to number the clouds? / Who lays the sky's waterskins on their side,
38:38 when the soil pours together in one lump / and the clods glue to one another?
## Lions and ravens
38:39 Will you track down food for the lion / and fill up the young lions' bellies,
38:40 as they lie low in their caves, / sitting in ambush in the brush?
38:41 Who has the raven's meal ready / when its chicks call to God for help / and stumble around without food?
## Goats, donkeys and the wild ox
39:1 Do you know the season when the ibex of the crags give birth? / Have you watched over the deer as they calve?
39:2 Will you number the months they fill up? / Do you know the hour of their delivering?
39:3 They kneel and break open to bring their young out, / and they cast off their pains.
39:4 Their little ones get strong and big out in the wild. / Off they go, and they never return to their mothers.
39:5 Who set the wild donkey loose to roam? / Who undid the straps of the onager,
39:6 the one whose home I made the desert flats, / whose dens are in the salt country?
39:7 It laughs at the uproar of the town / and never hears a driver yelling.
39:8 It scouts out the mountains as its pasture / and tracks down every green plant.
39:9 Would the wild ox be glad to work for you, / or bed down by your feed box?
39:10 Can you rope the wild ox to plow a furrow? / Will it drag the harrow down the valleys after you?
39:11 Its power is huge; will you count on it? / Will you hand over your hard labor to it?
39:12 Can you rely on it to bring your grain back / and pile up your threshing floor?
## The ostrich and the horse
39:13 The ostrich flaps its wings with delight, / but are they a stork's wings and feathers?
39:14 It abandons its eggs to the earth / and leaves the dirt to keep them warm.
39:15 It forgets that someone's foot could smash them, / or a beast of the field could stomp on them.
39:16 It is rough with its chicks, like a stranger to them. / Its work may come to nothing, and it has no fear,
39:17 for God has let wisdom slip from its mind / and handed it no portion of sense.
39:18 Yet when it springs up and takes off, / it mocks the horse and the one riding it.
39:19 Is it you who gives the horse its power, / who dresses its neck in a rippling mane?
39:20 Can you set it bounding like a locust? / The splendor of its snorting strikes terror.
39:21 It scrapes the valley floor, glad of its strength, / and charges out toward the weapons.
39:22 Fear makes it laugh; nothing shakes it. / It will not turn away when the sword comes.
39:23 Above it the quiver rattles, / and spear and javelin glint.
39:24 Quivering and wild, it gulps down the ground. / At the blast of the ram's horn it will not hold still.
39:25 Each time the ram's horn blows, it cries 'Aha!' / It sniffs out the fight from a long way off, / the roar of officers, the shout of war.
## Hawk and eagle
39:26 Does your insight make the hawk fly, / stretching its wings toward the south?
39:27 Does the eagle climb the sky because you say so, / and place its nest far up?
39:28 The cliff is where it lives and sleeps, / on the rock's sharp tooth, its stronghold.
39:29 From up there it watches for its meal; / its eyes pick it out at a great distance.
39:30 Its chicks lap up blood; / wherever there are bodies, it is there."
## The LORD challenges Job
40:1 And the LORD answered Job. He said:
40:2 "Is the critic going to take the Almighty to court? / Let the one who corrects God give his answer."
## Job answers the LORD
40:3 Then Job answered the LORD. He said:
40:4 "Look, I am a lightweight. What could I say back to you? / I lay a hand across my mouth.
40:5 One time I talked, and I will not reply. / Two times, and I will not go on."
## The LORD speaks again
40:6 Then the LORD answered Job from the windstorm. He said:
40:7 "Get your belt tight, like a man. / I will ask you, and you tell me what you know.
40:8 Would you even overturn my justice? / Would you declare me guilty so that you come out innocent?
40:9 Is your arm as strong as God's? / Does your voice thunder the way his does?
40:10 Go on, put on pride and loftiness as jewelry; / wear splendor and beauty like clothes.
40:11 Let your fury overflow and spill. / Spot every proud one and knock him low.
40:12 Spot every proud one and make him bow; / stamp the wicked down on the spot.
40:13 Bury the whole lot of them in the dirt; / bind their faces in the hidden place below.
40:14 Then even I will give you credit: / your right hand is enough to rescue you.
## Behemoth
40:15 Look, please, at Behemoth, which I made as well as you. / Like cattle, it feeds on grass.
40:16 See: its might is in its hips, / its force in the cords of its belly.
40:17 Its tail stands rigid like a cedar; / the tendons of its thighs are braided tight.
40:18 Its bones are bronze pipes, / its limbs like iron bars.
40:19 It is the first of the ways of God. / The one who made it brings his sword near it.
40:20 The hills carry their crops to it, / and every beast of the field frolics there.
40:21 It rests beneath the thorny lotus bushes, / under cover of the reeds and the swamp.
40:22 The lotus bushes screen it with shade; / the poplars of the river valley ring it round.
40:23 Look: the river storms, and it does not panic. / It feels safe even when the Jordan bursts against its mouth.
40:24 Will someone catch it with its eyes wide open? / Will anyone hook it through the nose with snares?
## Leviathan
41:1 Will you haul Leviathan up on a fishhook? / Will you tie its tongue down with a line?
41:2 Will you thread a reed rope through its snout / and punch a hook through its cheek?
41:3 Will it plead and plead with you? / Will it talk to you sweetly?
41:4 Will it make a treaty with you / and become your slave for good?
41:5 Will you toy with it as with a pet bird? / Will you tie it up for your little girls to play with?
41:6 Will partners in trade haggle over it / and split it up among the dealers?
41:7 Will you stuff its skin full of barbs / and its head with fish spears?
41:8 Just put your hand on it once: / think of the fight, and you will not try again.
41:9 See, the hope of anyone who tries is a lie. / One glimpse of it, and he is thrown down.
41:10 None is daring enough to wake it. / So who is he who would take a stand against me?
41:11 Who has ever faced me and then made me repay him? / All that is beneath the whole sky belongs to me.
41:12 I will not keep quiet about its limbs, / about its strength and the beauty of its build.
41:13 Who has stripped off its outer clothing? / Who could get between its double jaws?
41:14 The doors of its face: who has pried them open? / Its teeth are ringed with dread.
41:15 Its pride is its rows of shields, / locked shut with a tight seal.
41:16 They press up against each other, one to one; / not a breath of wind slips through.
41:17 Each is welded to its brother; / they grip one another and will not come apart.
41:18 When it sneezes, light flares out; / its eyes are like the eyelids of dawn.
41:19 Torches shoot from its mouth; / fiery sparks escape.
41:20 From its nostrils smoke rolls out, / like a pot fanned to a boil, or bulrushes on fire.
41:21 Its breath makes coals catch fire; / a blaze goes out from its mouth.
41:22 Power makes its home in its neck, / and dread goes leaping ahead of it.
41:23 Its fleshy folds hang tight together, / cast solid on it, unbudging.
41:24 Its heart is cast like stone, / solid as the bottom millstone.
41:25 When it rears up, the gods are afraid; / they lose their wits as it thrashes.
41:26 Strike it with a sword, and the sword will not stick, / nor will spear or arrow or lance.
41:27 To it, iron is just straw, / and bronze is wood gone rotten.
41:28 No arrow makes it flee; / sling stones turn to husks against it.
41:29 A war club counts as husks to it; / it laughs when the javelin whirs.
41:30 Beneath it are jagged bits of pottery; / it drags a threshing sled across the mud.
41:31 It brings the deep to a boil, like a cooking pot; / it turns the sea into a perfume-maker's pan.
41:32 Behind it a path glows bright; / someone would think the deep had gone gray.
41:33 There is nothing like it on all the earth's dust, / a creature made to be fearless.
41:34 It stares down every lofty thing; / it is king of all the proud creatures."
## Job's last words
42:1 Then Job answered the LORD. He said:
42:2 "I know there is nothing you cannot do, / and nothing you intend can be held back from you.
42:3 'Who is this who hides the plan without knowing anything?' / So I talked without understanding, / about wonders beyond me that I did not know.
42:4 'Hear me, please, and I will do the talking. / I will ask you, and you tell me what you know.'
42:5 Before, I had only heard about you, / but now my own eye has seen you.
42:6 That is why I take back what I said, / and I change my mind about dust and ashes."
## The LORD rebukes the friends
42:7 When the LORD had finished saying these things to Job, he spoke to Eliphaz the Temanite. The LORD said, "I am furious with you and with your two friends. You did not say what is right about me, the way my servant Job did.
42:8 Now get yourselves seven bulls and seven rams. Go to my servant Job and offer them up as a burnt offering for yourselves. Job my servant will pray for you. I will accept him, and so I will not treat you with the disgrace your folly has earned. For you did not say what is right about me, the way my servant Job did."
42:9 So Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite went and did just as the LORD had spoken to them, and the LORD accepted Job.
## Job restored
42:10 When Job prayed for his friends, the LORD turned Job's fortunes around. The LORD added to everything Job had owned, until it was double.
42:11 His brothers came, all of them, and all his sisters, and everyone who knew him from earlier days. They shared bread with him in his house. They shook their heads with him and comforted him for all the harm the LORD had brought on him. Each one gave him one coin and one gold ring.
42:12 So the LORD blessed Job's later years more than his early ones. He came to have fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand pairs of oxen and a thousand female donkeys.
42:13 He came to have seven sons and three daughters.
42:14 He called the first one Jemimah, the second one Keziah, and the third Keren-happuch.
42:15 In the whole land no women could be found as beautiful as the daughters of Job. Their father gave them property to inherit side by side with their brothers.
42:16 Job lived on for a hundred and forty years after this. He lived to see his children and grandchildren, four generations of them.
42:17 Then Job died, old and satisfied with days.
