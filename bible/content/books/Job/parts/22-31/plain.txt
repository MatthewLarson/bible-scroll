## Eliphaz speaks
22:1 Then Eliphaz the Temanite spoke up:
22:2 "Can a human being be of any use to God? / The wise are of use only to themselves.
22:3 Does your being righteous please the Almighty? / Does he profit when you keep your ways wholehearted?
22:4 Is it because you fear him that he corrects you / and takes you to court?
22:5 Surely your evil is great, / and your wrongs have no end.
22:6 For you held your brothers' goods in pledge for nothing; / you took the very clothes off the half-dressed.
22:7 To the weary you gave not a drop of water; / from the hungry you kept back bread.
22:8 A man of muscle: the land was his. / A man of rank: he lived on it.
22:9 Widows you turned away with empty hands, / and the fatherless had their arms broken.
22:10 So traps surround you on every side, / and dread comes suddenly and shakes you,
22:11 or a darkness you cannot see through, / and a mass of waters closes over you.
## God sees through the clouds
22:12 Is God not on high, up in the heavens? / Look at the head of the stars: how high they stand!
22:13 So you say, 'What does God know? / Through the thick cloud, can he judge?
22:14 Clouds are his hiding place; he does not see. / He strolls about on the rim of the sky.'
22:15 Will you hold to the age-old road / where wicked men have walked?
22:16 They were seized when it was not yet their time; / a river poured over their foundations.
22:17 These were the ones who told God, 'Go away from us,' / and wondered what the Almighty would ever do to them.
22:18 Yet he was the one who had packed their homes with plenty. / The plans of the wicked are far from me!
22:19 The righteous watch and rejoice; / the innocent jeer at them:
22:20 'Surely those who rose against us are wiped out, / and fire has eaten what they had to spare.'
## Come back to the Almighty
22:21 Come to terms with him, and be at peace. / That is how good will come your way.
22:22 Take the law from his mouth, / and keep his sayings stored in your heart.
22:23 Come back to the Almighty, and you will be rebuilt. / Put wrongdoing far from your tent.
22:24 Lay your gold ore in the dust; / throw Ophir's gold among the rocks of the wadis,
22:25 and then your gold ore will be the Almighty, / and your silver piled high.
22:26 For then the Almighty will be your joy, / and you will raise your face to God.
22:27 You will plead with him, and he will listen; / you will keep the vows you made.
22:28 Settle on a plan, and it will hold for you; / light will gleam along your roads.
22:29 When men are thrown down and you say, 'Up!' / he will save the one with lowered eyes.
22:30 He will set free a man who is guilty; / that man will go free because your hands are clean."
## Job speaks
23:1 Then Job spoke up:
23:2 "Still today my complaint is defiant; / his hand lies heavy on me, though I groan.
23:3 Would that someone could tell me how to find him, / how to get as far as his seat!
23:4 I would set out my lawsuit in front of him / and pack my mouth with pleas.
23:5 I would learn the words of his reply / and make out what he says to me.
23:6 Would he argue against me with his great power? / No, he would surely take notice of me.
23:7 There the upright could reason things out with him, / and I would walk free from my judge forever.
23:8 Look: I walk ahead, and he is missing; / I turn back, and I cannot make him out.
23:9 He is busy on the left, but I cannot see him; / he hides on the right, and he is nowhere in sight.
23:10 Yet he knows the road I am on. / Once he has assayed me, I will come out like gold.
23:11 In his footsteps my foot has kept its grip; / his road I have kept without turning off.
23:12 From the command of his lips I have not drawn back; / more than my own portion I have stored up the words of his mouth.
23:13 But he is one, and who can make him turn? / His soul wants a thing, and he does it.
23:14 For he finishes off the sentence passed on me, / and he holds many more like it.
23:15 So I shake with fear before his face; / I think it over, and I am afraid of him.
23:16 It is God who has made my heart go weak; / it is the Almighty who has thrown me into panic.
23:17 For it is not the darkness that has undone me, / nor the gloom that hides my face.
## The poor and the cruel
24:1 Why has the Almighty laid up no set times? / Why do the people who know him not get to see his days?
24:2 Men nudge the boundary stones along; / they carry off a flock and pasture it as their own.
24:3 The fatherless child's donkey they lead off; / the widow's ox they keep as security.
24:4 They shove the needy off the road; / all at once the land's poor go into hiding.
24:5 Look: wild donkeys in the wilderness, / out at their work, scouring for food; / the open steppe is the bread for their young.
24:6 Out in the field they cut fodder; / the wicked man's vineyard they pick over for leftovers.
24:7 Naked they pass the night, with no clothes, / and nothing to wrap round them when it is cold.
24:8 Downpours in the hills drench them; / having no shelter, they press themselves against the rock.
24:9 Others pull a fatherless baby from the breast / and take the poor man's goods in pledge.
24:10 Naked they trudge about, without a stitch; / starving, they lug the sheaves.
24:11 Between their rows of olives they press out oil; / they trample the grapes in the vats and go thirsty.
24:12 In the city men groan, / and the wounded gasp out cries for help, / yet God takes no notice of the wrong.
## Those who rebel against the light
24:13 These are among the rebels against the light. / They pay no heed to its ways; / they do not live along its paths.
24:14 At first light the murderer gets up; / he kills the poor and the needy, / and by night he turns thief.
24:15 The adulterer's eye waits for evening. / 'No eye will catch me,' he says, / and he masks his face.
24:16 In the dark one digs through into houses; / by day they seal themselves in; / light is a stranger to them.
24:17 Morning, for every one of them, is deep darkness; / they are at home with the terrors of the deep dark.
## The end of the wicked
24:18 He is carried off, light, across the waters; / their plot of ground is under a curse; / no one turns down the road to the vineyards.
24:19 Snowmelt is swallowed by drought and heat; / so is the sinner by Sheol.
24:20 The womb that bore him forgets him; / the maggot sucks at him; / no one thinks of him again; / wrong is snapped off like a tree.
24:21 He feeds on the barren woman who never gave birth, / and he has never done a widow good.
24:22 Yet by his power he hauls away the strong; / he gets up, and no one is sure of staying alive.
24:23 He lets them rest secure, and they lean on it, / but he keeps his eyes on their ways.
24:24 They rise high for a while, and he is gone; / they are brought down, and like everyone they shrink away; / like heads of grain they wither.
24:25 And if not, who can make me out a liar / and bring my speech to nothing?"
## Bildad speaks
25:1 Then Bildad the Shuhite spoke up:
25:2 "Rule and dread are with him; / in his heights he makes peace.
25:3 Is there any count of his troops? / Is there anyone his light does not rise on?
25:4 How then can a mortal be in the right with God? / How can anyone born of a woman come out clean?
25:5 Look, even the moon does not shine clear, / and to his eyes the stars are not clean,
25:6 how much less a human, a maggot, / a mortal, a worm!"
## Job answers Bildad
26:1 Then Job spoke up:
26:2 "What a help you have been to the powerless! / What a rescue for the arm with no strength in it!
26:3 What counsel you have given a man with no wisdom! / How lavishly you have shared good sense!
26:4 To whom do you owe these words? / Whose breath is it that comes out of you?
## The edges of his ways
26:5 The shades writhe / below the waters and those who live in them.
26:6 Sheol lies naked before him; / there is no cover over Abaddon.
26:7 Over the empty waste he spreads the north; / on nothing at all he hangs the earth.
26:8 He bundles water up inside his clouds, / and the cloud does not split beneath it.
26:9 He screens off the sight of his throne, / spreading his cloud across it.
26:10 He has traced a ring across the surface of the waters, / where light and darkness reach their end.
26:11 Heaven's pillars totter / and stand amazed when he rebukes them.
26:12 By his power he quelled the sea; / by his skill he struck Rahab down.
26:13 His wind made the heavens bright; / his hand ran through the fleeing snake.
26:14 Look, these are the mere edges of his ways, / and a faint whisper of a word is all we catch of him. / Who could take in his thunder in its full might?"
## Job's oath: I will not let go
27:1 Job took up his poem again and said:
27:2 "By the life of God, who has stripped me of my right, / and of the Almighty, who has soured my very self,
27:3 for while there is still life's breath in me, / and God's wind is in my nostrils,
27:4 no wrong will pass my lips, / no deceit will my tongue whisper.
27:5 Never will I grant that you are right! / Until the day I die I will not set my integrity aside.
27:6 I have a grip on my righteousness, and I will not loosen it; / my heart finds no fault with any day of my life.
27:7 Let my enemy share the fate of the wicked, / and my assailant the fate of the wrongdoer.
27:8 For what is left for the godless to hope for / once he is cut off, once God draws his life out of him?
27:9 When trouble falls on him, / will God hear him crying out?
27:10 Is the Almighty his delight? / Does he call on God whatever the hour?
27:11 Let me teach you what is in God's hand; / I will not hide what the Almighty has in mind.
27:12 You have all seen it with your own eyes. / So why are you breathing out this empty air?
## The wicked man's share
27:13 This is the share a wicked person has with God, / the inheritance tyrants take from the Almighty:
27:14 His children may be many, but the sword will have them, / and his descendants will go short of bread.
27:15 Those he leaves behind will be buried by plague, / and no widow of his will weep.
27:16 He may pile silver as high as the dust / and stock up clothes like mounds of clay;
27:17 he stores it, and a righteous man wears it; / the silver goes to the innocent to share out.
27:18 Like a moth he built his house, / like a lean-to a watchman puts together.
27:19 Rich he lies down, but there will be no next time; / his eyes come open, and he is not there.
27:20 Like water, terrors catch up with him; / in the night a storm steals him away.
27:21 The east wind picks him up, and off he goes; / it whisks him away from where he stood.
27:22 It flings itself on him and does not spare him, / while he runs and runs to get out of its hand.
27:23 They clap their hands at him / and whistle him off from his place.
## Silver has its mine
28:1 For there is a place where silver comes out of the ground, / and a place for the gold that people refine.
28:2 From the dust iron is taken, / and stone is melted down into copper.
28:3 The miner sets an end to darkness, / and he probes to the farthest edge / for stone in gloom and deep dark.
28:4 He breaks open a shaft far from any settler, / in places forgotten by the foot; / the miners hang there, far from humankind, and sway.
28:5 The earth: bread grows out of it, / and under it, all is churned as fire churns.
28:6 Down in its stones is the home of the sky-blue gem, / and there is gold dust in it.
28:7 It is a track the bird of prey has never learned, / and the kite's eye has not caught sight of it.
28:8 The beasts that strut have not walked it, / nor has the lion ever padded over it.
28:9 Against the flint the miner sets his hand / and tips the mountains up from their roots.
28:10 Through the rocks he splits open tunnels, / and his eye lights on every precious thing.
28:11 He binds up the rivers so they do not seep, / and what lay hidden he brings out into the light.
## Where can wisdom be found?
28:12 But wisdom: where can it be found? / Where does understanding have its home?
28:13 No human knows what it is worth, / and the land of the living does not have it.
28:14 'It is not in me,' says the Deep; / 'I do not have it,' says the Sea.
28:15 No fine gold can be handed over for it, / no silver weighed out to pay for it.
28:16 Ophir's gold will not pay for it, / nor rare carnelian, nor the sky-blue gem.
28:17 Gold and glass are no match for it, / nor can vessels of fine gold buy it.
28:18 Coral and crystal do not even come to mind; / getting wisdom costs more than pearls.
28:19 Set Cush's topaz beside it, and it falls short; / pure gold is not the coin to weigh it with.
28:20 But wisdom: where does it come from? / Where does understanding have its home?
28:21 It is veiled from the eyes of all that lives, / and from the birds of the heavens it has been kept secret.
28:22 Abaddon and Death say, / 'With our own ears we have heard a report of it.'
## God knows the way to it
28:23 God has the insight into its road; / he is the one who knows its whereabouts.
28:24 For his gaze reaches the ends of the earth; / he sees all that lies under the whole sky.
28:25 When he fixed how much the wind would weigh / and meted out the waters by measure,
28:26 when he gave the rain its law / and a road for the thundercloud,
28:27 then he saw it and took its measure; / he fixed it firm and probed it through.
28:28 And he said to humankind, / 'Look: the fear of the Lord is wisdom, / and to keep away from evil, that is understanding.'"
## Job remembers the old days
29:1 Job took up his poem again and said:
29:2 "Who will make me as in the old months, / as in the days of God's watching over me,
29:3 when his lamp was shining above my head / and his light carried me through the dark,
29:4 as I was in my harvest days, / when God's close friendship was over my tent,
29:5 when I still had the Almighty with me, / and my lads were all about me,
29:6 when my feet waded in curds, / and the rock poured streams of oil out beside me!
## Honored at the city gate
29:7 When I left home for the gate above the town / and set up my seat in the open square,
29:8 the young caught sight of me and kept out of the way; / the aged got to their feet and stayed standing;
29:9 leaders held back their words / and put a hand over their mouths;
29:10 the nobles' voices hid away, / and each tongue stuck to the palate.
29:11 Any ear that heard me called me blessed, / and any eye that saw me spoke up for me,
29:12 for when the poor cried out, I came to their rescue, / and to the fatherless who had no helper.
29:13 The blessing of the dying came on me, / and I gave the widow's heart a song.
29:14 I put on righteousness, and it put me on; / like a cloak and a turban was my justice.
29:15 To the blind I was eyes; / to the lame I was feet.
29:16 For the needy I was a father, / and I looked into the case of someone I did not know.
29:17 I smashed the jaws of the wrongdoer / and tore the prey out of his teeth.
## The life he expected
29:18 And I told myself, 'Along with my nest I will breathe my last, / and I will make my days as many as the sand.
29:19 My root is open to the water; / the dew spends the night in my branches.
29:20 My honor is always new in me, / and the bow in my hand will keep its spring.'
29:21 To me they listened, and they waited; / they fell silent for my counsel.
29:22 Once I had spoken, no one answered back; / my words fell on them drop by drop.
29:23 Like rain they waited for me, / and their mouths gaped wide as for the spring showers.
29:24 I laughed with them, and they would not believe it; / the light of my face they never let drop.
29:25 I chose their course and sat as their head; / I made my home among them like a king with his army, / like someone who brings the mourners comfort.
## Mocked by outcasts
30:1 And now they make fun of me, / people younger than I am, / whose fathers I thought too low / to rank with the dogs that guard my sheep.
30:2 Their strong hands, what were they to me? / All their vigor was spent.
30:3 Gaunt with want and hunger, / they chew the dry ground / in the gloom of waste and ruin.
30:4 They strip the salty orache by the bushes; / broom roots are their bread.
30:5 From the community they are driven out; / people holler at them like thieves.
30:6 They must make their homes on the slopes of the wadis, / in cracks in the earth, and in the rocks.
30:7 In the scrub they bray like donkeys; / they crowd together beneath the thistles.
30:8 Sons of fools, sons without a name, / they were lashed and driven from the land.
30:9 And now I am their mocking song; / to them I am a joke people tell.
30:10 They find me disgusting and stay far off; / they do not spare my face their spit.
30:11 He has slackened my cord and brought me low, / so they throw off the bridle in front of me.
30:12 On the right a brood rises up; / they send my feet sliding / and heap their roads of ruin up against me.
30:13 They wreck my path; / they work for my downfall, and it pays them; / they need nobody's help.
30:14 They come as if a wall had broken wide open; / they tumble in under the wreckage.
30:15 Terrors are rolled against me; / like the wind it hunts down my honor, / and my rescue has drifted off like a cloud.
## You have become cruel to me
30:16 And now my life spills out over me; / days of misery have laid hold of me.
30:17 By night my bones are bored through, / and the pains that gnaw at me never lie down.
30:18 With great force my clothes are pulled out of shape; / like the neck of my shirt, he wraps me tight.
30:19 He has flung me down into the mud, / and I am no better than dust and ashes.
30:20 I call out to you, and you give me no answer; / I stand there, and you just look me over.
30:21 You change into someone cruel to me; / with your strong hand you bear me a grudge.
30:22 You raise me into the wind and set me riding on it; / you dissolve me in the crashing storm.
30:23 For I know it: to death you will bring me back, / to the house where all the living meet.
30:24 Surely, from the rubble, a man stretches out his hand, / and in his disaster he calls for help.
30:25 Did I not weep for the man having a hard day? / Was I not sick at heart over the needy?
30:26 I looked for good, and evil arrived; / I hoped for light, and gloom set in.
30:27 My insides churn and will not be still; / days of misery stand in my way.
30:28 I go about in black, where no sun shines; / in the assembly I get up and plead for help.
30:29 Jackals are my brothers now, / ostriches my companions.
30:30 Black is my skin, and it comes off me; / my frame is scorched with the heat.
30:31 My harp is given over to mourning, / my flute to the sound of people weeping.
## Job swears he is innocent
31:1 I cut a covenant with my eyes. / So how could I fix my gaze on a young girl?
31:2 For what does God above hand out as a share? / What inheritance comes from the Almighty on high?
31:3 Does calamity not belong to the wrongdoer, / and misfortune to those who work harm?
31:4 Is he not watching my ways, / counting all my steps?
31:5 If I have kept company with falsehood, / if my foot rushed off toward deceit,
31:6 let him set me on the scales of righteousness, / and God will recognize my integrity.
31:7 If my steps have strayed off the road, / or my heart has gone where my eyes led, / or a blot has clung to my hands,
31:8 then may I sow and someone else eat, / and may my crops be torn out by the roots.
31:9 If a woman has led my heart astray, / or I crouched waiting by my neighbor's doorway,
31:10 then may my wife turn another man's millstone, / and may other men crouch over her.
31:11 That is a shameful outrage, / a crime for the judges.
31:12 It would be a fire eating its way down to Abaddon, / ripping out by the roots everything I had grown.
## Slaves, the poor and the fatherless
31:13 If I waved away the case of my slave or my slave woman / when they had a quarrel with me,
31:14 what could I do when God stood up? / When he examined me, what would I say back to him?
31:15 The one who made me in the belly, did he not make him too? / Did not one Maker shape us both in the womb?
31:16 If I have kept from the poor what they longed for, / or made the widow's eyes grow dim,
31:17 if I have eaten my piece of bread by myself, / and the fatherless got none of it,
31:18 for since I was young he has grown up with me as if I were his father, / and I have been her guide since I left my mother's womb,
31:19 if I ever watched someone dying because he had no clothes, / or a needy man with nothing to cover him,
31:20 if his loins did not bless me / since my sheep's wool kept him warm,
31:21 if I have brandished my hand over the fatherless / because I saw I had backing in the gate,
31:22 then may my shoulder drop from its blade, / and my arm snap off at the elbow.
31:23 For disaster from God was a terror to me, / and before his majesty I could do nothing.
## Gold, the sun, enemies and guests
31:24 If I have leaned on gold for my confidence, / or told fine gold, 'You are what keeps me safe,'
31:25 if I was glad that I had great wealth, / that my hand had come by so much,
31:26 if I watched the light as it blazed, / or the moon as it walked in glory,
31:27 and in secret my heart was taken in, / and my hand blew a kiss from my mouth,
31:28 this too is a crime for the judges, / since I would have denied the God above.
31:29 If I was glad when someone who hated me was ruined, / or thrilled when harm found him,
31:30 no, I never gave my palate leave to sin / by cursing him and asking for his life,
31:31 if those who lived in my tent never said, / 'Show us anyone who has not eaten his fill of his meat!'
31:32 Out in the street no foreigner passed the night; / my doors I opened onto the road,
31:33 if I have hidden my wrongs as Adam did, / by burying my guilt in my bosom,
31:34 because I feared the great crowd, / and the scorn of the clans terrified me, / and I held my tongue and stayed inside my door,
## Let the Almighty answer me
31:35 If only someone would give me a hearing! / Here is my mark: now let the Almighty answer me! / Let the man who brings the case against me write it down!
31:36 I would hoist it onto my shoulder; / I would wrap it around my head like crowns.
31:37 I would count out for him every step I have taken; / I would walk up to him like a prince.
## If my land cries out
31:38 If the soil I farm has cried out over me, / and its furrows have joined in weeping,
31:39 if I have eaten what it grew and paid no money for it, / or squeezed the breath out of its owners,
31:40 then in place of wheat let thorns come up, / and in place of barley, reeking weeds." / Here Job's words come to an end.
