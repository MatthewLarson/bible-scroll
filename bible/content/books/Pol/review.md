# Hostile review: Polycarp to the Philippians (`Pol`)

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-12. Scope: `content/books/Pol/` (plain.txt, notes.json, variants.json, glosses.json, chapters.json, topics.json, book.json, sources.json) and the topic cores `polycarp`, `ignatius`, `irenaeus`, `marcion` in `content/proposals/works/topics/`. Checked against `texts/orig/Pol.json`, `texts/src/Pol.app.json`, Lake's scan (`.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers01lake.txt`, lines 14403-15500, which carries footnotes the app.json lacks), Lightfoot (`texts/en/Pol.json`), the site's own plain.txt for every quoted verse, and, this session, New Advent: Eusebius, Church History 3.36, 4.14, 5.20; Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.3.4; Jerome, Lives 16, 17, 35; Ignatius, To Polycarp 7-8 and To the Smyrnaeans 2, 8, 13; the Catholic Encyclopedia "St. Polycarp". `node tools/build.js --check Pol` is 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The translation is sound. I checked all 38 verses against Lake's Greek and the Latin, word by word, and found no wrong tense, name or number, no omitted clause that matters, and no invented source, reading or fragment (so no critical findings). The New Testament quotations in plain.txt use the site's wording exactly where claimed (Acts 2:24, 1 Peter 1:8, Ephesians 2:8, Matthew 7:1-2, 26:41, Ephesians 4:26, 1 Peter 2:22-24, Tobit 4:10), and the variant entries match Lake's footnotes. The book's problems are in sourcing discipline, which the batch rulings make mandatory. Much of the one-letter or two-letter question, the Marcion debate, the dating, the calendars and the Dilexi te item rests on "as Wikipedia reports" relays. There are several unsourced "first / earliest / oldest" claims. Several views are credited to "many", "most" or "some" scholars with no named holder. There is a hedge in a note title ("from memory?"). One word in the translation (1:2 "held out") contradicts the book's own note and its term for the word family. The rest is small: a few quotation mismatches, a miscounted "three times", a term collision at 10:3 and a thin canon block.

Findings: 0 critical, 5 major, 17 minor.

## Major

1. **Wikipedia relays throughout (batch ruling: a view must rest on a source opened and named, or be cut).** Wikipedia was opened, but the content repeats it as "as Wikipedia reports". Locations:
   - book.json: facts "Author" ("Wikipedia's article, citing Hartog"); facts "Date" ("as Wikipedia reports", Eusebius's Chronicle and Ignatius); authorship "Most scholars today"; audience `views` ("as Wikipedia reports"); `audience.s` and `canon.s` carry `wp-polycarp-philippians-nov2026`; section "The Greek, Eusebius and the Latin" (Latin in "thirteen or fourteen manuscripts", "a Greek text older than the Greek manuscripts' ancestor", Syriac lines); section "One letter or two?" ("continues to enjoy widespread support", "a few think the covering letter a later forgery"); spec ("Crisis Letter (reported by Wikipedia)"); canon `accepted_by` for Catholic, Anglican and Lutheran ("as Wikipedia reports", source `wp-saints-calendars-nov2026`).
   - notes.json: #32 (Butterfield dissertation, "eight early Christian texts"), #43 (Pope Leo XIV, Dilexi te, "as Wikipedia's article reports"), #54 (Harrison and "many scholars find it unconvincing"), #65 and #96 ("most widely accepted", "some continue to argue for unity", "later forgery"), #74 ("Crisis Letter", "as Wikipedia's article reports").
   - chapters.json: chapter 6 `C` (Leo XIV), chapter 7 deep, chapter 9 deep, chapter 11 `H`, chapter 13 deep (names Pervo and Barnes, Harrison's 135-137), chapter 13 settings `views`, and the `s` lists citing `wp-polycarp-philippians-nov2026`.
   - Topic cores: `polycarp` section 2 ("as Wikipedia's articles on these calendars report").
   
   Fix: for each, either open the primary work (Harrison 1936; Hartog 2013; Pervo; Barnes; Butterfield; the Vatican text of Dilexi te, paragraph 39; the Church of England, ELCA/LCMS and Roman calendars themselves) and cite it, or cut the claim. Where the claim stays only as "Harrison argued X (1936)", say that and drop "widespread", "most widely accepted" and "many scholars". The calendar facts are probably true: the writer's own fetched pages show the Roman memorial, the CofE "Bishop of Smyrna, Martyr, c.155" and the Lutheran "Commemoration" entries. They should be cited to the calendars. The Lutheran calendar prints 156, not Lake's 155, which is worth a clause. Remove `wp-*` keys from `sources.json` once nothing rests on them. A source key that names Wikipedia as the authority for a scholarly view should not stay.

2. **plain.txt 1:2 "He held out for our sins" contradicts note #8 and the book's own term.** Note #8 (title "Endured for our sins") says "The Plain English Translation keeps 'endure' and 'endurance' for this word family, as the site's Romans and 4 Maccabees do", and cites 8:1 ("endured everything") and 8:2, 9:1 ("endurance"). The term entry for ὑπομονή/ὑπομένω is "endure / endurance" (terms.json; proposals/works/terms.json). Greek ὑπέμεινεν. Fix: "He endured for our sins until he reached death itself." (The site's 2 Timothy 2:12 "if we hold out" is a different verb choice in a different book and does not justify this one.)

3. **Unsourced "first / earliest / oldest / one window" claims (batch ruling: only if credited to an opened source).**
   - "the first known collection of Ignatius's letters": book.json lead; chapters.json chapter 13 summary; notes.json #95 title and `p`; topic core `ignatius` ("the first known collection of them"). Lake never says "first". He says Polycarp sends copies of the Ignatian epistles he had (intro, lines 14420-14430).
   - "the first church Paul had founded in Europe": book.json lead, notes.json #2 `p`. Acts 16 does not say it, and no opened source is given.
   - cited_in `Philippians 1:1`: "The two letters are the earliest evidence for leadership at Philippi."
   - topics.json `philippi`: "Polycarp's letter is the one window on the church at Philippi after the New Testament."
   - topic core `ignatius`: seven letters "are among the oldest Christian writings after the New Testament" (no source listed; its `sources` is only `eusebius-history`).
   - chapters.json chapter 2 summary: "among the most-studied lines in the letter".
   
   Fix: delete the superlative, or credit a named opened source with its words.

4. **Views credited to "many / most / generally / some" without named holders (batch ruling).**
   - facts "Author": "Scholars generally agree the letter is his".
   - authorship heading "Most scholars today".
   - reliability: "generally accepted as Polycarp's", "generally dated within Polycarp's lifetime".
   - facts "Date": "some scholars date Ignatius to the 130s or 140s". The Ignatius topic core says "as some scholars argue, somewhat later". Chapter 13 names Pervo and Barnes, but only through Wikipedia (finding 1).
   - notes #54 and chapter 7 deep: "many scholars have found the argument unconvincing".
   - notes #96: "some, Wikipedia notes, argue the covering letter is a later forgery". No holder is named.
   
   Fix: name the scholar and the opened work, or say only what Lake and the Catholic Encyclopedia (both opened) say. The Catholic Encyclopedia's own list (anti-episcopalians of the 17th century, the Tübingen School) is a properly sourced version of "who doubted it".

5. **Hedge language in content (STYLE rule 2).**
   - notes.json #29 title: "1 Timothy 6, from memory?".
   - book.json spec: "not a published one as far as the sources opened here show".
   - chapters.json chapter 13 deep: "Lake's introduction, as opened here".
   - Repeated settings `by`: "overseer of Smyrna (as later sources call him)". This one is sourced (Eusebius 3.36.1, Jerome 17) and could simply say "overseer of Smyrna, as Eusebius and Jerome call him". It is not wrong, but it is the same hedge shape across 14 chapters.
   
   Fix: retitle #29 "Love of money, after 1 Timothy 6" and cut the other phrases. A claim labeled "Claude's theory" needs no "as far as the sources show".

## Minor

6. **notes.json #6 quotes a verse of another book wrongly.** It says the Martyrdom "says Polycarp was 'crowned with the crown of immortality' (Martyrdom of Polycarp 17:1)". The site's MPo 17:1 reads "crowned with the crown of incorruption". The build does not catch this shape. Fix the quotation. Same note: Revelation 19:12 "many diadems" (site: "many royal crowns"). Note #70: Daniel 4:27 "by righteousness" (site: "by doing what is right"). Note #91: "those who persecute you" in quotes against Matthew 5:44 (site: "the people who persecute you"). Each should use the site wording or drop the quotation marks.

7. **notes.json #26 "Polycarp names Paul three times"** but the same note lists four places (3:2, 9:1, 11:2, 11:3). "Draws on more of his letters than of any other writer" is unsourced and unclear. Fix the count; cut or source the comparison.

8. **notes.json #19 "warns against it six times".** The note itself counts five Greek uses (2:2, 4:1, 4:3, 5:2, 6:1) plus two Latin uses of avaritia (11:1, 11:2), so "six" matches no count. The note's `p` also says six. Say "five times in the Greek and twice in the Latin".

9. **Marcion attribution to the Catholic Encyclopedia (note #54; chapter 7 deep).** The note says Lightfoot's reply is that nothing touches Marcion's teaching "(two Gods)" and that "loose living" does not fit. The Catholic Encyclopedia (opened) says "the antinomianism ascribed to 'the first-born of Satan' is inapplicable to the austere Marcion" and mentions the God of the Old and New Testament. "Two Gods" is the writer's addition and "loose living" paraphrases "antinomianism". Quote what the source says, and keep the paraphrase out of its mouth.

10. **plain.txt 10:3 "self-control" for sobrietas.** The site and this letter use "self-control" for ἐγκράτεια (4:2, 5:2) and "clear head"/"level head" for νήφω and sobrius (7:2, 11:4), and the gloss on 10:3 says "a clear and sober head". The text and its own gloss disagree and the key term collides. Fix: "teach everyone to keep the clear head you yourselves live in" (or similar), and drop the gloss or match it.

11. **plain.txt 12:2 "patience, forbearance, endurance".** The Latin is patientia, longanimitas, tolerantia. In the site's terms μακροθυμία is "patience" (Galatians 5:22 list) and ἐπιείκεια is "forbearance" (batch ruling). The three Latin words cannot be mapped back to Greek, and no note says so. Add a short note at 12:2, and take care that "forbearance" is not used for a word that would have been μακροθυμία (Lightfoot's English maps them differently: forbearance, long suffering, patient endurance).

12. **plain.txt 1:1 "the living copies of true love".** "Living" is not in the Greek (δεξαμένοις τὰ μιμήματα τῆς ἀληθοῦς ἀγάπης). The note admits the addition, but a Plain English Translation that adds a word to decide a debated reading (Lake: followed the pattern; Lightfoot: the followers of the true Love) should not, or should put the word in the gloss only. Suggest "the copies of true love" with the gloss.

13. **variants.json: no entry for Lake's footnote at 11:3.** Lake's footnote (scan line ~15318) proposes the lost Greek "τοῖς οὖσιν ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐπιστολαῖς αὐτοῦ", "who were his epistles in the beginning" (2 Corinthians 3:2), and Lightfoot's English follows it (checked in texts/en/Pol.json: "who were his letters in the beginning"). The plain.txt follows the Latin ("you who are at the beginning of his letter"). The note #80 explains it but a reader who wants the readings side by side has no entry. Add one (witnesses: L; Lake's conjecture; Lightfoot). Not a departure from Lake's text, so not required by the ruling, but a careful reader should see it.

14. **notes.json #62 "'Blessed' (makarios) was used of the faithful dead."** No source. Cite one opened source or cut.

15. **notes.json #7 "Psalm 18:5 in the Hebrew numbering".** The site's Psalm 18:5 is the English-versification verse ("The ropes of Sheol were wound around me"). The Hebrew numbering of that line is 18:6. Say "the site's numbering".

16. **chapters.json settings and views stated as fact where the letter is silent.** Chapter 12 settings `why`: "To ask a church angry over Valens to keep its anger from turning to sin." The letter never says Philippi was angry. Chapter 1 `C`: "A church that escorted prisoners was taking a risk: Christians walking with condemned men could be marked too" has no source. Chapter 2 `C` "the Philippians would soon have to judge". Chapter 11 note #74 "The 'once' suggests he no longer held the office". Soften to "may" and label the inference, or cite.

17. **Canon block rows.** The brief for tier `early` asks for one row per church family (Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Ethiopian, Anglican, Lutheran and Reformed, Baptist and Free churches), each from a source, or dropped. Present: Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Ethiopian, Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed. Missing without comment: other Oriental Orthodox (Coptic, Armenian, Syriac), Baptist and Free churches. "Reformed and other Protestant" rests on Westminster alone, so "other Protestant" is unsourced. The Lutheran row names no confessional source for the canon, only a calendar. Add rows with sources, or retitle rows to what the source covers.

18. **canon `reliability` section 4 says "Conflicts: none found."** The block says (sensibly) that 10:2 "alms rescue from death" goes beyond the 66 and that churches differ, but the brief asks for each church's reading, not a pointer. The site's cards at Proverbs 16 and Tobit 4 carry them. Add one sentence per side, or state that the four-way reading is on the cards, and avoid "none found" for a verse a Protestant reader may call a conflict with Ephesians 2:8-9.

19. **notes.json #70 and canon reliability: "Protestants do not count it as Scripture."** Anglicans and Lutherans, in the confessions the site's own cards use, read Tobit "for example of life" without counting it Scripture; Reformed confessions reject it. One sentence naming the confessions (the writer fetched Article VI and the Westminster Confession) would be fairer than the single word "Protestants".

20. **notes.json #95 and #24 depend on Lake's footnotes that are missing from `texts/src/Pol.app.json`.** Lake's footnotes at 6:1, 7:1, 11:3, 12:1 and 12:3 exist in the scan (verified: they say what the notes quote), but the app.json has only eight notes. The writer's slips.md already lists this for the lead. Confirm the lead has the list (the fix belongs in `tools/`, not in this book).

21. **Topic core `irenaeus`: "(about AD 180)" and topic core `polycarp`: "Irenaeus (about AD 180)"** carry no source. The Jerome quotation in `irenaeus` ("a presbyter under Pothinus the bishop who ruled the church of Lyons") is accurate (Lives 35, checked), but the writer's sources-checked.md lists only Lives 16-17. Give the chapter number, or open the Irenaeus date source.

22. **`ignatius` aka "Ignatius the God-Bearer"** has no source and the core's sources omit the letters and Lake. Cut it or source it (Ignatius's own greeting "Theophorus" is in the letters the writer opened).
