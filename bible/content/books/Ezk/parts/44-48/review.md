# Hostile review: Ezekiel 44-48

Reviewer's scope: plain.txt (138 verses) checked verse by verse against texts/orig/Ezk.json and `node tools/literal.js Ezk`; Greek read in Swete (Vaticanus) with `python tools/lxx.py Eze`; scrolls checked with `tools/dss.py` (confirmed: no scroll preserves 44-48); notes, variants, glosses, chapters, topics and sources read in full; the 40-43 card and book.json Temple card compared. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Ezk/44-48` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings. Web checks: Menachot 45a (Sefaria), Rashi on 44:2 (Sefaria), the Orthodox Vespers readings (OCA).

## Verdict

The part is clean mechanically and mostly careful in its facts, but it is not ready. Three places in the Plain silently choose or smooth a hard Hebrew text without telling the reader (47:18 "Tamar", 45:7 "each part", 48:9/48:13 "the part of it" and the unflagged 10,000 total). About a dozen verses still keep the NIV/ESV sentence skeleton after the rebuild. The chapter-44 "witnesses read" paragraph admits unchecked work ("I did not have Ziegler's apparatus open") while the notes and variants still cite Ziegler. Both traditions cards are weaker than the 40-43 card and the book-level Temple card: the Orthodox answer on the shut gate is true but unsourced, the Baptist answer is unsourced, and the chapter 45 sacrifices card duplicates the 40-43 card with a different `top`, omits the Orthodox, and uses duplicate source keys. The Orthodox Vespers claim checks out and should be kept with a source. Rashi on 44:2 and most of the Menachot 45a claims check out. The land arithmetic of 48:8-20 works once the totals are read with the Greek, but the Plain as written does not say so everywhere.

Counts: 0 critical, 7 major, 14 minor.

## Major findings

### 1. 47:18 silently emends the Hebrew (plain.txt 47:18; notes.json 47:18 "Seas and a wadi"; variants.json lacks an entry)
The MT reads `תָּמֹדּוּ` (tammodu), "you shall measure": "the Jordan, from the border, to the eastern sea you shall measure". The Plain says "as far as Tamar". The Greek has "Phoinikon" (palm-grove, i.e. Tamar), and Tamar appears at 47:19 and 48:28, so the emendation is reasonable. But `literal.js` prints "Tamar" for this word, so the writer apparently never saw the MT form. No variant and no note says the Plain has changed the text. The brief forbids hiding a crux in smooth English and names Tamar for checking.
Fix: add a variant at 47:18 (MT tammodu "you shall measure" against Greek Phoinikon / the Tamar reading, with Swete named), say in the note that the Plain follows the Tamar reading and why, and say it in the "follow" reason. Do not call it the Hebrew.

### 2. 45:7 misstates the geometry (plain.txt 45:7)
"Each part runs the same length as a tribal portion, from the western border to the eastern border." The Hebrew has one clause: the length matches one of the tribal portions, "from the western border to the eastern border". The two leader's parts (one west, one east of the district and the city) together with the district fill that width. Each part alone does not run from sea to Jordan. The Plain's "each" is wrong and makes 45:7 contradict 48:21.
Fix: "Together with them, the length matches a tribal portion, from the western border to the eastern border."

### 3. Unflagged additions and totals in the land sums (plain.txt 48:9, 48:13; variants.json 48:9; notes.json 48:8)
- 48:9 Hebrew has `hatterumah asher tarimu laYHWH`, the same phrase as 48:8 (where the width is 25,000), with width 10,000. The Plain adds "The part of it", which the Hebrew does not have, and so removes an internal contradiction of the MT without saying so. The variant's "why" asserts the Hebrew "describes the priests' strip", which is the commentators' harmonization, not what the Hebrew says. Mark it as the Plain's reading (a note) or drop "part of it" and let the note explain.
- 48:13 MT: "all the length 25,000 and the width 10,000" (`kol-orekh ... verochav asereth alafim`). The Plain follows it ("In all, the length is 25,000 and the width 10,000"), but the sum of the two strips of 48:10 and 48:13 is 20,000. The Greek reads "πᾶν τὸ μῆκος ... καὶ εὖρος εἴκοσι χιλιάδες" (checked in Swete). There is no variant and no note at 48:13, and the 48:8 note does not mention it. The reader sees "in all ... 10,000" two verses before "the whole contribution is a square" (48:20).
- 48:10 Greek omits the east side ("north 25,000, west 10,000, south 25,000"); not mentioned.
Fix: variant at 48:13 (MT 10,000 against Greek 20,000, with the arithmetic 10,000 + 10,000 + 5,000 = 25,000), a sentence in the 48:8 note naming 48:13 as a third place where the MT total fails, and a note or variant for "the part of it" at 48:9.
Arithmetic otherwise confirmed: 45:6/48:15 city strip 5,000 x 25,000; 48:16-17 city 4,500 each side plus 250 open land each side = 5,000; 4 x 4,500 = 18,000 (48:35); remainder 25,000 - 5,000 = 20,000, 10,000 east and 10,000 west (48:18); seven tribes north plus five south = twelve strips.

### 4. Residual NIV/ESV sentence shape (112 verses rebuilt; these still match)
Hebrew forces much of the order, but the following keep the skeleton or key phrases of the published versions, which the brief says to check. I quote only a few words.
- 47:5: "a river I was not able to cross. The water had risen until it was water to swim in, a river no one could cross." Same four-part shape and wording as the NIV ("a river that I could not cross ... risen ... swim in ... a river that no one could cross"). Rebuild, e.g. "a river I could not wade across, for the water had risen. It was swimming water, a river that could not be crossed on foot", or split it into short sentences.
- 47:10: "Fishermen will stand along the shore" is the NIV's supplied phrase (Hebrew only has "stand by it" and "sea" is the antecedent). "a place for spreading nets" is the ESV rendering. Rewrite ("Fishermen will stand beside the sea ...").
- 47:8: "eastern region" is the NIV/ESV word for `haggelilah haqqadmonah` and "flows ... goes down into the Arabah" keeps both versions' clause order; the variant itself glosses the Hebrew "eastern district". Use "district".
- 47:2: "led me out through the north gate and around the outside to the outer gate that faces east. There the water was trickling out" tracks the ESV and NIV ("trickling").
- 46:2: nearly clause for clause the ESV order (enter by the porch from outside, stand by the post, priests offer his burnt offering and peace offerings, worship at the threshold, go out, gate not shut until evening).
- 45:19: "put it on the doorposts of the house, on the four corners of the ledge around the altar, and on the posts of the gate of the inner courtyard" is the ESV list almost word for word.
- 46:9: first sentence matches the ESV/NIV "enters by the north gate ... go out by the south gate" pattern. Keep the pairing but vary the verbs.
- 44:30: "first of your dough ... a blessing will settle on your home" and "every contribution of every kind out of all your contributions" follow the ESV.
- 44:2 first two sentences ("must stay shut. It is not to be opened, and no one may go through it") follow the NIV; 44:7 ("brought in foreigners ... to be in my sanctuary and to profane it ... when you offered up my food, the fat and the blood") the ESV; 44:16 and 46:16 are close to the ESV but nearly forced.
- 46:6-7 and 47:6 ("Then he led me back to the bank of the river") are identical to the ESV in a clause.
The rest of the rebuilt verses are acceptable. Fix: rewrite at least the first seven.

### 5. Reader-facing admission of unchecked work and unread sources (chapters.json ch. 44 "deep" first paragraph; ziegler-ezekiel citations)
The paragraph says "I did not have Ziegler's apparatus open, so the notes name only the Greek that Swete prints" and says Thackeray and Tov "are cited in the notes". They are not cited anywhere in notes.json. Notes (45:5, 46:22, 47:8, 47:9, 48:8) and variants nevertheless list `ziegler-ezekiel` as a source for readings that were taken from Swete. The brief and the lead decision forbid admitting unchecked work in reader text and require "the Greek" to mean Swete's Vaticanus unless Ziegler or P967 was read. Also several notes and views say "the Greek" with no qualifier (45:12, 46:14 "The Greek has the same", 46:22, ch. 47 views "The Greek reads 'Galilee'").
Fix: rewrite the paragraph in the neutral form used by the other parts ("The Greek means Swete's text of Codex Vaticanus; Ziegler's edition and Papyrus 967 were not read for this part. No Qumran scroll preserves any verse of 44-48 (checked with tools/dss.py)"), drop Thackeray and Tov, remove `ziegler-ezekiel` from every entry that rests on Swete only, and write "Swete's Greek (Vaticanus)" wherever "the Greek" is used. Also remove "the Hebrew word list used here" and "The word list used for this Plain" (46:22 note): say "the Leningrad Codex".

### 6. Card at 44: the shut gate and Mary (chapters.json ch. 44 traditions)
The conflict is real and the card is the right one. Problems:
- Orthodox: "Ezekiel 43:27 to 44:4 at the Vespers of Marian feasts" is TRUE (verified: the Vespers readings for the Nativity of the Theotokos are Genesis 28:10-17, Ezekiel 43:27-44:4 and Proverbs 9:1-11; OCA lectionary and OrthodoxWiki), but the entry has an empty source list. Keep the claim, name the Nativity of the Theotokos (8 September) and the three readings, and add a source key (the Festal Menaion / OCA lectionary) to the entry. "Ever-Virgin (Aeiparthenos)" needs the church's own text (Constantinople II, 553, and the Orthodox hymns for the feasts), also with a key.
- Baptist: no source at all, and two factual claims ("No Baptist confession teaches it"; Baptists commonly take the "brothers" as Mary's later children). Source to a confession (the 1689 London Baptist Confession and the Baptist Faith and Message are silent on it) or to a named Baptist teacher, or soften.
- Reformed: the only source is Calvin on Matthew 1:25, but the key `calvin-harmony-gospels` is defined (in the 1 Maccabees sources) as a citation "on Matthew 24:15". Re-define or add a new key for the passage used. "The gate is commonly read as the LORD's own entry" is unsourced; Calvin's Commentary on Ezekiel at 44:2 would be the church-text source if read; otherwise say "reported by". The brief also asked for Zwingli's and Luther's own positions; the Reformed entry omits that early Reformed leaders held perpetual virginity.
- Lutheran: "Yes, as a doctrine" overstates; say the confessions call her "always virgin" (Smalcald Articles I.4; Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration VIII) and that the Lutheran churches do not tie it to Ezekiel 44:2.
- Catholic: fine (Catechism 499-501), but add that the gate reading is a figure in the Fathers (Ambrose, Jerome) and liturgy, not a defined doctrine, to match the Orthodox entry.
- Card-level `s` has only `jerome-helvidius`. Add the sources per entry.

### 7. Card at 45: sacrifices return (chapters.json ch. 45 traditions; book.json Temple card; 40-43 card)
- Duplicate and inconsistent with 40-43: the 40-43 card asks "Will animal sacrifices be offered again, as in Ezekiel 43:18-27?" with `top: sacrifice` and five views (Dispensationalist, Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran). The 44-48 card asks the same question about 44-46 with `top: temple` and four views, no Orthodox. The brief asks for the same `top` links across the Temple cards. The book.json card ("What is the Temple of Ezekiel 40-48?") is `top: temple`. The sacrifice cards should share one `top`, and the 45 card should either point to the 40-43 card ("see the card at 43") or add only what is new (45:18-20 purification twice a year, the leader's bull, 46:13-15 daily offering).
- Missing churches the brief required: Orthodox, Baptist (including Baptist premillennialists), postmillennial and Anglican; the ideal-blueprint (critical) view and the Jewish views are only in notes. The Orthodox answer is already on the 40-43 card and the book card; copy it.
- Sources: duplicate key for the same article (`whitcomb-sacrifices` here, `whitcomb-sacrifices-gtj` in 40-43). Reuse the earlier key; delete the new one from this sources.json. Same for `hoekema-future` (global, correct) against `hoekema-bible-future` (40-43 and Tob) and `catechism-catholic` against `ccc`; the 44-48 card uses `catechism-catholic` for CCC 1362-1367, the book card uses `ccc`. Use one key within Ezekiel. `s: []` at card level.
- The Catholic and Lutheran entries answer "no new sacrifices" from the Catechism and the Apology, which do not discuss Ezekiel; say so ("these texts do not mention Ezekiel; the answer follows from ...") and add Gregory the Great/Jerome from the book card to the Catholic entry.
- The Reformed entry says "Beale ... and Hoekema ... hold the same line". Hoekema's chapter on the millennium is not shown to treat Ezekiel 40-48; state it as "reported by" or cite the page.
- Dispensational entry: the book card says "Not every dispensationalist takes the sacrifices the same way"; repeat that, or the two cards seem to disagree.

## Minor findings

8. Menachot 45a. Verified (Sefaria): it cites Ezekiel 44:31 on priests and carcasses/torn animals, asks why 45:18 says "purify" for a burnt-offering bull, asks what "the seventh of the month" (45:20) refers to, has R. Not verified, remove or source: notes.json 45:20 "Rabbi Yochanan answers that it is for the community's bull of error (Leviticus 4:13-21)"; chapters.json ch. 44 "J" "the rabbis noticed that Ezekiel's rules on priestly marriage ... (Menachot 45a)" (no such discussion found); note 44:31 says the Talmud "asks why Ezekiel names only priests" (it cites the verse on whether priests may eat carcasses; reword to what it says). The "teku" in the brief is not in this daf; the writer correctly did not use it.

9. 45:9 "Remove violence and injustice": `chamas vashod` is "violence and plunder/destruction". "Injustice" is not in the Hebrew. The rest of the verse keeps the ESV order (Enough, leaders of Israel! ... justice and righteousness ... evictions).

10. 44:6 "the rebellious one" departs from the lead's fixed "rebellious house". The variant does explain it, and the MT really has `el-meri`. Add one sentence in a note at 44:6 (not only the variant) and in the chapter-page settings (which quote 'the rebellious one, the house of Israel') that the term elsewhere is "rebellious house".

11. Missing Greek variants (Swete): 45:12 (the Greek mina numbers differ; the note mentions it but there is no entry, and the brief names it); 47:18 (see 1); 48:10 (east side missing); 48:13 (see 3). 44:20 Greek "cover their heads" and 46:22 Greek "small" are in notes, which is fine.

12. Note 44:25 "Ezekiel gives the same list but leaves out the wife": Leviticus 21:2-3 does not list the wife either (rabbinic tradition adds her). As written it implies Leviticus has her. Rewrite.

13. ch. 44 "F" says "Reformed and Catholic readers hold that the priesthood's work is complete in Christ." Catholic teaching keeps a ministerial priesthood (Catechism 1536-1600). Say "the Levitical priesthood's work".

14. ch. 44 summary: "told it will stay shut for ever": the Hebrew has "shut", not "for ever". Delete "for ever" or say "stays shut".

15. Term consistency: "foreigners" renders both `ben nekhar` (44:7, 9) and `ger` (47:22-23) in the Plain, while note 47:22 insists they are different. terms.json sets `ger` as "foreigner who lives among you"; use "foreigners who live among you" consistently for `ger` and "foreigner" alone only for `ben nekhar` (44:7, 9 are "foreigner" and 47:22 already adds "who live among you", so this is mostly fine), or gloss the first use. Also `achuzzah` appears as "holding" (44:28; 46:16-18), "possession" (45:5, 45:8) and "property" (45:6-7, 48:20-22), and 48:11 says "kept guard over what I had given them to guard" where note 44:8 promises "charge every time (44:14, 15, 16; 48:11)". Fix 48:11 to "kept my charge" and pick one word for `achuzzah`.

16. Hard Hebrew with no note: 47:8 `hayyamah el-hayyamah hammutsa'im` ("to the sea, to the sea, the ones brought out") is flattened to "comes to the sea. When it is let out into the sea" without comment (Greek: "the water of the outlet"); 47:9 Plain "wherever the two rivers reach" has no note-level help for an 8th-grade reader (the note exists but the Plain is confusing); 47:13 "two measured portions": the Hebrew has `chavalim` (plural) with no "two"; "two" comes from 48:4-5. Say so in the note; 45:21 note does not mention that many commentators emend `shevu'ot` to `shiv'at` ("seven of"), the usual explanation.

17. topics.json `promised-land` says "thirteen parallel strips"; the text has twelve tribal strips (seven north, five south) plus the holy district. Note 47:12 (views) cites Zechariah 14:8 for the Mount of Olives splitting (that is 14:4).

18. Note 47:10 gives Ein Feshkha for En-eglaim with no source; other proposals are Ein Hajlah/Ein el-Ghuweir. Write "reported by" with a commentary, or "identified by some with".

19. Note 62 (47:13 views) says "Jewish readers differ too" without naming any view or source, though the brief asks for the Zionist, anti-Zionist, Christian Zionist and nonliteral readings with their holders. Add holders or cut the sentence.

20. Note 65 (48:1): "Few tribes lie where Joshua put them: Dan moved north in Judges 18, but Gad's old land was east of the Jordan" is muddled; say which tribes keep their old region (Asher, Naphtali, Benjamin, Judah roughly) and which move.

21. Small mechanics: note 47:8 `orig` has `haqqadmoni` (variants has the correct `haqqadmonah`); gloss 45:12 mina "around 600 grams" (60 shekels at about 11.4 g is about 680 g; say "about 700 grams"); gloss 47:8 Arabah "runs from Galilee down to the Red Sea" (say Gulf of Aqaba); mixed tenses in 46:4, 46:6, 46:8 ("brings", "goes in") against "will" elsewhere; note 18 (44:22) lists `bavli-shabbat` as source for a marriage rule it does not cover; note 50 (46:22) claims the root of `qeturot` "usually means 'make smoke'" and suggests kitchens (speculation; label it).
