# Hostile review: Ezekiel 1-7 (part 01-07) and book.json

Reviewer: hostile-reviewer (not the writer). Checked: plain.txt verse by verse against texts/orig/Ezk.json; the Greek with `python tools/lxx.py Eze` (Swete, Vaticanus); the scrolls with `python tools/dss.py Ezk` (11Q4, 4Q74, 1Q9); Rashi, Menachot 45a (Sefaria); the Scofield note (secondary transcriptions and a JETS citation); terms against content/terms.json and every proposals terms.json; `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Ezk/01-07`.

## Verdict

The translation is accurate and the Hebrew is followed closely; I found no mistranslation of names, numbers or who-does-what. The Greek and Qumran readings that are given are real (4QEzek-b "their likeness" in 1:22, the Greek "I swore" in 6:9 and the 190 days in 4:5 and 4:9 all check out), and the Talmud references (Hagigah 13a, Shabbat 13b, Menachot 45a, Mishnah Megillah 4:10), the Josephus reference and the Scofield note are real. The problems are these. The recognition-formula count is wrong in four places. The 437-day note is wrongly typed `spec` and its argument is weak. Several verses sit too close to ESV/NIV sentence shape. The variant apparatus misses real witnesses (5:7, the Greek 1:14 and 1:3, Vulgate 6:9) and the 3:15 Qere is misprinted. Term consistency fails on niddah. The two traditions cards are thin and uneven: the Gog card has no real Lutheran, Orthodox or non-dispensational premillennial view, and the Catholic entry does not answer the question. Several view labels in chapters.json are filler under the wrong lens. `--check Ezk/01-07` shows 0 errors and 0 warnings for Ezk. The two errors it prints are in Dan 04-07, Ezr 01-06 and Jer 50-52 (duplicate source keys "xenophon-cyropaedia" and "beaulieu-nabonidus") and are not Ezekiel's, but the lead must clear them before a build.

Findings: 0 critical, 9 major, 14 minor.

## Major

1. **Recognition-formula count is wrong (four places).** Where: chapters.json ch 6 `deep` ("By my count of the Hebrew, 61 verses"); notes.json 5:13; book.json lead ("sixty-one verses"); proposals/prophets/terms.json entry 11. Evidence: a script over texts/orig/Ezk.json finds 64 verses with a form of yd' followed by "ki ani YHWH" (5:13; 6:7, 10, 13, 14; 7:4, 9, 27; 11:10, 12; 12:15, 16, 20; 13:14, 21, 23; 14:8; 15:7; 16:62; 17:21, 24; 20:38, 42, 44; 21:5; 22:16, 22; 24:27; 25:5, 7, 11, 17; 26:6; 28:22, 23, 26; 29:6, 9, 21; 30:8, 19, 25, 26; 32:15; 33:29; 34:27, 30; 35:4, 9, 12, 15; 36:11, 23, 36, 38; 37:6, 13, 14, 28; 38:23; 39:6, 7, 22, 28). Five more have "ki ani Adonai YHWH" (13:9; 23:49; 24:24; 28:24; 29:16), so 69 verses in all. The "I the LORD have spoken" count of 14 and its verse list are correct. 217 for "the Lord GOD" and 93 for "son of man" are also correct. Fix: state "about seventy verses" or the exact 64 + 5, and say how it was counted. Take out "By my count" (a first-person count in reader text) in both places and use "A count of the Hebrew shows".

2. **The 437-day note is typed `spec`, and the argument has a hole.** Where: notes.json 4:5 (type `spec`). (a) The observation that 7 + 390 + 40 days do not fit between 1:2 and 8:1 is the sort of thing commentators have raised, and the note itself says "it may well appear in the commentaries". STYLE and the brief reserve `spec` for an unpublished theory after searching. A published or probable observation is `com` with a source, or must be cut. Search properly and retype it. (b) The count ignores intercalation. 1:2 to 8:1 is 14 months, about 413 days, only if no leap month falls between. One intercalary month in the Babylonian or Judean calendar gives about 443 days, which holds 437 comfortably, so the "problem" may vanish. The note must say so. (c) The text never says the 390 + 40 days ended before 8:1, only that Ezekiel was sitting at home with the elders on that date. The note assumes continuity; say so. Also, chapters.json ch 4 "Read as a vision or literary scene... since the days do not fit the book's dates" turns this shaky arithmetic into a reason for a reading. Soften it to match.

3. **5:7 has a known textual problem that is not given.** Where: variants.json (no entry); notes.json 5:7. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac lack the "not" in the last clause (conforming it to 11:12), and in the same verse the Syriac and some manuscripts read "my rulings" for "the rulings" of the nations (search result: Biblia and Open Bible Data footnotes; check BHS/BHQ, Zimmerli and Greenberg). The note cites only Swete's agreement with the Hebrew and offers "two charges / different audience / slip". Add a variant entry (MT "not" followed; Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac without it), say what 11:12 does, and drop the claim that only exegetical explanations exist. The note's "Another reading is 'more abundant'" has no source.

4. **Drift from ESV/NIV sentence shape.** The brief says earlier parts had 5 to 25 drifted verses; these need re-working from the literal line.
   - 5:1: "take a sharp sword. Use it as a barber's razor: take it and pass it over your head and your beard. Then take balance scales and divide the hair." This follows ESV's order and nearly its words ("Use it as a barber's razor and pass it over your head and your beard. Then take balances for weighing and divide the hair"). The Hebrew has "a sharp sword, a barber's razor, you will take for yourself".
   - 4:14: "From my youth until now I have never eaten what died by itself or was torn by animals, and no foul meat has ever come into my mouth." This matches ESV's "From my youth up till now... what died of itself or was torn by beasts... tainted meat come into my mouth" and the NIV's "From my youth until now". Rebuild.
   - 2:3: "I am sending you to the Israelites, to rebellious nations that have rebelled against me. They and their ancestors... right up to this very day." This is NIV's "I am sending you to the Israelites, to a rebellious nation... they and their ancestors... to this very day". Rebuild the shape.
   - 6:9: "they will remember me among the nations where they were taken captive, how I was broken by their prostituting heart that turned away from me, and by their prostituting eyes that went after..." This is ESV ("remember me among the nations where they are carried captive, how I was broken over their whoring heart that turned away from me and over their whoring eyes"). The Hebrew forces "broken" but not the whole frame. Rework (for example start from "who are taken captive", and put "heart" and "eyes" in a different order).
   - 5:5: "This is Jerusalem. I have set her in the middle of the nations, with lands all around her" is ESV's "in the center of the nations, with countries all around her", changed in two words.
   - 5:15 ("a disgrace and a taunt, a warning and a horror to the nations around you, when I carry out judgments on you in anger and in rage and in furious rebukes") and 5:16 ("the deadly arrows of famine, which are for destruction") follow ESV/NIV. The Hebrew says "evil arrows".
   - 5:7 ("more turbulent than the nations... not even acted by the rulings of the nations") has ESV's "more turbulent" and "have not even acted according to the rules of the nations".
   - Minor drift: 3:21 ("because he took the warning, and you will have saved your own life"), 3:27, 5:2, 6:2-4.

5. **6:14 "more than the wilderness toward Diblah" is the KJV reading, and the note's reason for the Riblah conjecture is incomplete.** Hebrew mimmidbar Diblatah is read by most modern versions as a span "from the wilderness to Diblah/Riblah", the whole land from south to north. The Plain English Translation makes min comparative without saying so. The note says Riblah is suggested because Nebuchadnezzar judged Zedekiah there, which is not the argument. The gloss "a desert place" fits neither reading. Fix: note the span reading, state which one the Plain English Translation follows and why, give the real reason for the Riblah conjecture, and rewrite the gloss.

6. **3:15 Ketiv/Qere entry misprints the Qere.** Where: variants.json 3:15. The data (texts/auto) shows that the Qere is "va'esheb" (K = "va'asher"). The Leningrad text is therefore "wa'esheb hemmah yoshevim sham" with no "asher". The entry's Qere line "va'esheb asher hemmah yoshevim sham" contains an "asher" that no witness has and joins the two readings. Correct the Hebrew, the "en" line and the plain. Also add the Greek, which has no Tel-abib at all (it reads "I went around those living by the Chobar") and has a different construction.

7. **Variants missing for real witness differences.**
   - 1:14 is absent in Swete's Vaticanus (lxx.py prints an empty line). Give a variant.
   - 1:3: the MT says "the hand of the LORD was on him" in the third person, while the Greek reads "on me" (and the Syriac and Targum read "me"). The note at 1:3 discusses the shift in voice but cites no witness.
   - 6:9: the entry says "no witness read here has" the active "I broke". The Vulgate (contrivi) and the Targum and Syriac traditions are reported with the active, and some manuscripts of the Greek read synetrips(a). Check and list them, or remove the claim.
   - Also consider 1:7, where the Greek has "winged feet" for "a calf's foot", and 1:20, where the Greek has "cloud" for "spirit".
   - 3:12: the note correctly says "when it rose" (berum) is a conjecture, but it does not say that the Greek and Targum already show the "blessed" reading's long history. It does give "Greek Vaticanus". Fine; but the Targum is cited elsewhere, so give its rendering too.

8. **Niddah is rendered "impure thing" in 7:19, 20, against the site's wording.** Lev 20:21 and Lam 1:8, 1:17 all say "an unclean thing", and the Lamentations term entry (proposals, "niddah ... => an unclean thing") lists Ezekiel 36:17. Terms must match across the Bible (CLAUDE.md). Change to "an unclean thing" in 7:19 and 7:20 and fix note 7:19. (The related 6:4-5 "filthy idols" against Lev 26:30 "your idols" for the same word gillulim is a smaller version of the same problem: reconcile or note it.)

9. **The Gog card has no real Lutheran, Orthodox or non-dispensational premillennial view, and the Catholic entry does not answer the question.** Where: book.json traditions[1]. The Lutheran entry quotes Augsburg Confession 17 against chiliasm and then says the confession "does not mention Ezekiel". That is not a Lutheran view of Gog. Luther's 1530 preface to Ezekiel 38-39 reads Gog as the Turk. The Catholic entry gives CCC 676 on the millennium and a claim ("traditionally read Revelation 20 as describing the present age... following Augustine") that CCC 676 does not support. It says nothing of Ezekiel 38-39 itself. The Orthodox, Baptist and historic (non-dispensational) premillennial answers are missing; Revelation 20 is the main premillennial text. Also the Reformed entry is really an amillennial and postmillennial view that Catholics and Lutherans share. The Dispensationalist entry's wording "identified Gog with the northern power headed by Russia" is slightly off. The Scofield note (checked in transcriptions, 1917 edition p. 883) says "the primary reference is to the northern (European) powers, headed up by Russia, all agree", and keeps the "Gog" prince as a separate idea. Reword it so; the note also reads Revelation 20:7-9 as the later revolt, which the card has right. Fix: write a Lutheran answer from Luther or the confessions, give the Catholic entry a source for Revelation 20, add Orthodox (for example Andrew of Caesarea on Revelation 20:8) and historic premillennial, and fix the full stop (the Lutheran entry ends a clause with a comma before "The confession").

## Minor

10. **Torrey's rejection (book.json audience.views[2]).** "the Qumran copies show the book was read widely by the second century BC" does not answer a date of about 230 BC, and the Ezekiel scrolls are dated roughly to the first century BC and AD. Use Ben Sira 49:8-9 (about 180 BC) and the Hebrew-language argument (Hurvitz, already a source) instead.

11. **Rashi on "son of man" is garbled.** notes.json 2:1 says "Rashi gives two explanations... and, in Rashi's own view, [the second]". Rashi (Sefaria) gives two reasons (he moved among the angels, so he is told he is a human; to keep him from pride). The second is not "Rashi's own view". Same issue in topics.json (son-of-man) and chapters.json ch 2 `J`. On the "thirtieth year", Rashi says the following verse was inserted by the holy spirit to name the date, and the note (chapters.json ch 1 `deep`) says only that it "teaches us what the count refers to". Make it say what he said.

12. **book.json audience.why says a priest "forbidden to mourn" spent "seven years acting out sieges".** The ban on mourning is specific to the wife's death (24:15-27), not a general rule for priests. The sign acts run from 593 to about 588 and the muteness to 33:22 (about seven and a half years), not seven years of sign acts. Reword.

13. **Unsupported superlative.** book.json section "Ezekiel in the New Testament": "Revelation draws on Ezekiel more than on any other prophet". Isaiah and Daniel are strong competitors, and no source is given. Say "heavily" or cite a count.

14. **The Catholic entry on the Temple card (book.json traditions[0]).** "has never taught that a temple like Ezekiel's will be built" is an unsourced negative; CCC 756 is not evidence for it. Origen's commentary on Ezekiel is lost (only the homilies on early chapters survive), so "Origen... in their commentaries" cannot be checked; drop Origen or write "as reported by". The Orthodox entry has an empty source list. The card also omits Lutheran, Baptist, historic premillennial and the Jewish expectation of a Third Temple (the brief asks for the Jewish views), though the latter may belong in the J lens.

15. **Filler under the wrong lens in chapters.json.** `J` for ch 4 ("Ezekiel's objection to unclean food reflects a priest's rules") and for ch 6 ("the Greek renders the incense stands as 'shrines'") are not Jewish tradition. `X` for ch 5 ("Christian commentators have generally kept to the siege") and ch 7, and `J` for ch 7 ("Jewish commentators read the chapter as the end of the first Temple") are unsourced generalities. `X` for ch 6 ties 6:8-10 to Romans 11:5, which is a stretch. `H` for ch 6 ("archaeology of Judah shows widespread local shrines") needs a source (Arad, Beersheba). Ch 4 `C`: "Zimmerli and Greenberg differ" says nothing about what either holds. Replace each with sourced content or cut.

16. **Ch 4 `readings`: "Most modern commentators take this view" and ch 7 "The traditional view" are unsourced** and reduce the settings `views` to labels with no content. Several settings entries (ch 4 `4:9-17`, ch 5 both) have empty `views`.

17. **Note 1:15 versus the scroll list.** chapters.json says 4QEzek-b preserves "1:10-13, 15-24", but dss.py prints an empty line for 1:15. Recheck. Also 1:20, where only a few words survive.

18. **Note 1:15 ("Some manuscripts and translators changed the singular to a plural")** has no source. Cite or remove.

19. **Note 1:10, "Most scholars think the older word was cherub".** This is an unsourced consensus. Add the Jewish tradition (Hagigah 13b, the ox face changed lest it recall the calf) and name holders of each view. Irenaeus's matching of the faces differs from Jerome's; the note's "Luke's symbol" is Jerome's matching only.

20. **Note 7:5 "the BSB has... the WEB has..."** are unverified quotations of other versions; check or drop. Note 7:7 says "the Greek and Vulgate have words for 'crushing'". The Vulgate has contritio, but Swete's Greek at the matching place (7:7) has the end, so check the Greek claim. Note 7:23: ratoq "occurs only here" while Isaiah 40:19 has the plural and Nahum 3:10 a related form; say "only here in the singular".

21. **Gloss 6:14 and unconventional glosses.** "terebinth ... a big oak-like tree" is fine. "Diblah ... a desert place" see item 5. Glosses on 1:22 ("so bright it was scary") and 3:7 ("bullheaded") are slangy but acceptable.

22. **Plain English Translation 1:22 "the heads of the living creature"** keeps the Hebrew singular but reads oddly for an 8th grader beside plural "their heads"; add a note (the singular is in note 1:20) or a gloss.

23. **Cross-book alignment (for the lead).** The Ezekiel term "dever = plague" conflicts with the Jeremiah proposal "deber = disease (14:12)", while Jeremiah's own text uses "plague" at 21:6 and 24:10. Reconcile.
