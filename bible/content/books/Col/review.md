# Hostile review: Colossians (Col), one part, flat folder

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-10-29. `node tools/build.js --check Col` (VERBOSE=1): 0 errors, 0 warnings. Content files were not edited.

## Verdict

Colossians is a strong, careful draft. The Plain is faithful to the Greek nearly everywhere, it tracks Ephesians' Plain exactly where the Greek matches (Col 3:6, 3:22-23, 4:5, 4:7-8, 3:16, 2:19, 1:14 all checked word for word against Eph 5:6, 6:5-7, 5:16, 6:21-22, 5:19, 4:16, 1:7) and differs where the Greek differs (3:21 / Eph 6:4; 4:1 / Eph 6:9; 1:14 / Eph 1:7). Most quotations of other books' Plain match the current Plain. The Lightfoot, Tacitus, Athanasius, Salvifici Doloris, Heidelberg Catechism, Westminster and most Catechism citations hold up against sources I opened. The problems are these. (a) The 1:7 variant misreports NA28, and the Plain's reading is defended with an argument that the NET note itself reverses. (b) The "7 traditions cards" are really 5, and the household code, headship and slavery, which the brief ranked first, have no card at all. (c) The cards that exist leave out the Orthodox and Lutheran voices on four of five questions, and one Calvin quotation is actually Augustine's. (d) The authorship section lists commentaries as sources that were not opened, gives only one side's arguments in detail, and never says which view is the majority. (e) There is some NIV/ESV drift at 3:17-18 and a gloss that settles the Sabbath debate. Nothing is invented outright, so I found no critical item. Several details could not be checked, and these are listed so the fixer can verify them or cut them.

Verification key: "V" means I verified it this session in a source I opened (named). "U" means I could not verify it and recommend removal or checking.

## Major

### 1. 1:7 variant: NA28 reading misreported, and the "why" argues against the NET note it cites
Location: variants.json 1:7; plain.txt 1:7; chapters.json ch.1 settings 1:3-8 ("for our sake"); book.json section "The Greek text".
- `texts/auto/Col.json` and the word list (1:7, `ὑμῶν` marked `NnTB`) show that NA27/28, the TR and Byzantine read ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν, and that Tyndale (THGNT), SBLGNT, WH and Tregelles read ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν. The entry lists NA28 nowhere. Reading 1 ("your") names only the corrector, C, Ψ and the versions, plus the TR.
- The Plain follows SBLGNT/THGNT against NA28 here, as it follows NA28/THGNT against SBLGNT at 1:21. book.json's "The Greek text" section announces only the 1:21 departure.
- The "why" says "'Your' fits the surrounding 'you' and may come from a copyist." V (biblia.com NET note, Col 1:7): the NET editors choose ἡμῶν on external evidence alone, and say ὑμῶν is the *harder* reading (it ties Epaphras's role to Paul, not to the readers) and that the internal evidence can be read either way. The entry should say so.
- The ch.1 setting ("Epaphras is Paul's trusted representative ('for our sake')") is built on the contested reading as if settled.
- Witness lists otherwise match the NET note (V): ἡμῶν = P46, ℵ*, A, B, D*, F, G, 326*, 1505; ὑμῶν = ℵ², C, D¹, Ψ, 075, 33, 1739, 1881, Majority, Latin, Syriac, Coptic. Add 326* and 1505 to the "our" side's weight, and note that these are the NET's lists.
- Fix: add NA28 (and NA27) to reading 1; state that Plain, SBLGNT and THGNT read "our" and NA28 reads "your"; rewrite the "why" to give the NET's actual argument (external evidence vs. the harder reading); add the 1:7 departure to the "Greek text" section; soften the ch.1 setting to "on our behalf (or 'on your behalf', see the variant)".

### 3. The existing cards leave out traditions that have a distinct answer, and one card's framing does not match its answers
Location: chapters.json traditions.
- 1:24 (`suffering`): only Catholic and Reformed. The brief names Orthodox and Lutheran too. Add them from their own sources, or say why they are absent.
- 2:11-12 (`baptism`): Reformed, Catholic, Baptist. Missing: Lutheran (infant baptism with baptismal regeneration, which the brief names), Orthodox (infant baptism and chrismation), Anglican/Methodist. The question is "support baptizing infants?"; the baptismal-regeneration view of 2:12 ("raised with him") is not covered at all.
- 2:16-17 (`sabbath`): Catholic, Reformed, Adventist. Missing: Orthodox, Lutheran, a Baptist/Sabbatarian voice, and the Messianic Jewish reading the ch.2 `views.J` text itself mentions. The question "Does 2:16-17 end the Sabbath?" is answered by Catholic with a catechism line that is about Sunday, not about 2:16.
- 2:18 (`angels`): the question is "May Christians honor and call on angels?" The Catholic answer (CCC 335, 352) speaks of liturgy and veneration but never addresses invoking/calling on angels, which is the half of the question that 2:18 and Laodicea canon 35 are about. Orthodox (veneration; the second Council of Nicaea) and Lutheran (Augsburg Confession on honoring saints) are absent. A Catholic answer that skips the contested half reads as evasive; give the Catholic answer on invocation (a source opened in the session) or reframe the question.
- 1:15 (`son-of-god`): Jehovah's Witnesses are listed as one of three equal "views". The brief says: "as a non-church reading only if labeled outside the traditions". The card does not label it.
- Fix: add the missing traditions where they have a distinct answer (sourced), and label the Witness reading as outside the historic churches.

### 6. sources.json credits commentaries that were not opened, and several scholar attributions rest on Wikipedia alone
Location: sources.json (`dunn-colossians`, `moo-colossians`, `lohse-colossians`, `wright-colossians`); book.json `sources`, `reading`, `authorship`; notes 1:1, 2:8.
- STYLE rule 2 and the project memory ("no memory sourcing") forbid naming what a scholar says without opening it. The authorship table's scholar attributions came from the Wikipedia article on Colossians (as the lead notes). I opened that article: it supports Mayerhoff 1838, Koester's 48 and 33 word counts (1982, 1987), Kümmel's liturgical-hymnic style, the Irenaeus/Clement/Tertullian/Origen/Eusebius list, Martin, Caird and Moule (via the New Jerome), Schweizer (joint authorship), and Lohse (about AD 80, a Pauline school, probably Ephesus). But it lists Dunn only as citing the *amanuensis* possibility (Dunn 1996, p. 38). It does not mention Holtzmann, Moo or Wright's authorship views. It also does not give Hooker's position.
- The book lists Dunn, Moo, Lohse and Wright under `sources` and `reading` as though they had been consulted. They were not opened. Either open them or remove them from the source list and say the attributions come from a secondary survey.
- Dunn claim: book.json and note 1:1 say "James Dunn proposed that Timothy wrote it during Paul's lifetime." V (web summaries, secondary only): Dunn floats Timothy writing at Paul's behest, but tentatively ("fairly fluid"); one reviewer says before Paul's death. State it as a possibility Dunn raises, with its source, or cut.
- Hooker: V (secondary): her 1973 essay argues the letter does not target an organized false-teaching group and that the warnings concern ideas "in the air". "No organized false teaching at all" is close but sharper than Bruce's summary; attribute carefully or cite the essay.
- Holtzmann (Lightfoot preface): V in Lightfoot's text, which says Colossians "hangs together as a whole" against "any theory of interpolation such as that recently put forward by Professor Holtzmann". The authorship row is right.
- DeWitt (Epicurean): V (Wikipedia: DeWitt 1954). Acceptable but give the source.

### 7. Authorship section: one side's arguments in detail, the other side's replies missing, and no word on which view the majority holds
Location: book.json `authorship` (and `facts`, ch.1 settings, ch.4 readings).
- brief-pauline.md requires "state each view with its best arguments and its named scholars, say which is the majority in critical scholarship and which is the traditional view". The table gives the doubters' four arguments in detail (vocabulary, style, cosmic Christ and "body", realized eschatology) but gives the defenders only names (Martin, Caird, Moule). It omits their replies: the secretary/amanuensis explanation; heavy use of traditional (hymnic) material, which Perrin himself concedes can explain the language (V, Wikipedia); Philemon's shared names; O'Brien's argument that the gap is smaller than claimed; Barth's priorities. - Date: "Ephesus (about 54-56) or Caesarea (about 57-59)" has no source (U; Wikipedia gave no years for these; Lightfoot has about 63 from Rome). Remove or source.
- Fix: add the defenders' best arguments and the traditional-material point from sources opened in the session; label the majority question honestly; source or cut the dates.

### 8. Drift toward NIV/ESV wording at 3:17-3:18 (and smaller echoes)
Location: plain.txt 3:17, 3:18, 3:16; also 3:24-25, 1:5, 1:9. Compared in my head only; I quote at most a few words.
- 3:18 "Wives, submit yourselves to your husbands, as was fitting in the Lord." This is the NIV sentence with one tense changed ("as is" to "as was"). The reflexive "yourselves" is not required by the Greek (`ὑποτάσσεσθε` is a plain imperative; "yourselves" is the NIV's choice), and the changed tense is awkward English. Rebuild from the literal line: for example "Wives, put yourselves under your husbands' leadership" would be interpretive, so keep it plain: "Wives, be subject to your husbands, as it is right in the Lord", or a wording of the writer's own that says what `ἀνῆκεν` says. Note that Ephesians' Plain at 5:21 has "submitting yourselves", so keep the two letters aligned whichever way is chosen, and tell the lead.
- 3:17 "do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus. Give thanks to God the Father through him." Both halves track the NIV's phrasing closely ("do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus", "giving thanks to God the Father through him").
- 3:16 "singing with gratitude in your hearts to God" uses exactly the NIV's words in a different order; "psalms, hymns and songs from the Spirit" is the NIV's rendering of `ᾠδαῖς πνευματικαῖς`. It is shared with Ephesians 5:19, so the Ephesians reviewer may have flagged it; check Eph's review and fix both together.
- 3:24 "It is the Lord Christ you serve" is the NIV's sentence minus "are"; 3:25 "and there is no favoritism" is close.
- 1:5 "Both come from the hope" adds "Both" and takes `διὰ τὴν ἐλπίδα` ("because of the hope") as "come from", the same interpretive move some versions make. 1:9 "the understanding the Spirit gives" echoes the NIV clause.
- Fix: redo 3:18 first, then 3:17 and 3:16 from `literal.js`; consider 1:5 "because of the hope" (the note can keep the "faith and love flow from hope" reading).

### 9. Gloss at 2:16 settles the Sabbath question the card says is open
Location: glosses.json 2:16, "Sabbaths": "the weekly day of rest, Saturday".
- `σαββάτων` in a list with festivals and new moons is exactly what the Adventist card says means ceremonial sabbaths, and the note and `deep` list three readings. The gloss rules for one of them (and against the Adventist and the "festival sabbaths" reading) in the margin where readers will see it first.
- The 3:5 gloss "sexual immorality: sex outside marriage" similarly narrows `porneia`, which is a debated term elsewhere on the site; check it against the terms.json entry and the other books' glosses.
- Fix: gloss 2:16 neutrally ("Sabbath days; see the card"), and align the 3:5 gloss with the site's `porneia` usage.

### 17. Household-code deep dive overstates Colossians' distinctness

Ephesians 5:22-6:9 does all three in almost the same words, as the Plain itself shows. ### 18. Plain readings that resolve a debated point silently, or are inconsistent
- 2:11 "In him ... circumcision" and 2:12 "In it you were also raised" translate the same relative `ἐν ᾧ`. The Plain picks "baptism" as the antecedent in 2:12 (the note says "both readings are found"), in the verse the baptism card turns on. Use the same rendering in both ("in whom") or keep the note's two options visible.
- 2:15 "by it" picks the cross for `ἐν αὐτῷ`; the note admits it may be "in him". The Plain's "stripped ... bare" is Jerome's active sense, which Lightfoot (V) calls a "common error" against the middle voice; the note says so, and the Plain needs only a clearer pointer to the variant readings.
- 2:2 `συμβιβασθέντες` is "held together in love" and 2:19 `συμβιβαζόμενον` is "held firm". Ephesians 4:16 has "held firm" for the same word. The key-term rule and the 2:19 note's emphasis on the shared verb argue for one rendering.
- 1:24 "Now I am glad to suffer for you" turns the noun `παθήμασιν` ("sufferings", with `ἐν`) into an infinitive; keep the noun ("I am glad in my sufferings for you").
- 1:21 "as your evil deeds showed" adds an inference; the Greek is `ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις τοῖς πονηροῖς`, "in the evil works".
- 2:16 "eating and drinking" (see item 10).
- 3:18 "as was fitting": the odd tense obscures the `ἀνῆκεν` note.
