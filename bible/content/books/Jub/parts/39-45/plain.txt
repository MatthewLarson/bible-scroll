## Joseph in Potiphar's House
39:1 Jacob settled in the land where his father had lived as a foreigner, the land of Canaan.
39:2 This is the family history of Jacob. Joseph was seventeen years old when they took him down to Egypt, and Potiphar, a eunuch of Pharaoh's, the chief of the cooks, bought him.
39:3 He put Joseph in charge of his whole house. The Lord's blessing came on the Egyptian's house because of Joseph, and the Lord made everything he did succeed.
39:4 The Egyptian left everything in Joseph's charge, because he saw that the Lord was with him and that the Lord made everything he did succeed.
39:5 Joseph was good-looking, very handsome to see. His master's wife raised her eyes and looked at Joseph. She fell in love with him and pleaded with him to lie with her.
39:6 But he did not give himself up to her. He remembered the Lord, and the words his father Jacob used to read aloud from the words of Abraham: no man may commit adultery with a woman who has a husband. A sentence of death has been decreed for that man in the heavens before the Lord Most High, and the sin goes up against him into the eternal books, for all time, before the Lord.
39:7 Joseph remembered these words and would not lie with her.
39:8 She kept pleading with him for one year and then a second, but he refused to listen.
39:9 She threw her arms around him and held him in the house, to force him to lie with her. She shut the doors of the house and held on to him. But he left his garment in her hand, broke through the door, and ran outside, away from her.
39:10 When the woman saw that he would not lie with her, she slandered him to his master. "Your Hebrew servant, the one you love, tried to force me to lie with him," she said. "When I raised my voice, he ran off. He left his garment in my hand when I grabbed him, and he broke through the door."
39:11 The Egyptian saw Joseph's garment and the broken door, and he heard his wife's story. So he threw Joseph into prison, into the place where the men the king had locked up were held.

## Joseph in Prison
39:12 Joseph was there in the prison. The Lord gave Joseph favor and kindness in the eyes of the chief of the prison guards, because he saw that the Lord was with him and that the Lord made everything he did succeed.
39:13 The chief of the prison guards left everything in his charge and paid no attention to anything Joseph handled, because Joseph did everything, and the Lord brought it to completion. He stayed there two years.
39:14 In those days Pharaoh, king of Egypt, grew angry with two of his eunuchs, the chief cupbearer and the chief baker. He put them in custody in the house of the chief of the cooks, in the prison where Joseph was held.
39:15 The chief of the prison guards assigned Joseph to wait on them, and he waited on them.
39:16 The two of them had dreams, the chief cupbearer and the chief baker, and they told them to Joseph.
39:17 Things turned out for them just as he explained them. Pharaoh put the chief cupbearer back at his work, and the baker he hanged, as Joseph had explained.
39:18 But the chief cupbearer forgot Joseph in the prison, though Joseph had told him what was going to happen. He did not remember to tell Pharaoh what Joseph had said to him, because he forgot.

## Pharaoh's Dreams
40:1 In those days Pharaoh had two dreams in one night about a famine that was coming on the whole land. He woke from his sleep and called all the dream interpreters in Egypt, and the magicians. He told them his two dreams, but they could not explain them.
40:2 Then the chief cupbearer remembered Joseph and spoke to the king about him. They brought Joseph out of the prison, and Pharaoh told him his two dreams.
40:3 Joseph told Pharaoh that his two dreams were one. He said to him, "Seven years will come with plenty across the whole land of Egypt. After them will come seven years of famine, a famine like none there has ever been in all the land.
40:4 So now let Pharaoh appoint overseers throughout the land of Egypt. Let them store up food in each city during the years of plenty. Then there will be food for the seven years of famine, and the land will not be wiped out by the famine, because it will be very severe."
40:5 The Lord gave Joseph favor and kindness in Pharaoh's eyes, and Pharaoh said to his servants, "We will not find a man as wise and understanding as this man, because the spirit of the Lord is with him."

## Joseph Rules Egypt
40:6 Pharaoh made him second in his whole kingdom and gave him authority over all Egypt. He had him ride in Pharaoh's second chariot.
40:7 He clothed him in garments of fine linen and hung a gold chain around his neck. A herald called out ahead of him, "El El wa Abirr!" Pharaoh put a ring on his hand, made him ruler over his whole household, and gave him high honor. He said to him, "I will be no greater than you, except for the throne."
40:8 Joseph ruled over the whole land of Egypt. All Pharaoh's officials loved him, and all his servants, and everyone who did the king's work. For he lived uprightly. He had no pride or arrogance, he showed no favoritism, and he took no bribes. He judged all the people of the land fairly.
40:9 The land of Egypt was at peace under Pharaoh because of Joseph, because the Lord was with him. The Lord gave him favor and kindness for all his generations with everyone who knew him and everyone who heard about him. Pharaoh's kingdom was well ordered, and there was no satan and no evil.
40:10 The king gave Joseph the name Sephantiphans. He gave Joseph the daughter of Potiphar as his wife, the daughter of the priest of Heliopolis, the chief of the cooks.
40:11 On the day Joseph stood before Pharaoh, he was thirty years old, when he stood before Pharaoh.
40:12 In that year Isaac died. Everything happened as Joseph had said in his explanation of Pharaoh's two dreams, just as he had told him. There were seven years of plenty across the whole land of Egypt. The land produced a huge harvest: one measure of seed yielded eighteen hundred measures.
40:13 Joseph gathered food into each city until they were full of grain, until people could no longer count it or measure it, there was so much.

## Judah and Tamar
41:1 In the forty-fifth jubilee, in the second week, in its second year, Judah took a wife for his firstborn, Er, from the daughters of Aram. Her name was Tamar.
41:2 But Er hated her and would not lie with her, because his mother was one of the daughters of Canaan. He wanted to take a wife from his mother's people, but his father Judah would not let him.
41:3 This Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil, and the Lord put him to death.
41:4 Then Judah said to Onan, his brother, "Go in to your brother's wife and do a brother-in-law's duty for her. Raise up seed for your brother."
41:5 But Onan knew that the seed would not count as his, only as his brother's. He went into the house of his brother's wife and spilled his seed on the ground. What he did was evil in the Lord's eyes, and the Lord put him to death.
41:6 Then Judah said to his daughter-in-law Tamar, "Stay in your father's house as a widow until Shelah my son is grown, and I will give you to him as his wife."
41:7 Shelah grew up, but Bedsuel, Judah's wife, would not let her son Shelah marry her. Bedsuel, Judah's wife, died in the fifth year of this week.
41:8 In its sixth year Judah went up to Timnah to shear his sheep. Someone told Tamar, "Look, your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep."
41:9 She laid aside the clothes of her widowhood, put on a veil, made herself beautiful, and sat in the gate beside the road to Timnah.
41:10 As Judah was going along, he came upon her and took her for a prostitute. He said to her, "Let me come in to you." She said, "Come in," and he went in.
41:11 She said to him, "Give me my pay." He said to her, "I have nothing with me except the ring on my finger, my necklace, and the staff in my hand."
41:12 She said to him, "Give them to me until you send me my pay." He said to her, "I will send you a little goat." He gave them to her, and he lay with her, and she became pregnant by him.
41:13 Judah went back to his sheep, and she went to her father's house.
41:14 Judah sent the little goat by his shepherd, an Adullamite, but he did not find her. He asked the people of the place, "Where is the prostitute who was here?" They told him, "There is no prostitute here. We have no prostitute among us."
41:15 He came back and told Judah that he had not found her. He said, "I asked the people of the place, and they told me, 'There is no prostitute here.'" Judah said, "Let's go, so that we do not become a joke."
41:16 When she had finished three months, it could be seen that she was pregnant. Someone told Judah, "Look, your daughter-in-law Tamar is pregnant from prostitution."
41:17 Judah went to her father's house and said to her father and her brothers, "Bring her out and let them burn her, because she has done something unclean in Israel."
41:18 When they brought her out to burn her, she sent her father-in-law the ring, the necklace and the staff, with this message: "Recognize whose these are, because I am pregnant by him."
41:19 Judah recognized them and said, "Tamar is more in the right than I am. Do not let them burn her."
41:20 For this reason she was not given to Shelah, and Judah never came near her again.
41:21 After that she gave birth to two sons, Perez and Zerah, in the seventh year of this second week.
41:22 Then the seven years of plenty that Joseph had told Pharaoh about came to an end.

## Judah's Repentance and the Law
41:23 Judah knew that what he had done was evil, because he had lain with his daughter-in-law. It was hateful in his eyes. He knew that he had done wrong and gone astray, because he had uncovered his son's robe. He began to mourn and to plead with the Lord because of his wrongdoing.
41:24 We told him in a dream that he was forgiven, because he had pleaded hard and mourned, and he did not do it again.
41:25 He was forgiven because he turned away from his sin and from his ignorance, for he had done great wrong before our God. Anyone who does this, anyone who lies with his mother-in-law, must be burned in fire, so that he burns up in it, because uncleanness and defilement are on them. They must be burned in fire.
41:26 As for you, command the Israelites that there must be no uncleanness among them. For anyone who lies with his daughter-in-law or with his mother-in-law has done something unclean. They must burn in fire the man who lay with her, and the woman too. Then he will turn away anger and punishment from Israel.
41:27 To Judah we said that his two sons had not lain with her. For this reason his offspring was established for a second generation and will not be uprooted.
41:28 For he had gone with an undivided heart and sought justice. Because of the command of Abraham that he gave his sons, Judah wanted to burn her in fire.

## The Famine Begins
42:1 In the first year of the third week of the forty-fifth jubilee, the famine began to come on the land. The rain refused to be given to the earth, because none at all came down.
42:2 The land became barren. Only in the land of Egypt was there food, because Joseph had gathered the land's grain in the seven years of plenty and kept it.
42:3 The Egyptians came to Joseph for him to give them food. He opened the storehouses where the grain of the first year was kept, and he sold it to the people of the land for gold.
42:4 Jacob heard that there was food in Egypt, and he sent his ten sons to bring him food from Egypt. But Benjamin he did not send. They arrived along with the others who were going.

## The Brothers' First Journey
42:5 Joseph recognized them, but they did not know who he was. He spoke with them and questioned them. He said to them, "Aren't you spies? Haven't you come to scout out the ways into the land?" And he locked them up.
42:6 After that he set them free. He kept only Simeon and sent his nine brothers off.
42:7 He filled their sacks with grain and put their gold back in their sacks, and they did not know it.
42:8 He ordered them to bring their youngest brother, because they had told him that their father was alive and that they had a youngest brother.
42:9 They went up from the land of Egypt and came to the land of Canaan. They told their father everything that had happened to them, how the lord of the country had spoken harshly to them and had seized Simeon until they brought Benjamin.
42:10 Jacob said, "You have robbed me of my children! Joseph is gone, Simeon is gone, and you are taking Benjamin away. Your wickedness has come down on me."
42:11 He said, "My son will not go with you, or he might fall sick. Their mother bore two sons, and one is lost, and now you would take this one from me too. If he caught a fever on the road, you would bring my old age down to death in grief."
42:12 For he saw that each man's gold had come back in his sack, and so he was afraid to send him.
42:13 The famine grew worse and was severe in the land of Canaan, and in every land except the land of Egypt. Many of the Egyptians had stored up their grain for food from the time they saw Joseph gathering grain, putting it in storehouses and keeping it for the years of famine.
42:14 The people of Egypt fed themselves on it in the first year of the famine.

## Benjamin Goes Down to Egypt
42:15 When Israel saw that the famine was very severe in the land and there was no relief, he said to his sons, "Go back and bring us food, so that we do not die."
42:16 They said, "We will not go. Unless our youngest brother goes with us, we will not go."
42:17 Israel saw that if he did not send him with them, they would all die because of the famine.
42:18 Reuben said, "Put him in my hands. If I do not bring him back to you, put my two sons to death in exchange for his life." But Jacob said to him, "He will not go with you."
42:19 Then Judah came forward and said, "Send him with me. If I do not bring him back to you, let me carry the guilt before you all the days of my life."
42:20 Jacob sent him with them in the second year of this week, on the first day of the month. They reached the land of Egypt with all the others who were going. They had gifts in their hands: stacte, almonds, terebinth nuts and pure honey.
42:21 They went and stood before Joseph. He saw his brother Benjamin and recognized him. He said to them, "Is this your youngest brother?" They said to him, "It is." And he said, "May the Lord have mercy on you, my son."
42:22 He sent Benjamin into his house, and he brought Simeon out to them. He gave a feast for them, and they presented to him the gift they had brought in their hands.
42:23 They ate in front of him. He gave each of them a portion, but Benjamin's portion was seven times larger than any of theirs.
42:24 They ate and drank, then got up and spent the night with their donkeys.
42:25 Joseph thought of a plan to find out what they were thinking, whether they had peaceful thoughts toward one another. He said to the man in charge of his house, "Fill all their sacks with food for them, and put their gold back among their goods. And put my cup, the silver cup I drink from, in the youngest one's sack. Then send them away."

## The Silver Cup
43:1 He did as Joseph had told him. He filled all their sacks with food for them, put their gold in their sacks, and put the cup in Benjamin's sack.
43:2 Early in the morning they left. When they had gone out from there, Joseph said to the man in charge of his house, "Go after them! Run and seize them, and say, 'You have paid me back evil for good. You have stolen the silver cup my lord drinks from.' Bring their youngest brother back to me. Bring him quickly, before I go out to my judgment seat."
43:3 He ran after them and said these words to them.
43:4 They said to him, "Your servants would never do this, or steal any article from your lord's house! We your servants even brought back from the land of Canaan the gold we found in our sacks the first time.
43:5 So how would we steal any article? Here we are, and here are our sacks. Search them. Whichever one of us you find the cup with, let him be put to death, and we and our donkeys will serve your lord."
43:6 He said to them, "No. Only the man I find it with will I take as a slave. The rest of you will go home in peace."
43:7 He searched through their bags, starting with the oldest and finishing with the youngest, and it turned up in Benjamin's sack.
43:8 They tore their clothes, loaded their donkeys, and went back to the city. They came to Joseph's house, and all of them bowed down to him, faces to the ground.
43:9 Joseph said to them, "You have done evil." They said, "What can we say? How can we defend ourselves? Our lord has caught his servants in their wrong. Here we are, your servants, our lord's, and our donkeys too."
43:10 Joseph said to them, "I too fear the Lord. As for you, go home, and let your brother be my slave, because you have done evil. Don't you know that a man takes delight in his cup, as I do in this cup? And you stole it from me."

## Judah Pleads for Benjamin
43:11 Judah said, "Please, my lord, let your servant say a word in my lord's ear. Your servant's mother bore our father two brothers. One went away and was lost, and no one has found him, and this one alone is left of his mother's sons. Your servant our father loves him, and his life is bound up with this boy's life.
43:12 When we go to your servant our father and the boy is not with us, he will die, and we will bring our father down to death in grief.
43:13 Now let me, your servant, stay as my lord's slave instead of the boy, and let the lad go with his brothers. I stood as his guarantor to your servant our father. If I do not bring him back, your servant will carry the guilt before our father for all time."

## Joseph Makes Himself Known
43:14 Joseph saw that all their hearts were united, each man with his brother, for good. He could not hold himself back, and he told them that he was Joseph.
43:15 He spoke with them in the Hebrew language. He threw his arms around their necks and wept. But they did not recognize him, and they began to weep.
43:16 He said to them, "Do not weep over me. Hurry and bring my father to me. Let me see him before I die, while the eyes of my brother Benjamin are seeing too.
43:17 For look, this is the second year of the famine, and five more years are still to come with no harvest, no fruit on the trees, and no plowing.
43:18 Come down quickly, you and your households, so that you do not die in the famine. Do not worry about your goods, because the Lord sent me ahead of you to set things in order, so that many people would live.
43:19 Tell my father that I am still alive. And you can see for yourselves that the Lord has made me like a father to Pharaoh, to rule in his house and over all the land of Egypt.
43:20 Tell my father about all my glory, and all the wealth and glory the Lord has given me."
43:21 At the order of Pharaoh's own mouth, he gave them carts and food for the road. He gave all of them clothes of many colors, and silver.
43:22 To their father he sent clothes, silver and ten donkeys carrying grain. Then he sent them away.
43:23 They went up and told their father that Joseph was alive, that he was measuring out grain to all the peoples of the earth, and that he ruled over all the land of Egypt.
43:24 Their father did not believe it, because his mind was stunned. But when he saw the carts Joseph had sent, his spirit came back to life, and he said, "It is enough for me that Joseph is alive. I will go down to see him before I die."

## Jacob at the Well of the Oath
44:1 Israel set out from Haran, from his house, on the first day of the third month. He went by the road to the Well of the Oath, and on the seventh of that month he offered a sacrifice to the God of his father Isaac.
44:2 Jacob remembered the dream he had seen at Bethel, and he was afraid to go down to Egypt.
44:3 He was thinking of sending word to Joseph to come to him, so that he himself would not go down. He stayed there seven days, in case he might see a vision about whether to stay or to go down.
44:4 He kept the harvest festival, the first crops of the grain, with old grain. In all the land of Canaan there was not a handful of seed anywhere in the land, because the famine was on every wild animal, on the livestock and the birds, and on people too.
44:5 On the sixteenth the Lord appeared to him and said to him, "Jacob, Jacob." He said, "Here I am." He said to him, "I am the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham and Isaac. Do not fear going down to Egypt, because I will make you a great nation there.
44:6 I am going down with you, and I will bring you up again. In this land you will be buried, and Joseph will lay his hand on your eyes. Do not be afraid. Go down to Egypt."
44:7 His sons and his grandsons got up, and they set their father and their goods on carts.
44:8 Israel set out from the Well of the Oath on the sixteenth day of this third month, and he went to the land of Egypt.
44:9 Israel sent Judah on ahead to his son Joseph to look over the land of Goshen, because Joseph had told his brothers to come and live there, so that they would be near him.
44:10 It was the best land in Egypt, and near him, for all of them and for the livestock too.

## The Seventy Who Went Down
44:11 Here are the names of Jacob's sons who went into Egypt with Jacob their father.
44:12 Reuben, Israel's firstborn. These are the names of his sons: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron and Carmi, five.
44:13 Simeon and his sons. These are the names of his sons: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul the son of Phinisewat, seven.
44:14 Levi and his sons. These are the names of his sons: Gershon, Kohath and Merari, four.
44:15 Judah and his sons. These are the names of his sons: Shelah, Perez and Zerah, four.
44:16 Issachar and his sons. These are the names of his sons: Tola, Puvah, Jashub and Shimron, five.
44:17 Zebulun and his sons. These are the names of his sons: Sered, Elon and Jahleel, four.
44:18 These are the sons of Jacob, and their sons, whom Leah bore to Jacob in Mesopotamia: six, and Dinah, their one sister. All the persons of Leah's sons and their sons who went with Jacob their father into Egypt were twenty-nine, and with Jacob their father they were thirty.
44:19 The sons of Zilpah, Leah's servant, the wife of Jacob, whom she bore to Jacob: Gad and Asher.
44:20 These are the names of their sons who went with him into Egypt. The sons of Gad: Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni, Ezbon, Eri, Areli and Arodi, eight.
44:21 The sons of Asher: Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi and Beriah, and Serah their one sister, five.
44:22 All the persons were fourteen, and all those of Leah were forty-four.
44:23 The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife: Joseph and Benjamin.
44:24 Born to Joseph in Egypt before his father came into Egypt, whom Asenath, the daughter of Potiphar, priest of Heliopolis, bore to him: Manasseh and Ephraim, three.
44:25 The sons of Benjamin: Bela, Becher, Ashbel, Gera, Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim and Ard, eleven.
44:26 All the persons of Rachel were fourteen.
44:27 The sons of Bilhah, Rachel's servant, the wife of Jacob, whom she bore to Jacob: Dan and Naphtali.
44:28 These are the names of their sons who went with them into Egypt. The sons of Dan: Hushim, Samon, Asudi, Iyaka and Salomon, six.
44:29 They died in Egypt in the year they arrived, and only Hushim was left to Dan.
44:30 These are the names of the sons of Naphtali: Jahziel, Guni, Jezer, Shallum and Iv.
44:31 Iv, who was born after the years of famine, died in Egypt.
44:32 All those of Rachel were twenty-six.
44:33 All the persons of Jacob who went into Egypt were seventy persons. These, then, are his sons and his grandsons, seventy in all, and five of them died in Egypt before they married, and had no children.
44:34 In the land of Canaan two of Judah's sons, Er and Onan, had died, and they had no children. The Israelites buried those who died, and they were counted among the seventy nations.

## Jacob in Goshen
45:1 Israel came into Egypt, into the land of Goshen, on the first day of the fourth month, in the second year of the third week of the forty-fifth jubilee.
45:2 Joseph came to meet his father Jacob in the land of Goshen. He fell on his father's neck and wept.
45:3 Israel said to Joseph, "Let me die now, because I have seen you. Blessed now be the Lord, the God of Israel, the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac, who has not held back his mercy and his kindness from his servant Jacob.
45:4 It is enough for me that I have seen your face while I am alive. For the vision I saw at Bethel is true. Blessed be the Lord my God forever and ever, and blessed be his name."
45:5 Joseph and his brothers ate bread and drank wine in front of their father. Jacob was filled with very great joy, because he saw Joseph eating with his brothers and drinking in front of him. He blessed the Creator of all things, who had kept him safe and had kept his twelve sons safe for him.
45:6 Joseph gave his father and his brothers, as a gift, the right to live in the land of Goshen and in Rameses, with all the country around it that he ruled under Pharaoh. Israel and his sons lived in the land of Goshen, the best of the land of Egypt. Israel was a hundred and thirty years old when he came into Egypt.
45:7 Joseph fed his father and his brothers, and their livestock too, with as much bread as they could eat for the seven years of famine.

## Joseph Buys Egypt for Pharaoh
45:8 The land of Egypt suffered because of the famine. Joseph acquired the whole land of Egypt for Pharaoh in exchange for food, and the people and their livestock too, and everything became Pharaoh's property.
45:9 The years of famine came to an end. Joseph gave the people in the land seed and food so that they could sow in the eighth year, because the river had flooded the whole land of Egypt.
45:10 For in the seven years of famine it had not watered the land, except for a few places on the banks of the river. But now it flooded. The Egyptians sowed the land, and it produced plenty of good grain that year.
45:11 That year was the first year of the fourth week of the forty-fifth jubilee.
45:12 From the grain of the harvest, Joseph took a fifth for the king, and he left four parts to them for food and for seed. Joseph made it a rule for the land of Egypt that stands to this day.

## Jacob's Death and Burial
45:13 Israel lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years. All the days he lived were three jubilees, a hundred and forty-seven years. He died in the fourth year of the fifth week of the forty-fifth jubilee.
45:14 Before he died, Israel blessed his sons. He told them everything that would happen to them in the land of Egypt, and he made known to them what would come on them in the last days. He blessed them, and he gave Joseph two portions in the land.
45:15 He lay down to rest with his fathers. He was buried in the double cave in the land of Canaan, near Abraham his father, in the grave he had dug for himself in the double cave in the land of Hebron.
45:16 He gave all his books, and the books of his fathers, to his son Levi, so that he would keep them and renew them for his sons until this day.
