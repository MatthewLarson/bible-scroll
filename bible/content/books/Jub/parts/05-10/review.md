# Hostile review: Jubilees 5-10 (part 05-10)

Reviewer note: checked against `texts/orig/Jub.json` (VanderKam Ge'ez + `tr`; `gap`/EOTC), Charles 1913 (`texts/en/Jub.json`), Charles 1902 (Source Library 69e012414e6773d06085614a, pp. xiii [scan 19], 140-147 opened now; the writer's saved pages 148-186 reread), Grintz (EJ, encyclopedia.com, opened now), the site's Genesis, 1 Enoch and NT wording. `node tools/build.js --check Jub/05-10` runs at 0 errors, 0 warnings; `python tools/works-check.py Jub` lists nothing for this part. Where I could not verify something I say so.

## Verdict

The translation is a careful, mostly faithful rendering of VanderKam's Ge'ez, and the notes lean honestly on Charles. The Ge'ez checks I made (5:1-6:38, 7:7-7:22, 8:12-16, 9:3, 10:8-10:35) found no dropped or invented verse. It is not ready to publish. Two attributions are false (a view given to Charles that he does not state, and a stock "readers" line that says more than Charles p. xiii and contradicts Grintz, whom the same part cites). The calendar, which this part owns, is thin and contradicts itself on 1 Enoch. About 167 of 190 verses share runs of five or more words with Charles 1913, while the drift audit reports seven, and no literal working lines exist to show the Ge'ez forces the rest. The Ethiopian, "most churches" and Jewish reception claims in the `views` blocks are unsourced. Several unclear Ge'ez forms are turned into names ("Wadafa River", "Yarbeh") with no variant entry. About twenty smaller defects follow.

## Critical

1. **Charles credited with a view he does not state (5:8).** `notes.json` 5:8 ("Charles reads it this way", `s: charles-jubilees-1902`) and the `settings` entry for 5:8 in chapter 5 (`s: charles-jubilees-1902`). Charles 1902 pp. 141-143 (opened) has notes on 5:1, 2, 4, 10 and 10b-12 only. There is no note on verse 8 and nothing that makes the 120 years the giants' limit. Fix: drop the attribution and the `s` key; state the reading as the writer's observation about where Jubilees places Genesis 6:3, label it `spec` if it is a theory, or cite a source actually opened.
2. **"Most scholars date the book to the 100s BC; R. H. Charles (1902) dated it ... and thought the writer a priestly Pharisee."** Repeated word for word in the `readers` of chapter 5 (5:13-18), chapter 6 (6:10-16 and 6:23-38), chapter 7 (7:20-39) and chapter 10 (10:3-6). Charles p. xiii [scan 19] (opened): "written in Hebrew by a Pharisee between the year of the accession of Hyrcanus ... in 135 and his breach with the Pharisees ... 105 B.C." He does not say "priestly" there. "Most scholars" has no source. Grintz (opened now, the same article the part cites in 6:17-38) says "it is obvious that the author was not a Pharisee." The brief says "never one stock line" for `readers`. Fix: delete the stock line from all five; write a `readers` line per passage from evidence opened. Where the date is mentioned, use `book.json` `audience` and give Charles (Pharisee, 135-105 BC) and Grintz (not a Pharisee) as two views, without "most scholars" or "priestly".

## Major

3. **The calendar (chapter 6, `calendar` topic, notes 6:23-38) is not the "full treatment" the brief assigns, and it contradicts itself.**
   - Note 6:36, `views` and `deep` say Charles read Jubilees as a polemic against 1 Enoch 73-74 "which also follows the moon". Note 6:32 and the `deep` text say Jubilees, like 1 Enoch's Astronomical Book, teaches a 364-day year, citing 1 Enoch 74:12, 75:2 and 82:6. The site's 1 Enoch 74:10 and 74:12 say exactly 364 days. The existing `calendar` topic (section "The 364-day year") presents the Enochic and Jubilees years as one scheme, with VanderKam's view that it was the priestly calendar. A reader gets "allies" and "enemies" in one page. Charles 1902 (p. 155, opened) also notes that the polemic reading is his and that the Samaritan Chronicle attacks the book. Label the polemic as Charles's 1902 theory, say how it fits 74:12, or remove it.
   - "The rabbinic calendar ... is the kind Jubilees condemns" (note 6:36; chapter 6 `deep` "Against the moon") and "the rabbis kept a calendar set by the moon, with added months, and fixed Shavuot by counting fifty days from Passover" (`views.J`) are stated as fact. No source for them is in `sources-checked.md`. The rabbinic fixed calendar is far later than the book. Charles's only rabbinic/Pharisee remark on 6:31 is Beer's suggestion, which Charles rejects (p. 153-154). Fix: source it (`vanderkam-calendars`, `ben-dov-head`, `beckwith-calendar` are in the site's calendar topic; open them) or cut it.
   - The brief asks for "Qumran's calendar texts ... only from sources opened". None was opened (log: "Qumran ... nothing said"). The only Qumran mention is Grintz's phrase "familiar from the Book of Enoch and the Dead Sea Scrolls", reused as a claim about readers at 6:23-38. Book-level `sources.json` now has `djd13-jubilees`, `monger-4q216`, `vanderkam-jubilees-abd`; the fixer should use what was opened there or say nothing.
   - The year's structure is given as "30 days except the 3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th, which have 31" (Grintz; verified). Charles 1902 pp. 153-154 (opened) gives a different structure (12 months of 30 days, "4 intercalary days") and reports Frankel (13 months of 28) and Eppstein (civil 12 months, 8 of 30 and 4 of 31, plus an "ecclesiastical" 13 x 28). Jubilees' own verses give only 13 weeks per quarter and 52 weeks. State the month lengths as Grintz's description, say the book's text fixes only quarters and weeks, and name the other views.
   - No pointer to the existing 1 Enoch 072-082 card ("keeping a set calendar"), though the brief says point to it. Add one in notes 6:32 and 6:36.
4. **Drift audit is not borne out.** By script, 167 of 190 verses have a run of five or more words in the same order as Charles 1913; 40 or more have runs of ten or more (examples: 5:12 fourteen words; 5:18 fourteen; 6:17 sixteen; 6:25 sixteen; 6:34 sixteen; 6:37 fifteen; 7:34 two runs of fourteen; 8:16 twenty; 8:19 eighteen; 8:20 eighteen; 8:21 sixteen; 9:9 seventeen; 9:13 twenty-one; 10:1 seventeen; 10:15 fourteen). Many follow Ge'ez order or the site's Genesis wording, which the audit allows, but `drift-audit.md` lists only seven changed verses and says every other verse was checked "no run of 5+ words beyond what the Ge'ez or the site's Genesis forces". The writer kept no literal lines, so this cannot be shown. The brief says "fresh 8th-grade English, never a modernized Charles". Clearest cases where the Ge'ez does not force Charles's shape: 8:20 (full clause), 8:21 (list frame), 8:16, 8:11, 9:9, 10:15, 10:18, 10:35. Fix: redo the literal line for these verses, rephrase, then keep the working lines in `.scratch/Jub-05-10/` (I found none). Describe the matches with Charles, not NIV/ESV; I found no NIV/ESV run.
5. **Unsourced claims about what churches and Jews believe.** `views` blocks: every `E` entry (chapters 5-10) states how the Ethiopian Orthodox church reads the chapter ("as true account", "in harmony with the Orit", "reads Mastema ... as revealed history", "God limits what evil spirits may do and hears prayer against them"). None is sourced; `sources-checked.md` has no Ethiopian source. Also unsourced: chapter 5 `W` "Many of them read Genesis 6:2 of Seth's line"; chapter 10 `W` "Most churches ... agree with the chapter's main point" (and Job 1:12 / Luke 10:17-20 as church agreement); chapter 7 `J` and note 7:20 "Later rabbis taught seven commandments ... Jubilees' list is earlier and different"; chapter 5 `J` "Later rabbinic tradition turned away from that reading" (Charles p. 141 says the angel view "was condemned probably as early as the first cent. A.D.", which supports a milder statement). CLAUDE.md and STYLE.md rule 2 forbid stating what a church or scholar says without an opened source. Fix: source (for the Ethiopian view, a source opened such as `cowley-ethiopian`, `ullendorff-ethiopia`) or cut to "the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church receives the book as Scripture".
6. **5:22 "in the second jubilee of years": the variant misses Charles's evidence and the Plain makes a cardinal into an ordinal.** Ge'ez `bäkləʾetu ʾiyobelwu` ("in the two (m.) jubilee"). Charles 1902 note on 5:22 (opened, p. 146): "So we should emend the reading of all the MSS [original: twenty-second jubilee]", and he emended to twenty-seventh. VanderKam's "two" is likely "twenty-two" with the tens lost; `variants.json` and note 5:22 say only that Charles "corrected" to twenty-seventh and never give the manuscripts' "twenty-second". Fix: add that reading and its source to the variant; either translate "in the two jubilee" with the note or keep "second" and say in the variant that the Ge'ez has a cardinal. Also state what 1307 A.M. rests on (Charles's emendation).
7. **8:16 and nearby: doubled, bracketed and re-directed text with no note, and a note that misattributes to Charles.**
   - Ge'ez `mängälä sämenu läsämen`. The Plain has "on its south side, to the south". Charles brackets "[To the south]" as dittography and notes "South. Better translate 'north'" (p. 169, opened). Neither is mentioned in the note on 8:16 or in the variants. Add a variant or note. (Ge'ez `sämen` = south and `däbub` = north is consistent with Charles throughout 8:12-8:26; I checked that the Plain follows it.)
   - Note 8:16 says "Charles notes that 1 Enoch seems to place the garden in the northwest." I searched Charles 1902's notes on chapter 8 (pp. 166-170) and found no such remark (only 1 Enoch 24-25 on a south-eastern mountain, in the note on 8:19). Remove or source.
   - 8:6: Charles brackets the first clause "[And in the fourth year he was born]" as dittography (p. 165); the Plain translates it as a sentence with no note, while the variant discusses only "thirtieth" and the stray `bäʿamät`. Add the dittography to the note, as note 6:33 does for its doubled sentence.
8. **Unclear Ge'ez turned into names or readings with no variant entry.** The brief says every departure from VanderKam needs a variant, and any Charles reading that changes meaning does too.
   - 9:3 `wädäfa fäläg`: Plain "it goes up to the Wadafa River". Charles 1902 (p. 168, opened) calls `wadafâ falag` "untranslateable" and says only "if wadafâ were the name of a river" one could emend. The Plain presents the river as established. Add a variant (reading, Charles's emendation "and skirts the river"), and mention in the note that no such river is known.
   - 7:22 `yarbəḥ`: Plain "Yarbeh killed the Naphil". Charles 1913 has "the Giants slew the Naphil", paired with the Greek of 1 Enoch 7 that note 7:22 itself cites (great giants, Nephilim, Eliud). Four names in the sentence read as four kinds of giant, contradicting the note's "three kinds". Recommend "the giants" with the Ge'ez form in the note, or keep the name and add a variant naming Charles's reading.
   - 7:18 `ʾäram wəlud`: Plain "Aram and Lud" reads `wəlud` ("born", "children") as `wä` + `lud`. Charles emended and moved Lud before Aram. The note says it is uncertain; there is no variant.
   - Also unvarianted though Charles's English differs: 7:24 (his added verb), 9:2 (his "Ham"), 10:6 (his emendation "canst exercise dominion"), 7:36 (his "and all things").

## Minor

9. **Note 8:1 `orig` is empty (`" ()"`).** Supply `ቃይናም (qaynam)` as written in the note text.
10. **Note 10:30 `orig` is the wrong word.** The note is about `hakak` ("rebellion", Ge'ez `በሀከክ`, three times in the verse); `orig` gives `ወደቂቅከሂ` ("and your children also"). Replace.
11. **Variant 6:2: "the two differ by one letter" is wrong.** `ʾästärʾäyä` (አስተርአየ) and `ʾästäsräyä` (አስተሰርየ) differ in two letters (a swap of `r`/`ʾ` with `s`/`r`). Also Charles 1902 (p. 147, opened) prints "made atonement" twice and notes that Jewish haggadah knows of no such act; the writer's "same verb later in the verse" is right, the "one letter" is not.
12. **Note 5:28, "No one has identified it on a map."** Not in Charles, not in the log. Remove or source.
13. **Note 5:24 "`mänbäḥbaḥt` means a sluice or channel"; note 5:23 "`śärq`, 'rising'"; term proposal "`mälʾäk` ... here 'leader'".** Lexical claims with no dictionary opened. Charles translates only. Source them or give Charles's rendering as his. For 10:8 the Ge'ez is literally "angel of the spirits" (`mälʾäkä mänafsət mästema`); the Plain's "chief" is Charles's rendering, so say that. The term proposal also spells `mänafəst`; the verse has `mänafsət`.
14. **Note 8:16, 8:30, 8:12 and others state facts with no source:** "Greek writers said the same of their own lands" (8:30); "The Don was the old boundary between Europe and Asia" (8:12); "Many readers see ... a weapon of war hung up" (6:16); Greek Genesis 6:4 "gigantes" (5:1, not in the log; verify in the LXX). Verify or delete.
15. **Note 6:35 offers the writer's own idea, "The writer may also have had in view the Greek customs ... (1 Maccabees 1:41-50)", and "that is, the moon-based calendars of Israel's neighbors"** as plain statement. Label as `spec` or cut; Charles says only "Gentiles".
16. **6:5 "be a blessing in it" and "I give the fear and the dread".** `maʾkälä` (among, in the midst of) stands before `fərhatkəmu`, and `laʿleha` ("upon it") belongs to the preceding "become many"; "in it" after "blessing" is Charles's "upon it", not the Ge'ez. `ʾəhub` is future/subjunctive ("I will put"), not "I give". Rework or note.
17. **5:6 "bound down there".** Ge'ez `ʾəsuran maʾkälomu wäbḥətwan` ("bound in the midst of them, and are kept separate", as Charles). "Down there" has no Ge'ez counterpart and is unmarked. Restore or note.
18. **7:21 "the command of their ordinances".** `kʷənänehomu` is the word rendered "judgment" at 5:13, 5:14 and 10:6; "ordinances" is the term for `śərʿat` (terms entry). Use "judgments" or add a note that Charles's "ordinances" is his choice.
19. **7:10 "A slave in bondage".** `qənuyä gäbrä` ("owned/enslaved servant"); note 7:10 says "a slave who is owned", the gloss says "owned outright", the Plain says "in bondage". Pick one and keep note, gloss and Plain aligned; the site's Genesis has "slave of slaves" and the note correctly says the Ge'ez differs.
20. **7:10 note, "Jubilees agrees that Ham was the younger".** Site Genesis 9:24 has "youngest"; the Ge'ez `zäynəʾs` has no comparative form, so "younger" and "not the youngest" are both inference. Say so.
21. **6:7 "Then your blood will not be demanded".** The Ge'ez (`kämä ʾiytəḫaśäś`) is a purpose clause ("so that"); "Then" reads as a result promised after not eating.
22. **10:11 "to be subject to Satan on the earth".** Ge'ez `kämä yəkʷännu qədmä säyṭan` ("before Satan"); the Plain drops `qədmä` and matches Charles 1913's wording "might be subject before Satan on the earth" for "be subject ... Satan on the earth". Note 10:11 already says the verb is ambiguous; give both senses in the translation or the variant.
23. **Satan rule (your question).** Site practice (`terms.json`: `ha-satan` = "the Accuser", a title; 1 Chronicles 21:1 `satan` without article = "Satan") fits capital "Satan" here: `säyṭan` without an article names a person, identified in note 10:11 with Mastema. Part 46-50 uses lowercase "satan" in "no satan and nothing evil" (a common noun, also 23:29, 40:9, 50:5). Recommend one rule, written once in `terms.json`: capital for a named adversary (10:11), lowercase for the "no satan" formula, with a note at first use in each part. The gloss at 10:11 ("Satan: the Accuser") clashes with the site's "the Accuser = ha-satan, with article"; change it to "the adversary Mastema" or drop it. The gloss "Mastema: the Hostile One" turns an abstract noun ("hostility", Hosea 9:7-8 per Charles) into a person label; Charles glosses "enmity/the adversary".
24. **`mastema` topic.** Not a duplicate of `satan` (`aka` of `satan` has no Mastema) and the brief names it as this part's core topic. But the `satan` contribution repeats the same 10:8-11 paragraph as `mastema`. Cut the `satan` text to one sentence plus "See Mastema", and add "Mastema" to `satan`'s `aka`. In `mastema`, the line "a move found also in 1 Chronicles 21:1 compared with 2 Samuel 24:1" is unsourced; the sections citing 17:16, 48:2, 48:9 and 49:2 rest on Charles's note on 10:8 (verified there), which is right, but the first section's "Charles notes ... only in the Acts of Philip" is Rönsch's remark as Charles reports it: say so.
25. **The four proposed terms** (`mälʾäkä mänafəst`, `śärq`, `ḫafrät`, `mäkfält`/`ləsan`): all consistent with the Plain. Fix the transliteration in the first (see 13). `śärq` entry is fine; the "rising" claim in note 5:23 is unsourced (see 13).
26. **Settings without `s` or evidence.** 7:10-12 and 10:22 (chapter 10) have no sources; 6:23-38 `readers` ("the readers were Jews who cared that the Temple's feasts fell on the right days") is the writer's inference. Add sources or label.
27. **Chapter 8 and 9 `N` views quote Acts 17:26 as "made from one every nation" / "the boundaries".** Site Acts 17:26 reads "He made every nation of humankind out of one person ... the limits of the places where they would live". Use the site's wording exactly.
28. **Glosses.** 5:6 "kept apart: held in solitary confinement" (Ge'ez `bḥətwan`, "separated"; "solitary confinement" is an addition); 7:30 "Do a good work for yourselves: earn credit for your own souls" (a doctrinal reading not in `məṣwat laʿlä näfsəkmu`); 8:11 "bosom ... fold of Noah's robe" fine.
29. **Chapter 9 `H` and note 9:1 "Canaan's lot was in Africa".** The text gives only "west of Put ... the sea"; "Africa" is Charles's inference (note on 9:1: "north-west Africa", p. 175). Say "Charles took it as north-west Africa".
30. **10:21 numerals.** The Ge'ez reads `ḫämsa məʾt wäʾärbaʿtu məʾt wäśälasa wäśälas bäʾmät` ("five hundreds, four hundreds, thirty, three cubits"). The Plain gives "5,433", Charles's figure. I could not verify from the Ge'ez how "five hundreds" yields five thousand; say in the note that 5,433 is Charles's reading and the Ge'ez words are as quoted.
31. **Reading level.** Sentences of 46 to 70 words at 5:1, 6:11, 6:35, 7:21, 8:10, 8:18, 8:21. Split where the Ge'ez allows.
