# Hostile review: Jubilees 46-50 (tier eth)

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-12. Checked: plain.txt, notes.json (99), variants.json (14), glosses.json (33), chapters.json (5), topics.json (18) against `texts/orig/Jub.json` (`gez`, `tr`, `eotc`, `gap`), Charles 1913 in `texts/en/Jub.json`, Charles 1902 (Source Library `69e012414e6773d06085614a`, pages 343-359 opened in full, pages 349-350 and 353 also as OCR), Dillmann 1859 p. 160 (Source Library `6953113277f38f6761bcbf62`, scan 174), the site's Exodus, John, Acts, Wisdom, 1 Maccabees, Judith and 1 Enoch wording, and `content/proposals/works/`. `node tools/build.js --check Jub/46-50` is 0 errors, 0 warnings. `python tools/works-check.py Jub` reports 0 errors for this part and lists 46:14 only as a flagged reading (see 11); per the brief the tool is unreliable for Jubilees.

## Verdict

A careful, honest part. The Plain English Translation follows VanderKam's Ge'ez closely verse by verse (all 83 verses checked word by word against `tr`; no dropped clauses, no wrong numbers, names or day counts; the dates 2242, 2263, 2303, 2330, 2372, 2410 all recompute from the jubilee arithmetic). Every Latin and Greek reading in the variants (et Oon, in terram Med(ian), in via in qua praeteristi eum in refectione, comedetis illud, Non erit tribulatio in filiis Istrahel in die hac, Cedrenus's "τῇ ιδ'") is printed in Charles 1902 on the pages the writer names, and each is attributed as "as Charles 1902 reports it", which is adequate: Charles's own conclusions are kept apart from the readings, and the Latin is used only in verses where it survives (46:12-48:5, 49:7b-22). Every quotation of Exodus, John, Acts, Wisdom, 1 Maccabees, Judith and 1 Enoch that I tested matches the site's wording exactly (I ran every double-quoted string in notes.json against all other books' plain.txt; the only apparent misses were broken quote pairing in my script, and Exodus 1:9-10 is spliced with an ellipsis-free join that is fair). Charles's notes are reported accurately in almost every case. The weaknesses are: one number in the text ("a million") and its supporting claim about the Ge'ez word for "thousand" are unsourced and probably wrong; the 48:17 treatment mixes witnesses and misreports what Dillmann's page shows; Qumran and VanderKam claims in a chapter 49 setting have no opened source; "three weeks" and "five weeks and one year" will be misread as days; and a cluster of smaller unsourced attributions, missing variant entries and one wrong `settings` line. No finding is critical (no invented source, reading or fragment).

## Findings

### Major

**1. Major. 48:14, plain.txt and note "A hundred ten-thousands" (47th note). "A million" rests on an unsourced and probably wrong claim about Ge'ez `ʾəlf`.**
The Ge'ez is `ምእተ ፡ እልፈ (məʾtä ʾəlfä)`. The note says "A Ge'ez ʾəlf is ten thousand (Ge'ez uses ʾəlf where Hebrew and Greek use a myriad), so the figure is 1,000,000." No source is named and nothing in sources-checked.md covers it. Charles 1902 (p. 350) prints "1,000,000" with no note on the numeral. `ʾəlf` is the ordinary Ge'ez word for "thousand" (the Ge'ez numeral grammar I found in a web search lists እልፍ ʾǝlf as the thousand term; I could not open Dillmann's or Leslau's lexicon, so I cannot confirm either way). The same verse writes 1,000 as `ዐሠርቱ ፡ ምእት (ʿäśärtu məʾt)`, "ten hundred", which would be odd if `ʾəlf` were already ten thousand. If `ʾəlf` = 1,000, `məʾtä ʾəlfä` is 100,000, not a million. This is the only occurrence of `ʾəlf` in Jubilees (script check), so there is no internal check.
Fix: do not assert "ʾəlf is ten thousand" unless a lexicon opened in this session says so. Either render a number the Ge'ez forces ("a hundred thousand of them", with a note that Charles prints 1,000,000) after checking Dillmann's *Lexicon* or Leslau, or keep "a million" and say only that Charles prints that figure and that the Ge'ez numeral is not settled. Change the note title ("A hundred ten-thousands") and the `plagues` contribution and any other place that repeats the figure if it changes.

**2. Major. 48:17, variants (two entries), note 52, chapter 48 `deep` table. The "hearts" departure is handled as a patch, and the second entry misreports Dillmann.**
(a) I opened Dillmann 1859 p. 160 (scan 174). The printed text reads `ወአጽንዑ ፡ አልባቢሆሙ ፡ ወአንየሎሙ ፡ ወተንየለ ፡ እምኀበ ፡ እግዚአብሔር` with apparatus "ወአጽንዑ T, ወጸንዑ A" and "ወተንየሎሙ T". So Dillmann's whole sentence differs from VanderKam's `ወአጽንዐ ፡ አልባሲሆሙ ፡ ወአኀየሎሙ ፡ ወተኀየሉ`: not only ቢ/ሲ (hearts/clothes) but the verb number (plural `ʾäṣnəʿu`, "they hardened", against singular `ʾäṣnəʿä`), and `ʾanyälomu` / `tänäyälä` (root *nayala*, "devise") against `ʾäḫäyälomu` / `täḫäyälu` ("made strong"). The writer's second 48:17 variant says Charles's "device was devised" "reflects a different reading; his note does not explain it." Dillmann's text, which the writer opened (sources-checked.md lists pp. 172-175), is exactly that reading, and it differs from VanderKam by one letter (ኀ/ን), the same kind of one-letter difference used to justify "hearts". Charles's "made them stubborn" also comes from the Dillmann-type text.
(b) The Plain English Translation takes Dillmann's noun ("hearts") but VanderKam's verb ("he hardened", singular) and VanderKam's "made bold", a combination no witness reads. The variant entry presents it as a one-word swap and does not say so. If a one-letter copyist's slip is enough to leave VanderKam for "hearts", the same reasoning applies two words later, and the entry's "VanderKam gives good sense and is followed" is inconsistent.
(c) Whether VanderKam's printed text really has `ʾälbasihomu` could not be checked: the Open Siddur transcription is the only source and the EOTC copy agrees with it (not independent after ch. 5, as the brief says). The variant should say that VanderKam's apparatus was not seen.
(d) The chapter 48 `deep` table lists the hardening at the sea as a deed Jubilees gives to Mastema ("after Mastema was loosed"), and note 52 correctly says the subject is only "he". The table states as settled what the note presents as Charles's reading.
Fix: put both Dillmann readings in the variants with the apparatus (T/A), state in the entry that the Plain English Translation's "He hardened their hearts and made them bold" is a blend (Dillmann's "hearts", VanderKam's verb forms), say VanderKam's apparatus was not seen, and either follow Dillmann for the whole clause (with "device was devised"/"plan") or defend keeping VanderKam for the rest. Soften the table cell to "Charles reads 'he' as Mastema (48:16-17)".

**3. Major. chapters.json ch. 49 `settings.readers`, `settings.views` ("A priestly rule book"); also `readers` in chs. 46, 47, 48, 50. Claims about Qumran and VanderKam with no opened source; a dating stated flat.**
Ch. 49: "copies of the book kept at Qumran show it was read by a group that cared about such things (see the book's introduction)" and "VanderKam thinks a priest wrote the book (see the book's introduction)." The brief's rule is explicit: cite a Qumran copy or what it shows only if a page giving it was opened in this session and named. sources-checked.md lists no Qumran source and no VanderKam commentary; the only keyed source on these entries is `charles-jubilees-1902`, a 1902 book that cannot report Qumran. "See the book's introduction" shifts the claim to the book-level writer; the part must not assert it unsourced, and CLAUDE.md bars hedges ("see...") as a substitute. The `readers` lines of chs. 46-50 all say "Jews in Judea in the 100s BC" as if settled; ch. 47 adds "under strong Greek influence", which no opened source states. The brief asks that a debated audience or date not be stated as settled and that each dating view carry who holds it.
Fix: delete the Qumran and VanderKam sentences (or source them from a page actually opened and name it); write the dating as Charles's (and name that it is his) or as the book-level audience card's range; drop "under strong Greek influence" or tie it to a source.

**4. Major. plain.txt 47:9, 48:1, 50:4. Durations written "three weeks", "five weeks and one year", "one week and two years" read as days.**
`subaʿe` is "week of years". The brief says render "week of years" at first use and "week" only where a dating formula repeats. 46:1 and 47:10 do say "weeks of years", but 47:9 ("When you had completed three weeks, he brought you into the royal court"), 48:1 ("lived there for five weeks and one year") and 50:4 ("forty-nine jubilees ... and one week and two years") are durations, not dating formulas, and an 8th-grade reader will take "three weeks" at court and "five weeks and one year" in exile as a few weeks. Notes explain it later (47:9 history note, 48:1 note) but the translation text should not need them.
Fix: "three weeks of years" (47:9), "five weeks of years and one year" (48:1), "one week of years and two years" (50:4).

### Minor

**6. Minor. note 12 (46:12).** "Most readers, Charles among them, take both the shutting of the gates and the plot as the king of Egypt's acts." **7. Minor. chapters.json ch. 47 `settings.when`.** "on Mount Sinai, forty years after the events (48:1)". **11. **12. (b) Charles 1902 (p. 349, note on 48:9) says manuscripts a and b read "the prince **of the** Mastêmâ" in 48:9, 12, 15 and that c and d, and "all manuscripts ... in 17:6, 48:2", give "prince Mastêmâ". **13.
