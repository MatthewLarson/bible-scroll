# Hostile review: Jubilees (Jub), part 32-38

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-12. Scope: plain.txt, notes.json, variants.json, glosses.json, chapters.json, topics.json, sources.json of `parts/32-38`, plus the proposal entry `prayer-of-joseph` in `content/proposals/works/works.json`.

## Verdict

A careful part. I compared all 178 verses with VanderKam's Ge'ez (`texts/orig/Jub.json`, transliteration and script) and with Charles 1913 for sense. I found no mistranslation that changes a name, number or who-does-what, apart from one wrong rendering at 36:6. The three Latin departures (32:4 twice, 34:4) are justified and each has a variant entry. The kept-VanderKam places (32:8, 36:6, 36:13, 38:1), 34:20, 32:29, 37:20 and the 34:2 page slip are handled honestly in variants and notes. Every Ge'ez quotation in variants and notes is found in the named verse (checked by script). Every quoted verse of the 66 books matches the site's wording. `node tools/build.js --check Jub/32-38` gives 0 errors and 0 warnings. `python tools/works-check.py Jub` flags only the known false positive at 34:20 for this part. No EOTC reading is claimed in any gap verse, and `auto` has no entries in 32-38.

I opened Charles 1902 (the writer's scratch copies of Source Library pp. 289-321, and the archive.org scan `bookofjubileesor00char`) and the Gutenberg Whiston text in the writer's scratch. Charles's notes were checked for: Syncellus and Cedrenus on 32:3 and the Syncellus citation on 35:9, the Origen/Prayer of Joseph Greek at 32:21, retentatio and epischesis/epithesis at 32:27, "very corrupt" at 32:29, Neeman as "the invention of an Ethiopic scribe" and the Syriac "Adni" at 34:20, wakama for waemze at 36:6, 'ehub at 36:13, the Yalkut passages at 37:14, 38:4 and 38:9, the Test. Judah 9 quotations, Adora and Hyrcanus at 38:8, and the guardian angel at 35:17. All are accurate. Josephus Ant. 16.6.2 ("Hyrcanus the high priest of the Almighty God") and 13.9.1 ("Dora and Marissa") are in Whiston as quoted. Shabbat 55b and Yoma 8:9 are quoted correctly from the Davidson English.

The main weakness is not the translation. It is the chapter `settings`, where the "readers" paragraph is the stock line the brief forbade (finding 1), together with a few claims about churches and a handful of hedges that STYLE rule 2 forbids. No finding is critical: I found no invented source, reading or fragment.

## Findings

### Major

**1. Stock pasted "readers" paragraph in ten settings.** chapters.json, `settings[].readers` at 32:17-19, 35:1-8, 35:9-17, 35:18-26, 36:1-11, 36:12-16, 37:2-8, 37:17-23, 38:1 and 38:15-24. Each carries the same sentence: "Most scholars: Jews in the land of Israel in the 100s BC, very likely a priestly circle, for whom the book showed that the law was older than Moses (see the book's opening pages). Hebrew copies of Jubilees were kept at Qumran. Ethiopian Orthodox readers receive it as Scripture, the revelation given to Moses on Sinai." The brief says "Never one stock line." The same paragraph is attached to a mother's private request (35:1-8), to Isaac's deathbed oath and to Judah's shout (38:1). It has no `s` key in most of these settings, and "very likely a priestly circle" is a dating and authorship claim given with no source. It answers "who read the book", not "who first heard these words, and why", and `readers` is meant for evidence about readers of the book (see the brief, "Who it was for, and why"). The first hearers inside the story are already in `to`.
Fix: write a specific `readers` per setting from evidence actually opened, or leave `readers` out. Good ones already exist in this file: 32:10-15 (Tobit 1:7), 33:10-20 (the Mishnah, `s` set), 37:17-23 (Charles on Hasmonean readers), 34:18-19 (Qumran calendar). For the Qumran claim, name the source (for example `vanderkam-calendars`, or the Jub book-level `djd13-jubilees`). Do not repeat the Ethiopian sentence ten times; it belongs once, at book level.

**2. 36:6, "After him I too worshiped him."** The Ge'ez is `wäʾməz ʾänäni ʾämläkwo`, "and after this I too worshiped him" (VanderKam; Charles's note on 36:6: "How = wakama emended from waemze = 'and after this'"). `ʾəmz` means "after this" or "from now on", never "after him". The Plain English Translation changes "this" to "him" and so invents a claim: that Isaac worshiped after Abraham. The variant entry then prints the Ge'ez reading as "and after that I too worshiped him" while the `plain` column says "After him". The two do not match, and the `why` ("after Abraham, Isaac served the same God") defends an interpretation as if it were the text.
Fix: "And after this I too worshiped him" (or a freer rendering that still keeps "after this"), with the note saying that Charles emended to "and how" for sense. Correct the variant `plain` to match.

**3. Church and tradition claims asserted without any source opened (STYLE rule 2, canon-tiers).**
- notes.json 36:7: "most churches allow oaths in court, while Anabaptists and Quakers refuse them." No confession or catechism is cited. Either cite one per tradition or cut it down to what Matthew 5:34 and James 5:12 say.
- chapters.json 35 `views.E`: "Jacob's guardian is read as God's care, or as the angel God sets over him." This is stated as an Ethiopian Orthodox reading with no source. The other E entries ("Read as Scripture, the chapter shows God's order...") are the writer's own application, labeled with no source. The same applies to `W` at 33 ("Most churches read Genesis 35:22 as the record of a real sin..."), 37 ("Most churches read Genesis' account ... as the true end of their quarrel") and 38 ("Most churches read Genesis' kings of Edom as ... Edom's early rulers"). I opened nothing that supports these, and nothing in the part cites a church source.
Fix: re-word E and W as plain statements of what the book does (without "Ethiopian readers read it as..."), or cite an EOTC source (for example `cowley-ethiopian`) and a source for the W claims, or mark them as Claude's observation.

**4. STYLE rule 2 hedges still in content.** notes.json 32:8 (spec): "I have not seen it in published work." 34:18 (spec): "I could not check whether later commentators do." Rule 2 bans publishing an unchecked detail behind a hedge. "As Charles reports it" is used in chapters.json (two places), notes.json (three: 34:15, 38:2 and one more) and topics.json (dinah). I confirmed those Charles statements, so they are attributions, not hedges; but the brief's wording asks for plain "Charles says" with the source key. Fix: delete the two "I" sentences (the labels `spec` and "Claude's theory" already carry the status) and change the "as Charles reports" phrasing to "Charles notes".

**5. 34:20, "The name of Zebulun's wife was Neeman." (plain.txt, notes.json)** VanderKam prints the name in brackets with a question mark. Charles's manuscripts a, b and d omit it, and he says it "may be the invention of an Ethiopic scribe." The Plain English Translation gives it as firmly as the eleven other names, and there is no note at this verse: the doubt exists only in the variant entry. A reader of the plain line gets a false certainty. Fix: add a `word` or `history` note anchored on "Neeman" saying that the name is doubtful (VanderKam's brackets and question mark; missing in a, b, d; Syriac list "Adni"), or mark it in the line, for example "Neeman (the name is doubtful)". Separately, the Syriac letters `ܥܕܢܝ` in the variant were typed by the writer. Charles's printed Syriac is garbled in the Source Library OCR ("Adni" is the transliteration Charles gives), so the Syriac spelling was not checked against a page image. Verify it or print only "Adni".

### Minor

**6. Source key `charles-jubilees-1902` ("read in the Source Library").** The Source Library transcription of pp. 292-294 (32:16-34) contains the translation only, with none of Charles's notes. I found the notes (forsakes Gen. xxxv. 12, Prayer of Joseph, retentatio/Rönsch, "very corrupt" at 32:29, the Latin readings) in the archive.org scan `bookofjubileesor00char`, and the writer's quotations match them exactly. But `sources-checked.md` says the notes were read in the Source Library scans 289-321, which is not what the Source Library page shows for pp. 292-294. Fix: name where those notes were actually read; if it was the archive.org scan, say so in the shared source line or the log.

**7. `josephus-ant-whiston-web` is defined (in `His` 02-02) as covering "10.11.7, 19.9.1, 20.5.2, 20.7.1-3".** This part cites 13.9.1 and 16.6.2 under it. Both passages check out (Gutenberg 2848), but the key's own text does not list them. Fix: lead to extend the key's string (add the sections), or add a line in this part's `sources.json` naming them.

**8. 36:18 "twenty-five weeks and five years", and 35:27 "three jubilees and one week and one year old".** The brief fixes "week of years" for subaʿe. At 35:27 the note at 35:6 explains the arithmetic; at 36:18 (Isaac, 180) nothing says a "week" here is seven years, and a reader will take "weeks" as seven days. Fix: "twenty-five weeks of years and five years" (and likewise 35:27), or add a one-line note at 36:18 (25 x 7 + 5 = 180).

**9. 32:4 "fourteen young bulls" but 32:6 "two bulls".** Both are `ʾäswarä` in the Ge'ez. "Young" has no basis. Fix: "bulls" in both.

**10. Glosses.**
- 33:7 "I am not clean for you" glossed "I can no longer be your wife." The verse says she is defiled with respect to Jacob; it does not say the marriage ends, and 33:9 says only that he never came near her again.
- 32:15 "engraved" glossed "carved in stone." The heavenly tablets are never said to be stone (the note says tablets, Ge'ez ṣəlat; Moses' stone tablets are a different thing in the brief).
- 34:8 "a tribute" glossed "a yearly payment to the winner." "Yearly" is not in the Ge'ez or Charles (38:13 says only that they kept paying).
- 32:1 "God Most High" glossed "the highest God of all." That suggests a rank among gods; "Most High" is a title.
Fix each (the first two are plain errors).

**11. 38:12 note.** "The Testament of Judah 9 says Edom paid two hundred measures of wheat a year until Jacob's family went down to Egypt (Charles)." Charles's Greek quotation reads "πυροῦ κόρους διακοσίους ... ἕως ὅτε κατήλθομεν εἰς Αἴγυπτον", glossed "two hundred kor of wheat ... until we went down into Egypt." "A year" does not appear in the quoted text (an ellipsis follows the wheat). Fix: "two hundred kor of wheat" and drop "a year".

**12. 36:18 `p` line** says "Genesis 35:29 gives the same age"; the age is in Genesis 35:28 (the `b` text says 35:28). Fix: 35:28.

**13. Unsourced or unlabeled claims in notes.**
- 37:10: "In the Dead Sea Scrolls the Kittim are the great enemy from the west", "which most readers take as the Romans" (Daniel 11:30), and "may reflect the wars of the writer's own time." No source opened, and the last is a theory not labeled `spec`. Cite or cut.
- 32:16: "The angel's words leave the honor of the one chosen place of 32:10 for Jerusalem's sanctuary." Jubilees does not name Jerusalem here; state it as the book's likely reading, with who holds it.
- 37:21: "Christian readers see the peace of Isaiah 11 begun in Christ (Ephesians 2:14-17)." Unsourced generalization.
- 32:8 note and variant: "Every witness has 'he gave.'" The Latin breaks off at 32:8a, so the Latin is not a witness for the negative; say "every Ge'ez manuscript Charles used".

**14. Settings missing for a vision and several speeches.** The brief asks for a setting for every speech, testament, blessing, prayer, law and vision. Not covered: Levi's dream (32:1, a vision), Bilhah's report to Jacob (33:7), Jacob's lament (34:13, 34:17), Esau's renunciation of the birthright (36:14). Also the `when` of the 32:21-25 setting ("the night of the twenty-second of the seventh month") is an inference: the verse says only "this month", and the seventh month comes from the Feast of Booths in 32:4. Say "the seventh month (the feast month)" or leave the month out.

**15. Lens placement.** chapters.json 36 `views.J` offers the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs as Jewish reception of Isaac's testament. They survive only in Christian transmission (Greek, Armenian), and Charles's point is shared phrasing ("slept the eternal sleep"), not Jewish reception. Move it to a neutral lens or name it as shared vocabulary.

**16. Unmarked corrupt or doubtful lines.** 32:29 and 37:20 are printed as continuous English ("goes up into the testimony of the feast days"; "The breasts were parted from their mother"). Both are flagged in notes and variants and the Ge'ez does read so, but the plain line gives no signal. Optional: end the note's `p` line with "the Ge'ez here is damaged" so the one-line card warns the reader. (Not required by the brief; sibling parts do the same.)

**17. Variants not entered for Latin readings the writer's own log lists.** `sources-checked.md` lists the Latin at 34:3 "pusillanimes" (faint-hearted, against "sorrowful"), 32:30 ("of this place", d and Latin) and 36:20 (a note exists but no variant). None changes who does what, so none is a defect; add them only if the lead wants every Latin difference entered.

## What I checked and found sound
- Drift audit against Charles 1913: the sentence shapes follow the Ge'ez, not Charles (for example 33:4, 35:6, 37:17, 37:24). Where the Plain English Translation matches Charles it is forced (names, the king list, 36:23).
- No copyrighted translation matched. The wording of 32:2, 32:3 and 34:13 is shaped by the Ge'ez order, not by VanderKam or Wintermute as far as I can tell; I did not have their text open, so I make no claim beyond that.
- Names: "Rebekah" throughout (Genesis spelling); Charles's "Rebecca" is not used. "Bilhah ... slave woman" matches Genesis 29:29 and 30:3. "Hanoch" follows Genesis 46:9. Kings of Edom keep the Ge'ez forms with the Genesis forms in notes.
- Genesis wording: 35:8, 35:10, 35:16, 35:18, 35:22, 37:36 and 46:9 quoted exactly. No misquotes found.
- Works naming: Testament of Levi, Reuben and Judah match works.json `testaments-twelve` aka; "Prayer of Joseph" is proposed in `content/proposals/works/works.json` as tier `other`, "lost Jewish writing known only from quotations in Origen" (accurate; Charles quotes Origen); "Added by Jub 32-38" belongs in the proposal log, not in the public `note`, so remove that sentence before merge.
- Source key for Talmud Shabbat 55b is defined once in this part's sources.json and used only in the 33:15 note and chapters 33. No clash found.
- Cross-references: forward and back references within the book (45:16, 38:2, 6:32, 21:8, 30:7-17) resolve to the right verses in the texts I could see (45:16 and 21:8 not opened).
- Traditions cards: none, and none is required. Tithes (Mal, 1Co, 2Co) and oaths are not carded by this part; if oaths are to be carded, that is the lead's call, with finding 3.
