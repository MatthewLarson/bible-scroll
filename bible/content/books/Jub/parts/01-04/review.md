# Hostile review: Jubilees, part 01-04 (prologue 1:0 and chapters 1-4)

## Verdict

The translation is sound. I read every verse of 1:0-4:33 against `gez`/`tr` and found no mistranslation of substance. Names, numbers, dates (the anno mundi sums at 4:1, 4:7, 4:29, 4:33 all check out), the 22 works and the Genesis wording (all quoted Genesis, Exodus, Psalm, 2 Peter, 2 Corinthians and Acts lines match the site's current text) hold. `node tools/build.js --check Jub/01-04` gives 0 errors and 0 warnings. `python tools/works-check.py Jub` lists nothing for 01-04. The Epiphanius readings at 2:2 and 2:23 are quoted faithfully from Charles 1902 (Source Library 69e012414e6773d06085614a, pp. 112, 113, 115, 116 opened this session). The weak points are in the apparatus and settings. Three typing slips in the transcription are shown as VanderKam's readings and not corrected silently. The plain text says "new moon" against a term fixed by a sibling part and against the writer's own 2:9 note. A real departure at 1:11 has no variant entry. One stock "readers" paragraph is pasted into four settings. No critical findings. Several details I could not verify are listed under findings 8 to 12.

No duplicate topic: I searched titles and `aka` of `content/topics/` and `content/proposals/*/topics/`. `heavenly-tablets` clashes with none. `book-of-life` is separate, and `ten-commandments` has the `aka` "the tablets" but is a different subject. The terms entries match the brief and the existing 1 Enoch entries (the Lord, the Most High, the Watchers, the heavenly tablets, Beliar, "the angels of holiness").

## Findings

### 1. MAJOR. variants.json 4:2, 4:4, 4:5 (and silent slips at 3:12, 3:20): transcription slips are listed as witnesses' readings
- **What is wrong.** The three entries put a typing slip in a reading row labelled "VanderKam's Ge'ez text (1989)". At 4:2, `ወልደ በታስዕ ኢዮቤልዉ …` is 4:14's tail pasted in. At 4:4, `እፓኑሁ` is a non-word. At 4:5, `ጽሳተ` is for `ጽላተ`. These are not manuscript readings. The lead already treats such cases as corrected text: `fixed` in `texts/orig/Jub.json` holds the 5:27 and 34:2 slips. A reader sees "VanderKam reads X, Dillmann reads Y" and takes it for a textual debate. The entries also cannot say whether the slip is in the printed VanderKam or only in the transcription. 4:2's `p` and `why` do say "as transcribed", which is honest, but the `readings` rows still give it as a witness. The EOTC digital copy repeats the 4:2 slip exactly. That shows the EOTC file is not independent here (the entry says so), but it also hints the slip may be older than Open Siddur. Nobody has checked.
- **Also at 4:2.** The entry says "Charles's four manuscripts a, b, c, d" have the clause "(God) accepted from his hand the offering, and from Cain's hand did not accept his sacrifice". Charles's note on 4:2 (scan 128) says something different. All four MSS read "the sacrifice at his hand" for Abel. "Offering" is in a and c only. "Of Cain" is in a only, and b, c, d have "at the hand of Cain". Charles's English has "sacrifice of Abel ... offering of Cain". The plain text and the entry's English give the reverse: Abel's "offering" and Cain's "sacrifice". The entry's Dillmann reading rests on his OCR Latin, and the writer's own log says "OCR is poor".
- **Fix.** Drop the slip rows as "readings". Report them as one note at each verse: "The Ge'ez as transcribed is corrupt here; the text is corrected from Dillmann (1859) and Charles." Ask the lead to add 4:2, 4:4, 4:5, 3:12 and 3:20 to the `fixed` list in `texts/orig/Jub.json`. Check whether the printed VanderKam has the slip. If it cannot be checked, say so. At 4:2 restate the manuscript distribution exactly as Charles gives it. Swap "offering/sacrifice" to match Charles (Abel's sacrifice, Cain's offering) or say why not. At 4:4 and 4:5 quote Dillmann's Ge'ez only if read from the page image.

### 2. MAJOR. plain.txt 3:32 and 1:14: "new moon" against the fixed term and the book's own argument
- **What is wrong.** 3:32 has "On the new moon of the fourth month" for `ʾämä śärqä wärḫ rabʿ`. 1:14 has "new moons". The sibling part's entry in `content/proposals/works/terms.json` ("śärq, of a month ... Added by Jub 05-10") fixes it as "the first day (of the month) ... Never 'new moon': Jubilees' months are not set by the moon (6:36)." The 01-04 note at 2:9 says the book "leaves the moon out" and argues against counting by the moon. The plain text then dates the expulsion by a moon. Charles's English has "new moon" and the plain text probably follows it.
- **Fix.** 3:32 "On the first day of the fourth month". For 1:14 follow the list rule in that entry ("the start of the month" in lists) or give a note saying that Charles reads "new moons" and the Ge'ez word is `śärq`. Record the term in 01-04's own entry.

### 3. MAJOR. plain.txt 1:11 (note only): a departure from VanderKam with no variant entry
- **What is wrong.** The note "Past tense in a prophecy" says that VanderKam's Ge'ez has `wägäbru`, `wäsägädu` (past forms), that the EOTC copy has futures, and that the Plain English Translation "keeps the future". The brief says "Every departure of the Plain English Translation from VanderKam's Ge'ez needs a variant entry." `auto` lists both differences at 1:11, and the verse is not in `gap`. The EOTC witness may be cited. That makes the choice a textual one, not only a style one.
- **Fix.** Add a variant entry at 1:11 with both forms, the witness names, and the follow decision. Keep the note.
- **Related, minor.** The same rule applies at 1:4. VanderKam has "all the days, and the law, and the testimony". The EOTC has "the days of the law and the testimony". The plain text follows VanderKam but has no entry. Likewise 1:12 `ኀቤሆሙ` against `ላዕሌሆሙ` ("to" against "upon"), 1:15 and 1:17 ("their God"). Enter only those that change the meaning, and say so.

### 4. MAJOR. chapters.json settings: one stock "readers" line pasted into four settings
- **Where.** The "Jews of Judea in the 100s BC. Charles 1902 found in the book allusions to events as late as the early Maccabean period, and the Damascus Document ... (column 16) ..." paragraph is word for word in 1:0, 2:1-16, 3:8-14 and 4:5-6. The brief says "Never one stock line."
- **Second problem.** The claim attributed to Charles 1902 ("allusions to events as late as the early Maccabean period") is not in the writer's `sources-checked.md`, which lists only Charles pp. 96-111 and 115-140 and no page of the introduction on dating. I did not find it on the pages I opened and could not verify it. The 1:0 and 2:1 entries may keep the Damascus Document point (the writer opened Cook col. 16).
- **Fix.** Write a reader line for each setting: who first hears this law or story in the book's frame (Moses; "all who know the judgment of the law" at 3:31), and who the dated readers are. Cite the dating claim to a page opened this session, or cut it. Also use the evidence that the settings have: 3:31 and the gymnasium, 1:14 and the calendar. Settings for 3:17-19, 3:23-25, 4:26 and 4:32 have empty `readers`. That is fine if intended, but chapter 4's 4:26 setting should say who "the hearers" are.

### 5. MINOR. notes.json 4:11 / plain.txt 4:11: "in its fourth year" is ambiguous and breaks the arithmetic
- The Ge'ez (`wäbärabʿu`) names no week. The plain text's "in its fourth year" reads as the same fifth week. Charles supplies "(year of the sixth week)". Only that gives Enosh's birth in year 235, which is Seth's 105 years of Genesis 5:6 added to 130. The year-count notes elsewhere (4:7, 4:29, 4:33) do the same sums.
- **Fix.** Say "in the fourth year" (no "its") and add a one-line note giving Charles's supplied week and the Genesis 5:6 sum. Do not state the week as the Ge'ez's own.

### 6. MINOR. notes.json 2:7: Charles's emendation from the Greek is not reported
- The note "An unclear phrase" says Charles's English has "all plants after their kind". Charles 1902 (scan 112-113) says that "(plants after their kind)" is "supplied from G in Epiphanius". He also says that "MSS add 'for pleasure'" and that "in Eden" is superfluous. He suggests that `latadlâ` is a dittography or is corrupt for `taklât` = `phyta`. The Greek witness is the reason for Charles's wording.
- **Fix.** State that the Greek of Epiphanius, as quoted in Charles, is the source of "plants after their kind". Add a variant entry (VanderKam: "for delight and for all"; Epiphanius: "all the plants according to their kind"). Follow the Ge'ez as the plain text already does.

### 7. MINOR. plain.txt 2:22: "He made his desires rise" gives a corrupt verse a confident sense
- Charles marks 2:22 "His commands, etc. Corrupt" and renders `fäqadatihu` "His commands". The plain text uses "desires" with no note or variant. The 2:22 note mentions only the corruption of the start.
- **Fix.** Add to the note that the Ge'ez noun is `fäqad` ("wish, will, command"), that Charles has "commands", and that the verse is probably damaged. Consider "his will".

### 8. MINOR. plain.txt 3:27: the incense list
- The Ge'ez has five items after "incense" (`ʿəṭanä səḥinä wäqnəʾätä wämayä ləbn wäsänbältä`). The plain text has four ("frankincense, galbanum, stacte and spices"), which is Charles's English. The write-up does not say how each Ge'ez word maps to each English one, and `sänbält` is not equal to a general "spices" in any source I opened. I could not verify the mapping, so it is open.
- **Fix.** Check `sänbält`, `qənʾät`, `mayä ləbn` in a Ge'ez lexicon this session or note that the list follows Charles.

### 9. MINOR. topics/heavenly-tablets.json: citations and sources
- The 1 Enoch/Jubilees section cites Jubilees "1:29; 3:10; 3:31; 4:5; 4:32" as passages that speak of "the heavenly tablets". The Ge'ez at 1:29 is "tablets of the division (of the years)". At 3:31 the Ge'ez lacks "heavenly" (the 3:31 note itself says so). At 4:5 the reading is a slip (finding 1). The claim that "the law of Moses is their earthly form" is Charles's reading (3:10 note) and is stated as fact. `sources` is empty, though Charles 1902 and the 1 Enoch 81:1-2 and 93:2 citations were opened.
- **Fix.** Cite only 3:10 and 4:32 as "heavenly tablets"; list 1:29, 3:31 and 4:5 as related with their different wording. Attribute the "earthly copy" idea to Charles. Fill `sources` (`charles-jubilees-1902`, `vanderkam-jubilees-1989`).

### 10. MINOR. chapters.json chapter 1, view J: "The rabbis did not keep the book"
- Stated as a fact, with no source. The writer's log has no page for it. **Fix.** Cut it or cite a source opened. The same chapter's other J claim (Moses was shown more than the written Torah) rests on Charles's Megillah 19b citation, which is fine.

### 11. MINOR. unsourced claims in notes (could not verify; recommend cite or cut)
- 3:12: that Genesis 2:15's two verbs "were later used of the priests' service" (Numbers 3:7-8). The note uses no source key for this scholarly point. Add one or drop the sentence.
- 4:12: the Hebrew verb `huchal` "can mean 'it was begun' or 'it was profaned'". It is not in the pages I opened. The rest of the note (Septuagint, Syriac, Josephus, Jerome, Onkelos, Genesis Rabbah 23) is Charles's report and was not checked by me beyond the writer's log.
- 4:31: "A later Jewish and Christian legend instead has blind Lamech kill Cain". It is not attributed to Charles or to any source, and the J view cites "Genesis Rabbah 22" for "a stone or a reed" only.
- 1:0: "The Ge'ez of Jubilees, made from a Greek translation". The writer's log shows no page for it. Charles's introduction §§3-8 in the brief would give it.

### 12. MINOR. notes 4:15 variant: "Charles 1902 says all his manuscripts read 'father's sister'"
- I could not open the page of Charles's note on 4:15 (scan 130-131 did not return that text), so I could not check the claim or the Greek manuscript "r" and Epiphanius 39.7 details. The plain text follows the Ge'ez, which is right, and the Ge'ez at 4:15, 4:16, 4:20, 4:27, 4:28 is `ʾəḫtä ʾäbuhu`, "father's sister", while 4:33 has `ʾəḫtu`, "his sister" (checked in `tr`). The variant entry for 4:33 is correct to follow the Ge'ez. If the Charles page cannot be re-opened, say that the statement rests on the writer's reading.

## Checked and found sound (so the fixer need not redo them)
- Verse-by-verse drift against `gez`/`tr`: no omission, addition or mistranslation found. Examples checked: 1:5 `śərʿat` "ordinance"; 1:12 "hunt down"; 2:2's list; 2:19 "my nations"; 2:23 "down to him"; 3:19 "not you!" - The anno mundi arithmetic and the two jubilee counts (49 years) at 1:0, 4:1, 4:7, 4:29, 4:33.

- Genesis, Exodus, Psalm 90:4, 2 Peter 3:8, 2 Corinthians 6:15, Deuteronomy 27:24, Acts 7:53 quotations match the site text.
- Qumran: the writer says nothing about Qumran copies of chapters 1-4, which satisfies the brief's rule. Only the Damascus Document (Cook col. 16) is cited.
- EOTC: no EOTC reading is cited from a verse in `gap`; 4:3-14 is not treated as an omission.
- No traditions cards are present and none is forced. Sabbath, angels and Watchers are pointed to existing cards.
