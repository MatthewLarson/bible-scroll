# Hostile review: Exodus 25–40 (part `Exo/25-40`)

Reviewer: independent agent. Files reviewed: `parts/25-40/` plain.txt, notes.json (182), variants.json (10), glosses.json (139), chapters.json (16), topics.json (29 contributions), sources.json; the 12 new topic cores in `content/topics/`; the lenses in the shared `book.json`. Checked against `texts/orig/Exo.json` (WLC with word glosses), `texts/en/Exo.json` (BSB, KJV), `texts/auto/Exo.json`, and my knowledge of the ESV, NIV, NASB and NRSV. `node tools/build.js --check Exo/25-40`: 550 verses, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The scholarship around the text is strong. The notes, chapter pages and topics are dense, mostly accurate and usually fair. They are unusually good on rabbinic material (Rashi and Ramban on the order of the calf and the tabernacle, Tetzaveh, Yoma 86a, Rosh Hashanah 17b, Berakhot 55a), on the Septuagint problem in chapters 35–40, and on the calf. The translation is the problem. Across chapters 25–40 the Plain English Translation is built on ESV and NIV sentences, not made fresh from the Hebrew. I count about 360 to 400 of 550 verses that follow ESV or NIV structure and wording closely. About 150 of them are near-verbatim: the same clauses in the same order, with one or two words swapped ("utensils" to "tools", "overlay" to "cover", "blot" to "wipe"). The drift is worst in the narrative chapters 32–34 and in the fulfilment chapters 38–40, where ESV clauses come through almost untouched. In at least one place the NIV wording has carried in an NIV mistranslation (29:24). Under STYLE rule 1 this needs a systematic redo, not spot fixes. The repetitive building chapters (35–40) inherit the drift from 25–31, so a fix to the command chapters has to be carried through to the matching fulfilment verses. The second tier of problems: the variants are good but too few (several firm Qumran and Samaritan readings are missing, and Wevers' own view is not given); one note misstates Paul's verb in 2 Cor 3; several internal cross-references are broken; and the 12 new topic cores are leads only, with no whole-Bible overview sections.

**Findings: 3 critical, 14 major, 22 minor (39 total).**

## Critical

### 1. Critical: Plain English Translation copies the ESV/NIV throughout (about 360–400 verses, about 150 near-verbatim)
**Location:** plain.txt, every chapter. Full verse list in the appendix below.
**What is wrong:** STYLE rule 1 says a reviewer checks every verse against the ESV and NIV, and that close matches must be redone. The drift here is systematic, not occasional. Typical patterns:
- **Near-verbatim ESV narrative.** 32:17 "When Joshua heard the noise of the people shouting, he said to Moses, 'There is a noise of war in the camp.'" (ESV: "When Joshua heard the noise of the people as they shouted, he said to Moses, 'There is a noise of war in the camp.'"). 32:30 "You have sinned a great sin. But now I will go up to the LORD. Perhaps I can make atonement for your sin" (ESV identical but for "And now"). 32:21, 32:25 ("the people had broken loose. Aaron had let them break loose"), 32:31–32, 32:35 ("because they had made the calf, the one that Aaron made"), 33:7, 33:8, 33:10–12, 33:16, 34:4, 34:10, 34:28–31, 39:43, 40:17–22, 40:24–27, 40:29–35.
- **Near-verbatim NIV.** 26:9 "Join five of the curtains into one set and six ... Fold the sixth curtain double at the front of the tent." 28:4 (the whole garment list plus "so that they can serve me as priests"). 28:21, 28:26–27 ("close to the seam just above the woven band of the ephod"). 29:12, 29:30, 29:36 ("Purify the altar by making atonement for it, and anoint it ..."). 29:40. 30:12–13, 30:18–20, 30:36–37. 32:8, 32:11–12. 33:22. 34:1, 34:3, 34:18, 34:26. 36:4, 36:6. 37:25. 38:21, 38:23. 39:32, 39:42. 40:4, 40:6, 40:9–10, 40:13–15.
- **ESV signature renderings, not just literal convergence:** 31:13 "Above all, you must keep my Sabbaths" (ESV's distinctive "Above all" for *akh*); 26:6, 26:11, 36:13 "one whole" (ESV "a single whole"); 25:36 and 37:22 "a single piece of hammered work in pure gold"; 29:13 and 29:22 "the long lobe of the liver"; 28:14 and 28:22 "twisted like cords"; 35:21 "everyone whose spirit moved him"; 38:26 "a beka a head".
- **NASB:** 31:15 and 35:2 "Work may be done for six days ... a Sabbath of complete rest".
- **BSB copied** (allowed "for checking, never for copying"): 26:20 and 36:25 verbatim; 33:17 "I will do this very thing you have asked"; 37:24.
**Why literalness is no excuse:** tabernacle prose is formulaic, so some convergence is unavoidable. But the choices of clause order, connectives ("so that", "Then"), and the particular English idioms match one modern version verse after verse, and the matches switch between ESV and NIV within a single verse (e.g. 27:16, 30:34, 32:12, 32:13, 33:5), which is the sign of recall, not independent rendering.
**Fix:** Redo chapters 25–40 from the Hebrew word list, verse by verse, then compare against BSB/KJV only. Use the freedom the 8th-grade target allows (e.g. 26:9 "Sew five curtains together into one panel and the other six into a second panel. Let the sixth one hang folded over at the front of the tent."). Because 35–40 repeat 25–31, revise the command verse and its fulfilment verse together (25↔37, 26↔36, 27↔38, 28↔39, 29↔40:12–15, 30↔37:25–29/38:8, 31:2–5↔35:30–33), so that the deliberate echo is kept but in your own English. Also check the note quotations listed in finding 6.

### 2. Critical: 29:24 copies an NIV rendering against the Hebrew
**Location:** plain.txt 29:24 "Put all of these in the hands of Aaron and his sons, and have them wave them as a wave offering."
**Evidence:** Hebrew וְהֵנַפְתָּ אֹתָם תְּנוּפָה, "and *you* shall wave them" (Moses waves, with the gifts in the priests' palms; compare Leviticus 8:27, where Moses does it). "Have them wave them" is the NIV's paraphrase, and it changes who does what.
**Fix:** "...and you are to wave them as a wave offering before the LORD." If you prefer the view that the priests' hands are moved by Moses (Rashi: Moses' hands under theirs), add a sentence to the 29:24 note.

### 3. Critical: 34:7 "Yet he does not simply wipe guilt away" weakens an emphatic Hebrew form, and contradicts its own note
**Location:** plain.txt 34:7, the note "'Will by no means clear'", and the chapter 34 summary and deep section, which repeat the wording.
**Evidence:** וְנַקֵּה לֹא יְנַקֶּה uses an infinitive absolute for emphasis: "he will certainly not acquit / leave unpunished". The note itself says so. "Not *simply*" suggests he partly does, which reverses the emphasis toward mercy. This is the very theological softening STYLE forbids ("do not add interpretation to the text"). It is also the most-quoted verse in the section.
**Fix:** "Yet he will by no means let guilt go unpunished" (or "he will surely not clear the guilty", without the KJV's added "the guilty"). Keep the rabbinic split reading in the note, and add the Onkelos paraphrase there (see finding 15).

## Major

### 4. Major: Several firm Qumran and Samaritan variants are missing, and one is hedged when it need not be
**Location:** variants.json.
- **27:19.** The entry says the Samaritan addition is "perhaps also" in 4QpaleoExod-m and that you "could not check". The scroll preserves it: 4Q22 at 27:19 reads "And you shall make clothing of blue and purple and scarlet in which to serve in the holy place," matching the Samaritan Pentateuch (Sanderson, DJD IX; see the Dead Sea Scrolls Bible). **Fix:** name 4QpaleoExod-m firmly and drop the "could not check" sentence.
- **32:10.** This claim holds. 4Q22 preserves "...and Moses prayed for Aaron" after 32:10, with the rest reconstructed to match the Samaritan Pentateuch and Deuteronomy 9:20. Say "partly preserved, the rest restored from the Samaritan" rather than "as far as the damaged scroll preserves it".
- **Missing, 29:21.** 4QpaleoExod-m and the Samaritan Pentateuch do not have 29:21 at this point (the Samaritan places it after 29:28, so that the sprinkling follows the waving). This is a real transposition and should get an entry.
- **Missing, 40:17.** The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint (ἐκπορευομένων αὐτῶν ἐξ Αἰγύπτου) and 4QExod-Lev-f add "after they came out of Egypt" (literally "of their going out of Egypt"). This is a standard harmonizing plus, and the Plain English Translation gloss on this verse already relies on the idea.
- **Missing, 34:19.** MT תִּזָּכָר is usually emended to הַזָּכָר "the male", with the Septuagint τὰ ἀρσενικά, Theodotion, Vulgate and Targum support. Several modern versions footnote it. The Plain English Translation silently follows the emendation ("every first male ... every firstborn male of your livestock").
- **Missing, 29:2.** 4QpaleoExod-m with the Samaritan Pentateuch lacks "anointed (smeared) with oil" (it is marked as a scroll difference). Minor, but cheap to add.
- **Possibly missing, 28:23–29.** The Septuagint's breastpiece passage is shorter and differently arranged than the Masoretic 28:23–28 (Wevers, *Notes*). Verify and add one entry or a sentence in the 36:8 entry.
**Fix:** add the entries; with them the part reaches about 15 variants, which is reasonable for a section this heavily witnessed.

### 5. Major: The 35–40 Septuagint entry leaves out Wevers' view and the "variant literary edition" category
**Location:** variants.json 36:8 (and the textual-history topic and the 37:25 note).
**What is wrong:** The entry gives Gooding (a later Greek reworking) and Aejmelaeus (a different Hebrew Vorlage), plus Wade. It never gives John Wevers' own position, although he is cited. Wevers (*Notes on the Greek Text of Exodus*, 1990; "The Building of the Tabernacle", JNSL 19, 1993) argued that the translator himself worked from a Hebrew text much like the Masoretic one and deliberately abridged and reordered it. That is a third position, different from Gooding's "later editor". Also missing: Tov's and Ulrich's framing of 35–40 as a possible "variant literary edition", which is the standard textbook category the reader will meet in Tov; and the older views (Popper 1862: a different and later Hebrew; Finn; Nelson; Bogaert). Wade's conclusion should be checked. As I recall it, she found the *same* translator for both accounts with *consistent* technique, which cuts against "a freer method". "Dead Sea fragments ... as far as preserved" should name them (4QExod-Lev-f, 4QpaleoExod-m, 2QExod-a/b).
**Fix:** state the three positions in one sentence each (Gooding; Wevers; Aejmelaeus with Bogaert, Tov and Ulrich), name the Qumran witnesses, and recheck Wade's conclusion.

### 7. Major: The 34:33 note misstates Paul's verb and its own source
**Location:** note 34:33 "Paul and Moses' veil".
**Evidence:** The note renders 2 Cor 3:13 as "what was fading away" and then says "Exodus says nothing about fading." The Greek is τὸ τέλος τοῦ καταργουμένου, "the end of what was being set aside / brought to an end". The cited source, Hafemann (1995), argues at length that καταργέω does *not* mean "fade". So the note puts a contested older-NIV/RSV gloss in Paul's mouth and then criticizes Paul for it.
**Fix:** "so that the Israelites would not gaze at the end of what was being brought to an end"; explain the debate (fading glory vs. glory rendered inoperative, Hafemann); keep the Jewish response.

### 8. Major: 32:29 variant garbles the Hebrew forms
**Location:** variants.json 32:29, "why".
**Evidence:** The entry says the same consonants מלאו can be read as a command (*mil'u*, MT, piel imperative 2mp) or as "a past tense (mille'u, 'they have filled')". But the Greek and Latin it cites have the *second* person ("you have filled", ἐπληρώσατε, *consecrastis*). A third-person perfect would not produce that. The Greek either reflects מִלֵּאתֶם, or it interprets the imperative freely. Also, most modern versions (ESV "Today you have been ordained", NIV "You have been set apart") follow the versions' sense, not the "they" reading.
**Fix:** "MT vocalizes an imperative, 'fill your hands'. The Greek and Latin have 'you have filled', which reflects either a different Hebrew form (*mille'tem*) or a free rendering. Some scholars read the consonants as a perfect." Keep the Plain English Translation on the MT.

### 10. Major: The tabernacle historicity views are fair in outline but miss key positions, and one attribution is probably inverted
**Location:** note 26:30 "History or ideal?", tabernacle topic "History or priestly ideal?", chapter 25 and 26 views.
**What is wrong:**
- "Menahem Haran thought P drew on the old shrine at Shiloh." As I recall, Haran ("Shiloh and Jerusalem: The Origin of the Priestly Tradition in the Pentateuch", JBL 81, 1962; *Temples and Temple-Service*, 1978) argued that P's tabernacle reflects the *Jerusalem* temple, as a retrojection, and that P is Jerusalemite, written under Hezekiah. Verify this. If I am right, the sentence reverses his view.
- The C lens ("probably written in the exile or soon after") presents a late date for P as if agreed. The Israeli school (Kaufmann, Haran, Milgrom, Hurvitz on linguistic grounds, Knohl) dates P before the exile. That is a major critical position, not a conservative one.
- Missing: Daniel Fleming, "Mari's Large Public Tent and the Priestly Tent Sanctuary", VT 50 (2000), which shows West Semitic tent shrines centuries before Moses. This is the strongest non-apologetic argument that the *idea* is old. Richard Friedman's tabernacle-inside-the-temple view appears only in the chapter 26 deep section. It belongs in the 26:30 note and the topic, since he is an M-lens scholar named in book.json.
- Kadesh: state the strongest H argument (an exilic writer would hardly know a 13th-century Ramesside camp layout; Homan, Hoffmeier) and the strongest reply (military tent camps and portable royal pavilions persisted into the Iron Age, so the parallel does not date the text). At present only the general "such objects were known for centuries" reply is given (note 25:14).
**Fix:** correct the Haran sentence after checking; add Fleming, the pre-exilic-P school, and the Kadesh argument and reply.

### 16. Major: The twelve stones are derivative and partly unexplained
**Location:** plain.txt 28:17–20 and 39:10–13; note 28:17; gems topic.
**What is wrong:**
- Rows 2–4 match the NIV (2011) except for row-4 "chrysolite" (where the NIV has topaz) and row-2 "quartz" (NIV emerald). Row 1 mixes the NIV and NRSV.
- *Pitdah* "topaz" and *tarshish* "chrysolite" are reasonable choices, since LXX τοπάζιον, ancient "topaz", was probably peridot.
- *Yahalom* "quartz" is a guess, and the note does not explain it. Plain English Translation also gives "jasper" for *yashpeh*, while LXX has ἴασπις at *yahalom* and ὀνύχιον at *yashpeh*. So the Greek and Hebrew lists are offset, which matters for Revelation 21.

**Fix:** add a word note or table giving each Hebrew name, the LXX name, and the Plain English Translation choice with a confidence word. Explain "quartz"; mention the LXX row-4 offset and Josephus.

## Minor
