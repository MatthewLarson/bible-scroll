## Israel grows in Egypt
1:1 These are the names of the sons of Israel who came to Egypt with Jacob. Each man came with his household:
1:2 Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah,
1:3 Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin,
1:4 Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.
1:5 The persons who came from Jacob's body numbered seventy in all, and Joseph was in Egypt.
1:6 Joseph died, and so did all his brothers and everyone of that generation.
1:7 But the Israelites were fruitful and swarmed. They grew many and became very, very strong, and the land was filled with them.
## A king who did not know Joseph
1:8 Then a new king came to the throne of Egypt, a king who did not know Joseph.
1:9 He said to his people, "Look, the Israelite people have grown bigger and stronger than we are.
1:10 Come on, let us outsmart them, or they will multiply still more. And when a war comes, they will side with those who hate us, make war on us, and go up from the land."
1:11 So they put slave-gang bosses over them to beat them down with heavy loads. They built supply cities for Pharaoh: Pithom and Rameses.
1:12 Yet however hard they pressed them down, that much more they multiplied, and that much more they burst out. The Egyptians grew sick with fear of the Israelites.
1:13 The Egyptians made the Israelites serve them with crushing cruelty.
1:14 They turned their lives bitter with hard labor in clay and bricks and every kind of labor in the field. Every labor they made them do, they did with crushing cruelty.
## The midwives who feared God
1:15 The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives. The name of one was Shiphrah, and the name of the second was Puah.
1:16 He said, "When you help the Hebrew women give birth, watch the two stones. If it is a son, kill him. If it is a daughter, she may live."
1:17 But the midwives feared God. They did not carry out what the king of Egypt had told them, and they kept the boys alive.
1:18 So the king of Egypt called in the midwives and said to them, "Why did you do this thing and keep the boys alive?"
1:19 The midwives said to Pharaoh, "It's because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women. They are full of life. Before the midwife reaches them, they have already given birth."
1:20 God treated the midwives well, and the people grew many and became very strong.
1:21 And since the midwives feared God, he made households for them.
1:22 Then Pharaoh commanded all his people: "Every son who is born, you must throw into the Nile. Every daughter you shall keep alive."
## Moses is born and drawn from the water
2:1 A man from Levi's family went and took a daughter of Levi as his wife.
2:2 The woman conceived and bore a son. She saw him, that he was good, and she hid him three months.
2:3 But she could not hide him any longer, so she took a papyrus ark for him and smeared it with tar and pitch. She laid the child in it and placed it in the reeds by the bank of the Nile.
2:4 His sister took her place far off, to learn what would be done to him.
2:5 Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the Nile, while her young women walked along the bank. She spotted the ark among the reeds, sent her slave woman, and took it.
2:6 She opened it and saw the child. There was a little boy, crying! Her heart went out to him, and she said, "This one is from the Hebrews' children."
2:7 His sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Should I go and find a nursing mother from the Hebrew women for you, so she can nurse the child for you?"
2:8 "Go," Pharaoh's daughter told her, and the young woman went off and called the child's mother.
2:9 Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I myself will pay you a wage." The woman took the child and nursed him.
2:10 The child grew, and she took him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became a son to her. She called his name Moses, and she said, "From the water I drew him."
## Moses kills an Egyptian and flees
2:11 In those days, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his brothers and looked at their heavy labor. He saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew man, one of his brothers.
2:12 He turned this way and that and saw no one there, so he struck the Egyptian dead and hid him in the sand.
2:13 On the second day he went out, and there were two Hebrew men struggling with each other. He said to the guilty one, "Why are you hitting your own companion?"
2:14 The man said, "Who put you as a chief and a judge over us? Are you saying you will kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?" Moses was afraid. He said to himself, "Then the matter is known for sure."
2:15 When Pharaoh heard of this, he looked for a way to kill Moses. Moses ran from Pharaoh and went to live in the land of Midian, and he sat down by the well.
2:16 Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water, and they filled the drinking troughs to give their father's flock a drink.
2:17 Some shepherds came and chased them off, but Moses rose up to save them, and he gave their flock water.
2:18 When they came to Reuel their father, he said, "Why are you home so early today?"
2:19 They said, "An Egyptian man rescued us from the shepherds' hands. He even drew all the water for us and watered the flock."
2:20 He said to his daughters, "Then where is he? Why ever did you leave the man there? Invite him to eat bread with us."
2:21 Moses was willing to live with the man, who gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses.
2:22 She bore a son, and Moses named him Gershom, because he said, "I have become a foreigner living in an alien land."
## God hears the cry of Israel
2:23 A long time passed, and the king of Egypt died. The Israelites sighed from their labor and cried out. Their plea for help rose up to God from their labor.
2:24 God heard their groans. God remembered his covenant, the one with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob.
2:25 God looked on the Israelites, and God knew.
## The bush that burned
3:1 Moses was shepherding the sheep and goats of Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He drove the flock past the edge of the wilderness and came to God's mountain, toward Horeb.
3:2 The angel of the LORD appeared to him in a blaze of fire out of the middle of a thornbush. He looked, and there was the bush, burning with fire, and yet the bush was not being eaten up.
3:3 Moses said, "Let me turn aside and look at this great sight: why does the bush not burn away?"
3:4 When the LORD saw that he had turned aside to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush: "Moses! Moses!" He answered, "Here I am."
3:5 He said, "Come no closer. Pull your sandals off your feet. The place you stand on is holy ground."
3:6 Then he said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Moses covered his face, for he was afraid to gaze at God.
## I have seen my people's suffering
3:7 The LORD said, "I have seen, yes seen, the suffering of my people who are in Egypt. I have heard their outcry because of the men who drive them. I know what they are going through.
3:8 I have come down to snatch them out of Egypt's hand and to lead them up from that land to a good land and a wide one, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.
3:9 Now look, the outcry of the Israelites has come to me. I have also seen how the Egyptians are squeezing them.
3:10 So now, go. I am sending you to Pharaoh. Bring my people, the Israelites, out of Egypt."
3:11 Moses said to God, "Who am I to go to Pharaoh? Who am I to bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"
3:12 He said, "Because I will be with you. And this will be the sign for you that I am the one who sent you: when you bring the people out of Egypt, you will all serve God on this mountain."
## God tells Moses his name
3:13 Moses said to God, "Look, I am going to the Israelites, and I will tell them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you.' And they will say to me, 'What is his name?' Then what do I tell them?"
3:14 God said to Moses, "I will be who I will be." And he said, "Tell the Israelites this: 'I Will Be has sent me to you.'"
3:15 God said further to Moses, "Tell the Israelites this: 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name for all time, and by this name people will call me to mind, age after age.
3:16 Go and gather the elders of Israel, and tell them, 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, has appeared to me. He said: I have surely come to help you, and I have taken note of what is being done to you in Egypt.
3:17 And I have declared: I will lead you up out of the misery of Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.'
3:18 They will heed your voice. Then you and Israel's elders will go to the king of Egypt and say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. So please, let us make a three days' journey into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God.'
3:19 But I know that the king of Egypt will not give you leave to go, except under a strong hand.
3:20 So I will reach out my hand and strike Egypt with every marvel I will do in its midst. After that he will send you out.
3:21 I will give this people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians. Then, when you go, you will not go empty-handed.
3:22 Each woman will ask her neighbor, and any woman staying in her house, for silver things, gold things and clothing. You will dress your sons and daughters in them. So you will strip Egypt bare."
## Three signs for Moses
4:1 Moses answered, "Look, they won't trust me, and they won't heed my voice. They will say, 'The LORD never appeared to you.'"
4:2 The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A staff."
4:3 He said, "Throw it on the ground." So he threw it on the ground, and it became a snake. Moses ran from it.
4:4 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Reach out your hand and grab it by the tail." He reached out his hand and caught hold of it, and it became a staff in his palm.
4:5 "This is so they will believe that the LORD, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you."
4:6 The LORD also said to him, "Now put your hand into the fold of your robe." He put his hand into the fold of his robe, and when he took it out, there was his hand, struck with a skin disease, like snow.
4:7 He said, "Put your hand back into the fold of your robe." So he put his hand back into the fold of his robe, and when he took it out again, there it was, made new again like the rest of his flesh.
4:8 "If they do not believe you, and do not heed the voice of the first sign, they will believe the voice of the second sign.
4:9 And if they do not believe even these two signs, and do not heed your voice, take some of the Nile's water and pour it out on the dry ground. The water you take from the Nile will become blood there on the dry ground."
4:10 Moses said to the LORD, "Please, my Lord, I have never been a man of words, not yesterday, not the day before, and not since you began to speak to your servant. I am heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue."
4:11 The LORD said to him, "Who put a mouth in a human being? Or who makes someone mute, or deaf, or sighted, or blind? Isn't it I, the LORD?
4:12 So go now. I myself will be with your mouth and will teach you what to say."
4:13 But he said, "Please, my Lord, send it by the hand of someone else, whoever you will send."
4:14 The LORD's anger flared at Moses. He said, "Isn't there Aaron your brother, the Levite? I know that he is a speaker, a good one. In fact, he is on his way out to meet you right now. He will see you, and his heart will be glad.
4:15 You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach the two of you what you are to do.
4:16 He will speak to the people for you. He will become your mouth, and you will become as God to him.
4:17 And this staff, take it in your hand. With it you will do the signs."
## Moses goes back to Egypt
4:18 Moses went back to Jether his father-in-law and said to him, "Please let me go back to my relatives in Egypt, to see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace."
4:19 In Midian the LORD said to Moses, "Go back to Egypt. The men who wanted your life have all died."
4:20 Moses took his wife and his sons, set them on the donkey and started back to the land of Egypt. In his hand Moses held the staff of God.
4:21 The LORD said to Moses, "As you go back to Egypt, see to it that you do before Pharaoh all the wonders I have placed in your hand. But I will make his heart stubborn, and he will not let the people go.
4:22 Then say to Pharaoh, 'This is what the LORD says: Israel is my son, my firstborn.
4:23 I told you, "Let my son go, so he may serve me." But you refused to let him go. Now I am going to kill your son, your firstborn.'"
## The bridegroom of blood
4:24 On the road, at the place where they camped for the night, the LORD met him and sought to kill him.
4:25 But Zipporah took a flint, cut off her son's foreskin, and touched it to his feet. She said, "Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me."
4:26 And he let go of him. At that time she said "a bridegroom of blood," about the circumcision.
## Aaron and the elders
4:27 The LORD told Aaron, "Go out to the wilderness to meet Moses." Aaron went, met him at God's mountain and kissed him.
4:28 Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD who had sent him, and all the signs he had commanded him to do.
4:29 Then Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the Israelites.
4:30 Aaron told them every word the LORD had spoken to Moses, and he worked the signs before the people's eyes.
4:31 The people believed. When they heard that the LORD had come to help the Israelites and had seen their misery, they bowed their heads and bent down low.
## "Who is the LORD?"
5:1 Afterward Moses and Aaron went in and said to Pharaoh, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Let my people go, and let them keep a feast for me in the wilderness.'"
5:2 Pharaoh said, "Who is this LORD, that I should heed his voice by letting Israel go? I do not know the LORD. And what is more, Israel I will not let go."
5:3 They said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us make a three days' journey into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God, or he may fall on us with plague or with the sword."
5:4 The king of Egypt said to them, "Why, Moses and Aaron, are you pulling the people away from their tasks? Off to your burdens!"
5:5 Pharaoh said, "Look, the people of the land are many now, and you would give them a rest from their burdens!"
## Bricks without straw
5:6 That very day Pharaoh gave orders to the drivers over the people and to its officers:
5:7 "Stop giving the people straw for making the bricks, the way you did before. Let them go out and scrape up straw for themselves.
5:8 But the count of bricks they have been making, you will lay on them just the same. Do not cut it back. They are slack. That is why they cry, 'Let's go, let's sacrifice to our God.'
5:9 Let the labor weigh heavy on the men, so they are kept busy with it and pay no mind to lying words."
5:10 So the people's drivers and its officers went out and said to the people, "This is what Pharaoh says: 'I am not giving you straw.
5:11 Go yourselves and get straw wherever you can find it. But not one thing will be cut from your labor.'"
5:12 So the people scattered over the whole land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.
5:13 The drivers kept pressing them: "Finish your work, each day's share on its day, the same as when there was straw."
5:14 And the Israelite officers, whom Pharaoh's drivers had set over them, were beaten. "Why have you not finished your set amount of bricks, yesterday or today, the way you did before?"
5:15 Then the Israelite officers went and cried out to Pharaoh: "Why do you treat your servants like this?
5:16 No straw is given to your servants, yet they tell us, 'Make bricks!' And look, your servants are beaten, but the wrong is your own people's."
5:17 He said, "Slack, that's what you are, slack! That is why you say, 'Let's go, let's sacrifice to the LORD.'
5:18 Now go and work. No straw will be given to you, but you will hand over the full count of bricks."
5:19 The Israelite officers saw that they were in a bad spot, being told, "You must not cut back on your bricks, each day's share on its day."
5:20 As they came out from Pharaoh, they ran into Moses and Aaron, who were standing there to meet them.
5:21 They said to them, "May the LORD look at you and judge! You have made us reek in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants. You have put a sword in their hand to kill us."
## Moses complains to the LORD
5:22 Moses turned back to the LORD and said, "My Lord, why have you brought harm on this people? Why ever did you send me?
5:23 From the time I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has done harm to this people, and as for rescuing, you have not rescued your people."
6:1 The LORD said to Moses, "Now you will see what I will do to Pharaoh. Forced by a strong hand, he will send them away. Forced by a strong hand, he will drive them out of his land."
## "I am the LORD"
6:2 God spoke to Moses and said to him, "I am the LORD.
6:3 To Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob I appeared as God Almighty. But my name, the LORD, I did not make known to them.
6:4 I also set up my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land where they lived as outsiders.
6:5 I myself have also heard the groans of the Israelites, whom Egypt is working as slaves, and I have called my covenant to mind.
6:6 So say to the Israelites: 'I am the LORD. I will lead you out from under Egypt's heavy loads. I will snatch you out of their service. I will redeem you with my arm stretched out and with great judgments.
6:7 I will take you to myself as a people, and I will be God to you. And you will know that I am the LORD your God, the one who leads you out from under Egypt's heavy loads.
6:8 I will bring you to the land I lifted my hand in oath to give to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, and I will give it to you to possess. I am the LORD.'"
6:9 Moses spoke these words to the Israelites, but they did not listen to Moses, because their spirit was worn short and their labor was hard.
6:10 Then the LORD said to Moses,
6:11 "Go in and speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt, so that he lets the Israelites go out of his land."
6:12 But Moses spoke before the LORD: "Look, the Israelites have not listened to me. So how will Pharaoh listen to me, and I with uncircumcised lips?"
6:13 But the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron. He gave them orders for the Israelites and for Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt.
## The family of Moses and Aaron
6:14 These are the heads of their fathers' houses. The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn: Hanoch and Pallu, Hezron and Carmi. These are the clans of Reuben.
6:15 The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jakin, Zohar, and Shaul, the son of a Canaanite woman. These are the clans of Simeon.
6:16 These are the names of the sons of Levi, by their generations: Gershon, Kohath and Merari. The years of Levi's life were one hundred and thirty-seven.
6:17 The sons of Gershon, clan by clan: Libni and Shimei.
6:18 The sons of Kohath: Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel. The years of Kohath's life were one hundred and thirty-three.
6:19 The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These are the clans of Levi by their generations.
6:20 Amram took Jochebed, his father's sister, as his wife, and she bore him Aaron and Moses. The years of Amram's life were one hundred and thirty-seven.
6:21 The sons of Izhar: Korah, Nepheg and Zichri.
6:22 The sons of Uzziel: Mishael, Elzaphan and Sithri.
6:23 Aaron took Elisheba, the daughter of Amminadab and sister of Nahshon, as his wife, and she bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.
6:24 The sons of Korah: Assir, Elkanah and Abiasaph. These are the clans of the Korahites.
6:25 Eleazar son of Aaron took one of the daughters of Putiel as his wife, and she bore him Phinehas. These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites, clan by clan.
6:26 This is the same Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said, "Bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, in their ranks like armies."
6:27 They were the ones who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt about bringing the Israelites out of Egypt. This was Moses, and Aaron.
## Moses objects again
6:28 On the day the LORD spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,
6:29 the LORD said to Moses, "I am the LORD. Speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I speak to you."
6:30 But Moses said before the LORD, "Look at me: my lips are uncircumcised. How would Pharaoh listen to me?"
## Moses as God to Pharaoh
7:1 The LORD said to Moses, "See, I have set you as God to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother will be your prophet.
7:2 You will speak all that I command you, and Aaron your brother will speak to Pharaoh, and he will send the Israelites out of his land.
7:3 But I will make Pharaoh's heart hard, and I will heap up my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.
7:4 Pharaoh will not listen to you. Then I will lay my hand on Egypt, and I will lead my armies, my people the Israelites, out of the land of Egypt by great judgments.
7:5 And Egypt will know that I am the LORD, when I stretch out my hand over Egypt and bring the Israelites out from among them."
7:6 Moses and Aaron did it. As the LORD commanded them, so they did.
7:7 Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three, when they spoke to Pharaoh.
## The staff that swallowed staffs
7:8 The LORD said to Moses and Aaron,
7:9 "When Pharaoh speaks to you and says, 'Show a wonder to prove yourselves,' say to Aaron, 'Take your staff and throw it down in front of Pharaoh.' It will become a serpent."
7:10 So Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and did as the LORD had commanded. Aaron threw down his staff in front of Pharaoh and in front of his servants, and it became a serpent.
7:11 Then Pharaoh, for his part, called for the wise men and the sorcerers, and they too, the magicians of Egypt, did the same with their spells.
7:12 Every one of them threw down his staff, and the staffs became serpents. But Aaron's staff swallowed up their staffs.
7:13 Yet Pharaoh's heart was stubborn, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.
## The first blow: the Nile turns to blood
7:14 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is heavy. He refuses to let the people go.
7:15 Go to Pharaoh in the morning. Look, he is going out to the water. Stand ready to meet him on the bank of the Nile, and take in your hand the staff that was turned into a snake.
7:16 Say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, sent me to you to say: Let my people go, so they may serve me in the wilderness. But look, until now you have not listened.
7:17 This is what the LORD says: Here is how you will know that I am the LORD. Look, I am about to strike the water of the Nile with the staff in my hand, and it will be turned to blood.
7:18 The fish in the Nile will die, and the Nile will stink, and Egypt will find it cannot bear to drink water from the Nile.'"
7:19 The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron, 'Take your staff and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt, over its rivers, over its canals, over its ponds and over every pool of its water, so they become blood.' There will be blood in the whole land of Egypt, even in wood and in stone."
7:20 Moses and Aaron did so, as the LORD had commanded. He lifted the staff and struck the water in the Nile before the eyes of Pharaoh and the eyes of his servants, and all the water in the Nile was turned to blood.
7:21 The fish in the Nile died. The Nile reeked, and Egypt was unable to drink water from it. The blood was everywhere in the land of Egypt.
7:22 But the magicians of Egypt did the same with their spells. So Pharaoh's heart was stubborn, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.
7:23 Pharaoh turned away and went into his house. He did not set his heart even on this.
7:24 All Egypt dug around the Nile for water to drink, because they could not drink from the water of the Nile.
7:25 Seven days were completed after the LORD struck the Nile.
## The second blow: frogs
8:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and say to him, 'This is what the LORD says: Let my people go, so they may serve me.
8:2 If you refuse to let them go, look, I am going to strike all your territory with frogs.
8:3 The Nile will swarm with frogs, and up they will come. They will go into your house and the room where you sleep and onto your bed, into your servants' houses and among your people, into your ovens and your bread bowls.
8:4 Onto you, onto your people and onto all your servants the frogs will climb.'"
8:5 The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron, 'Stretch out your hand with your staff over the rivers, over the canals and over the ponds, and bring the frogs up over the land of Egypt.'"
8:6 Aaron reached out his hand over Egypt's waters. Up came the frogs, and they covered the land of Egypt.
8:7 But the magicians did the same with their spells, and they brought frogs up over the land of Egypt.
8:8 Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, "Plead with the LORD to remove the frogs from me and from my people. Then I will let the people go, and they can sacrifice to the LORD."
8:9 Moses said to Pharaoh, "The honor is yours, over me: for when shall I plead for you and your servants and your people, so that the frogs are cut off from you and your houses and stay only in the Nile?"
8:10 He said, "Tomorrow." Moses said, "Let it be by your word, so that you know that none is like the LORD our God.
8:11 The frogs will leave you and your houses and your servants and your people. They will stay only in the Nile."
8:12 Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh, and Moses cried out to the LORD about the frogs he had laid on Pharaoh.
8:13 The LORD did what Moses said. The frogs died off from the houses, from the courtyards and from the fields.
8:14 They heaped them up in piles and piles, and the land stank.
8:15 But when Pharaoh saw that he had breathing room, he made his heart heavy and did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.
## The third blow: gnats
8:16 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron, 'Stretch out your staff and strike the dust of the ground, and it will become gnats in all the land of Egypt.'"
8:17 They did so. Aaron reached out with his staff in his hand and struck the dust of the ground, and gnats came on people and on animals. All the dust of the ground turned to gnats, all over the land of Egypt.
8:18 The magicians did the same with their spells to bring out the gnats, but they could not. And the gnats were on people and on animals.
8:19 The magicians said to Pharaoh, "It is the finger of God." But Pharaoh's heart was stubborn, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.
## The fourth blow: swarms
8:20 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Get up early in the morning and take your stand before Pharaoh. Look, he is going out to the water. Say to him, 'This is what the LORD says: Let my people go, so they may serve me.
8:21 For if you do not let my people go, look, I am going to send the swarm against you, your servants, your people and your houses. The houses of Egypt will be full of the swarm, and so will the very ground they stand on.
8:22 But on that day I will set apart the land of Goshen, where my people stay, so that no swarm will be there. Then you will know that I am the LORD, here in the middle of the land.
8:23 I will put redemption between my people and your people. Tomorrow this sign will happen.'"
8:24 The LORD did just that. A heavy swarm came into Pharaoh's house and his servants' houses. All over the land of Egypt, the swarm brought the land to ruin.
8:25 Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, "Go, sacrifice to your God in the land."
8:26 But Moses said, "It would not be right to do that. What we sacrifice to the LORD our God is a thing the Egyptians abhor. If we sacrifice what the Egyptians abhor before their eyes, won't they stone us?
8:27 We will go three days' travel into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God, as he tells us."
8:28 Pharaoh said, "I will let you go to sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness. Only, you must not go far, not far at all. Pray for me."
8:29 Moses said, "Look, I am going out from you, and I will plead with the LORD, and tomorrow the swarm will turn away from Pharaoh, from his servants and from his people. Only let Pharaoh not go on toying with us by not letting the people go to sacrifice to the LORD."
8:30 So Moses went out from Pharaoh and pleaded with the LORD.
8:31 The LORD did what Moses said. He turned the swarm away from Pharaoh, from his servants and from his people. Not one was left.
8:32 But Pharaoh made his heart heavy this time too, and he did not let the people go.
## The fifth blow: plague on the livestock
9:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and tell him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go, so they may serve me.
9:2 For if you refuse to let them go, and you keep your grip on them,
9:3 look, the hand of the LORD will fall on your livestock out in the field, on the horses, on the donkeys, on the camels, on the cattle and on the flocks: a plague, a very heavy one.
9:4 But the LORD will set apart Israel's livestock from Egypt's livestock, and nothing at all that belongs to the Israelites will die.'"
9:5 The LORD set a time. He said, "Tomorrow the LORD will do this thing in the land."
9:6 On the next day the LORD did as he had said. All Egypt's livestock died, but of the Israelites' livestock not one died.
9:7 Pharaoh sent to find out, and look, not even one of Israel's livestock had died. But Pharaoh's heart was heavy, and he did not let the people go.
## The sixth blow: sores
9:8 Then the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "Fill both your hands with soot from a kiln, and let Moses fling it skyward before Pharaoh's eyes.
9:9 It will turn to fine dust over all the land of Egypt, and on people and on animals it will turn into sores that burst open in blisters, in all the land of Egypt."
9:10 So they took soot from the kiln and stood before Pharaoh, and Moses flung it skyward. It became sores bursting into blisters on people and on animals.
9:11 The magicians were unable to stand before Moses because of the sores, since the sores had broken out on the magicians and on all Egypt.
9:12 But the LORD made Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had told Moses.
## The seventh blow: hail
9:13 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Get up early in the morning, take your stand before Pharaoh, and say to him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go, so they may serve me.
9:14 For this time I am going to send all my blows into your heart, and on your servants and your people, so that you will know that there is none like me in all the earth.
9:15 For by this time I could have sent out my hand and struck you and your people with disease, and you would have vanished from the earth.
9:16 But for this reason I have kept you standing: to let you see my strength, and so that my name will be told in all the earth.
9:17 You still hold yourself high against my people, and you do not let them go.
9:18 Look, at this hour tomorrow I am going to rain down a very heavy hail, the like of which has not been in Egypt from the day it was founded until now.
9:19 So now send word, and bring your livestock and all you have in the field to safety. Every person and every animal that is found in the field and is not gathered into the house, the hail will come down on them, and they will die.'"
9:20 Those of Pharaoh's servants who feared the word of the LORD sent their slaves and their livestock running to shelter in the houses.
9:21 Those who did not set their heart on the LORD's word left slaves and livestock out in the field.
9:22 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, so there will be hail in all the land of Egypt, on people and on animals and on every plant of the field in the land of Egypt."
9:23 Moses stretched out his staff toward the sky, and the LORD sent thunder and hail, and fire went down to the earth. The LORD rained hail on the land of Egypt.
9:24 So there was hail, with fire catching and flaring in the middle of the hail, very heavy, the like of which had not been in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.
9:25 In all the land of Egypt the hail struck everything that was in the field, from people to animals. The hail struck every plant of the field and shattered every tree of the field.
9:26 The land of Goshen, where the Israelites were, was the only place with no hail.
9:27 Then Pharaoh sent and called for Moses and Aaron. He said to them, "I have sinned this time. The LORD is the righteous one, and I and my people are the guilty ones.
9:28 Plead with the LORD. Enough of God's thunder and hail! I will let you go. You need not stay any longer."
9:29 Moses said to him, "When I have gone out of the city, I will spread out my hands to the LORD. The thunder will stop and the hail will be no more, so that you may know that the earth is the LORD's own.
9:30 But as for you and your servants, I know you do not yet fear the LORD God."
9:31 The flax and the barley were struck down, since the barley had formed its ears and the flax was budding.
9:32 The wheat and the spelt were not struck down, since they are late crops.
9:33 So Moses left Pharaoh and went out of the city and spread out his hands to the LORD. The thunder and the hail stopped, and no more rain poured down on the earth.
9:34 When Pharaoh saw that rain, hail and thunder had stopped, he sinned once more. He made his heart heavy, both he and his servants.
9:35 So Pharaoh's heart was stubborn, and he did not let the Israelites go, just as the LORD had said through Moses.
## The eighth blow: locusts
10:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have made his heart heavy, and the heart of his servants, so that I can set these signs of mine in their midst,
10:2 and so that you can tell in the ears of your son and your son's son how I made sport of Egypt, and the signs I set among them. And you will know that I am the LORD."
10:3 So Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and said to him, "This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: 'Until when will you refuse to bend low before me? Let my people go, so they may serve me.
10:4 For if you refuse to let my people go, look, tomorrow I am going to bring locusts into your territory.
10:5 They will cover the eye of the land, so that no one can see the land. They will eat what little is left to you, what escaped the hail, and they will eat every tree of yours that sprouts in the field.
10:6 They will fill your houses, the houses of all your servants and the houses of all Egypt, a thing your fathers and your fathers' fathers never saw from the day they came onto the earth until this day.'" Then he turned and went out from Pharaoh.
10:7 Pharaoh's servants said to him, "Until when is this fellow going to be a snare for us? Send the men off to serve the LORD their God. Have you still not grasped that Egypt is lost?"
10:8 So Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh. He said to them, "Go, serve the LORD your God. Who exactly are the ones going?"
10:9 Moses said, "With our young and with our old we will go. With our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds we will go, because we have a feast of the LORD to keep."
10:10 He said to them, "So may the LORD be with you, as surely as I will let you go with your little ones! Look, there is evil right in front of your faces.
10:11 Not so! You men go and serve the LORD, since that is what you are asking for." And they were driven out from Pharaoh's presence.
10:12 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts, so they come up over the land of Egypt and eat every plant of the land, all that the hail has left."
10:13 Moses stretched out his staff over the land of Egypt, and the LORD drove an east wind across the land all that day and all that night. By morning the east wind had brought the locusts.
10:14 The locusts came up over all the land of Egypt and came to rest in all the territory of Egypt, very heavy. Never before had there been such locusts as these, and after them there never will be.
10:15 They covered the eye of the whole land, and the land went dark. They ate every plant of the land and all the fruit of the trees that the hail had left. Nothing green was left, not on tree or on plant of the field, in all the land of Egypt.
10:16 Pharaoh hurried to call for Moses and Aaron. He said, "I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you.
10:17 So now, please, carry away my sin just this one time, and plead with the LORD your God only to take this death away from me."
10:18 Moses left Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD.
10:19 The LORD turned the wind into a very strong sea wind, and it lifted the locusts and drove them into the Sea of Reeds. Not one locust was left in all the territory of Egypt.
10:20 But the LORD made Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and he did not let the Israelites go.
## The ninth blow: darkness
10:21 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, so there will be darkness over the land of Egypt, and let people grope in the darkness."
10:22 Moses stretched out his hand toward the sky, and darkness upon darkness lay over all the land of Egypt for three days.
10:23 A man could not see his brother, and no one got up from where he was for three days. But for all the Israelites there was light in their homes.
10:24 Then Pharaoh called for Moses and said, "Go, serve the LORD. Only your flocks and your herds will be held back. Even your little ones may go with you."
10:25 But Moses said, "You yourself must also put sacrifices and burnt offerings into our hands, so we can offer them to the LORD our God.
10:26 And our livestock too will go with us. Not one hoof will stay behind, because from them we will take what we need to serve the LORD our God. And we ourselves do not know how we will serve the LORD until we arrive there."
10:27 But the LORD made Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and he was not willing to let them go.
10:28 Pharaoh said to him, "Go away from me! Watch yourself. Do not see my face again, for on the day you see my face, you will die."
10:29 Moses said, "You have said it. I will not see your face again."
## One more blow
11:1 The LORD said to Moses, "One more blow I will bring on Pharaoh and on Egypt. After that he will let you go from here. When he lets you go, he will drive you out from here completely, drive you right out.
11:2 Speak now in the ears of the people. Let every man ask his neighbor, and every woman ask her neighbor, for things of silver and things of gold."
11:3 The LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians. The man Moses was also held in high honor in the land of Egypt, both by Pharaoh's servants and by the people.
11:4 Moses said, "This is what the LORD says: 'Around the middle of the night I am going out through the middle of Egypt.
11:5 Every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the slave woman at the hand mill, and every firstborn of the animals.
11:6 There will be a great cry in all the land of Egypt, the like of which there has never been, and the like of which there will never be again.
11:7 But against all the Israelites not a dog will sharpen its tongue, against man or animal, so that you will know that the LORD sets Egypt and Israel apart.'
11:8 Then all these servants of yours will come down to me and bow low before me and say, 'Go out, you and all the people who follow at your feet.' After that I will go out." And he left Pharaoh, burning with anger.
11:9 The LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that my wonders will be many in the land of Egypt."
11:10 Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, but the LORD made Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and he did not let the Israelites go out of his land.
## The first Passover
12:1 The LORD said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt,
12:2 "This month will be the head of the months for you. It will be first among the months of your year.
12:3 Speak to the whole assembly of Israel and say: On the tenth day of this month, let them each take a lamb, by fathers' houses, one lamb to a house.
12:4 If the household is too few for a lamb, then he and his neighbor next to his house will take one, by the count of persons. You will reckon the lamb by what each person can eat.
12:5 It is to be a sound and whole animal, a male in its first year. You may take it from the sheep or from the goats.
12:6 Keep it under watch until the fourteenth day of this month. Then the whole assembled community of Israel will slaughter it between the two evenings.
12:7 They will take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the beam above the door of the houses where they eat it.
12:8 That night they will eat the meat, roasted in the fire. They will eat it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.
12:9 Do not eat any of it raw, or boiled at all in water, but roasted over fire: its head along with its legs and its inner parts.
12:10 Do not leave any of it until morning. Whatever is left of it until morning, burn in the fire.
12:11 And eat it like this: your waist wrapped for travel, your sandals on your feet, your walking stick in your hand. Eat it in a rush. It is a Passover to the LORD.
12:12 On this night I will cross through the land of Egypt and strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both human and animal. And on all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments. I am the LORD.
12:13 The blood will serve you as a sign on the houses where you are. I will see the blood and pass over you, and no blow will fall on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.
12:14 This day will be a day of remembrance for you. You will keep it as a feast to the LORD. Generation after generation you will keep it, a rule for all time.
## The Feast of Unleavened Bread
12:15 Seven days you will eat unleavened bread. Starting the very first day, you will get rid of the leaven in your houses. Anyone who eats leavened food between the first day and the seventh day will be cut off from Israel.
12:16 On the first day you will have a holy gathering, and on the seventh day a holy gathering. No work at all may be done on them. Only what every person needs to eat, that alone may be prepared by you.
12:17 Keep the unleavened bread, for on this very day I brought your armies out of the land of Egypt. Keep this day, generation after generation, a rule for all time.
12:18 In the first month, from the evening of the fourteenth day of the month, you will eat unleavened bread until the evening of the twenty-first day of the month.
12:19 Seven days there must be no leaven found in your houses. Anyone who eats leavened food will be cut off from the community of Israel, foreigner or native-born alike.
12:20 Do not eat anything leavened. In all the places where you live, you will eat unleavened bread."
## Blood on the doorposts
12:21 Then Moses called all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Go, pick out animals from the flock for your clans, and slaughter the Passover.
12:22 Take a bunch of hyssop and dip it into the blood in the basin. With that blood, touch the beam above the door and the two doorposts. Not one of you may go out the door of his house until morning.
12:23 When the LORD passes through to strike Egypt and sees the blood on the beam above and on the two doorposts, the LORD will pass over that doorway. He will not let the destroyer come into your houses to strike you.
12:24 Keep this word as a rule for you and your children, for all time.
12:25 When you come into the land that the LORD will give you, as he has said, keep this service.
12:26 And when your children say to you, 'What is this service to you?'
12:27 you will say, 'This is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD. He passed over the Israelites' houses in Egypt when he struck Egypt, and he rescued our houses.'" And the people bowed their heads and bent down low.
12:28 The Israelites went and did it. As the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
## The death of the firstborn
12:29 In the middle of the night the LORD struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive in the pit-house, and every firstborn of the animals.
12:30 Pharaoh got up in the night, he and all his servants and all Egypt. A great cry went up in Egypt, for not one house was without its dead.
## Israel goes out
12:31 In the night he summoned Moses and Aaron and said, "Up! Get out from among my people, both you and the Israelites! Go and serve the LORD, just as you said.
12:32 Take both your flocks and your herds, as you said, and go. And bless me too."
12:33 Egypt leaned hard on the people to hurry and send them out of the land, for they said, "We are all dead men."
12:34 So the people picked up their dough before it rose, their kneading bowls bundled in their cloaks on their shoulders.
12:35 The Israelites had done as Moses told them. They had asked Egypt for silver things and gold things and clothing.
12:36 The LORD had given the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians, so they granted their requests. So they stripped Egypt bare.
12:37 The Israelites set out from Rameses toward Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, not counting the little ones.
12:38 A large mixed crowd also went up with them, and flocks and herds, livestock in very heavy numbers.
12:39 They baked the dough they had brought out of Egypt into round cakes of unleavened bread, because it had not risen. They had been driven out of Egypt and could not linger, and they had not even made food for the road.
12:40 The Israelites had lived in Egypt for a span of four hundred and thirty years.
12:41 Four hundred and thirty years passed, and on that very day, all the armies of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt.
12:42 That was a night of keeping watch for the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This night is the LORD's, a night of keeping watch for all the Israelites, generation after generation.
## Who may eat the Passover
12:43 The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the rule for the Passover: no foreigner may eat it.
12:44 But any man's slave bought with silver may eat of it, once you have circumcised him.
12:45 A settler or a hired worker may not eat of it.
12:46 It must be eaten inside one house. Do not take any of the meat outside, and do not break any of its bones.
12:47 The whole community of Israel will keep it.
12:48 A foreigner who lives among you and wants to keep the LORD's Passover must first have all his males circumcised. After that he may come and keep it, and he will be like a native of the land. But no uncircumcised man may eat it.
12:49 One law will hold for the one born in the land and for the foreigner who lives among you."
12:50 All the Israelites did it. As the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
12:51 And on that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, in their ranks like armies.
## The firstborn belong to the LORD
13:1 The LORD said to Moses,
13:2 "Set apart for me as holy every firstborn, the first to open any womb among the Israelites, of humans and of animals. It is mine."
13:3 Then Moses said to the people, "Remember this day, the day you came out of Egypt, out of the house of slaves, for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from here. Nothing leavened may be eaten.
13:4 Today you are going out, in the month of Abib.
13:5 When the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, which he swore to your fathers to give you, a land flowing with milk and honey, you will carry out this service in this month.
13:6 Seven days you will eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there will be a feast to the LORD.
13:7 Unleavened bread is to be eaten through the seven days. Nothing leavened may be seen in your keeping, and no leaven may be seen in your keeping, in all your territory.
13:8 On that day you will tell your son, 'It is because of this, what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.'
13:9 This will be a sign on your hand for you and a reminder between your eyes, so that the LORD's law may be on your lips. For the LORD brought you out of Egypt by a strong hand.
13:10 Keep this rule at its set time, from year to year.
13:11 When the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, as he swore to you and to your fathers, and gives it to you,
13:12 you will make every first to open the womb pass over to the LORD. Every first to open the womb among the young of your animals, the males, belongs to the LORD.
13:13 Every first to open the womb of a donkey you will ransom with a lamb. If you do not ransom it, break its neck. Among your sons, you will ransom every human firstborn.
13:14 In days to come, when your son asks you, 'What is this?' you will say to him, 'With strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, from the house of slaves.
13:15 Pharaoh made it hard for himself to let us go, so the LORD killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both human and animal. So I sacrifice to the LORD every male that first opens the womb, but each firstborn of my sons I ransom.'
13:16 It will be a sign on your hand and a band between your eyes. With strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt."
## God leads them the long way
13:17 Now when Pharaoh had sent the people off, God did not guide them by way of the Philistines' land, although that was close. God said, "Or the people may have regrets when they see fighting, and go back to Egypt."
13:18 So God led the people around by the wilderness road to the Sea of Reeds. The Israelites went up from the land of Egypt in battle order.
13:19 Moses took Joseph's bones with him, because Joseph had made the sons of Israel swear an oath. He had said, "God will surely come to help you, and you will carry my bones up from here with you."
13:20 They set out from Succoth and camped at Etham, at the edge of the wilderness.
13:21 The LORD was walking in front of them: by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them along the road, and by night in a pillar of fire to light their way, so they could go on day and night.
13:22 By day the pillar of cloud never moved away from the front of the people, nor the pillar of fire by night.
## Trapped by the sea
14:1 The LORD said to Moses,
14:2 "Tell the Israelites to turn back and pitch camp before Pi-hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea. Camp by the sea, opposite Baal-zephon, facing it.
14:3 Pharaoh will say of the Israelites, 'They are lost and confused in the land. The wilderness has closed in on them.'
14:4 I will make Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and he will chase after them. Then I will win glory for myself through Pharaoh and all his army, and Egypt will know that I am the LORD." The Israelites did so.
14:5 Word came to the king of Egypt that the people had fled. The heart of Pharaoh and his servants changed toward the people, and they said, "What have we done? We let Israel go, and now they no longer serve us!"
14:6 So he hitched up his chariot and took his people with him.
14:7 He took six hundred chosen chariots, all the chariotry of Egypt, with officers over all of it.
14:8 The LORD made the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt stubborn, and he chased after the Israelites. But the Israelites were going out with a hand raised high.
14:9 Egypt chased after them, every horse of Pharaoh's chariots, his horsemen and his army, and they caught up with them camped by the sea, beside Pi-hahiroth, in front of Baal-zephon.
14:10 Pharaoh came closer. The Israelites looked up, and look: Egypt on the march behind them! They were very much afraid, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.
14:11 They said to Moses, "Were there no graves in Egypt, that you brought us out here to die in the wilderness? Look what you have done to us by bringing us out of Egypt!
14:12 Isn't this the very thing we said to you in Egypt: 'Let us be, so we can serve Egypt'? Serving Egypt is better for us than dying in the wilderness."
14:13 Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. Take your stand, and see how the LORD will save you today. For as you have seen Egypt today, you will never see them again, ever.
14:14 The LORD will fight for you. And you? You will keep quiet."
## The sea is split
14:15 The LORD said to Moses, "Why cry out to me? Tell the Israelites to set out.
14:16 And you, lift up your staff and stretch out your hand over the sea and split it, so the Israelites can go into the middle of the sea on dry ground.
14:17 And I, look, I am going to make Egypt's heart stubborn, and they will go in after them. And I will win glory for myself through Pharaoh and through all his army, through his chariots and through his horsemen.
14:18 Egypt will know that I am the LORD when I win glory for myself through Pharaoh, through his chariots and through his horsemen."
14:19 The angel of God, who had been going ahead of Israel's camp, set out and went behind them. The pillar of cloud also set out from in front of them and took its stand behind them.
14:20 It came between Egypt's camp and Israel's camp. The cloud was there, and the darkness, and it lit up the night, and neither camp came near the other all night long.
14:21 Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. All night the LORD made the sea go back by a fierce east wind, and he turned the sea into dry ground. The waters split apart.
14:22 The Israelites went into the middle of the sea on dry ground, with the water standing as a wall for them on their right and on their left.
14:23 Egypt chased them and went in after them, every horse of Pharaoh, his chariots and his horsemen, into the middle of the sea.
14:24 In the morning watch the LORD looked down on Egypt's camp from the pillar of fire and cloud, and he threw Egypt's camp into confusion.
14:25 He made their chariot wheels swerve off, and they could hardly drive them. Egypt said, "Let me run from Israel's face, for the LORD is fighting for them against Egypt!"
14:26 Then the LORD told Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, and the waters will come back over Egypt, over their chariots and their horsemen."
14:27 Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and as morning came, the sea went back to its full strength. Egypt was fleeing straight into it, and the LORD shook Egypt off into the middle of the sea.
14:28 The waters came back and covered the chariots and the horsemen, all Pharaoh's army that had gone into the sea after them. Not one of them was left, not even one.
14:29 But the Israelites had walked through the middle of the sea on dry ground, with the water standing as a wall for them on their right and on their left.
14:30 So on that day the LORD saved Israel from the hand of Egypt, and Israel saw Egypt dead on the shore of the sea.
14:31 When Israel saw the great hand the LORD had used on Egypt, the people feared the LORD. They believed in the LORD and in Moses his servant.
## The Song of the Sea
15:1 Then Moses and the Israelites sang this song to the LORD. They said, "I will sing to the LORD, / for he has risen up high, so high. / Horse and its rider / he has flung into the sea.
15:2 My strength and my song is Yah, / and he has become my rescue. / This is my God, and I will honor him, / my father's God, and I will lift him high.
15:3 The LORD is a man of war. / The LORD is his name.
15:4 Pharaoh's chariots and his army / he has shot into the sea. / His chosen officers / sank down in the Sea of Reeds.
15:5 The deeps cover them. / They went down to the bottom like a stone.
15:6 Your right hand, O LORD, / is splendid in strength. / Your right hand, O LORD, / smashes the enemy.
15:7 In your towering majesty you throw down / those who stand up against you. / You let loose your fury; / it eats them like dry stubble.
15:8 By the wind from your nostrils / the waters were heaped up. / Running streams stood up like a mound. / The deeps went stiff in the heart of the sea.
15:9 The enemy said, 'I'll chase, I'll catch, / I'll split up the spoil. / My hunger will be glutted on them. / I'll bare my sword. / My hand will take all they have.'
15:10 You breathed out your wind, / and the sea closed over them. / Like lead they went under / in the mighty waters.
15:11 Who is like you among the gods, O LORD? / Who is like you, splendid in holiness, / awesome in praises, / maker of wonders?
15:12 You stretched out your right hand. / The earth swallowed them.
15:13 You led on in your loyal love / the people you redeemed. / You guided them in your strength / to your holy pasture-home.
15:14 Peoples have heard; they shake. / Pangs have gripped those who live in Philistia.
15:15 Then the chiefs of Edom are dismayed. / The rams of Moab, trembling grips them. / All who live in Canaan melt.
15:16 Terror and dread fall on them. / At the greatness of your arm / they are still as a stone, / until your people cross over, O LORD, / until this people you have made your own cross over.
15:17 You will bring them in and plant them / on the mountain that is your own, / the place, O LORD, you made for your throne, / the holy place, my Lord, your hands set firm.
15:18 The LORD will be king forever and ever."
15:19 For when Pharaoh's horses, with his chariots and his horsemen, went into the sea, the LORD brought the waters of the sea back over them, but the Israelites walked on dry ground in the middle of the sea.
## Miriam's song
15:20 Then Miriam the prophet, Aaron's sister, took the hand drum in her hand, and all the women went out after her with hand drums and with dancing.
15:21 Miriam sang back to them: "Sing to the LORD, / for he has risen up high, so high. / Horse and its rider / he has flung into the sea."
## Bitter water made sweet
15:22 Then Moses led Israel away from the Sea of Reeds, and they went out into the wilderness of Shur. For three days they walked through the wilderness and found no water.
15:23 They came to Marah, but the water at Marah was too bitter to drink. That is how it got the name Marah.
15:24 The people grumbled against Moses: "What are we going to drink?"
15:25 He cried out to the LORD, and the LORD pointed out to him a piece of wood. He threw it into the water, and the water turned sweet. There he set for them a rule and a law, and there he tested them.
15:26 He said, "If you will truly hear the voice of the LORD your God, and do what is upright in his eyes, and open your ears to his commands, and keep all his laws, then none of the sickness I laid on Egypt will I lay on you. For I am the LORD, your healer."
15:27 Then they came to Elim. Twelve springs of water were there, and seventy palm trees, and they camped there beside the water.
