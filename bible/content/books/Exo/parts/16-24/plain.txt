## Bread from the sky
16:1 Leaving Elim, the whole community of Israelites arrived in the Wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai. This was on the fifteenth day of the second month since they came out of the land of Egypt.
16:2 There in the wilderness the whole community of Israelites complained against Moses and Aaron.
16:3 The Israelites said to them, "If only the LORD's own hand had killed us in the land of Egypt! There we sat beside the pots of meat and ate bread until we were full. Instead you have brought us out into this wilderness so that this whole assembly will die of hunger."
16:4 Then the LORD said to Moses, "I am about to make bread fall like rain from the sky for you. The people are to go out and pick up each day's share on its own day. This is how I will test them: will they live by my law or not?
16:5 And on the sixth day, when they get ready what they have brought home, it will come to double the amount they gather day by day."
16:6 So Moses and Aaron told all the Israelites, "When evening comes, you will know it was the LORD who led you out of the land of Egypt.
16:7 And when morning comes, you will see the LORD's glory. He has heard your complaints against the LORD. And us, what are we? Why complain against us?"
16:8 Moses also said, "This will happen when the LORD gives you meat in the evening to eat, and bread in the morning to fill you, since the LORD has heard the complaints you keep making against him. And what are we? It is not us you are complaining against. It is the LORD."
16:9 Then Moses said to Aaron, "Tell the whole community of Israelites, 'Come close, into the LORD's presence, because he has heard your complaints.'"
16:10 While Aaron was still speaking to the whole community of Israelites, they faced toward the wilderness, and suddenly the LORD's glory could be seen in the cloud.
16:11 The LORD spoke to Moses:
16:12 "I have heard the Israelites complaining. Tell them, 'Between the two evenings you will eat meat, and in the morning you will eat bread until you are full. Then you will know that I am the LORD your God.'"
16:13 In the evening the quail came up and covered the camp, and in the morning dew lay spread all around the camp.
16:14 When the spread of dew went up, there on the face of the wilderness was something thin and flaky, thin like frost on the ground.
16:15 The Israelites saw it and asked one another, "What is it?" because they did not know what it was. Moses told them, "That is the bread which the LORD has given you for your food.
16:16 This is the word the LORD has commanded: 'Gather some of it, each person as much as he eats, an omer for every head, by the number of your people. Each of you is to gather for those in his tent.'"
16:17 So the Israelites did it. They gathered, the one who took much and the one who took little.
16:18 When they measured it by the omer, the one who gathered much had nothing left over, and the one who gathered little did not run short. Each had gathered as much as he could eat.
16:19 Moses told them, "Do not let anyone keep some back until morning."
16:20 But they would not listen to Moses. Some men saved a portion until the morning. It crawled with worms and smelled foul, and Moses grew angry at them.
16:21 Morning after morning they gathered it, each as much as he could eat. When the sun grew warm, it melted.
## The first Sabbath
16:22 On the sixth day what they collected was double bread, two omers apiece. All the chiefs of the community came and told Moses.
16:23 He said to them, "This is the LORD's word: Tomorrow is a rest day, a holy Sabbath for the LORD. Bake whatever you plan to bake, and boil whatever you plan to boil. Then set aside everything extra and keep it safe until morning."
16:24 They set it by till morning as Moses had ordered. It did not smell, and no worm was found in it.
16:25 Moses said, "Eat it today. Today is the LORD's Sabbath. Today you won't find any out in the field.
16:26 You will gather it six days, and the seventh day is Sabbath; there will be none on it."
16:27 When the seventh day came, a few of the people went out to gather, and they found nothing.
16:28 The LORD said to Moses, "How long will you all go on refusing to keep my commands and my laws?
16:29 Look: it is the LORD who gave you the Sabbath. So on the sixth day he hands you two days' bread. On the seventh day, each of you stay put in your own spot. No one is to go out from where he is."
16:30 And so on the seventh day the people rested.
## A jar of manna kept
16:31 The house of Israel gave it the name manna. It was like coriander seed, but white, and it tasted like a honey wafer.
16:32 Moses said, "Here is what the LORD commands: 'A full omer of it is to be kept for all the generations after you. Let them see the bread I fed you with in the wilderness, when I led you out of the land of Egypt.'"
16:33 Moses said to Aaron, "Get one jar and put in it a full omer of manna. Place it in front of the LORD, to be kept safe for the generations after you."
16:34 Aaron did what the LORD had commanded Moses: he set it down before the testimony, to be kept.
16:35 For forty years the Israelites ate the manna, until they came into a land with people living in it. The manna was their food until they arrived at the edge of the land of Canaan.
16:36 The omer is a tenth of an ephah.
## Water from the rock
17:1 The whole community of Israelites moved on from the Wilderness of Sin, stage by stage, as the LORD directed, and camped at Rephidim. The people had no water to drink there.
17:2 So the people picked a fight with Moses. They said, "Give us water so we can drink." Moses said to them, "What makes you fight with me? What makes you test the LORD?"
17:3 But the people were parched there, and the people complained against Moses: "What did you bring us up out of Egypt for? To make me and my children and my herds die of thirst?"
17:4 Moses cried out to the LORD, "What will I do about this people? Just a little longer, and they will stone me."
17:5 The LORD said to Moses, "Go across in front of the people, with some of Israel's elders beside you. Hold in your hand the staff you used to strike the Nile, and set off.
17:6 I will be there, standing before you on the rock at Horeb. You will hit the rock, and water will flow out of it, and the people will drink." Moses did this with the elders of Israel looking on.
17:7 He called the place Massah and Meribah, because the Israelites picked a fight there, and because they put the LORD on trial, saying, "Is the LORD here with us, or isn't he?"
## Amalek attacks
17:8 Then Amalek came and went to war against Israel at Rephidim.
17:9 Moses said to Joshua, "Pick out men for us and go fight Amalek. Tomorrow I will take my place at the top of the hill, holding God's staff in my hand."
17:10 Joshua carried out Moses' order and fought with Amalek. Moses, Aaron and Hur climbed to the hilltop.
17:11 As often as Moses raised his hand, Israel got the upper hand. As often as he let his hand drop, Amalek got the upper hand.
17:12 Moses' hands got heavy. So they brought a stone and set it under him, and he sat on it. Aaron and Hur propped up his hands, one on this side and one on that, and his hands stayed firm until sundown.
17:13 So Joshua beat down Amalek and its people by the edge of the sword.
17:14 Then the LORD said to Moses, "Write this down in the scroll as a reminder, and read it aloud to Joshua: I will wipe out, yes, wipe out the memory of Amalek from beneath the sky."
17:15 Moses built an altar and named it The LORD Is My Flag.
17:16 He said, "A hand on the throne of the LORD! The LORD will war with Amalek, one generation after another."
## Jethro visits Moses
18:1 Jethro, Midian's priest and father-in-law to Moses, got word of everything God had done for Moses and for Israel, his people. He heard that the LORD had led Israel out of Egypt.
18:2 Moses had sent his wife Zipporah back, and Jethro his father-in-law had taken her in,
18:3 together with her two sons. One son's name was Gershom, since Moses had said, "I have become an outsider living in a land not my own."
18:4 The other's name was Eliezer, since he had said, "The God of my father came to help me and snatched me from Pharaoh's sword."
18:5 So Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, came out to the wilderness with Moses' sons and wife, to where Moses was camping by the mountain of God.
18:6 He said to Moses, "It is I, Jethro, your father-in-law, coming to you, along with your wife and both her sons."
18:7 Moses came out to greet his father-in-law, bowed low, and kissed him. Each asked how the other was doing, and they went inside the tent.
18:8 Moses gave his father-in-law the whole story: what the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to Egypt on Israel's behalf, all the hard times that had met them on the road, and how the LORD had saved them.
18:9 Jethro rejoiced over all the good the LORD had done for Israel when he saved them from the grip of Egypt.
18:10 Jethro said, "Blessed is the LORD, who saved you out of Egypt's hand and Pharaoh's hand, who freed the people from under Egypt's hand.
18:11 Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods. He proved it when they acted with such pride against this people."
18:12 Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took a whole burnt offering and sacrifices for God, and Aaron came with all the elders of Israel to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God.
## Judges for the people
18:13 The next day Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.
18:14 Moses' father-in-law watched all that he was doing for the people and said, "What is this business you are doing for the people? Why are you sitting by yourself, with all the people standing over you from morning till night?"
18:15 Moses answered his father-in-law, "It is because the people come to me to seek God's answer.
18:16 When they have a matter, it comes to me. I judge between one man and his neighbor, and I make God's rules and his laws known."
18:17 Moses' father-in-law said to him, "The way you are doing this is not good.
18:18 You will surely wear down to nothing, you and also these people with you. This load is too heavy for you. You can't carry it by yourself.
18:19 Now hear what I say. Let me counsel you, and God be with you. You be the people's man in front of God, and you carry their matters to God.
18:20 Instruct them in the rules and the laws, and show them the road they must walk and the things they must do.
18:21 And you, pick out from the whole people men of worth, who fear God, men of truth who hate crooked profit. Set them over the people as captains of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties and of tens.
18:22 They will judge the people day in, day out. Any big matter they will bring to you, but every small matter they will judge themselves. Lift the weight off yourself, and let them carry it with you.
18:23 If you do this, and God commands you, you will be able to last, and all this people will go back to their place in peace."
18:24 Moses heeded his father-in-law and did everything he had proposed.
18:25 He chose men of worth out of all Israel and made them heads over the people, captains of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties and of tens.
18:26 They judged the people day in, day out. The hard matters they would bring to Moses, and every small matter they would judge themselves.
18:27 Moses sent his father-in-law off, and Jethro made his way back to his own land.
## Israel at the mountain
19:1 In the third month after the Israelites went out from the land of Egypt, on this same day, they arrived at the Wilderness of Sinai.
19:2 Setting out from Rephidim, they entered the Wilderness of Sinai and pitched camp in the wilderness. Israel camped there, facing the mountain.
19:3 Moses climbed up to God. The LORD called out to him from the mountain: "Say this to the house of Jacob, and announce it to the Israelites:
19:4 'You have seen with your own eyes what I did to Egypt. I lifted you up on the wings of eagles and brought you to me.
19:5 Now then, if you really listen to my voice and keep my covenant, out of all the peoples you will be my own private treasure. For the whole earth belongs to me,
19:6 and you, you will be for me a kingdom of priests, a holy nation.' These are the words to speak to the Israelites."
19:7 Moses went and summoned the elders of the people. He set out before them every one of these words that the LORD had charged him with.
19:8 The people all answered as one: "Whatever the LORD has said, we will do!" Moses carried the people's reply back to the LORD.
19:9 The LORD said to Moses, "Look, I am coming to you in the thick of the cloud, so that the people will hear when I speak with you, and will trust you too, always." Then Moses reported the people's words to the LORD.
## Getting ready to meet God
19:10 The LORD said to Moses, "Go to the people and set them apart as holy today and tomorrow. They must wash their clothes
19:11 and get ready for the third day, because on the third day, while all the people watch, the LORD will come down onto Mount Sinai.
19:12 Mark off a boundary for the people on every side, and say, 'Watch yourselves: do not climb the mountain, and do not touch even the edge of it. Whoever touches the mountain must be put to death.
19:13 No hand is to touch him. He must be stoned or shot, animal or human; he must not live.' When the ram's horn sounds a long blast, they may go up on the mountain."
19:14 Moses came down off the mountain to the people and set the people apart as holy, and they washed their clothes.
19:15 He told the people, "Be ready, three days from now. Do not come near a woman."
## Fire on Sinai
19:16 When morning came on the third day, there was thunder and flashes of lightning, a heavy cloud on the mountain, and the blast of a ram's horn, extremely loud. All the people in the camp trembled.
19:17 Moses brought the people out from the camp to meet God. They stood in place beneath the mountain.
19:18 Mount Sinai smoked from top to bottom, since the LORD had come down upon it in the fire. Its smoke went up like a furnace's smoke, and the whole mountain quaked hard.
19:19 As the horn's sound went on and grew stronger and stronger, Moses kept speaking, and God kept answering him with a voice.
19:20 The LORD came down upon Mount Sinai, onto the mountaintop. The LORD summoned Moses up to the mountaintop, and Moses climbed up.
19:21 The LORD said to Moses, "Go down and warn the people, so that they do not break through to the LORD to see, and many of them fall.
19:22 The priests too, who approach the LORD, must make themselves holy, or the LORD will burst out against them."
19:23 Moses said to the LORD, "The people aren't able to climb Mount Sinai. You already warned us: 'Fence off the mountain and treat it as holy.'"
19:24 The LORD told him, "Go, get down, and then come up, you and Aaron with you. But the priests and the people must not break through to come up to the LORD, or he will break out against them."
19:25 Moses went down to the people and spoke to them.
## The Ten Words
20:1 Then God spoke all these words:
20:2 "I am the LORD your God. I brought you out of the land of Egypt, that house of slaves.
20:3 You must not have any other gods in my presence.
20:4 You must not make yourself an idol, or the form of anything up in the sky, or down on the earth, or in the waters below the land.
20:5 You must not bow down to them or serve them. For I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God. I bring the fathers' guilt home to the children, to the third and the fourth generation of those who hate me,
20:6 but I show loyal love to thousands, for those who love me and keep my commands.
20:7 You must not take up the name of the LORD your God for something empty. The LORD will not count anyone innocent who takes up his name for something empty.
20:8 Remember the day of the Sabbath, and set it apart as holy.
20:9 You have six days to work and get all your jobs done,
20:10 but the seventh day is a Sabbath for the LORD your God. Do no work on it: not you, not your son or your daughter, not your slave, man or woman, not your animals, not the foreigner who lives in your towns.
20:11 For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and everything in them, and on the seventh day he rested. That is why the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and set it apart as holy.
20:12 Honor your father and your mother, so that your days will stretch out long on the land the LORD your God is giving you.
20:13 You must not murder.
20:14 You must not commit adultery.
20:15 You must not steal.
20:16 You must not testify against your neighbor as a lying witness.
20:17 You must not crave your neighbor's house. You must not crave your neighbor's wife, or his slave, man or woman, or his ox or his donkey, or anything at all that belongs to your neighbor."
## The people keep their distance
20:18 All the people were seeing the thunder and the torches of lightning, the sound of the horn, and the mountain smoking. When the people saw it, they shook, and they stood far back.
20:19 They said to Moses, "Let it be you who speaks with us, and we will listen. Let God not speak with us, for fear we die."
20:20 Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. God has come to test you, and so that the fear of him will be before your faces, so that you do not sin."
20:21 So the people kept their distance, and Moses went up close to the deep darkness where God was.
## How to build an altar
20:22 The LORD said to Moses, "This is what you will say to the Israelites: You have seen for yourselves that I spoke with you from the heavens.
20:23 Do not make anything to set beside me. Gods of silver and gods of gold, you must not make them for yourselves.
20:24 Make me an altar of earth, and on it slaughter your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your cattle. In every place where I make my name remembered, I will come to you and bless you.
20:25 If you make me an altar of stones, do not build it of cut stone. If you have swung your blade over it, you have made it unholy.
20:26 And do not go up to my altar by steps, so that your bare body is not exposed on it."
## Laws about slaves
21:1 "Here are the rulings you will lay before them.
21:2 If you acquire a Hebrew slave, he is to work six years. In the seventh he goes free, owing nothing.
21:3 If he came in by himself, he leaves by himself. If he was a married man, his wife leaves with him.
21:4 If his master gave him a wife and she has borne him sons or daughters, the woman and her children remain with her master, and he goes out on his own.
21:5 But if the slave says outright, 'I love my master, my wife and my children. I won't go free,'
21:6 his master is to bring him to God. He will bring him to the door or the doorpost, and his master will pierce his ear with an awl. Then he will serve him for life.
21:7 If a man sells his daughter as a slave woman, she does not go free the way male slaves go free.
21:8 If her master is not pleased with her, though he promised her to himself, he must let her be ransomed. He has no authority to sell her to a foreign people, since he broke faith with her.
21:9 If he promises her to his son, he must treat her the way daughters are treated.
21:10 If he takes another woman for himself, he must not cut back her food, her clothing, or her rights as a wife.
21:11 If he does not provide her these three, she goes free for nothing, with no money paid.
## Laws about violence
21:12 Whoever hits someone and he dies must be put to death.
21:13 But if he did not stalk him, and God brought it about by his hand, I will set aside a place for you where he can run for refuge.
21:14 But if someone schemes against his neighbor to kill him by a trick, take him away even from my altar to die.
21:15 Anyone who strikes his father or his mother must be put to death.
21:16 Whoever steals a person must be put to death, whether he has sold him or the person is found in his hand.
21:17 Anyone who curses his father or his mother must be put to death.
21:18 If men get into a fight, and one hits the other with a rock or a fist, and he does not die but is laid up in bed,
21:19 then if he gets up and walks around outside with his walking stick, the one who hit him is let off. He only pays for the time the man lost and sees that he is fully healed.
21:20 If someone beats his slave, man or woman, with a rod, and the slave dies under his hand, the death must be avenged.
21:21 But if the slave lives on for a day or two, there is no vengeance, because the slave is his money.
21:22 If men are brawling and they strike a pregnant woman, and her children come out, but no harm follows, the one responsible must be fined. He pays what the woman's husband sets for him, as the arbiters decide.
21:23 But if harm does follow, you are to give life for life,
21:24 eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,
21:25 burn for burn, cut for cut, bruise for bruise.
21:26 If someone hits his slave, man or woman, in the eye and ruins it, he must let the slave go free in exchange for the eye.
21:27 If he knocks out a tooth of his slave, man or woman, he must let the slave go free in exchange for the tooth.
## Laws about goring oxen and open pits
21:28 If an ox gores a man or a woman and the person dies, the ox must be stoned, and no one may eat its meat. But the ox's owner goes free of guilt.
21:29 But if the ox was a gorer in the past, and its owner was warned but did not guard it, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox is to be stoned, and its owner too is to be put to death.
21:30 If a ransom is set for him instead, he must pay whatever is set for him as the price of his life.
21:31 Whether it gores a son or gores a daughter, the same ruling applies to him.
21:32 If it is a slave, man or woman, that the ox gores, its owner gives the slave's master silver, thirty shekels, and the ox must be stoned to death.
21:33 If someone leaves a pit open, or digs a pit and doesn't cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls in,
21:34 the pit's owner must make it good. He pays silver to the animal's owner, and keeps the dead animal.
21:35 If one man's ox hurts his neighbor's ox and it dies, they are to sell the live ox and split the money, and split the dead one too.
21:36 Or if it was known that the ox had been a gorer in the past and its owner did not guard it, he must make it good, a live ox for the dead ox, and the dead one becomes his.
## Laws about property
22:1 If someone steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters or sells it, he must repay with five cattle in place of the ox and four sheep in place of the sheep.
22:2 If a thief is caught tunneling in and is struck and dies, no blood guilt is counted for him.
22:3 But if the sun had risen on him, there is blood guilt for him. The thief must surely repay. If he owns nothing, he himself is sold to cover the theft.
22:4 If the stolen animal, ox or donkey or sheep, is actually found alive in his hand, he repays two for one.
22:5 If someone lets a field or vineyard be grazed bare, turning his animals loose to feed in another man's field, he must pay it back from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.
22:6 If a fire goes out and finds thorns, and a stack of sheaves, the standing grain or the field is burned up, whoever lit the fire must make it good.
22:7 If someone gives his neighbor money or things to keep for him, and they are stolen from the man's house, then if the thief is found, he repays two for one.
22:8 If the thief is not found, the owner of the house is to be brought before God, to see whether he himself laid a hand on his neighbor's goods.
22:9 In any case of wrongdoing, whether over an ox, a donkey, a sheep, a garment, or anything lost of which someone says, 'This is the one,' both parties' case is to come before God. The one God finds guilty must repay his neighbor two for one.
22:10 If someone gives his neighbor a donkey, an ox, a sheep or any animal to keep, and it dies, or is hurt, or is carried off, with no one seeing it,
22:11 an oath by the LORD will settle it between the two of them, that he did not lay a hand on his neighbor's goods. Its owner is to accept it, and he will not have to repay.
22:12 But if it really was stolen from him, he must pay its owner back.
22:13 If it was torn apart by a beast, let him bring what is left as a witness. He does not repay for the torn animal.
22:14 If someone asks to borrow an animal from his neighbor, and it is hurt or dies when its owner is not with it, he must surely repay.
22:15 If its owner was with it, he does not repay. If it was a hired animal, the loss came with the hire.
## Laws for a holy people
22:16 If a man lures away a virgin who is not promised in marriage and lies with her, he must pay her bride-price and take her as his wife.
22:17 If her father absolutely refuses to give her to him, he must weigh out silver like the bride-price for virgins.
22:18 A woman who works sorcery must not be left alive.
22:19 Anyone who has sex with an animal must be put to death.
22:20 Anyone who sacrifices to the gods, and not to the LORD alone, is to be put under the ban and destroyed.
22:21 Do not mistreat a foreigner living among you, and do not press him hard, for you were foreigners living in the land of Egypt.
22:22 Never treat a widow or a fatherless child harshly.
22:23 If you do treat him harshly, and he cries out to me, be sure I will hear his cry.
22:24 My anger will blaze, and I will kill you by the sword. Then your own wives will be widows and your children fatherless.
22:25 If you lend money to my people, to the poor one among you, do not act toward him like a debt collector. Do not charge him interest.
22:26 If you take your neighbor's coat to hold as security, give it back to him by sundown,
22:27 because it is his only covering, the cloak for his skin. What will he sleep in? When he cries out to me, I will hear, for I am gracious.
22:28 Do not treat God with contempt, and do not curse a leader of your people.
22:29 Do not be slow to give from your full harvest and from your dripping presses. Give me the firstborn of your sons.
22:30 Do the same with your oxen and your flock. Seven days it may stay with its mother; on the eighth day give it to me.
22:31 You are to be people set apart as holy to me. Do not eat flesh torn by beasts in the field; throw it to the dogs.
## Justice in court
23:1 Do not pass along a false rumor. Do not join your hand to a wicked man's and become a witness who serves violence.
23:2 Do not follow the many into evil. When you speak in a court case, do not turn aside after the many and push justice out of the way.
23:3 And do not tip the scales for a poor man in his case.
23:4 If you meet your enemy's ox or his donkey straying, you must bring it back to him.
23:5 If you see the donkey of someone who hates you lying down under its load, hold back from leaving him alone with it. You must work it loose together with him.
23:6 Do not push aside the right of the needy among you when his case is heard.
23:7 Keep far from a false charge. Do not kill the innocent and the righteous, for I will not count the wicked as righteous.
23:8 Do not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds those who see clearly and turns the words of the righteous upside down.
23:9 Do not press a foreigner hard. You know the life of a foreigner from the inside, because you were foreigners living in the land of Egypt.
## Sabbath years and festivals
23:10 For six years plant your land and bring in what it grows,
23:11 but in the seventh year let it go and leave it alone, so that the needy of your people can eat from it, and what they leave the wild animals can eat. This is what you are to do with your vineyard and your olive trees too.
23:12 For six days do your work, but on the seventh day stop, so that your ox and your donkey can rest, and the son of your slave woman and the foreigner can catch their breath.
23:13 Pay attention to everything I have said to you. Do not even mention the names of other gods; they must not be heard coming from your mouth.
23:14 Three times a year hold a feast for me.
23:15 Keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread. For seven days eat flat bread made with no yeast, as I commanded you, at the time set in the month of Abib, for in that month you came out of Egypt. No one is to appear before my face with empty hands.
23:16 Keep also the Feast of Harvest, for the first crops of your work, of what you plant in the field; and the Feast of Ingathering as the year goes out, when you gather the fruit of your work from the field.
23:17 Three times a year every male among you is to appear before the Lord, the LORD.
23:18 Do not sacrifice the blood of my sacrifice with anything that has risen with yeast. The fat of my feast must not be left until morning.
23:19 The first and best of the early crops of your ground you are to bring to the house of the LORD your God. Do not boil a young goat in the milk of its mother.
## The angel and the land
23:20 Here, I am sending an angel in front of you to keep you safe on the way and to bring you into the place I have made ready.
23:21 Be careful in his presence and listen to his voice. Do not provoke him, for he will not put up with your rebellion, because my name is in him.
23:22 But if you truly listen to his voice and do everything I tell you, I will be the enemy of your enemies and the foe of those who fight you.
23:23 For my angel will walk before you and bring you to the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Canaanites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, and I will make an end of them.
23:24 Do not bow down to their gods or serve them, and do not do what they do. Tear them down completely, and smash their standing stones to bits.
23:25 Serve the LORD your God, and he will bless your bread and your water. I will take sickness away from your midst.
23:26 In your land no woman will lose her young or be unable to bear. I will give you the full count of your days.
23:27 I will send my terror before you and put every people you go among into a panic. I will make all your enemies turn and show you the backs of their necks.
23:28 I will send the hornet in front of you, and it will drive out the Hivites, the Canaanites and the Hittites from before you.
23:29 I will not drive them all out in one year, so that the land does not become a waste and the wild animals grow too many for you.

## The covenant sealed in blood
24:1 To Moses he said, "Come up to the LORD, you and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel, and bow down while still far off.
24:2 Moses alone is to come near the LORD. They must not come near, and the people must not go up with him."
24:3 Moses came and went over with the people all the LORD's words and all the rulings. All the people answered with a single voice: "Every word the LORD has spoken, we will do."
24:4 Moses wrote all the LORD's words. He rose early in the morning and built an altar at the foot of the mountain, with twelve standing stones for the twelve tribes of Israel.
24:5 He sent young men from the Israelites, and they offered up burnt offerings and slaughtered bulls as peace offerings to the LORD.
24:6 Moses took half of the blood and put it in basins, and half of the blood he dashed on the altar.
24:7 Then he took the scroll of the covenant and read it out for the people to hear. They said, "All the LORD has spoken, we will do, and we will listen."
24:8 Moses took the blood and splashed it on the people. He said, "Here is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has cut with you on the terms of all these words."
## A meal in God's presence
24:9 Then Moses went up, with Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel.
24:10 They saw the God of Israel. Under his feet was what looked like a floor of blue lapis stone, as pure as the sky itself.
24:11 Against the nobles of the Israelites he did not put out his hand. They looked upon God, and they ate and drank.
## Moses enters the cloud
24:12 The LORD said to Moses, "Come up to me on the mountain and stay there. I will give you the stone tablets, with the law and the command that I have written to instruct them."
24:13 Moses got up, together with Joshua his helper, and Moses climbed the mountain of God.
24:14 To the elders he said, "Wait here for us until we return to you. Look, Aaron and Hur are with you. Whoever has a matter to settle can go to them."
24:15 When Moses went up the mountain, the cloud wrapped the mountain.
24:16 The glory of the LORD settled on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days. On the seventh day he called to Moses out of the middle of the cloud.
24:17 The LORD's glory looked like a fire that eats everything, on the top of the mountain, before the eyes of the Israelites.
24:18 Moses walked into the middle of the cloud and climbed the mountain. Moses stayed on the mountain forty days and forty nights.
