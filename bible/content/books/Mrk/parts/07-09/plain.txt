## Washed hands and the tradition of the elders
7:1 The Pharisees came together where Jesus was, along with some of the scribes who had arrived from Jerusalem.
7:2 They had noticed some of his disciples eating their bread with common hands, which means unwashed hands.
7:3 (The Pharisees, and all the Jews, do not eat unless they wash their hands with the fist. This is how they keep the tradition of the elders.
7:4 After the market, they do not eat until they have immersed themselves. They also received many other things to keep: the immersing of cups, jugs and copper pots, and dining couches.)
7:5 So the Pharisees and the scribes put a question to him: "Why don't your disciples walk in step with the tradition of the elders? They eat their bread with common hands!"
7:6 He said to them, "Isaiah hit the mark when he prophesied about you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people gives me honor with their lips, / but their heart keeps far away from me.
7:7 Their worship of me is empty; / the teachings they teach are human commands.'
7:8 You drop God's command and cling to a tradition made by humans."
7:9 He went on: "How neatly you push aside God's command, so you can keep your own tradition!
7:10 For Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'Anyone who speaks evil of father or mother must be put to death.'
7:11 But you teach this: suppose a man says to his father or mother, 'Whatever support you could have had from me is Korban' (that is, an offering).
7:12 From then on you do not allow him to do a thing for his father or mother.
7:13 In this way you cancel God's word with the tradition you pass down. And you do plenty of other things of the same kind."
## What makes a person unclean
7:14 He called the crowd over again and told them, "Hear me, all of you, and take this in.
7:15 Nothing that enters a person from outside has the power to make him unclean. It is what leaves a person that makes a person unclean."
7:16 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
7:17 He went into a house, away from the crowd, and his disciples questioned him about the parable.
7:18 He said to them, "So you too fail to understand? Don't you see? Everything that enters a person from outside has no power to make him unclean.
7:19 It does not go into his heart. It goes into his belly, and then out into the latrine." (With this he made all foods clean.)
7:20 He went on: "What leaves a person, that is the thing that makes a person unclean.
7:21 For it is from inside, from people's hearts, that the evil plans come out: acts of sexual immorality, thefts, murders,
7:22 adulteries, acts of greed, wicked acts, deceit, shameless indecency, an evil eye, slander, arrogance, senselessness.
7:23 Every one of these evils starts inside and comes out, and makes a person unclean."
## The Syrophoenician woman
7:24 From there Jesus set out and went away into the territory of Tyre. He went into a house and wanted no one to find out he was there. But he could not escape notice.
7:25 But at once a woman heard about him. Her young daughter had an unclean spirit. She came and threw herself down at his feet.
7:26 She was a Greek woman, by birth a Syrophoenician, and she kept asking Jesus to throw the demon out of her daughter.
7:27 He said to her, "Allow the children to eat their fill first. For it is not a fine thing to take the bread meant for the children and throw it to the little dogs."
7:28 But she answered him, "Lord, the little dogs under the table eat from the children's scraps too."
7:29 He said to her, "For this word of yours, go. The demon has gone out of your daughter."
7:30 She went away to her house and found the child laid out on the bed. The demon had gone out.
## A deaf man hears
7:31 He went out again from the territory of Tyre and came by way of Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, in the middle of the Decapolis territory.
7:32 Some people brought him a deaf man who struggled to speak. They pleaded with Jesus to put his hand on him.
7:33 Jesus drew him away from the crowd, to be with him alone. He stuck his fingers into the man's ears, spat, and touched his tongue.
7:34 He raised his eyes toward heaven, let out a groan and said to him, "Ephphatha!" That means, "Open up!"
7:35 His hearing opened up, the bond that held his tongue came untied, and he talked normally.
7:36 He gave them strict orders not to say anything to anyone. But the more he ordered them, the more widely they proclaimed it.
7:37 Their astonishment knew no bounds. "All that he has done, he has done well," they said. "He makes the deaf able to hear and the speechless able to speak."
## Four thousand fed
8:1 In those days a big crowd was with him again, and they had nothing to eat. Jesus summoned his disciples and said to them,
8:2 "My heart goes out to this crowd. They have been staying with me three days now, and they have nothing left to eat.
8:3 If I send them off home without food, they will collapse on the road. Some of them have traveled from far off."
8:4 His disciples answered him, "Where could anyone get bread to fill up these people, here in the wilderness?"
8:5 He asked them, "How many loaves have you got?" "Seven," they answered.
8:6 He gave orders for the crowd to recline on the ground. Then he picked up the seven loaves, gave thanks and broke them. He kept handing them to his disciples to set in front of the people, and they set them in front of the crowd.
8:7 They also had a few little fish. He said the blessing over them and told them to set these in front of the people as well.
8:8 They ate until they were full, and they picked up seven big baskets of leftover pieces.
8:9 The people numbered about four thousand. And he sent them off.
8:10 Right away he stepped into the boat with his disciples and came to the district of Dalmanutha.
## The demand for a sign
8:11 Out came the Pharisees, and they started to dispute with him. They wanted him to give them a sign from heaven, putting him to the test.
8:12 He let out a deep groan from his spirit and said, "Why does this generation go hunting for a sign? Amen, I tell you: this generation will get no sign at all."
8:13 Leaving them behind, he climbed aboard again and went away to the far shore.
## The yeast of the Pharisees and of Herod
8:14 They had forgotten to take any loaves along. Except for one loaf, they had nothing with them in the boat.
8:15 He kept warning them: "Look out! Be on your guard against the yeast of the Pharisees and the yeast of Herod."
8:16 They began talking it over with one another, saying that they had no loaves.
8:17 He knew what they were saying and asked them, "Why are you arguing that you have no loaves? Haven't you caught on yet, or understood? Have your hearts turned hard?
8:18 With eyes, you don't see? With ears, you don't hear? And don't you recall
8:19 the five loaves I broke for the five thousand? How many baskets full of pieces did you pick up?" "Twelve," they said to him.
8:20 "And the seven for the four thousand: how many big baskets full of pieces did you pick up?" They said to him, "Seven."
8:21 He kept saying to them, "Don't you understand yet?"
## A blind man at Bethsaida
8:22 They came to Bethsaida. People brought him a blind man, and they pleaded with Jesus to touch him.
8:23 Jesus took hold of the blind man's hand and brought him out past the edge of the village. He spat into the man's eyes, placed his hands on him and asked, "Is there anything you can see?"
8:24 He looked up and said, "I see the people! I can see them as trees, walking around."
8:25 Next he placed his hands on the man's eyes again. The man looked intently. He was restored, and now he could make out everything plainly, even at a distance.
8:26 He sent him off to his house with the words, "Do not so much as set foot in the village."
## Peter's confession
8:27 Jesus went out with his disciples to the villages of Caesarea Philippi. On the road he put a question to his disciples: "Who are people saying I am?"
8:28 They answered, "John the Baptist. Others say Elijah. Still others say one of the prophets."
8:29 And he asked them, "But you yourselves, who do you say I am?" Peter gave the answer: "You are the Messiah."
8:30 Then he rebuked them, telling them to speak to no one about him.
## The way of the cross
8:31 Then he began to teach them that the Son of Man had to suffer much. He had to be thrown out by the elders, the chief priests and the scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise.
8:32 He was speaking the message openly. Peter pulled him aside and started to rebuke him.
8:33 He turned around, looked at his disciples and rebuked Peter. "Go, get behind me, Satan!" he said. "Your thoughts are not on God's things but on human things."
8:34 He called the crowd over, along with his disciples, and said to them, "Anyone who wishes to come behind me must say no to himself, pick up his cross and follow me.
8:35 For anyone set on saving his life will lose it. But anyone who loses his life because of me and the good news will save it.
8:36 What good does it do a person to win the whole world and pay for it with his life?
8:37 For what could a person hand over to buy his life back?
8:38 Suppose anyone is ashamed of me and my words in this generation, adulterous and sinful as it is. Then the Son of Man will be ashamed of that person too, when he comes in the glory of his Father, along with the holy angels."
9:1 He also told them, "Amen, I tell you: among those standing here are some who will never taste death before they see the kingdom of God arrived in power."
## The transfiguration
9:2 Six days later Jesus took Peter, James and John along, and brought them up onto a high mountain, away from everyone, just them. Before their eyes he was transformed,
9:3 and his clothes turned glistening and extremely white, whiter than any cloth-bleacher on earth could make them.
9:4 Elijah, along with Moses, appeared to them, and they were in conversation with Jesus.
9:5 Peter responded, "Rabbi, it's a fine thing that we are here! Let's put up three booths, one for you, one for Moses and one for Elijah."
9:6 He did not know what answer to give, because fear had overwhelmed them.
9:7 A cloud came, casting its shadow over them, and a voice came out of the cloud: "This is my dearly loved Son. Listen to him!"
9:8 All at once they looked all around, and there was nobody with them anymore, only Jesus.
## Elijah has come
9:9 On their way down the mountain he gave them an order: they were not to report what they had seen to anyone, not until the Son of Man had risen from among the dead.
9:10 They held on to that saying, and argued with one another about what "to rise from among the dead" meant.
9:11 They put a question to him: "Why do the scribes say Elijah has to come first?"
9:12 He told them, "Yes, Elijah comes first and sets everything right again. Then how is it that Scripture says of the Son of Man that he must suffer much and be counted as nothing?
9:13 But I tell you, Elijah has in fact come, and they treated him any way they pleased, as the Scriptures say of him."
## A boy set free
9:14 They rejoined the other disciples and found a big crowd surrounding them, with scribes disputing with them.
9:15 The moment they saw him, the whole crowd was stunned. They came running up to him and welcomed him.
9:16 He asked them, "What is this dispute you are having with them?"
9:17 One of the crowd answered him, "Teacher, I have brought my son to you. A speechless spirit has hold of him.
9:18 Anywhere it catches hold of him, it dashes him to the ground. He foams, grits his teeth and goes stiff. I asked your disciples to throw it out, but they were not strong enough."
9:19 He answered them, "Oh, faithless generation! How long must I be with you? How long must I endure you? Bring him to me."
9:20 They brought him to Jesus. The moment the spirit saw him, it shook the boy violently. He dropped to the ground and kept rolling over, foaming.
9:21 Jesus questioned the boy's father: "How much time has passed since this came on him?" "Ever since he was little," he said.
9:22 "Many times it has hurled him into fire and into water, trying to destroy him. If there is anything you are able to do, let your heart go out to us, and help us!"
9:23 Jesus said to him, "'If you are able'? For the one who believes, anything is possible."
9:24 Right away the boy's father shouted, "I believe! Help me with my unbelief!"
9:25 Jesus saw a crowd quickly gathering. He rebuked the unclean spirit: "Speechless and deaf spirit, it is I who command you. Get out of him, and do not ever go back into him."
9:26 With a scream and many violent shakings it came out. The boy lay there like a dead body, so that most people were saying, "He has died."
9:27 Jesus, though, grasped his hand and raised him, and he rose up.
9:28 When he went into a house, his disciples asked him away from the others, "Why weren't we able to throw it out?"
9:29 He told them, "Nothing can make this kind come out, except prayer."
## He speaks again of his death
9:30 They set out from there and traveled on through Galilee. He did not want anyone to find out,
9:31 for he was teaching his disciples. He was telling them, "The Son of Man is being handed over into human hands. They will put him to death, and when he has been put to death, after three days he will rise."
9:32 The saying made no sense to them, and they were too frightened to question him.
## Who ranks highest
9:33 They arrived at Capernaum. Once he was indoors, he asked them, "What were you talking over on the road?"
9:34 They said nothing. On the road they had been debating with each other which of them ranked highest.
9:35 He sat down and called the Twelve over. "If anyone wants to be first," he told them, "he will be last of everyone and everyone's servant."
9:36 He took a little child and had the child stand in the middle of them. Then, with his arms around the child, he said to them,
9:37 "Anyone who takes in one child like this for my name's sake is taking me in. And anyone who takes me in is taking in not me, but the one who sent me."
## The man who was not one of us
9:38 "Teacher," John said to him, "we saw a man throwing out demons in your name. We kept trying to hinder him, since he was no follower of ours."
9:39 Jesus said, "Stop hindering him. Nobody who does an act of power in my name will be able, soon after, to speak evil of me.
9:40 Anyone who is not against us is on our side.
9:41 Whoever gives you a cup of water to drink on the grounds that you are Christ's own, amen, I tell you, he will not lose his reward at all.
## Snares
9:42 "And if anyone trips up one of these little ones who believe in me, he would be better off if a donkey's millstone were put around his neck and he were thrown into the sea.
9:43 If your hand trips you up, cut it off. Better to go into life maimed than to keep two hands and go off into Gehenna, into the fire that cannot be put out.
9:44 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
9:45 If your foot trips you up, cut it off. Better to go into life limping than to keep two feet and be thrown into Gehenna.
9:46 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
9:47 If your eye trips you up, throw it out. Better to go into the kingdom of God with one eye than to keep two eyes and be thrown into Gehenna,
9:48 where their worm never dies and the fire is never put out.
## Salted with fire
9:49 For everyone will be salted with fire.
9:50 Salt is a good thing. But if the salt becomes saltless, what will you season it with? Keep salt in yourselves, and keep peace with one another."
