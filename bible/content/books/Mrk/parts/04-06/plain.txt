## The sower
4:1 Once again he began teaching by the sea. A huge crowd gathered around him, so he climbed into a boat on the sea and sat down, while the whole crowd stayed on the land, facing the sea.
4:2 He taught them many things in parables. As he taught, he told them,
4:3 "Listen! Look! There was a sower who went out to scatter seed.
4:4 While he was scattering it, some seed landed beside the road. The birds came along and gobbled it up.
4:5 Other seed landed on the rocky patch, where there was not much earth. It came up right away, since the earth was not deep.
4:6 When the sun came up, it burned the seed, and the seed dried up because it had no root.
4:7 Other seed landed in the thornbushes. The thornbushes came up and choked it, and it gave no crop.
4:8 And other seeds landed on the good earth. They came up and grew and went on giving a crop, bearing one thirty times as much, another sixty, another a hundred."
4:9 Then he said, "Let anyone who has ears to hear listen."
## Why parables
4:10 When he was by himself, the people around him, together with the Twelve, questioned him about the parables.
4:11 He told them, "You have been given the mystery of the kingdom of God. But to those outside, everything comes in parables,
4:12 so that 'they may look and look, but not see, / and listen and listen, but not understand, / in case they turn back and are forgiven.'"
## The sower explained
4:13 Then he said to them, "You don't know what this parable means? Then how will you come to know any of the parables?
4:14 The sower sows the message.
4:15 These are the ones beside the road where the message is sown: when they hear, Satan comes right away and carries off the message sown in them.
4:16 In the same way, the ones sown on the rocky patches are those who hear the message and right away take it in gladly.
4:17 But they have no root in themselves. They last only for a while. Then when hardship or persecution comes because of the message, right away they trip and fall.
4:18 Then there are the ones sown in the thornbushes. These have heard the message,
4:19 but the worries of the age, the trickery of wealth and cravings for everything else creep in and choke the message, and it bears no fruit.
4:20 And the ones sown on the good earth are those who hear the message, welcome it and bear fruit, one thirty times as much, another sixty, another a hundred."
## A lamp and a measure
4:21 He said to them, "Does the lamp come in to be put under the grain basket or under the bed? Doesn't it come to be put on the lampstand?
4:22 There is nothing hidden that is not meant to be shown, and nothing became secret except to come out into the open.
4:23 If someone has ears to hear, that person should listen."
4:24 He also said to them, "Watch what you are hearing. The measure you use is the one that will be used to measure out to you, and you will be given more besides.
4:25 For anyone who has something will be given more. But anyone who does not have will lose even what he has."
## Seed that grows by itself
4:26 He said, "This is how it is with the kingdom of God: it is as if someone threw seed on the ground.
4:27 He goes to sleep and gets up, night and day, and the seed sprouts up and gets taller, in a way he himself does not understand.
4:28 The earth bears its crop on its own: first a green blade, then a head of grain, then the head full of grain.
4:29 When the crop is ready, he sends in the sickle right away, for harvest time is here."
## A mustard seed
4:30 He said, "How should we picture the kingdom of God? Or what parable should we put it in?
4:31 It is like a seed of mustard. When it is sown on the ground, it is the tiniest of all the seeds on the earth.
4:32 But once it is sown, it comes up and grows bigger than all the vegetable plants. It puts out big branches, and the birds of the sky can settle under its shade."
4:33 With many parables like these he spoke the message to them, in the measure they were able to hear.
4:34 Without a parable he would not speak to them. Privately, though, he explained everything to his own disciples.
## Wind and sea obey him
4:35 That same day, as evening came on, he told them, "Let's cross over to the far shore."
4:36 So they sent the crowd off and took him with them, the way he was, in the boat. Some other boats came along with him too.
4:37 Then a great gale of wind blew up. The waves kept pounding into the boat, and it was already filling up.
4:38 He himself lay in the stern on the cushion, asleep. They woke him up. "Teacher," they said to him, "doesn't it matter to you that we are going down?"
4:39 He woke up, spoke sternly to the wind and said to the sea, "Quiet! Be muzzled!" The wind dropped, and a great calm came.
4:40 He said to them, "Why are you timid? You still don't have faith?"
4:41 A great fear gripped them, and they kept saying to one another, "Who can this be? The wind and the sea both do what he says!"
## The man with the legion
5:1 They came to the far shore of the sea, into the land of the Gerasenes.
5:2 The moment Jesus stepped out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit met him, coming from the tombs.
5:3 He made his home among the tombs. No one could tie him down any longer, even with a chain.
5:4 Many times people had put shackles and chains on him, but he had ripped the chains apart and smashed the shackles. Nobody was strong enough to tame him.
5:5 Night after night and day after day he was out in the tombs and the hills, shrieking and gashing himself with stones.
5:6 When he caught sight of Jesus a long way off, he ran and bowed down before him.
5:7 He let out a loud shriek: "What is there between me and you, Jesus, Son of God Most High? I put you under oath by God: do not torment me!"
5:8 For Jesus had been telling him, "Unclean spirit, come out of the man!"
5:9 Jesus asked him, "What is your name?" He told him, "Legion is my name, because there are a lot of us."
5:10 And he begged Jesus over and over not to banish them from the region.
5:11 A big herd of pigs happened to be grazing there, near the hillside.
5:12 The spirits begged him, "Send us into the pigs, so that we can go into them."
5:13 He let them. The unclean spirits came out and went off into the pigs, and the herd, about two thousand of them, charged down the cliff into the sea and drowned in the sea.
5:14 The herdsmen ran away and spread the news in the town and around the farms. And people came out to find out what had taken place.
5:15 They came to Jesus and looked at the man who had demons. He was sitting there with clothes on, his mind sound, the same man who had held the legion. And fear took hold of them.
5:16 The eyewitnesses gave them the whole account of what had happened to the man who had demons, and about the pigs.
5:17 So they started urging Jesus to move away from their territory.
5:18 While Jesus was stepping into the boat, the man who had had demons kept pleading to be allowed to be with him.
5:19 Jesus did not allow it. Instead he told him, "Go back to your home, to your own people. Report to them all that the Lord has done for you, and the mercy he has shown you."
5:20 So he went away and started announcing throughout the Decapolis what Jesus had done for him. And they were all astonished.
## Jairus' daughter and a sick woman
5:21 Jesus crossed back over in the boat to the far shore, and a large crowd gathered around him there by the sea.
5:22 One of the synagogue leaders came, a man named Jairus. Seeing Jesus, he dropped down at his feet
5:23 and kept pleading with him. "My little daughter is near the end," he said. "Come and place your hands on her, so that she may be saved and live."
5:24 Jesus went off with him. A large crowd kept following him and pressing in on him.
5:25 Among them was a woman who had had a flow of blood twelve years long.
5:26 Many doctors had put her through a great deal, and she had spent every penny she owned. It had done her no good at all; in fact she had only gotten worse.
5:27 She had heard about Jesus. She came through the crowd from behind and touched his cloak.
5:28 For she kept saying, "If I touch even just his clothes, I will be saved."
5:29 Right away the source of her bleeding dried up. She knew from her body that she had been cured of her affliction.
5:30 Right away Jesus was aware in himself that power had left him. Turning around among the people, he said, "Who touched my clothes?"
5:31 "You can see how the crowd is crushing in on you," his disciples said to him, "and you ask, 'Who touched me?'"
5:32 He kept glancing all around to see the woman who had done this.
5:33 The woman knew what had happened to her. Frightened and shaking, she came and fell down in front of him, and she told him all the truth.
5:34 He said to her, "Daughter, your faith has saved you. Go in peace, and be well, rid of your affliction."
5:35 He was still talking when people arrived from the synagogue leader's house. "Your daughter has died," they said. "Why keep bothering the teacher?"
5:36 But Jesus paid no attention to what was said. He told the synagogue leader, "Stop being afraid. Just keep believing."
5:37 He would not let anyone but Peter, James, and John the brother of James come along with him.
5:38 When they reached the synagogue leader's house, Jesus saw an uproar: people crying and wailing a great deal.
5:39 Going inside, he asked them, "What is all this uproar and weeping? The child has not died. She is asleep."
5:40 They kept laughing at him. But he drove them all out. Then, taking the child's father and mother and his own companions, he went in to where the child lay.
5:41 He took hold of the child's hand and said to her, "Talitha koum," which means, "Little girl, I tell you, get up."
5:42 Right away the little girl stood up and started walking around. She was twelve. At once they were beside themselves with amazement.
5:43 He warned them firmly that no one was to find out about this, and he said she should be given something to eat.
## Rejected at home
6:1 From there he went on to his hometown, and his disciples came along behind him.
6:2 On the Sabbath he started teaching in the synagogue. Many of those listening were stunned. "Where does he get these things from?" they said. "And what wisdom is this that was given to him? And such acts of power coming about through his hands!
6:3 This is the builder, isn't it, the son of Mary, and the brother of James, Joses, Judas and Simon? And his sisters are here among us, aren't they?" So they were tripped up by him.
6:4 Jesus told them, "A prophet gets honor everywhere except in his hometown, with his relatives, and in his own family."
6:5 He was not able to do any act of power there. He only placed his hands on a handful of sick people and healed them.
6:6 Their unbelief astonished him. Then he went around the villages nearby, teaching.
## The Twelve sent out
6:7 He summoned the Twelve and started sending them out in pairs, and he put the unclean spirits under their authority.
6:8 His orders were that they should carry nothing on the road but a staff. No bread, no bag, no copper coins in their belts.
6:9 They could wear sandals, but they were not to put on a second tunic.
6:10 He told them, "Wherever you go into a house, stay there until you move on from there.
6:11 If a place does not receive you or hear you, then as you go out from there, shake off the dust beneath your feet as evidence to them."
6:12 Then they set out and announced that people must repent.
6:13 They threw out many demons. Many of the sick they anointed with oil and healed.
## The death of John
6:14 Word reached King Herod, since Jesus' name was now widely known. People were saying, "John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead. That is why the powers work in him."
6:15 Others were saying, "He is Elijah." Still others said, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets."
6:16 Herod, though, on hearing it, kept saying, "The John I beheaded: he has been raised!"
6:17 For Herod himself had sent men who arrested John and put him in chains in prison, on account of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, because Herod had married her.
6:18 For John had kept telling Herod, "The law does not allow you to have your brother's wife."
6:19 So Herodias had it in for John. She wanted him dead, but she could not manage it,
6:20 because Herod was afraid of John. He knew John was a righteous and holy man, so he kept him from harm. Each time he heard John, Herod was left deeply confused, and still he enjoyed listening to him.
6:21 Then the right day came along. Herod held a dinner on his birthday for his top officials and army commanders and for the first men of Galilee.
6:22 Herodias' own daughter entered and danced, and she delighted Herod and the guests at his table. "Whatever you like, ask me for it," the king told the girl, "and I will give it to you."
6:23 He swore an oath to her: "Anything you ask me for, I will give you, even if it is half my kingdom."
6:24 She stepped out and asked her mother, "What should I request?" "The head of John the Baptizer," her mother said.
6:25 Straight away she rushed back in to the king with her request. "My wish is that you give me, this minute, on a platter, the head of John the Baptist," she said.
6:26 The king was grieved to the core. Yet with his oaths and his table guests in mind, he was unwilling to turn her down.
6:27 Right away the king dispatched an executioner and commanded him to bring back John's head. Off he went and cut off John's head in the prison,
6:28 carried the head in on a platter and handed it over to the girl. And the girl handed it over to her mother.
6:29 John's disciples heard about it and came. They carried away his corpse and placed it in a tomb.
## Bread for five thousand
6:30 The apostles came back together with Jesus and gave him a report on everything: all they had done and all they had taught.
6:31 He said to them, "Come along, just you, off by yourselves to a lonely spot, and rest for a little while." The comings and goings were so many that they had no spare moment even to eat.
6:32 They set off privately in the boat for a lonely spot.
6:33 But they were seen going, and many people knew who they were. From all the towns people raced together on foot to the place, and arrived before them.
6:34 As he stepped out of the boat he saw a huge crowd, and his heart went out to them, for they were like sheep that have no shepherd. So he set about teaching them many things.
6:35 When the hour was already late, his disciples approached him. "There is nothing out here," they said, "and the hour is already late.
6:36 Dismiss them, so they can go off to the farms and villages all around and buy themselves food."
6:37 He answered them, "Feed them yourselves." "Should we go and spend two hundred denarii on bread," they asked him, "and feed it to them?"
6:38 "How many loaves have you got?" he asked them. "Go and look." They checked and told him, "Five. And two fish."
6:39 He told them to have everyone recline, party by party, on the green grass.
6:40 So they reclined in groups like garden beds, in hundreds and in fifties.
6:41 He took the five loaves and the two fish, raised his eyes toward heaven and said the blessing. He broke the loaves and kept handing them to his disciples to set in front of the people. The two fish, too, he shared out among them all.
6:42 Everyone ate until they were full.
6:43 And they picked up twelve full baskets of leftover pieces, and some of the fish.
6:44 The men who ate the loaves numbered five thousand.
## Walking on the sea
6:45 Right away he made his disciples get into the boat and cross ahead to the far shore, toward Bethsaida, while he himself sent the crowd away.
6:46 After saying goodbye to them, he went off to the mountain to pray.
6:47 Evening fell. The boat was in the middle of the sea, while he was on land by himself.
6:48 He saw them tormented as they rowed, since they had a head wind. Around the night's fourth watch, he came toward them, walking on the sea. He was intending to go past them.
6:49 Seeing him walk on the sea, they took him for a phantom and screamed.
6:50 Every one of them had seen him, and they were in a panic. But right away he spoke with them. "Courage!" he said to them. "I am. Stop being afraid."
6:51 He went up to them, into the boat, and the wind dropped. Inwardly they were completely beside themselves,
6:52 since they had not grasped what the loaves meant. Instead, their hearts had turned hard.
## Healing at Gennesaret
6:53 They made the crossing to land, came to Gennesaret and tied up there.
6:54 They had no sooner left the boat than people knew who he was.
6:55 They hurried all over that region and began carrying the sick around on mats, to any place they heard he was.
6:56 Whatever village or town or farm he went into, people set the sick down in the marketplaces. They pleaded with him just to let them touch even the tassel of his cloak. And everyone who touched him was saved.
