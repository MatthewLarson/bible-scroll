## John the Baptist sends a question
11:1 When Jesus finished giving orders to his twelve disciples, he left that place to teach and to preach in their towns.
11:2 News of the Messiah's works reached John in prison, and he sent word through his disciples
11:3 and asked him, "Are you the Coming One, or should we wait for someone else?"
11:4 Jesus answered them, "Go and give John a report of what you are hearing and seeing.
11:5 The blind see again and the lame walk. People with skin disease are made clean and the deaf hear. The dead are raised, and the poor are told the good news.
11:6 And blessed is anyone who is not tripped up because of me."
## What Jesus says about John
11:7 While they were on their way, Jesus began talking to the crowds about John. "When you went out into the wilderness, what did you go to gaze at? A reed that the wind shakes?
11:8 No? So what was it you went out to see? A man wrapped in soft garments? Look, the people who wear soft garments are in the houses of kings.
11:9 What did you go out for, then? To see a prophet? Yes, I tell you, you saw one who is even more than a prophet.
11:10 It is about him that it is written: 'Look, I am sending my messenger before your face. He will make your road ready ahead of you.'
11:11 Amen, I tell you, no one born of a woman has ever been raised up who is greater than John the Baptist. But the smallest one in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he is.
11:12 Ever since John the Baptist's time, right up to now, the kingdom of heaven is under violent attack, and violent people are grabbing at it.
11:13 For all the prophets, and the Law too, prophesied up until John.
11:14 And if you are ready to receive it, John himself is Elijah, the one who was going to come.
11:15 Let anyone who has ears listen.
11:16 "What shall I liken this generation to? It is like little children who sit in the town squares and shout to the others,
11:17 'We piped a tune for you, but you would not dance. We wailed a funeral song, but you would not beat your chests.'
11:18 John came, not eating and not drinking, and people say, 'There's a demon in him.'
11:19 The Son of Man came, eating and drinking, and they say, 'Look at the man! He overeats and he is a heavy wine drinker, a friend to tax collectors and sinners!' But wisdom is proved right by the things she does."
## Woe to the towns that did not repent
11:20 Then he started to reproach the towns where most of his acts of power had happened, because they had not repented.
11:21 "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! Suppose the acts of power that happened in you had happened in Tyre and Sidon. They would have repented long ago, dressed in sackcloth and sitting in ashes.
11:22 But I tell you this: on judgment day Tyre and Sidon will find it easier to bear than you will.
11:23 And you, Capernaum: will you be raised high, as far as heaven? You will go down as far as Hades! If the acts of power that happened in you had happened in Sodom, it would have stood until this very day.
11:24 But I tell you this: on judgment day the land of Sodom will find it easier to bear than you will."
## Come to me and rest
11:25 At that time Jesus spoke up and said, "Father, Lord of heaven and earth, I openly thank you. You hid these things from wise and clever people, and you uncovered them for little children.
11:26 Yes, Father, because that is what seemed good in your sight.
11:27 "Everything has been handed over to me by my Father. Only the Father truly knows the Son. And only the Son truly knows the Father, along with anyone the Son wants to show him to.
11:28 "Come to me, all of you who are worn out and loaded down, and I myself will give you rest.
11:29 Lift my yoke onto yourselves and learn from me, because I am gentle and my heart is humble. And you will find rest for your souls.
11:30 For my yoke is kind to wear, and the load I give is light."
## Lord of the Sabbath
12:1 Around that time, on a Sabbath, Jesus was walking through fields of grain. His disciples got hungry, so they started pulling off heads of grain and eating them.
12:2 The Pharisees saw it and told him, "See here! Your disciples are doing something that is not allowed on a Sabbath."
12:3 He replied, "Surely you have read about David, when he and his men were hungry. What did he do?
12:4 He went into God's house, and they ate the Presence Bread. He was not allowed to eat it, and neither were the men with him. Only the priests were.
12:5 Or have you not read in the Law that every Sabbath the priests in the temple treat the Sabbath as common, and yet they are blameless?
12:6 But I tell you, here is something greater than the temple.
12:7 You would not have condemned people who are blameless if you had understood what this means: 'Mercy is what I want, not sacrifice.'
12:8 For the Son of Man is Lord over the Sabbath."
## A shriveled hand on the Sabbath
12:9 He moved on from there and came into their synagogue.
12:10 One of the men there had a withered hand. Looking for a way to accuse Jesus, they asked him, "Is it allowed to heal on the Sabbath?"
12:11 He answered, "Suppose one of you owns just one sheep, and it falls into a ditch on the Sabbath. Won't he grab it and pull it out?
12:12 And a human being is worth so much more than a sheep! So doing good on the Sabbath is allowed."
12:13 Then he said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and it became healthy again, like his other hand.
12:14 The Pharisees left and met together to plan against him, looking for a way to destroy him.
## God's chosen servant
12:15 Jesus learned of it and left that place. Crowds in great numbers followed him, and he healed every one of them.
12:16 He gave them strict orders not to make him public.
12:17 This happened so that what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet would be fulfilled:
12:18 "Look: my servant, whom I chose, / my beloved, in whom my soul takes delight. / I will place my Spirit on him, / and he will announce justice to the nations.
12:19 He will not argue, and he will not shout. / No one will hear his voice in the open streets.
12:20 He will not snap a crushed reed, / and he will not put out a smoking lampwick, / until he leads justice on to victory.
12:21 And the nations will hope in his name."
## Jesus and Beelzebul
12:22 Then someone brought him a man with a demon, who was blind and could not speak. Jesus healed him, and the mute man began to speak and to see.
12:23 The crowds were all astonished. They kept saying, "This man can't be the Son of David, can he?"
12:24 When the Pharisees heard this, they said, "This man throws out the demons only with the help of Beelzebul, the ruler of the demons."
12:25 But he knew what they were thinking, and he said to them, "Any kingdom split into sides fighting each other is turned into ruins. And any town or family split against itself cannot last.
12:26 And if Satan throws Satan out, he has split against himself. Then how can his kingdom last?
12:27 And if I throw demons out with Beelzebul's help, with whose help do your own sons throw them out? That is why they themselves will be your judges.
12:28 But if it is God's Spirit by whom I throw out demons, then God's kingdom has already reached you.
12:29 Or think about this: how could anyone break into a strong man's house and seize his goods, without first tying the strong man up? Only then will he rob the house.
12:30 Anyone who is not on my side is against me. Anyone who does not help me gather is scattering.
12:31 So I tell you this: people will be forgiven for any sin and any blasphemy. But for blasphemy against the Spirit there will be no forgiveness.
12:32 If someone says something against the Son of Man, he will be forgiven. But if someone speaks against the Holy Spirit, he will not be forgiven, not in this age and not in the age that is coming.
## A tree is known by its fruit
12:33 "Either count the tree healthy and its fruit healthy, or count the tree rotten and its fruit rotten. You can tell a tree by its fruit.
12:34 You offspring of vipers! You are evil, so how can you say good things? What overflows from the heart is what the mouth speaks.
12:35 A good person takes good things out of a good storeroom. An evil person takes evil things out of an evil storeroom.
12:36 And I tell you: every idle word people say, they will have to answer for on judgment day.
12:37 It is by your words that you will be justified, and by your words that you will be condemned."
## The sign of Jonah
12:38 Then some scribes and Pharisees spoke up. "Teacher," they said, "we would like you to show us a sign."
12:39 He told them in reply, "A generation that is evil and adulterous keeps hunting for a sign. It will be given no sign except the sign of Jonah the prophet.
12:40 Jonah spent three days and three nights in the belly of the sea monster. In the same way the Son of Man will spend three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.
12:41 At the judgment the people of Nineveh will stand up alongside this generation and condemn it, because when Jonah preached they repented. And look, here is something greater than Jonah.
12:42 At the judgment the queen from the south will be raised alongside this generation and condemn it, because she came from the far edges of the earth to hear how wise Solomon was. And look, here is something greater than Solomon.
12:43 "When the unclean spirit leaves a person, it roams through dry country, searching for a place to rest, and it finds none.
12:44 Then it says, 'I'll go back to my house, the one I came out of.' It arrives and finds the place standing empty, swept and tidied.
12:45 So it goes off and gathers seven more spirits, worse than itself, and they move in and make their home there. In the end that person is worse off than at the beginning. It will be like that for this evil generation, too."
## Jesus' true family
12:46 Jesus was still talking to the crowds when his mother and his brothers arrived and stood outside. They were trying to get a word with him.
12:47 Someone said to him, "Your mother and your brothers have been standing outside, wanting to speak with you."
12:48 Jesus replied to the man who had told him, "Who is my mother? And who are my brothers?"
12:49 He stretched out his hand toward his disciples and said, "Look! My mother and my brothers!
12:50 For anyone who does what my Father in heaven wants, that person is my brother and my sister and my mother."
## The sower
13:1 On that day Jesus left the house and sat down beside the sea.
13:2 So many people crowded around him that he climbed into a boat and sat down there. The whole crowd stood along the beach.
13:3 He spoke to them about many things in parables. "Look!" he said. "There was a sower who went out to scatter seed.
13:4 While he was scattering it, some seeds landed beside the road. Birds came along and gobbled them up.
13:5 Other seeds landed on rocky patches where there was not much earth. They came up right away, since the earth was not deep.
13:6 When the sun came up, it burned them, and they dried up because they had no roots.
13:7 Other seeds landed in thornbushes. The thornbushes came up and choked them.
13:8 And other seeds landed on good earth and gave a crop. Some gave a hundred times as much, some sixty, some thirty.
13:9 Let anyone who has ears listen."
## Why Jesus speaks in parables
13:10 His disciples came to him. "Why do you use parables when you talk to them?" they asked.
13:11 He answered, "You have been given the gift of knowing the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven. They have not been given it.
13:12 For anyone who has something will be given more, more than enough. But anyone who does not have will lose even what he has.
13:13 This is the reason I use parables with them: they look, but they do not see; they listen, but they do not hear, and they do not understand.
13:14 For them, Isaiah's prophecy is coming true in full: 'You will listen and listen, but never understand. / You will look and look, but never see.
13:15 For this people's heart has become sluggish. / They hardly hear with their ears, / and they have shut their eyes tight, / so that they never see with their eyes / or hear with their ears / or understand with their heart / and turn, and I heal them.'
13:16 But how blessed your eyes are, because they see! And your ears, because they hear!
13:17 For, amen, I tell you, many prophets and righteous people wanted badly to see what you are seeing, and they did not see it. They wanted to hear what you are hearing, and they did not hear it.
## The sower explained
13:18 "Now you, listen to the parable of the man who sowed.
13:19 Someone hears the kingdom's message but does not understand it. Then the evil one comes and grabs what was planted in that person's heart. That is the one who was sown beside the road.
13:20 The one sown on the rocky patches is the person who hears the message and right away takes it in gladly.
13:21 But he has no root in himself. He lasts only for a while. When hardship or persecution comes because of the message, right away he trips and falls.
13:22 The one sown in the thornbushes is the person who hears the message, but the worries of the age and the trickery of wealth choke the message, and it bears no fruit.
13:23 And the one sown on the good earth is the person who hears the message and understands it. That person does bear fruit and yields a crop, one a hundred times as much, another sixty, another thirty."
## Weeds among the wheat
13:24 He set another parable in front of them: "The kingdom of heaven is like this. A man sowed good seed in his field.
13:25 But while people were sleeping, his enemy came, sowed weeds over the top of the wheat, and went off.
13:26 When the green blades sprang up and put out heads of grain, the weeds showed up as well.
13:27 The slaves of the head of the household came to him. 'Master,' they said, 'it was good seed you sowed in your field, wasn't it? So how does it have weeds?'
13:28 He told them, 'Some enemy did this.' The slaves asked him, 'Then would you like us to go out and collect them?'
13:29 'No,' he says. 'When you collect the weeds, you could tear out the wheat along with them.
13:30 Leave them both to grow side by side until harvest. When it is time to harvest, I will tell the harvest workers, "Collect the weeds first and tie them up in bundles for burning. But bring the wheat together into my barn."'"
## A mustard seed and yeast
13:31 He set another parable in front of them: "The kingdom of heaven is like a seed of mustard. A man took it and sowed it in his field.
13:32 Of all the seeds it is the tiniest, but once it is grown, it is bigger than the vegetable plants. It turns into a tree, and the birds of the sky come and settle in its branches."
13:33 He told them one more parable: "The kingdom of heaven is like yeast. A woman took it and hid it away in three measures of flour, until all of it had risen."
13:34 Jesus told the crowds all these things in parables. Without a parable he would tell them nothing.
13:35 This happened so that what was spoken through the prophet would be fulfilled: "I will open my mouth in parables. / I will bubble out things hidden since the beginning."
## The weeds explained
13:36 Then Jesus sent the crowds away and went indoors. His disciples came up to him. "Tell us plainly what the parable about the weeds in the field means," they said.
13:37 He replied, "The sower of the good seed is the Son of Man.
13:38 The field is the world. As for the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom, and the weeds are the sons of the evil one.
13:39 The enemy who sowed them is the devil. The harvest is the completion of the age, and the harvest workers are angels.
13:40 So in the same way that the weeds are collected and burned up in a fire, that is how it will be at the completion of the age.
13:41 The Son of Man will send out his angels. Out of his kingdom they will collect everything that trips people up and everyone who practices lawlessness.
13:42 They will throw them into the furnace of fire. There people will weep and grind their teeth.
13:43 Then the righteous will blaze out like the sun in their Father's kingdom. Let anyone who has ears listen.
## Treasure, a pearl and a net
13:44 "The kingdom of heaven is like a treasure buried in a field. A man came across it and hid it. He is so happy that he goes off, sells everything he owns, and buys that field.
13:45 "Or again, the kingdom of heaven is like a man who traded goods and was searching for beautiful pearls.
13:46 He found one pearl worth a great deal. So he went away and sold off everything he had, and he bought it.
13:47 "Or again, the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet. It was cast into the sea and pulled in some of every kind.
13:48 Once it was full, they hauled it up onto the beach. Then they sat down and sorted the good ones into containers and threw the rotten ones out.
13:49 It will be like that at the completion of the age. The angels will go out and set apart the evil people from among the righteous,
13:50 and they will throw them into the furnace of fire. There people will weep and grind their teeth.
## Things new and old
13:51 "Have you understood all this?" he asked. "Yes," they said to him.
13:52 He said to them, "So every scribe who has learned to be a disciple of the kingdom of heaven is like the head of a household who brings new things and old things out of his storeroom."
## Rejected at home
13:53 When Jesus finished these parables, he moved on from there.
13:54 He came to his own hometown. As he was teaching them in their synagogue, they were stunned. "Where does he get this wisdom from?" they said. "And these acts of power?
13:55 This is the builder's son, isn't it? His mother is the one called Mary, isn't she? And his brothers are James, Joseph, Simon and Judas, aren't they?
13:56 And his sisters are all here among us, aren't they? So where does he get all this?"
13:57 So they were tripped up by him. But Jesus told them, "A prophet gets honor everywhere except in his hometown and in his own family."
13:58 And because they did not believe, he did not do many acts of power there.
