# Hostile review: 2 John (2Jn)

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-10-29. Checked against `texts/orig/2Jn.json` (SBL, Byzantine, word list with edition marks), the writer's scratch folder `.scratch/nt/2Jn/` (sources-checked.md, drift-audit.md, saved Wikipedia wikitext, saved commentaries), the Plain of 1 John and of John/Mark, and pages I opened this session (New Advent Eusebius, Church History book 3, and the Didache page; Wikipedia "Antilegomena" and "Diotrephes"; Swete's Septuagint through `tools/lxx.py`). `node tools/build.js --check 2Jn` gives the one known error (`1-john` topic) and 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The translation is sound. I found no mistranslation, no wrong names or numbers, and the verse-by-verse Plain follows the Greek and the 1 John Plain closely (2 John 5 / 1 John 2:7, 2 John 7 / 1 John 4:2, 2 John 12 / 1 John 1:4 are aligned). The witness lists in variants.json match the saved Wikipedia list entry by entry (I compared the lines for 1:3, 1:7, 1:8, 1:9, 1:12 and 1:13 against it); the edition claims (NA28, SBL, Tyndale House, Westcott-Hort, Tregelles) match the marks in the word list. The NA28-over-SBL choice at 1:12 ("our joy") is permitted by brief-nt.md and brief-general.md and is honestly labeled. Note that at 1:8 NA28 and SBL do not differ (both `εἰργασάμεθα`, marks NnSW); the real split there is with Tyndale House and Tregelles. The weakness is sourcing. Several statements put in a named authority's mouth are not in the page the writer opened, one Eusebius "quotation" is a Wikipedia paraphrase presented as Eusebius, the canon summary overstates ("without any lower rank"), the claim that churches "still differ" on 1:10-11 has no church document behind it, one Greek claim about the Septuagint is wrong, and reader-facing prose names Wikipedia as its authority in a dozen places. None of these changes the translation. All can be fixed in content. No critical findings; six major; nine minor.

## Findings

### Major

**1. A quotation attributed to Eusebius is not Eusebius (book.json, section "2 John among the books of the Bible"; also 3Jn book.json section "3 John among the books").**
The text says Eusebius placed 2 and 3 John among the disputed books "though, as he says, 'well-known and acknowledged by most' (Church History 3.24.17; 3.25.3)". I opened Church History book 3 at New Advent (the translation the source key names): 3.25.3 reads "Among the disputed writings, which are nevertheless recognized by many, are extant the so-called epistle of James and that of Jude..." and "those that are called the second and third of John, whether they belong to the evangelist or to another person of the same name." The phrase "well-known and acknowledged by most" appears in the saved Wikipedia article on 3 John (`.scratch/nt/3Jn/wiki-3john.txt` line 65), as Wikipedia's own wording, not in the cited translation. 3.24.17 ("the other two are disputed") is quoted correctly.
Fix: quote the phrase New Advent gives ("recognized by many"), or drop the quotation marks and say only that Eusebius listed them among the disputed books that many nonetheless recognized.

**2. "Every other church today ... includes the letter without any lower rank" is contradicted by a source I opened (book.json "2 John among the books"; topics.json `canon`; 3Jn book.json).**
Wikipedia "Antilegomena" (opened): "Current Lutheran usage expands this questioning to also include 2 Peter, 2 John, and 3 John." The writer even links "Antilegomena" in book.json `links`. A Lutheran reader will see "without any lower rank" as wrong. Same paragraph: "the Assyrian Church of the East still does not count 2 and 3 John ... according to its 2020 catechism as Wikipedia reports it." That is a hedge STYLE rule 2 forbids, and the catechism was not opened (I tried the PDF at bethkokheh.assyrianchurch.org; the server returned HTTP 500, so I could not verify it either). The only evidence is a Wikipedia canon-table footnote.
Fix: say what the sources show (the Peshitta tradition omitted them; the Harklean of 616 added them) and either open the catechism and quote its list, or cut the "still" sentence. Replace "every other church ... without any lower rank" with a statement the Lutheran material allows, naming who treats them as fully canonical and noting the Lutheran category with a source.

**3. Views credited to named scholars that the opened source does not contain (chapters.json spec box; book.json `reading`; also 3Jn).**
(a) The spec box says "John Painter, as Wikipedia summarizes him, also links 2 John 9 with the hospitality theme of 3 John and identifies 2 John as the letter Diotrephes refused (3 John 1:9)", and book.json `reading` says "Painter argues that 2 John is the letter Diotrephes refused (3 John 9)". The saved Wikipedia text (3 John, line 29) cites Painter 374-375 only for Diotrephes excommunicating Gaius's kind and for "a previous letter ... suppressed by Diotrephes". It never says Painter identifies that letter with 2 John, and it says nothing about 2 John 9. Plummer (saved Cambridge Bible, 3 John 9) says the lost letter cannot be 1 or 2 John.
(b) book.json `facts`/sections: "scholars think each was written to fit on one sheet of papyrus (Wikipedia's article on 3 John, citing A. E. Brooke)". Wikipedia says "probably because they were both written to fit on one papyrus sheet" (Brooke, one author). "Scholars think" is a generalization from one cite.
(c) Authorship table: "a minority, following Rudolf Bultmann, thought 2 John an imitation modeled on 3 John", and chapters.json C: "(Rudolf Bultmann, as Raymond Brown reports him)". This rests on one second-hand Wikipedia line. "A minority, following Bultmann" is not shown by any source opened.
Fix: for (a) remove the identification or open Painter (or a page that reports it) and cite that; for (b) say "Brooke suggested"; for (c) say only "Bultmann, as Brown reports (Wikipedia, 3 John)" or drop the "minority" count.

**4. "Churches still differ on how to apply it" has no church behind it, and the case for "no traditions card" was tested against one confession (chapters.json `views.T`, deep dive "Closing the door"; topics.json `church-discipline`; progress.md).**
The T view and the church-discipline contribution list four practices (refusing a pulpit, hospitality, communion, shunning) with no named church, confession or theologian. The only church document opened is the Dordrecht Confession (1632), which by the writer's own log does not cite 2 John. So the section proves that one famous shunning confession does not use this verse, which is the opposite of a documented divergence. brief-2jn.md told the writer to check Anabaptist and other practice "verify". The log shows no such check beyond Dordrecht, and `progress.md` states "no documented church divergence on 2 John 10-11 was found" as if a search had been made. I did only a short search: it surfaced nothing for Menno Simons or Darby that I could open, and one Jehovah's Witnesses study page that I could not open, so I cannot confirm any particular church. There is one real divergence already in the writer's own material that the deep dive leaves out: Aurelius of Chullabi used 1:10 on the Cyprianic side at Carthage in 256, the side on which re-baptizing those baptized by heretics was required; Rome under Stephen took the other side (a broad, well-known fact, but the deep dive should say so with a source such as Cyprian's letters or Augustine against the Donatists, opened this session). Giving only Aurelius presents one side of an old dispute between churches as the verse's history.
Fix: add the other side with a source; either name and source the churches that apply 1:10-11 (open their own documents) or cut the list of four practices to what is sourced; reword `progress.md` to say what was and was not searched. Whether a `traditions` card is then warranted is for the lead; the evidence now in the folder is too thin to say "none exists".

**5. Wrong claim about the Greek Old Testament (notes.json 1:12 "face to face"; also the J lens in book.json).**
The note says "*Stoma pros stoma* ... comes from the Greek Old Testament: of Moses God says, 'Mouth to mouth I speak with him'" (Numbers 12:8) and refs list Numbers 12:8. The Greek of Numbers 12:8 (Swete, via `python tools/lxx.py Num 12:8`) is `στόμα κατὰ στόμα λαλήσω αὐτῷ`, with *kata*, not *pros*. The exact words *stoma pros stoma* are in the Greek of Jeremiah 32:4 (Swete Jer 39:4: `λαλήσει στόμα αὐτοῦ πρὸς στόμα αὐτοῦ`), where a king of Babylon speaks to Zedekiah. The Hebrew idiom is the same in both (`peh el peh`), so the J lens point can stand on the Hebrew, but "same words in the Greek Old Testament" is false for Numbers.
Fix: say that Numbers 12:8 has the Hebrew idiom (Greek `κατὰ στόμα`) and that the Greek *pros* wording is Jeremiah 32:4 (Greek 39:4); keep the Numbers link as Hebrew background.

**6. A gloss states a debated reading as fact and contradicts its own note (glosses.json 1:10; notes.json 1:10).**
Gloss on "into the house": "into your home, where the church met". The note on the same words says "No 'your' in the Greek: 'into a house'" and the chapter's setting for 1:7-11 lays out the house-church and private-home views as two readings. The gloss adds "your" and picks the first view. The Plain's "the house" also adds an article the Greek lacks (`εἰς οἰκίαν`; the word list brackets it "[the] house").
Fix: gloss it "into a house, a home (some think the house where the church met)"; the gloss must not add "your" or choose between the views. Consider "into a house" in the Plain.

### Minor

**7. Reader-facing prose names Wikipedia as the authority and uses report-hedges (many places).** Examples: notes 1:1 "(Armin Baum, as Wikipedia quotes him)"; book.json audience `views` "(a common reading, as Wikipedia's article summarizes it ...)"; "according to its 2020 catechism as Wikipedia reports it"; deep dive "Wikipedia's article on 2 John, citing J. L. Houlden" (the saved page does cite Houlden for the docetic claim, so this one is accurate); spec box "as Wikipedia summarizes him". STYLE rule 2 permits "reported by X" only when X's report (or a page quoting it) was read, and this was, but Wikipedia is not a church, scholar or ancient source and the readers are meant to see the original authority. The sources list also keeps `wiki-2jn-variants` described as "a secondary list, not the apparatus itself", which is honest but means the witness lists are second-hand. Fix: name the scholar and book, put the Wikipedia pointer in the source line only, and say once in the variants intro that witness lists come from a secondary compilation.

**8. Variants: small errors and over-claims (variants.json).**
(a) 1:8, first reading: the text lists "Codex Vaticanus (03) and minuscule 181" as witnesses to `ἃ εἰργασάμεθα`. The saved Wikipedia list gives `ἠργασάμεθα` (a different spelling, augment form) for B* and 181, and `εἰργασάμεθα` for B^c plus K L P and the Byzantine mass, who read `ἀπολέσωμεν` ("we lose"). The combination "you lose / we worked / you receive" is therefore built from separate per-word lists, not from one witness list; say so, or name only B^c and mark the spelling difference. (b) 1:8: "Tyndale House prints 'you worked'" and "Tregelles" are inferences from the word list's marks (those editions lack `εἰργασάμεθα`), not seen readings. Say "the word list shows Tyndale House and Tregelles do not have `εἰργασάμεθα`" or open THGNT. (c) 1:13: "Minuscules 429, 629, some Vulgate copies, the Armenian (with 'with you' plural in the Syriac and some Ethiopic copies)" is garbled. The saved list has `ἡ χάρις μετὰ σοῦ` (singular) in 442 (no Amen) and in 429 629 1758 1831 and some Vulgate and the Armenian (with Amen), and `μεθ' ὑμῶν` (plural) in the Syriac and some Ethiopic copies with Pseudo-Oecumenius and Theophylact. Rewrite as two readings. (d) 1:9 and 1:12 witness headings that say "NA28/ECM" rest on the marks plus one Wikipedia page on ECM; consider "NA28 (the word list)" so no ECM reading is asserted that was not seen.

**9. Variants a KJV reader would notice are missing (variants.json).** The Byzantine/TR differs visibly at: 1:2 `μένουσαν` / `ἐνοικοῦσαν` (A, 048, 1735; the note on 1:2 itself mentions KJV "dwelleth"); 1:5 word order `γράφων σοι καινήν` (Byzantine; KJV "write a new commandment unto thee") and `γράφω` against `γράφων`; 1:12 `γενέσθαι` ("be with") against Byzantine `ἐλθεῖν` ("come", KJV "come unto you"); 1:3 `μεθ' ἡμῶν` / `μεθ' ὑμῶν`; 1:11 the Latin addition noted in the saved Plummer ("Lo, I have told you beforehand ..."). brief-nt.md asks for an entry wherever the Byzantine/TR reads differently in a way a reader would notice.

**10. Plain: places to tighten.** (a) 1:8 "Be on your guard for yourselves": "for" is not in the Greek (`βλέπετε ἑαυτούς`, "watch yourselves"); it is copied from the Mark 13:9 Plain on purpose (drift-audit), but there the Greek has `δὲ ὑμεῖς`. The note claims the Greek is Mark's "word for word except for the emphatic 'you'"; Mark also has `δέ`. Say "nearly word for word". (b) 1:5 "I ask something of you": `ἐρωτῶ σε` has no "something"; "I ask you, lady" is enough. (c) 1:12 "face to face" for `στόμα πρὸς στόμα`: STYLE says keep images where English can carry them; "mouth to mouth" is understandable, and the same Plain choice in 3 John 14. This is the NIV/ESV idiom as well; the loss is explained in a note, so I leave the call to the lead. (d) 1:9 "that one has both the Father and the Son" and the opening run "Anyone who goes ahead ... does not have God. Whoever remains in the teaching ..." follow the shape that NIV/ESV also have; the Greek forces most of it, and "remain" is the site term, so I do not ask for a redo, but the drift audit logged this verse as "kept".

**11. Attribution slip: the "a forger would have named him" argument (book.json authorship).** The text credits it to "Plummer; Ellicott's commentary". Ellicott (saved) says the lack of a name "is in favour of authenticity" and gives a dignified-incognito explanation. Plummer says a writer personating John "would scarcely have chosen" the title "as being too indistinct", and that an elder who was not personating him would hardly call himself "the Elder". These are different arguments; state each.

**12. Consistency with 1 John: *peripateō*.** 2 John uses "walk" (1:4, 1:6) and 3 John "walk" (3:3-4), the note on 1:4 ties them to 1 John 1:7, but the 1 John Plain renders the same verb "walk around" (1 John 1:6, 1:7, 2:11) and "walk" only at 2:6; the Gospel of John Plain also has "walk around in the dark" (8:12). The reader who follows the word across the three letters loses it. Lead decision needed (brief-general.md did not fix this term); at least note it in the 1:4 note or align the two books.

**13. Setting claims stated as settled (chapters.json `settings`, book.json).** 1:1-3 says the lady lived "probably in the province of Asia" and that teachers "were visiting the churches of the region"; the tradition is Ephesus (Irenaeus), but the letter says nothing of its place, and the book facts line already says "in tradition". Make the settings say "in tradition" too.

**14. Lens promises not kept (book.json lenses).** The J lens promises "Jewish hospitality to travelers" and "Deuteronomy's test of a prophet (Deuteronomy 13)"; in 2 John Deuteronomy 13 appears only in the chapter's J view and the echoes list (no note), and hospitality is in 3 John, not here. Either add the note or trim the description.

**15. Small factual precision.** (a) notes 1:12: "scholars think each was written to fit on one sheet" (see finding 3b). (b) The Eusebius/two-tombs line in the authorship table credits the tombs to Eusebius; Church History 3.39.6 does give "two tombs in Ephesus ... called John's", as a report of "others" that Eusebius cites, so say "reports". (c) The Clement "Babylonian lady, by name Electa" comes from the Adumbrations, preserved only in Latin; say so. (d) The note at 1:7 offers a "second coming" reading without naming a holder; the saved Meyer commentary names Oecumenius for it. Name him.
