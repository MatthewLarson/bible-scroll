# Hostile review: 1 Peter (1Pe), one part, 105 verses

Reviewer: hostile-reviewer agent. Content files were not edited. `node tools/build.js --check 1Pe`: 0 errors, 0 warnings (the build cannot see any of the problems below).

## Verdict

The book is careful and mostly honest. I found no invented source, quotation or manuscript reading that I could prove false. The Plain follows the Greek closely and is clear of NIV/ESV drift apart from forced phrases. Where I could open the source, the writer's claims held: Polycarp ch. 1 and 8, Plutarch *Advice to Bride and Groom* 19, Eusebius on Peter's death and on 1 Peter as acknowledged, Calvin on 3:19, 3:21 and 5:13, the ISBE "Babylon" entry (Erasmus and Calvin, Strabo, "two cursives" with "in Rome"), the CCC numbers, and every Old Testament and New Testament verse the notes quote as the site's Plain (checked against the files). The problems are of four kinds. (1) The readers are called "mostly Gentile" as settled fact, with the ancient Jewish-readers view the writer himself read left out. (2) The three traditions cards are thin where the brief asked for each church's own document. They contain one Orthodox view that is not an Orthodox church document, one Lutheran view taken from Luther's personal writing, an unsourced Baptist sentence, and a Catholic entry that omits the Catholic tradition's most famous dissent from the Catholic reading of 3:19. (3) Two Plain choices at points of dispute (5:12 "I have sent", 3:21 "water saves you") pick a side without showing the other. (4) Heavy reliance on Wikipedia for the scholarly landscape, shown to readers as "the Wikipedia survey", plus a Stowe quotation I could not find. There are no critical findings.

Count: critical 0, major 9, minor 14.

## Major

### 1. Readers called "mostly Gentile" as settled fact; the Jewish-readers view is missing (check 7)
Locations: `book.json` facts "Readers" and `audience.to`; `audience.views`; note 1:1 "the Dispersion"; `chapters.json` ch. 1 deep dive "Who were the readers?", setting 1:1-2; proposals `topics/1-peter.json` lead and the `1-peter` entry in `topics.json`.
The audience views offer only social-fact versus spiritual-picture (Elliott, Chin). They never say that the Gentile reading is itself a position. The ancient church, in Eusebius, read the letter as written to Jews. Eusebius, *Church History* 3.1.2 (opened at newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm): "Peter appears to have preached in Pontus, Galatia, Bithynia, Cappadocia, and Asia to the Jews of the dispersion." The writer's own `sources-checked.md` lists Eusebius 3.1.2, so he read it. The "Dispersion" word, the Israel titles, and "apostle to the circumcised" (Galatians 2:7-9) are the reasons a Jewish or mixed readership has been held. The deep dive says "Three clues point to Gentiles", but 1:18 "your fathers" and 4:3 could also describe Gentile-born converts in a mixed church. A debated audience is stated as settled.
Fix: add a view to `audience.views` and the ch. 1 deep dive: "Jewish believers of the Dispersion (Eusebius 3.1.2), mixed churches, or mostly Gentile converts (the modern majority)". Change "mostly Gentile converts" in facts, setting 1:1-2 and the topic lead to "probably mostly Gentile converts, though some have held..." Only claim a "modern majority" if a scholar is opened and named.

### 2. 5:12 Plain says "I have sent you these few lines": the Greek says "I wrote" and the Plain picks the courier reading
Locations: `plain.txt` 5:12; topic `silas` (quotes the Plain: "'I have sent you these few lines through Silvanus'"); setting 5:12-14.
Greek: Διὰ Σιλουανοῦ ... δι᾽ ὀλίγων ἔγραψα, "through Silvanus ... I wrote". There is no verb "send". The note correctly gives secretary and courier, but the Plain has already chosen the courier (Elliott's view, which the book's own audience section reports). This is the authorship crux "at full weight". The Plain should not decide it.
Fix: "I have written you these few lines through Silvanus" (or "I wrote you ..."). Update the `silas` topic quote so it matches.

### 3. Priesthood card (2:5, 9): sources are not each church's own document, and views are missing
Location: `chapters.json` ch. 2 `traditions` (top `priesthood`).
- Orthodox. The only source is the 2019 North American Orthodox-Catholic Theological Consultation statement. A published summary of the document (found by web search, the hchc.edu release) says it "does not speak officially for either Church". It is an ecumenical consultation, not an Orthodox document. The card labels it "Orthodox". I could not open the PDF to check the two quotations ("priestly act of the entire People of God" did not turn up in my search). Use an Orthodox source, for example Philaret's *Longer Catechism*, which the writer already used elsewhere. If the consultation statement stays, label it as a joint statement.
- Lutheran. The card quotes Luther's *To the Christian Nobility* (1520), a personal treatise, not a confession. The Book of Concord's *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* cites 1 Peter 2:9 (I confirmed only through a secondary study page, so open the Treatise itself). That would be the church's own document.
- Reformed. Heidelberg Q&A 32 never mentions 2:5 or 2:9 or the word "priesthood". The sentence "Reformed churches have ministers, elders and deacons, not priests" has no source. Add a document that does cite the verse (for example Calvin's *Institutes* or commentary on 2:9, once opened), or cut the sentence.
- Missing. The brief asked for Anglican and Baptist. Latter-day Saints are included in the descent card but not here, where the LDS ordained priesthood is the most distinct answer. Pentecostal and Methodist views are also absent. All four views given are the liturgical or magisterial ones.

### 4. Descent card (3:19): the Catholic entry hides the Catholic dissent, other sources are missing, and "Reformed teaching is divided" has no support
Location: `chapters.json` ch. 3 `traditions` (top `descent-into-hades`).
- Aquinas. The note at 3:19 says "Thomas Aquinas followed" Augustine. I confirmed this in the *Summa* III q. 52 a. 2 (newadvent.org/summa/4052.htm): he says "Augustine ... furnishes a better exposition" and that the preaching "is not to be referred to Christ's descent into hell". The Catholic entry gives only the CCC, whose 632-634 read 3:19 as the descent. A reader would think Catholic tradition is of one mind. This is not fair to Catholics or to the other views. Add that the Catholic tradition's own Thomas read 3:19 as the Noah reading, while the CCC cites it for the descent.
- Lutheran. Formula of Concord SD IX does not cite 1 Peter 3:19. The entry gives no Lutheran reading of the verse. Either say so or add a Lutheran source that does read it.
- Reformed. "Reformed teaching is divided on this verse" is not shown by the two sources cited. Calvin is one reading. The Heidelberg Q&A 44 is about the creed's clause, not 3:19. Name a second Reformed reading with a source, or drop "divided".
- Missing. Anglican (Article 3 on the descent) and Presbyterian (Westminster Larger Catechism on the descent) are the obvious sources. The brief asked for the history of the creed's clause, and nothing in the book gives it.
- Orthodox. I could not re-open Philaret questions 214-216 (search returned nothing). The quotations and the claim that the catechism "cites 3:18-19 as its proof" are unverified by me.
- Calvin's quotations ("souls separated from their bodies", "the manifestation of Christ's grace was made to godly spirits") are verified at ccel.org (comment3/comm_vol45/htm/iv.iv.viii.htm).

### 5. Baptism card (3:21): unsourced Baptist sentence, and views the brief and the book's own note name are absent
Location: `chapters.json` ch. 3 `traditions` (top `baptism`); note at 3:21 "water saves you now as well".
- "Baptists read 3:21's 'not the removing of dirt' as showing that the water does not save; what saves is Christ's resurrection" is not in the Baptist Faith and Message art. 7. It is the writer's description of a reading, sourced to a document that does not say it. Cut it or source it.
- The note at 3:21 names Anglicans and Churches of Christ as baptismal-regeneration churches. The card gives them no voice. Anglican (Articles of Religion on baptism; the *Book of Common Prayer* catechism) and Churches of Christ documents exist. The Quaker and Salvation Army view that water baptism is not required is also missing; it matters because 3:21 says "not the removing of dirt".
- The card never says that the grammar is disputed (see #6), though that grammar decides which church can read the verse as it stands.

### 6. 3:21 Plain "water saves you now as well, in its counterpart, baptism" fixes a disputed parse without a note
Location: `plain.txt` 3:21; notes at 3:21; `variants.json` #15.
Greek: ὃ καὶ ὑμᾶς ἀντίτυπον νῦν σῴζει βάπτισμα. In the Greek, βάπτισμα is the grammatical subject of σῴζει, ὅ is a neuter relative with ὕδατος (3:20) as its antecedent, and ἀντίτυπον is nominative or accusative, not "in its counterpart". The Plain turns "water" into the subject and "baptism" into an appositive. The reading is possible, but it is the most sacramental-sounding one, and the Plain's "water saves you" contradicts the book's own note title ("Baptism now saves you") and card question ("Baptism 'now saves you'"). Notes 95 and 96 never mention the antecedent or the alternatives (baptism as the subject, "which [water] corresponds to this: baptism now saves you"; or ἀντίτυπον as an appositive of ὅ). The glosses add "counterpart: the matching copy".
Fix: either translate nearer the order ("A counterpart to this now saves you too: baptism") or keep the choice and add a `word` note listing the antecedent and appositive options with the Greek.

### 7. Wikipedia is the only source for the scholarly landscape, and it is shown to readers as "the Wikipedia survey"
Locations: `book.json` authorship ("Early use" row aside) rows "The traditional view" (Jobes quotation), "A Petrine circle" ("the Wikipedia survey says most"; Ehrman; Stanton), `audience.views` (Achtemeier); notes 1:1, 1:1 regions, 1:6, 3:19 (Spitta, Jeremias, Selwyn, Porter, Dalton, Aquinas), 4:6, 4:16, 5:9, 5:12; ch. 3 VIEW C ("widely held today"); source keys `wiki-1peter`, `wiki-spirits-in-prison`.
I opened both articles. They do say what the book reports: the Jobes quotation is exact, Achtemeier on Domitian is correct, Ehrman and Stanton are as described, Selwyn and Porter are as described, and Aquinas sits under the Augustinian view. But no primary scholar was opened. This breaks the spirit of rule 2 for a letter whose authorship, audience and date are the central questions, and it puts "the Wikipedia survey says" into reader-facing prose. Two items misframe their source:
- Note 5:9 cites Elliott as pointing to the verse "for the empire-wide spread of Christians' suffering", in a sentence about persecution. The article (opened) says Elliott reads it as empire-wide suffering and therefore as evidence **against** official Roman persecution (the first empire-wide persecution was under Decius). The note should say so.
- "The 'spirits in prison' are most often read today ... this reading is widely held today" (ch. 3 VIEW C) is a consensus claim resting on one encyclopedia page. Source it to a commentary or survey that was opened, or say "many scholars".
Fix: replace the Wikipedia keys with Elliott, Achtemeier, Jobes, Dalton and the like, each opened this session, or remove the specific attributions and keep the unsourced-but-labeled "many scholars". Take "Wikipedia survey" out of the prose.

### 8. Authorship is not given equal weight, and the lead leans critical
Locations: `book.json` `lead[0]` ("written in the name of the apostle Peter"), `authorship` rows; ch. 1 setting 1:1-2.
The lead states, before any view is given, that the letter was "written in the name of the apostle Peter". That is the pseudonymity position put as fact. The traditional row has one named scholar (Jobes, second-hand) and two unsourced reasons. The critical row has four reasons, Ehrman and Stanton. The secretarial case (the Greek quality answered by Silvanus; Silvanus' ties to Paul; early attestation by Polycarp and 2 Peter 3:1, if 2 Peter is taken as evidence) gets one sentence. Neither side's reasons are sourced to a scholar's argument opened this session.
Fix: say "a letter from 'Peter'" or "in Peter's name or by Peter" in the lead, and give each of the three views a row of the same size with its strongest reasons.

### 9. Stowe quotation in note 2:18 could not be found
Location: note 2:18 "the crooked ones" and topic `slavery`.
The note quotes Stowe's *Key*: "froward, peevish, and hard" (attributed to a sermon in Bishop Meade's collection) and that such teaching "fully justifies the worst kind of spiritual and temporal despotism". A web search confirmed that Part IV ch. 9 of the *Key* discusses and quotes Bishop Meade's sermon to slaves. I scanned about 500 KB of the Gutenberg text (offsets 300,000-900,000) and did not find either phrase. The only close wording was in Part II ch. 15 ("the worst kind of despotism ... over the body and over the soul"), which is a different sentence. I cannot say the quotation is invented, only that I could not verify it. A quotation is the one thing the project forbids getting wrong.
Fix: reopen the Key's ch. 9, copy the two phrases exactly, and say whether the second is Stowe's own comment or part of Meade. The `slavery` topic repeats the first phrase and needs the same check.
