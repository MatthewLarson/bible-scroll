## The oracle
1:1 The oracle that Habakkuk the prophet saw.
## The prophet's first complaint
1:2 LORD, how long have I been crying for help, with you not listening? / I shout to you, "Violence!" and still you will not save.
1:3 Why do you show me wickedness and then look on at trouble? / Ruin and violence are right in front of me. / There is a fight, and arguing flares up.
1:4 So the law goes numb, / and justice never comes out. / The wicked have the righteous surrounded, / so justice comes out twisted.
## The LORD's answer: the Chaldeans
1:5 Look among the nations, and watch. / Be amazed, be astonished! / For I am about to do a deed in your days / that you will not believe when it is told.
1:6 For look, I am raising up the Chaldeans, / a nation bitter and headlong, / which marches across the wide places of the earth / to take over homes that are not its own.
1:7 It is terrifying and dreadful. / Its justice and its grandeur come only from itself.
1:8 Its horses are faster than leopards / and fiercer than the wolves of evening. / Its war horses stamp and spring about. / Its horsemen come from far away. / They fly like an eagle hurrying to eat.
1:9 All of it comes for violence. / Their faces are set straight ahead, / and it gathers captives like sand.
1:10 At kings it sneers, / and rulers are a joke to it. / It laughs at every fortress; / it heaps up dirt and takes it.
1:11 Then it sweeps on like the wind and moves past, / a guilty one, whose own strength is his god.
## The second complaint
1:12 LORD, are you not from ancient times? / My God, my Holy One, we will not die. / LORD, you appointed it to carry out judgment. / Rock, you set it up to bring correction.
1:13 Pure are your eyes, so you cannot see evil; / you cannot gaze at trouble. / Why do you gaze at the treacherous? / Why do you keep silent when a wicked man swallows up / someone more righteous than himself?
1:14 You have made human beings like the fish of the sea, / like crawling things with no ruler over them.
1:15 He pulls up all of them with a hook, / drags them off in his net, / and collects them in his dragnet. / So he rejoices and is glad.
1:16 That is why he sacrifices to his net / and burns incense to his dragnet: / by them his share is rich, / and his food is fat.
1:17 Will he, then, go on emptying his net, / and keep on killing nations without pity?
## Standing at the watch
2:1 I will stand at my post, / and take my place on the tower. / I will keep watch to see what he will say to me, / and what answer I will give to my complaint.
## The vision
2:2 Then the LORD answered me. He said, / "Write the vision down, / and make it clear on the tablets, / so that the one who reads it can run.
2:3 For the vision is still waiting for its time. / It gasps toward the end, and it will not lie. / Even if it drags, wait for it. / It is sure to come; it will not be late.
2:4 Look, the one who is swollen with pride: the soul inside him is not upright. / But the righteous will live by his faithfulness.
2:5 And more: wine is a traitor, / and the arrogant man does not stay at home. / He opens his throat wide as Sheol, / and he is like death, never full. / He gathers to himself all the nations / and collects to himself all the peoples."
## First woe: the plunderer
2:6 Will not all of these lift up a saying against him, / a mocking song, with riddles about him? / They will say: / "Woe to the one who piles up what is not his. / How long will he load himself with pledges?"
2:7 Will your lenders not rise up suddenly? / Will those who shake you not wake? / Then you will be plunder for them.
2:8 You have plundered many nations, / so all the rest of the peoples will plunder you, / because of the blood of human beings / and the violence done to the land, to the town, and to all who live in it.
## Second woe: unjust gain
2:9 Woe to the one who grabs unjust gain for his house, / so that he can set his nest on high, / and be rescued from the hand of harm.
2:10 You have plotted shame for your own house, / ending many peoples, / and you sin against your own life.
2:11 For the stone in the wall cries out, / and the wooden beam answers it.
## Third woe: the city built on blood
2:12 Woe to the one who puts up a city with bloodshed / and makes a town stand firm by injustice.
2:13 Does it not come from the LORD of Armies / that peoples labor only to feed a fire, / and nations wear themselves out for emptiness?
2:14 For the earth will be filled / with knowing the glory of the LORD, / as waters cover the sea.
## Fourth woe: the one who shames his neighbor
2:15 Woe to the one who makes his neighbor drink, / pouring out your anger, and making them drunk as well, / so as to look at their nakedness.
2:16 You are full of shame instead of honor. / Drink, you too, and show that you are uncircumcised! / The cup from the LORD's right hand will turn to you, / and disgrace will cover your honor.
2:17 For the violence done to Lebanon will cover you, / and the ruin of its animals will terrify you, / because of the blood of human beings / and the violence done to the land, to the town, and to all who live in it.
## Fifth woe: the idol-maker
2:18 What use is a carved image, that its maker carved it? / A cast image, a teacher of lies? / For its maker trusts in what he made, / by making worthless gods that cannot speak.
2:19 Woe to the one who says to a piece of wood, "Wake up!" / and to a silent stone, "Rouse yourself!" / Can it teach? Look: it is plated with gold and silver, / and there is no breath inside it at all.
2:20 But the LORD is in his holy temple. / Hush before him, all the earth!
## A prayer of Habakkuk
3:1 A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, in the manner of shigionoth.
3:2 LORD, I have heard what is told of you, and I fear. / LORD, bring your work to life in the middle of the years. / In the middle of the years make it known. / In anger, remember compassion.
## The LORD comes
3:3 God comes from Teman, / and the Holy One from Mount Paran. Selah. / His splendor covers the heavens, / and the earth is full of his praise.
3:4 A brightness like the light appears. / Rays shoot from his hand, / and there his strength is hidden.
3:5 Plague goes in front of him, / and blazing fever comes out at his feet.
3:6 He stands, and the earth shakes. / He looks, and the nations jump. / The age-old mountains are shattered; / the ancient hills bow down. / His ways are the ways of long ago.
3:7 I see the tents of Cushan in distress. / The tent curtains of the land of Midian shake.
3:8 Was it against the rivers, LORD, that you burned? / Was your anger against the streams, / or your fury against the sea, / when you rode your horses, / your chariots of rescue?
3:9 You pull the cover off your bow. / Oaths are sworn on the arrows: a word. Selah. / You split the earth with rivers.
3:10 The mountains see you and twist in pain. / A flood of water rushes past. / The deep gives out its voice / and raises its hands up high.
3:11 Sun and moon stand still in their lofty home, / at the light of your arrows as they go, / at the brightness of your flashing spear.
3:12 In rage you stride over the earth; / in anger you thresh the nations.
3:13 You went out to rescue your people, / to rescue your anointed one. / You smashed the head of the house of the wicked, / and stripped it bare from its base to the neck. Selah.
3:14 With his own shafts you pierced the head of his warriors, / who stormed in to scatter me, / gloating as if to eat the poor in a hiding place.
3:15 You trod through the sea with your horses, / through the heaped-up mighty waters.
## The prophet waits and rejoices
3:16 I heard, and my stomach shook. / At the sound my lips quivered. / Rot came into my bones, / and I shook where I stood. / I will wait quietly for the day of trouble / to come up against the people who attack us.
3:17 Even if the fig tree never buds / and the vines have no fruit, / even if the olive harvest fails / and the fields give no food, / even if the flock is cut off from the pen / and there are no cattle in the stalls,
3:18 still I will exult in the LORD, / and be glad in the God of my salvation.
3:19 The Lord GOD is my strength. / He makes my feet like a doe's / and lets me tread on my high places. / For the music director, on my stringed instruments.
