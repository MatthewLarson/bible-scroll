## Copied by Hezekiah's men
25:1 These too are Solomon's proverbs, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied.
## Kings and their courts
25:2 The glory of God is to keep a matter hidden, / and the glory of kings is searching a matter out.
25:3 The heavens for height and the earth for depth, / and the heart of kings: no one can search it.
25:4 Clear the slag from the silver, / and out comes a vessel for the smith.
25:5 Clear the wicked from before the king, / and his throne will stand firm in righteousness.
25:6 Do not claim honor for yourself before a king, / and do not stand where the great belong.
25:7 It is better for him to say to you, "Come up here," / than for him to move you down in front of a noble / whom your eyes have seen.
## Quarrels and neighbors
25:8 Do not rush out to argue a case. / Or else, when it is over and your neighbor has shamed you, / what will you do then?
25:9 Take up your dispute with your neighbor directly, / and do not spill a secret that belongs to someone else,
25:10 or the one who hears will disgrace you, / and the bad name you get will not go away.
## Words at the right time
25:11 Golden apples set in silver: / a word spoken at the right moment.
25:12 A gold earring, an ornament of fine gold: / a wise person who corrects an ear that listens.
25:13 To those who send him, an envoy you can trust / is like a chill of snow in harvest time: / he brings his masters back to life.
25:14 Clouds and wind, and yet no rain: / that is someone who boasts of a gift that is a lie.
25:15 A ruler is won over by slowness to anger, / and a soft tongue breaks bone.
25:16 Have you found honey? Eat only what you need, / or you will have too much of it and throw it up.
25:17 Keep your visits to your neighbor's house rare, / or he will have too much of you and hate you.
25:18 A club, a sword and a sharpened arrow: / a man who testifies against his neighbor as a lying witness.
25:19 A cracked tooth and a foot that gives way: / trust in a traitor on a day of trouble.
25:20 Pulling off someone's coat on a cold day, / vinegar poured on soda, / and one who sings songs to an aching heart.
## Coals on an enemy's head
25:21 If someone who hates you is hungry, feed him bread, / and if he is thirsty, hand him water to drink,
25:22 for you will be scooping up burning coals onto his head, / and the LORD will pay you back.
25:23 The north wind brings forth rain, / and a tongue that talks in secret, angry faces.
25:24 Living in a corner up on the roof is better / than a shared house with a woman who picks fights.
25:25 Cool water for someone worn out: / a good report from a far-off land.
25:26 A trampled spring, a spoiled fountain: / a righteous person who totters in front of the wicked.
25:27 Eating much honey is not good, / and searching out their glory is glory.
25:28 A breached city with no wall left: / a person who cannot rein in his own spirit.
## Fools
26:1 Honor is as out of place for a fool / as snow in summer or rain at harvest.
26:2 Like a bird that flits and a swallow in flight, / a curse that has no cause does not land.
26:3 Whip for a horse, bit for a donkey, / and a rod for fools' backs.
26:4 Don't answer a fool in line with his folly, / or you too will become like him.
26:5 Answer a fool in line with his folly, / or he will be wise in his own eyes.
26:6 He chops off his feet and drinks down violence, / the one who sends word by a fool.
26:7 The legs of a lame man hang limp, / and a proverb in fools' mouths.
26:8 Giving honor to a fool / is like tying the stone into the sling.
26:9 A thorn comes up in a drunkard's hand, / and a proverb in fools' mouths.
26:10 An archer shooting at everyone in sight: / that is the one who hires a fool, the one who hires anyone passing by.
26:11 A dog goes back to its vomit, / and a fool does his folly over again.
26:12 You see a man who is wise in his own eyes? / A fool has more hope than he does.
## The lazy one
26:13 The lazy one says, "A lion is out on the road! / A lion in the open squares!"
26:14 The door turns on its pivot, / and the lazy one turns on his bed.
26:15 The lazy one sinks his hand into the bowl; / he is too weary to lift it back to his mouth.
26:16 In his own eyes the lazy one is wiser / than seven who can answer with good sense.
## Meddlers, gossips and liars
26:17 Someone who seizes a dog by the ears: / that is a passerby who gets angry over a fight that is not his.
26:18 Like a madman who shoots / flaming darts, arrows and death,
26:19 so is a man who tricks his neighbor / and says, "Wasn't I joking?"
26:20 When the wood runs out, the fire dies, / and when there is no gossip, the fighting calms down.
26:21 Charcoal for embers, wood for fire, / and a quarrelsome man to heat up a dispute.
26:22 A gossip's words are like treats gulped down; / they slide into the deepest rooms of the belly.
26:23 Silver slag coating a clay pot: / lips that burn and a heart that is evil.
26:24 The hater hides behind what his lips say, / and deep inside he lays up deceit.
26:25 When he makes his voice sweet, do not trust him, / for seven abhorrent things are in his heart.
26:26 His hatred may be wrapped in trickery, / but his evil will come to light before the assembly.
26:27 The one who digs a pit falls into it, / and the stone comes back on the one who rolls it.
26:28 A false tongue hates the people it has crushed, / and a smooth-talking mouth brings about a fall.
## Tomorrow, praise and friends
27:1 Don't brag about tomorrow, / since you cannot know what a day will give birth to.
27:2 Let an outsider praise you, and not your own mouth; / a foreigner, and not your own lips.
27:3 A stone is heavy and sand weighs a lot, / but a stubborn fool's provoking is heavier than both.
27:4 Rage is cruel and anger floods, / but who can hold out against jealousy?
27:5 A rebuke brought into the open is better / than love that stays out of sight.
27:6 Wounds from someone who loves you are true, / but kisses from someone who hates you come thick and fast.
27:7 A full appetite stamps on the honeycomb, / but to a hungry appetite anything bitter tastes sweet.
27:8 Like a bird that roams from its nest, / so is a man who roams from his place.
27:9 Oil and incense cheer the heart, / and a friend's sweetness beats your own counsel.
27:10 Your friend and your father's friend, do not abandon them. / On the day trouble hits you, do not go to your brother's house. / A neighbor nearby is better than a brother far off.
27:11 Become wise, my son, and gladden my heart, / and I will have a reply for the one who sneers at me.
27:12 A shrewd person sees trouble coming and takes cover; / the naive go right on by and are punished.
27:13 Take the garment of a man who stands good for an outsider; / take a pledge from him for a foreign woman.
27:14 When someone blesses his neighbor at the top of his voice, getting up early to do it, / it will be counted to him as a curse.
27:15 A drip that never stops on a day of downpour / and a woman who picks fights are just alike.
27:16 Whoever would hold her back holds back the wind, / and his right hand meets oil.
27:17 Iron is sharpened by iron, / and a person sharpens his friend's face.
27:18 The one who guards a fig tree gets to eat its figs, / and the one who watches over his master gains honor.
27:19 As a face looks back at a face in water, / so a person's heart shows back the person.
27:20 Sheol and Abaddon never have enough, / and the eyes of humans never have enough.
27:21 Silver goes in the melting pot and gold in the furnace, / and a person is measured by the praise he gets.
27:22 If you pound a stubborn fool in the mortar / along with the grain, using the pestle, / his folly still will not go away from him.
## Know your flock
27:23 Make sure you know the faces of your sheep; / set your heart on your herds.
27:24 For riches are not forever, / nor does a crown go on from age to age.
27:25 The hay disappears, the new grass comes up, / and the greens on the hills are gathered in.
27:26 Then lambs are there for your clothes, / and goats to pay the price of a field.
27:27 You will have goats' milk to spare, enough to feed you / and to feed your household, / and life for your servant girls.
## The righteous and the wicked
28:1 The wicked flee with no one after them, / but the righteous feel as safe as a young lion.
28:2 When a land rebels, it gets many princes, / but under a man who understands and knows, things hold firm for long.
28:3 A man who is poor himself and grinds down the poor: / rain that washes everything away and leaves no bread.
28:4 People who walk away from the law speak well of the wicked, / but people who keep the law take them on.
28:5 Justice makes no sense to evil men, / but those who seek the LORD understand it all.
28:6 A poor man living with integrity is better / than one who twists his two paths, though he is rich.
28:7 Whoever keeps the law is a son who understands, / and the one who runs with gluttons disgraces his father.
28:8 The one who grows his wealth through interest and profit / is storing it up for someone who is kind to the poor.
28:9 Whoever closes his ear so as not to hear the law: / his prayer, too, is abhorrent.
28:10 The one who leads the upright astray on an evil path / will himself drop into his own pit, / but the blameless will inherit good things.
28:11 In his own eyes a rich man is wise, / but a poor man with understanding will see through him.
28:12 When the righteous are glad, the splendor is great, / but when the wicked rise up, people have to be hunted for.
28:13 The one who hides his offenses will not get ahead, / but the one who admits them and turns from them will receive mercy.
28:14 Happy is the person who is always afraid, / but the one who makes his heart hard falls into disaster.
## Rulers good and bad
28:15 A roaring lion and a bear on the charge: / a wicked man ruling a people that is poor.
28:16 A ruler without understanding piles up oppression, / but one who hates dishonest profit will have a long life.
28:17 A man burdened by the blood of a life he took / will run until he reaches the pit. / Let no one take hold of him.
28:18 Whoever walks blameless will be kept safe, / but the one whose two ways are crooked will fall at once.
28:19 The man who tills his soil gets his fill of bread; / the man who chases empty things gets his fill of poverty.
28:20 A man you can rely on is rich in blessings, / but one who rushes to get rich will not escape punishment.
28:21 Favoring one face over another is not good, / and a man will sin for a crust of bread.
28:22 A man with a grudging eye rushes after riches, / not knowing that need is coming for him.
28:23 The one who rebukes a person will later win more favor / than the one with a smooth tongue.
28:24 A man who steals from his father and mother and says, "That's no crime," / keeps company with the destroyer.
28:25 A man with a wide-open appetite starts fights, / but the one who trusts in the LORD will grow fat.
28:26 The one who relies on his own heart is a fool, / and the one who walks in wisdom will escape.
28:27 The one who gives to the poor will never be in need, / but the one who looks the other way will be cursed again and again.
28:28 When the wicked rise up, people go into hiding, / and when they die out, the righteous grow in number.
## The stiff neck and the rod
29:1 A man who has been corrected again and again but keeps his neck stiff / in a flash will be smashed, and nothing will heal him.
29:2 When the righteous grow many, the people are glad; / when a wicked man is in charge, a people sighs.
29:3 A man who loves wisdom makes his father glad, / and a man who runs with prostitutes throws away his wealth.
29:4 By justice a king makes a land stand firm, / but a man greedy for levies wrecks it.
29:5 A man who smooth-talks his neighbor / is spreading a net for his steps.
29:6 In an evil man's wrongdoing there is a trap, / but a righteous man sings for joy and is glad.
29:7 A righteous man knows what the poor are owed by law; / a wicked man does not understand knowledge.
29:8 Mockers stir a town into flames, / but the wise turn anger back.
29:9 When a wise man takes a stubborn fool to court, / there is raging and laughing, and no peace.
29:10 Men of blood hate a blameless man, / and as for the upright, they hunt for their lives.
29:11 A fool lets out all his spirit, / but a wise man stills it and holds it back.
29:12 When a ruler pays attention to false words, / every one of his servants turns wicked.
29:13 A poor man and a man who oppresses meet; / it is the LORD who lights up the eyes of them both.
29:14 When a king gives the poor true judgment, / his throne will stand firm for all time.
29:15 Rod and rebuke give wisdom, / but a boy left to run loose brings shame to his mother.
29:16 As the wicked grow in number, so does rebellion, / and the righteous will watch them fall.
29:17 Discipline your son and he will put your mind at rest; / he will bring delights to your soul.
29:18 Where no vision is given, a people runs wild, / but happy is the one who keeps the law.
29:19 Words alone will not discipline a slave, / for he understands, yet gives no answer.
29:20 You see a man who is quick with his words? / A fool has more hope than he does.
29:21 Whoever pampers his slave from boyhood / will find that in the end it brings grief.
29:22 A man of anger starts fights, / and a master of rage does much wrong.
29:23 Pride brings a person down low, / and the one who is low in spirit takes hold of honor.
29:24 Whoever splits the loot with a thief hates himself; / he hears the curse spoken and says nothing.
29:25 Being afraid of people lays a trap, / but the one who trusts in the LORD is set high and safe.
29:26 Many seek the ruler's face, / but justice for a person comes from the LORD.
29:27 The righteous find a man of injustice abhorrent, / and the wicked find a man who walks straight abhorrent.
