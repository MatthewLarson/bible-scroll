## News from Jerusalem
1:1 The record of Nehemiah son of Hacaliah. It was the month of Kislev in the twentieth year, and I was in the fortress at Susa.
1:2 One of my brothers, Hanani, arrived with a group of men from Judah. I asked them about the Jews who had escaped, the ones left from the captivity, and about Jerusalem.
1:3 They told me, "The ones who are left from the captivity, back there in the province, are badly off and treated with scorn. Jerusalem's wall is breached, and fire has burned its gates."
1:4 These words made me sit down and weep. I went on mourning for days, fasting and praying before the God of the heavens.
## Nehemiah's Prayer
1:5 I said, "Please, LORD, God of the heavens, the great and fearsome God, who keeps his covenant and his loyal love with those who love him and keep his commands:
1:6 let your ear listen closely and your eyes be open, so that you hear your servant's prayer. I bring it before you now, day and night, for the Israelites, your servants. I confess the sins of the Israelites, the sins we have committed against you. I and my father's family have sinned.
1:7 We have done terrible wrong against you. We did not keep the commands, the rules and the rulings that you commanded your servant Moses.
1:8 Please remember the word you gave your servant Moses as a command: 'You will act unfaithfully, and I will scatter you among the peoples.
1:9 Then you will turn back to me and keep my commands and do them. Even if your scattered people are at the farthest edge of the sky, I will gather them from there. I will bring them to the place I have chosen to make my name live there.'
1:10 These are your servants, your people. You ransomed them by your great strength and your mighty hand.
1:11 Please, Lord, let your ear listen closely to the prayer of your servant, and to the prayer of your servants who take delight in fearing your name. Make your servant succeed today, please, and let him find compassion with this man." Now I served as the king's cupbearer.
## Before the King
2:1 It was the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes. Wine had been placed in front of the king, and I picked it up and handed it to him. Until then I had never looked sad before him.
2:2 The king said to me, "Why is your face so gloomy? You are not ill. This must be a troubled heart and nothing else." Then I became terribly afraid.
2:3 I said to the king, "May the king live forever! How could my face not be gloomy? The city that holds my ancestors' graves is a wasteland, and its gates have been eaten up by fire."
2:4 The king said to me, "What are you asking for, then?" So I prayed to the God of the heavens,
2:5 and I said to the king, "If the king thinks it good, and if your servant pleases you, send me to Judah, to the city of my ancestors' graves, to rebuild it."
2:6 The king, while the queen sat at his side, asked me, "How long will the trip be, and when will you come back?" The king approved and sent me off, once I had named a time.
2:7 I said further to the king, "If the king thinks it good, let me be given letters to the governors of Beyond the River, so that they let me pass through until I come to Judah.
2:8 Give me also a letter to Asaph, who keeps the king's park, telling him to give me wood to make beams for the gates of the temple's fortress, for the city wall, and for the house I will go into." The king gave them to me, for my God's good hand was on me.
2:9 I went to the governors of Beyond the River and handed them the king's letters. The king had sent army commanders and horsemen along with me.
2:10 Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, heard of it, and it upset them badly that a man had come to seek good for the Israelites.
## The Walls Inspected by Night
2:11 So I reached Jerusalem, and I spent three days there.
2:12 Then I rose by night, taking a few men with me. Nobody had heard from me what my God had laid on my heart to do for Jerusalem. The only animal with me was the one I rode.
2:13 I went out at night by the Valley Gate, past Serpent Spring, as far as the Dung Gate. All the while I was looking closely at Jerusalem's walls, which lay breached, and at its gates, which fire had eaten.
2:14 I passed on to the Spring Gate and the King's Pool. There my animal could find no way through.
2:15 That night I went up along the wadi, studying the wall. Then I turned around, came back in by the Valley Gate, and was home again.
2:16 The deputies had no idea where I went or what I was up to. So far I had told nothing to the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the deputies or the others who were going to do the work.
## "Let's Get Up and Build"
2:17 I told them, "You can see the bad state we are in. Jerusalem is a wasteland, and fire has burned its gates. Come on, let's rebuild Jerusalem's wall, so we are not a laughingstock anymore."
2:18 I told them about my God's hand, how good it had been on me, and also about the words the king had said to me. They said, "Let's get up and build!" They made their hands strong for this good work.
2:19 But Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arab heard of it. They laughed at us and looked down on us. They said, "What do you think you are doing? Are you in revolt against the king?"
2:20 I answered them, "The God of the heavens is the one who will make us succeed. We, his servants, will get up and build. But you have no share in Jerusalem, no rightful claim and no memorial there."
## The Builders of the Wall
3:1 Then Eliashib the high priest got up with his brothers the priests, and they built the Sheep Gate. They made it holy and put its doors in place. As far as the Tower of the Hundred they made it holy, as far as the Tower of Hananel.
3:2 The men of Jericho built beside him, and Zaccur son of Imri built beside them.
3:3 The sons of Hassenaah built the Fish Gate. They fitted its beams, hung its doors and put in its bolts and bars.
3:4 Beside them Meremoth son of Uriah, son of Hakkoz, made repairs. Beside them Meshullam son of Berechiah, son of Meshezabel, made repairs. Beside them Zadok son of Baana made repairs.
3:5 Beside them the men of Tekoa made repairs, but their nobles would not put their necks under the work of their masters.
3:6 Joiada son of Paseah and Meshullam son of Besodeiah repaired the Jeshanah Gate. They fitted its beams, hung its doors and put in its bolts and bars.
3:7 Beside them Melatiah the Gibeonite and Jadon the Meronothite made repairs, with the men of Gibeon and of Mizpah, which belonged to the seat of the governor of Beyond the River.
3:8 Beside him Uzziel son of Harhaiah, one of the goldsmiths, made repairs. Beside him Hananiah, one of the perfume makers, made repairs. They made Jerusalem whole again as far as the Broad Wall.
3:9 Beside them Rephaiah son of Hur, the official over half the district of Jerusalem, made repairs.
3:10 Beside them Jedaiah son of Harumaph made repairs in front of his own house. Beside him Hattush son of Hashabneiah made repairs.
3:11 Malchijah son of Harim and Hasshub son of Pahath-moab repaired a second stretch, along with the Tower of the Ovens.
3:12 Beside him Shallum son of Hallohesh, the official over half the district of Jerusalem, made repairs, he and his daughters.
3:13 Hanun and the people living in Zanoah repaired the Valley Gate. They rebuilt it, hung its doors and put in its bolts and bars. Their work also covered a thousand cubits of the wall, up to the Dung Gate.
3:14 Malchijah son of Rechab, the official over the district of Beth-haccherem, repaired the Dung Gate. He rebuilt it, hung its doors and put in its bolts and bars.
3:15 Shallun son of Col-hozeh, the official over the district of Mizpah, repaired the Spring Gate. He rebuilt it, put a roof over it, hung its doors and put in its bolts and bars. He also repaired the wall of the Pool of Shelah by the king's garden, as far as the steps that go down from the City of David.
3:16 After him Nehemiah son of Azbuk, the official over half the district of Beth-zur, worked as far as the spot across from David's tombs, as far as the pool that had been dug and the Warriors' House.
3:17 After him the Levites made repairs: Rehum son of Bani. Beside him Hashabiah, the official over half the district of Keilah, made repairs for his district.
3:18 After him their brothers made repairs: Bavvai son of Henadad, the official over half the district of Keilah.
3:19 Beside him Ezer son of Jeshua, the official of Mizpah, repaired a second stretch, across from the way up to the armory at the bend in the wall.
3:20 After him Baruch son of Zabbai worked hard to repair a second stretch, from the bend in the wall to the doorway of the house of Eliashib the high priest.
3:21 After him Meremoth son of Uriah, son of Hakkoz, repaired a second stretch, from the doorway of Eliashib's house to the far end of Eliashib's house.
3:22 After him the priests, the men from the surrounding plain, made repairs.
3:23 After him came Benjamin and Hasshub, repairing the part in front of their own house. After him Azariah son of Maaseiah, son of Ananiah, repaired the part beside his own house.
3:24 After him Binnui son of Henadad repaired a second stretch, from Azariah's house to the bend in the wall and on to the corner.
3:25 Palal son of Uzai worked across from the bend in the wall and the tower that juts out from the king's upper palace, by the courtyard of the guard. After him, Pedaiah son of Parosh
3:26 and the temple servants who lived on the Ophel worked as far as a point across from the Water Gate on the east, and the tower that juts out.
3:27 After him the men of Tekoa repaired a second stretch, from across from the great tower that juts out, as far as the wall of the Ophel.
3:28 From the Horse Gate on up, the priests worked, each across from his own house.
3:29 After them Zadok son of Immer made repairs in front of his own house. After him Shemaiah son of Shecaniah, the keeper of the East Gate, made repairs.
3:30 After him Hananiah son of Shelemiah and Hanun, the sixth son of Zalaph, repaired a second stretch. After him Meshullam son of Berechiah made repairs in front of his own room.
3:31 After him Malchijah, a member of the goldsmiths' guild, made repairs as far as the house of the temple servants and the traders, across from the Miphkad Gate, and as far as the upper room at the corner.
3:32 Between the upper room at the corner and the Sheep Gate, the goldsmiths and the traders made repairs.
## Mockery and Prayer
4:1 Word reached Sanballat that we were building the wall. It made him burn inside, and he grew very angry. He made fun of the Jews.
4:2 In front of his brothers and the army of Samaria he said, "What do these worn-out Jews think they are doing? Will they fix it up by themselves? Will they sacrifice? Will they be done in one day? Will they bring stones back to life out of piles of dust, and burned stones at that?"
4:3 At his side stood Tobiah the Ammonite. "That thing they are building!" he said. "If a fox went up on it, he would breach their wall of stones."
4:4 Listen, our God! We have become something to sneer at. Make their scorn fall back on their own heads. Hand them over as loot in a land where they are captives.
4:5 Do not cover up their wrongdoing. Do not let their sin be wiped out of your sight. They have stirred up anger right in front of the builders.
4:6 We kept building the wall, and the whole wall was linked up to half its height. The people's heart was in the work.
## A Plot and a Guard
4:7 Then Sanballat and Tobiah, with the Arabs, the Ammonites and the people of Ashdod, heard that Jerusalem's walls were healing and the breaks were beginning to be filled. Their anger burned hot.
4:8 Every one of them joined in a plot to come and attack Jerusalem and throw it into confusion.
4:9 We prayed to our God, and because of them we kept watchmen posted against them day and night.
4:10 And Judah said, "The porters' strength has stumbled, / and the rubble is too much. / We are not able to build the wall."
4:11 Our foes were saying, "They will not know a thing or see a thing until we are in the middle of them. We will kill them and bring the work to an end."
4:12 The Jews who lived near them kept coming and telling us, ten times, "From all the places where you turn back, they are upon us."
4:13 So in the lowest spots behind the wall, at the bare places, I set people in position. I posted them family by family, with their swords, their spears and their bows.
4:14 I saw how things stood. I rose and spoke to the nobles, the deputies and all the other people: "Do not fear them! Remember the Lord, the great and fearsome one, and fight for your brothers, for your sons and daughters, for your wives and your houses."
## Building with Weapons in Hand
4:15 Our enemies heard that their plan was known to us and that God had wrecked it. Then all of us went back to the wall, every man to his task.
4:16 From then on, half my young men worked at the job, and half held the spears, the shields, the bows and the coats of armor. The officers stood behind the whole house of Judah,
4:17 the people building on the wall. The ones who carried loads did their lifting with one hand on the work and the other hand gripping a weapon.
4:18 Every builder worked with his sword tied on at his waist. The man who blew the ram's horn was next to me.
4:19 I told the nobles, the deputies and everyone else, "There is a lot of work, and it covers a wide area. We are strung out along the wall, each far from his brother.
4:20 When you hear the ram's horn blow, gather to us at that spot. Our God will fight for us."
4:21 So we went on with the work. Half of them held spears from the time the dawn rose until the stars appeared.
4:22 I also gave the people this order at that time: "Every man is to stay the night inside Jerusalem, with his servant. Then at night they will guard us, and by day they will work."
4:23 None of us took our clothes off: not I, not my brothers, not my servants, not the guardsmen who followed me. Each man's weapon was with him at the water.
## The Outcry of the Poor
5:1 Then the people and their wives cried out loudly against their brothers, the Jews.
5:2 Some said, "Counting our sons and daughters, there are many of us. Let us take grain, so we can eat and live."
5:3 Others said, "Our fields, our vineyards and our houses are pledged as security, so that we can get grain in this famine."
5:4 And others said, "To pay the tribute to the king, we borrowed silver on our fields and vineyards.
5:5 We are made of the same flesh as our brothers, and our children are like their children. But look: we are handing over our sons and daughters to be slaves. Some of our daughters are slaves already, and there is nothing we can do, because other people own our fields and vineyards."
## The Debts Cancelled
5:6 Hearing their cry and what they said made me burn with anger.
5:7 My heart weighed it, and I took the nobles and the deputies to task. "You are lending on pledge, each of you against his own brother!" I told them. Then I called a great assembly against them.
5:8 I said to them, "We have done what we could to buy back our Jewish brothers who were sold to the nations. And you, you sell your brothers, and then they are sold to us!" They said nothing. They had no answer.
5:9 And I said, "This thing you are doing is wrong. Surely you should walk in the fear of our God, to keep the nations, our enemies, from scorning us.
5:10 I myself, my brothers and my young men are lending them silver and grain as well. Please, let us drop this lending on pledge.
5:11 Please, today, give them back their fields, vineyards, olive trees and houses. Give back also the hundredth part of the silver, the grain, the new wine and the fresh oil that you are lending them."
5:12 They answered, "We will return it, and we will ask nothing more of them. We will do exactly what you tell us." So I summoned the priests and had them put the men under oath to keep this word.
5:13 I also shook out the front of my robe. "This is how God will shake out every man who does not carry out this word," I said. "He will shake him out of his house and his earnings. Let him be shaken out like this and left empty." The whole assembly answered, "Amen," and they praised the LORD. And the people did what they had agreed.
## A Governor Who Did Not Take
5:14 There is more. For twelve years, from the day I was named their governor in the land of Judah, from King Artaxerxes' twentieth year to his thirty-second, I and my brothers never ate the bread due the governor.
5:15 The earlier governors who came before me weighed the people down. They took bread and wine from them, and forty shekels of silver on top of that. Their young men also bossed the people around. I did not do this, because I feared God.
5:16 I also kept a strong grip on the work of this wall. We did not get any field for ourselves. All my young men were gathered there at the work.
5:17 My table fed the Jews and the deputies, 150 men, and also those who came to us from the nations all around.
5:18 What was made ready for me each day was one ox, six of the best sheep and some birds. Once in ten days there was wine of every kind, plenty of it. Even so I did not ask for the governor's bread, because the labor lay heavy on this people.
5:19 My God, remember me for good, for all I have done for this people.
## An Invitation to Ono
6:1 News reached Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab and our other enemies: I had built the wall again, and not one break remained in it. (I had not yet hung the doors in the gates at that time.)
6:2 Sanballat and Geshem sent me word: "Come, let us meet with each other at Kephirim, in the valley of Ono." They meant to do me harm.
6:3 I sent men back to them with the message, "It is a big work I am doing, and I am not able to come down. Should the work come to a halt while I drop it and go down to you?"
6:4 They sent this message four times, and four times my reply was the same.
## The Open Letter
6:5 A fifth time Sanballat sent the same message, this time by his servant, who held an open letter.
6:6 Written in it was this: "Word has gone around among the nations, and Gashmu is saying it too: you and the Jews are planning to revolt. That is why you are building the wall. By this account, you are going to be their king.
6:7 And you have set up prophets to announce about you in Jerusalem, 'A king in Judah!' Now the king is going to hear about all this. So come, and let us make plans together."
6:8 I sent him an answer: "None of the things you talk about have taken place. You are dreaming them up out of your own heart."
6:9 They all wanted to scare us. They were saying, "Their hands will go limp and leave the work, and it will never get done." So now, make my hands strong!
## The Hired Prophet
6:10 I went to the house of Shemaiah son of Delaiah, son of Mehetabel. He was shut away indoors. He said, "Let's get together at the house of God, inside the sanctuary. Let's shut the sanctuary doors. They are coming to kill you. At night they are coming to kill you."
6:11 I answered, "Is a man like me going to run? And who like me could go into the sanctuary and stay alive? I am not going."
6:12 Then it dawned on me: God had not sent him at all. He had delivered that prophecy about me because Tobiah and Sanballat were paying him.
6:13 That is what he was paid for: to make me afraid, so that I would do this and sin. Then they would have something to ruin my good name with, and they could heap scorn on me.
6:14 My God, remember Tobiah and Sanballat for these deeds of theirs. Remember too Noadiah the prophetess, and the other prophets who kept trying to scare me.
## The Wall Finished
6:15 The wall was done in fifty-two days. It was finished on the twenty-fifth of the month Elul.
6:16 All our enemies heard of it. Every nation around us grew afraid, and they sank low in their own eyes. They knew that it was our God who had made this work happen.
6:17 In those same days, too, Judah's nobles kept up a busy flow of letters going to Tobiah, and letters from Tobiah kept coming to them.
6:18 Many people in Judah had sworn loyalty to him. Tobiah was a son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had taken as wife the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah.
6:19 They also kept praising his good deeds to my face, and they carried what I said back to him. And Tobiah sent letters to scare me.
## Guarding the City
7:1 The wall was built and I had hung its doors. Gatekeepers, singers and Levites were put in their posts.
7:2 I gave charge over Jerusalem to my brother Hanani and to Hananiah, the commander of the fortress. Hananiah was a man you could trust, and he feared God more than many others did.
7:3 I gave them orders: "Jerusalem's gates must stay closed until the sun grows hot. While the guards are still at their stations, they are to close the doors and bolt them. Set watches made up of Jerusalem's own people, each man at his post, each man in front of his own house."
7:4 The city was spread out wide and was big, yet only a few people lived inside it, and the houses were not built yet.
