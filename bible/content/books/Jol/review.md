# Hostile review: Joel (Jol), whole book

Reviewer: separate agent (not the writer). Checked: plain.txt against `node tools/literal.js Jol 1-3`; notes, variants, glosses, chapters, book.json, topics, sources; `texts/orig/Jol.json` (incl. Ketiv/Qere and the `heb` numbering map); Swete's Greek via `tools/lxx.py Joe 1-4`; `tools/dss.py Jol 1-3` (4Q78, 4Q82, Mur88); the Plain of Exodus, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Amos, Jonah, Obadiah, Ezekiel, Acts, Romans on disk; `content/atlas/Jol.json` and `corrections/Jol.json`; works.json. `node tools/build.js --check Jol` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The Plain is sound. Verse by verse it follows the Hebrew closely, keeps the site's terms (nacham as "changes his mind about the harm" matches Jeremiah 18:8-10, 26:3, 13, Amos 7:3, 6 and Jonah 3:9-10, 4:2; the Exodus 34:6 creed, Isaiah 13:6, Isaiah 2:4 reversed, Obadiah 15 and 17 are all matched exactly), and I found no verse that copies the NIV or ESV wholesale. The prose is clean (no em-dashes, no "from memory"), the numbering map in the Hebrew data is right, and the Ketiv/Qere at 3:1 is real. The problems are in the apparatus. (1) The book says the Greek numbers Joel like English; that is true only of Swete's printout, not of the Greek editions or the Orthodox lectionary, and it contradicts the writer's own Orthodox card. (2) The Jewish and patristic lenses are promised and then not delivered: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Radak/Kimhi, the Targum, Ephrem, Cyril, Theodoret, Pusey and Chrysostom appear in zero notes, so the locust and date debates lack their old voices. (3) Several scroll reports are inaccurate or incomplete (1:17, 3:3, 3:21, 2:11, the book-level summary). (4) Sukkah 52a is garbled and Hubbard is probably wrongly placed in the early-date camp. (5) Joel 3:16 and 3:18 do not match Amos 1:2 and 9:13 on disk, and a note misquotes Amos 9:13. There are about 25 smaller slips. Nothing is invented outright, so I list no critical findings; items 1 to 8 should be fixed before publishing.

Count: critical 0, major 8, minor 24.

## Major

**1. Major. book.json `facts` "Numbering"; notes 2:28 and 3:1; variants. The Greek numbering claim is wrong, and Greek chapter:verse is never given.**
book.json says "The Greek and Latin Bibles number as the English do"; the 2:28 note says the English chapters "follow the Greek Bible and the Latin Vulgate"; the 3:1 note says "The Greek and Latin Bibles number the verses as the English do." That is true of the Vulgate and of Swete's printout (`lxx.py Joe` prints 2:28-32 and 3:1-21). It is not true of the Greek editions in use. The Göttingen/Rahlfs text, Brenton, and the Greek Orthodox lectionary all number Joel 3:1-5 (= English 2:28-32) and 4:1-21 (= English 3). The writer's own Orthodox card (chapter 2) cites "Joel 2:23-3:5" for Pentecost Vespers, which is that Greek numbering, and brief-twelve says "the Greek of Hosea-Joel-Amos follows the Hebrew numbering except as the printout shows." brief-twelve also requires the Greek chapter and verse whenever the Greek is cited; none of the notes (2:23, 2:28, 2:32, 3:4, 3:11, 3:14, 3:21) gives it.
Fix: say the Hebrew and the critical Greek (Rahlfs) share the four-chapter numbering, and the Vulgate, the English, and Swete's printout use the three-chapter one. Add "Greek 3:1" and so on where the Greek is cited. Mention that Orthodox lectionaries use "Joel 2:23-3:5" for English 2:23-32.

**2. Major. Jewish, patristic and Reformation voices are promised and absent (notes.json, chapters.json, book.json lenses and `sources`).**
Fairness caution (4) in brief-twelve and the J and X lenses in brief-jol require Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi/Radak, the Targum, Jerome, Ephrem, Calvin, Pusey and the fathers on the locust debate and the date. Count of mentions: in notes.json, Rashi 0, Ibn Ezra 0, Radak 0, Kimhi 0, Targum 0, Cyril 0, Theodoret 0, Chrysostom 0, Ephrem 0, Pusey 0, Luther 0, Calvin 0, Peshitta 0, Syriac 0. In chapters.json each appears only in a single generic sentence ("Rashi and Ibn Ezra generally take the chapter as a real locust plague"; "Cyril of Alexandria and Theodoret wrote commentaries on the Twelve"). book.json lists `rashi-twelve`, `ibnezra-twelve`, `radak-twelve`, `cyril-twelve`, `theodoret-twelve`, `calvin-twelve`, `pusey-minor` and `cathcart-gordon-targum-minor` in `sources`, but no note cites them. Gaps that matter: the four locusts at 1:4 and 2:25 (Targum, Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Radak on kinds, stages or nations; Mishnah Taanit 3:5 lists the same locust words, which is a nice point and not used); 2:20 "the northern one" (Radak and others on Gog; Ephrem and Jerome on the Assyrian or end-time foe); 2:23 (the Targum's reading of moreh; Rashi and Ibn Ezra on "rain"); 3:2 (Rashi and Ibn Ezra on the valley as Jehoshaphat's victory, 2 Chronicles 20; the Jewish reading is mentioned only as "real valley near Tekoa"); 3:21 (the old versions, the brief says "hard in Hebrew, Greek and Syriac" and no Syriac is mentioned); Pusey, Theodoret, Cyril and Ephrem on 2:28-32 and on the locusts as an army. Also the chapter 1 line "Rashi and Ibn Ezra generally take the chapter as a real locust plague" is not sourced to any edition or passage.
Fix: add notes (or fill the J and X lens sentences) with named, checkable statements and sources from each commentator, or cut the names from the lens descriptions and `sources` and say plainly that the notes do not survey them. Verify each claim before adding; do not add a name without a passage.

**3. Major. chapters.json ch. 2 `views.J` (and the matching claim in the lead): Sukkah 52a is garbled.**
The text says the Talmud reads "the northern one" as the evil inclination, "which it says lives in the north of the heart." Sukkah 52a does not say the inclination lives in the north. It plays on the Hebrew: hatsefoni is read as tsafun, "hidden", the yetzer hara being the one hidden (tsafun) in a person's heart; the baraita lists it as one of the seven names of the evil inclination. The pun is the point.
Fix: "reads hatsefoni (the northern one) as tsafun, 'hidden': the evil inclination, which hides in a person's heart (Sukkah 52a, among its seven names for the inclination)."

**4. Major. notes 1:1 (views), book.json `facts` Date and `authorship`, topics.json `joel` uncommon: Hubbard is probably misplaced in the early-date camp.**
Hubbard is named three times with Stuart as a ninth-century (Joash) advocate. A search for his Tyndale commentary (1989) turns up a reviewer saying it largely agrees with Dillard on the date, and Dillard favors an early post-exilic date; the replacement TOTC volume by Hadjiev finds little to demand an early date. I could not read Hubbard's own conclusion, but the attribution is unverified and likely wrong. Also the book.json "Early date" row says "and by some Jewish tradition", while the same page says Seder Olam places Joel under Manasseh (a late pre-exilic date) and no Jewish authority is named for the ninth century.
Fix: check Hubbard's "The Date of the Book" section; if he is not early, replace him (Stuart; Garrett only if checked) in all three places, and name the Jewish authorities or drop the phrase. Keep `hubbard-joel-amos` as a source only if cited for what he does say.

**5. Major. variants.json 1:17, 3:21, 3:4; notes 1:17, 1:12, 2:11; book.json "The Hebrew and Greek texts": the scroll reports are inaccurate or incomplete.**
Checked against the `dss.py` printout.
- 1:17: the note says 4Q78 "has different spellings of the first and last words"; the printout (and the variant) show the first and second words, «עפשו» «פורות», against MT עבשו פרדות. The variant says "too short to decide", but misses the point that פורות (no dalet) is exactly the form that can be read פרות, "heifers", which is what the Greek has. The scroll is the best Hebrew support in the whole book for a Greek reading and the variant and "why" should say so (while noting that it may be a spelling of perudot).
- 3:21: the variant says 4Q78 "preserves 'their blood' and the second 'I cleared', and the rest of the verse is restored." The printout shows `[ונקיתי] דמם [לוא] נקיתי ויהוה שכן בציון`: the last clause, "and the LORD dwells in Zion", is intact, and the first verb is restored (so the scroll does not confirm the doubled "I will clear").
- 3:3: Mur88 reads «ועל» עמי (`al`, "over my people") where the MT has ואל עמי (`el`). A real, if small, variant that is nowhere mentioned.
- 2:11: 4Q78 reads «יכלכלנו» (the note mentions it only as "sustain it"); 4Q82 has the MT form restored. Fine in the note, but book.json says the scrolls "agree with the Masoretic Text, except for small spellings and the readings in the variants at 1:17, 2:19 and 3:8." That is wrong in three ways: it leaves out 2:11, 3:3, and the transposition at 1:12 (4Q78 puts "pomegranate" after "apple", which the note mentions but 4Q82 agrees with MT, which the note does not say); and 3:8 is not a reading at all (the variant itself says it is a restoration).
- Places where a scroll preserves a verse and the notes say nothing (brief-twelve: "mention every scroll that preserves a place, including those that agree"): 2:13 (4Q78 preserves the whole creed, with only "slow to anger" and "the harm" restored; 4Q82 stops at "return to the LORD"), 2:17 (4Q78 has limshol, consonants unpointed), 2:20 (4Q78 and Mur88 both read hatsefoni), 3:4 (Mur88 agrees with the MT; the variant lists only 4Q82), 3:19 (4Q78 repeats lamidbar, a visible doubling).
Fix: correct the 1:17 note and variant, the 3:21 "why", add 3:3 (Mur88) as a variant or note, and rewrite the book.json sentence from the printout.

**6. Major. variants.json: two significant Greek and Acts differences are missing, and brief-jol's "likely entries" are mostly missing.**
- 2:28 and 2:29: the Greek (and Acts 2:17-18 after it) has ἐκχεῶ ἀπὸ τοῦ πνεύματός μου, "I will pour out of / some of my Spirit", where the Hebrew has the plain object "my Spirit". The long note on 2:28 lists nine differences between Acts and Joel and omits this one, which matters for the Pentecost card and the continuationist debate. Add it to the note and as a variant. (The Acts Plain "I will pour out my Spirit" hides it too; flag for the Acts alignment pass.)
- 2:32: the Greek ends "and those who are given good news / who announce good news, whom the LORD has called" (καὶ εὐαγγελιζόμενοι), reading the Hebrew ubasseridim as a form of the root for "tell good news", not "survivors". The 2:32 note says nothing. It is a famous reading and Romans 10:15 is nearby.
- brief-jol lists likely entries 1:12, 1:17, 2:2, 2:20, 2:23, 2:28, 3:6, 3:13, 3:21. The book has 8 variants and none at 1:12, 2:2, 2:20, 2:28, 3:6 or 3:13 (2:2 shachar/shachor and 3:13 redu are handled in notes only). Make 2:2, 2:28, 2:32 and 3:1 (Ketiv אשוב / Qere אשיב, which `texts/auto` records and Mur88 shows restored) variants, and point to them from the notes.

**7. Major. plain.txt 3:16 and 3:18 versus Amos 1:2 and 9:13 on disk (the alignment the lead asked for).**
The exact wording now on disk:
- Joel 3:16: "The LORD roars from Zion / and gives out his voice from Jerusalem."
- Amos 1:2: "The LORD roars from Zion / and sends out his voice from Jerusalem."
The Hebrew is identical (יתן קולו) and Joel's own 2:11 uses "gives out his voice". The Amos Plain is the one that is off ("sends out"); fix there, or choose one pair. The notes say the lines are "word for word" in the Hebrew, which is right, so the English should be identical. Note the Hebrew of Amos 1:2 opens "He said", so only the second line matches in full.
- Joel 3:18: "On that day sweet wine will drip from the mountains, / milk will run down the hills, ..."
- Amos 9:13: "The mountains will drip with sweet wine, / and all the hills will flow with it."
The shared Hebrew is יטפו ההרים עסיס / "the mountains drip sweet wine" in the same order. Joel recasts it ("sweet wine will drip from the mountains"), so the match cannot be seen. The notes (3:18, and the lead) say they are "nearly" the same, but the English hides it. Fix: "The mountains will drip with sweet wine, / and the hills will flow with milk" (or match whichever form the lead sets).
The 3:18 note also misquotes Amos 9:13: it says Amos has "all the hills will melt" and that Amos 9:13 "closes Amos"; the Amos Plain has "all the hills will flow with it" and the book closes at 9:15. Fix the note to quote the Amos Plain exactly.

**8. Major. notes 3:2 and topic `valley-of-jehoshaphat`, atlas: the Valley is put on a map point in debated territory (and 3 mapped places are missing).**
This is lower on the scale than the others but affects what readers see: `content/atlas/Jol.json` puts "Valley of Jehoshaphat" at 31.772, 35.2366 (the Kidron). Joel's name is a pun (the LORD judges) and the note itself says the identification with the Kidron is a later (fourth-century) tradition. brief-twelve says the Valley is symbolic and "not necessarily a mapped valley." `corrections/Jol.json` drops esau, jehoshaphat-3 and israel, which is right (see the check below), but does nothing about the point. Also missing from the harvest: Philistia (3:4), Sheba (3:8) and Greece/Ionia (3:6), all named in the Plain and all with settings and notes.
Fix: in `content/atlas/points.json` set the Valley's lat/lon to null, or at least add a `why` saying the Kidron is a later tradition; add Philistia and Sheba through a correction `add`.

## Minor

9. **plain.txt 1:3.** "They will tell their children, and those children a later generation." The Hebrew has no verb in the second and third clauses and continues the imperative of the first ("recount"), so the sense is "let your children tell". The Plain turns a command into a prediction. Fix: "Tell your children about it. Let them tell their children, and those children the next generation."

10. **notes 1:6 (views).** "Proverbs 30:25-27 calls ants and locusts 'a people'". In Proverbs 30 the ants are "a people" (v. 25), the hyraxes too (v. 26), and the locusts "have no king" (v. 27). Fix: "calls ants a people and says locusts have no king, yet go out in ranks".

11. **notes 1:11 (and 1:12, 1:17).** The note is garbled. It says hovish "in 1:10, 12 ... and yavesh in 1:12 and 1:17 come from the root yavesh"; 1:17 is hovish (the printout and MT: כי הביש דגן), not yavesh; yavesh appears in 1:12 (the trees) and 1:20 (the channels). "In the written form the two roots look alike" is misleading: bosh and yavesh are different roots whose hiphil forms (hovish) coincide. Rewrite plainly: the hiphil of bosh ("be ashamed") and the hiphil of yavesh ("be dry") are the same form, hovish; the Plain chooses "Be ashamed" at 1:11, "has gone dry" at 1:10, 1:17, "dried up" at 1:12.

12. **notes 1:15 (history).** "more than in any other short book". Zephaniah has the phrase or its near-forms at 1:7, 1:14 (twice), 1:18 and 2:2-3, and Amos 5:18 (twice) and 5:20. Fix: "five times, as many as in Zephaniah".

13. **plain.txt 1:15 versus 2:1 and 3:14.** qarov is "close" at 1:15 (to match Isaiah 13:6) and "near" at 2:1, 3:14, and in Ezekiel 30:3 and Obadiah 15. The note says the Plain matches Isaiah but does not explain why the other three differ within one book. Add a clause, or use "near" in all (and let Isaiah differ).

14. **plain.txt 2:2.** Hebrew order is "a day of darkness (choshekh) and gloom (afelah)"; the Plain reverses it ("gloom and darkness") without reason, and adds "comes" to "a people comes". Fix: "A day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and deep darkness."

15. **notes 2:3.** "Joel 2:3 is the only place where Eden is a picture of what is about to be destroyed." Overclaim: Ezekiel 31:8-9, 16-18 (the cedar and the trees of Eden brought down) is a close parallel. Say "one of the few".

16. **plain.txt 2:17 and 2:18.** "Have pity" (chusah) and "took pity" (vayachmol) translate two roots. The note on 2:18 is fine, but an English reader will not see the echo as the Hebrew has it. Consider "Spare your people" at 2:17, as Plain readers will know from the Hebrew chus, and keep "took pity" at 2:18. Not required.

17. **plain.txt 2:23.** (a) "People of Zion" for benei Tsiyyon ("children/sons of Zion"); check the site's term (Psalm 149:2, Lamentations 4:2) and use it. (b) "the early rain for righteousness" is opaque to an eighth-grade reader, and the note then says only that it "leaves the sense open". Consider a clearer phrase in the Plain ("the early rain, as a sign that he has set things right") with the Hebrew in the note; the choice is yours, but the line as it stands reads like a mistake.

18. **variants.json 2:23 and notes 2:23.** "Jerome's Vulgate (about AD 405)". The source key `jerome-twelve` dates his Twelve commentary to about AD 406, and the Vulgate Twelve was translated about AD 393. Use "Jerome's Vulgate (about AD 393)" or just "the Vulgate".

19. **plain.txt 2:30.** The Hebrew order is "I will set wonders in the heavens and on the earth: blood and fire and pillars of smoke." The Plain turns it around ("Blood and fire ...: these are the wonders I will set"), adding "these are" and moving the signs first. The note defends it ("turns the sentence around to put the signs first"), but the ordinary order is easy English; the restructure looks like drift-avoidance, which the brief does not ask for. Fix: restore the Hebrew order.

20. **plain.txt 2:32 versus Acts 2:21 and Romans 10:13 (site Plain).** Joel has "will be rescued"; Acts and Romans on disk have "will be saved". The note explains yimmalet and sothesetai, but a reader who follows the quotation sees two different words. Either use "saved" in Joel (with the Hebrew gloss in the note) or leave a sentence in the note stating the choice.

21. **notes 3:13 (Come down, or tread?).** "radah, 'tread, rule' (as in Isaiah 63:3's treading of the winepress)". Isaiah 63:3 uses darakh (and puroh for the press), not radah. The image is shared; the verb is not. Fix the sentence.

22. **notes 3:16 (myth).** "Psalm 29 uses 'the voice of the LORD' seven times ... and Joel's 'the LORD gives out his voice' uses the same Hebrew expression (natan qol)." Psalm 29 uses qol YHWH, not natan qol; the cited parallels for natan qol are Psalm 18:13, 46:6, 68:33. The Baal citation (KTU 1.4 vii) is right. Fix the sentence.

23. **traditions cards (chapter 2).** (a) Holy-spirit card: no Baptist view, though brief-jol plans it and Baptists (Southern Baptist, BF&M) are a distinct voice on tongues; (b) the Anglican and Baptist views on the fasting card have no source key and the gods-regret card's Methodist and Evangelical views have none either, with `s: []`, against "source each to the church's own teachers" (cite the Book of Common Prayer or the Articles; the Baptist Faith and Message; Wesley's Notes on the New Testament or his sermon on divine foreknowledge); (c) gods-regret card has no Lutheran view though brief-twelve lists it (Formula of Concord, or Luther's lectures on Genesis, for example).

24. **chapters.json ch. 2 `settings` 2:28-32 and ch. 2 `views.H`.** The "why" says "Knowing the first hearers shows that the promise first meant Israel." That is one of the two listed views (Israel first; all peoples), stated as settled. Say "on the first reading" or keep it in the `views`. Likewise `audience.why` in book.json calls chapter 2 "the answer to a prayer already prayed", which assumes the past reading of 2:18 that the 2:18 note and the 2:18-27 settings view keep open.

25. **chapters.json ch. 3 `views.C` and `views.H`.** Typo "Chapters 3 (Hebrew 4)". `H` says "the mention of Greeks as buyers fits best after the eighth century": the 3:6 note says an early date can find a place for Greek traders (Genesis 10, Assyrian records of Ionians on the coast in the later eighth century); keep the two sentences consistent and state the early-date reply.

26. **glosses.json 3:17 and 3:3.** "outsiders = foreign invaders and strangers" picks one reading of zarim that the 3:17 note keeps open (invasion or a ban on foreigners). Use "strangers, people from outside". The 3:3 gloss "threw dice" for "cast lots" is loose; "drew lots" is safer.

27. **notes 2:10.** The NT echoes list Matthew 24:29 only; add Mark 13:24-25 and Luke 21:25-26. The Spirit-poured-out echoes are also missing: Acts 10:45, Titus 3:5-6 and John 7:39. (Brief-twelve: "a note on each" use of Joel 2:28-32; Acts 2 and Romans 10 are covered.)

28. **notes 1:12 (4Q78).** The note reports the order change but not that 4Q82 has the MT order and "pomegranate" before "palm and apple". Add it.

29. **notes 1:19 / 3:21 / 2:17 (minor wording).** 3:18 note: "Amos 9:13 closes Amos" (see item 7). 2:17 note describes the infinitive of mashal "as a noun" confusingly (two homonymous roots, mashal I "rule" and mashal II "be a byword"). Rewrite the sentence.

30. **notes 1:8 (symbol).** "a bride whose husband has died before the wedding feast" is not in the Hebrew: ba'al can mean a husband or a betrothed man (Deuteronomy 22:23-24). Say that, and delete the "before the wedding feast" detail.

31. **notes 2:25 (spec) and 2:27 (spec).** Both are labeled Claude's theory and are fair. The first says "I know of no published argument for either"; commentators (e.g. Wolff, Crenshaw) do discuss the reversed order in 2:25, so soften to "I have not found a published argument".

32. **chapters.json ch. 1 `views.F`, ch. 2 `views.X`.** "Calvin's commentary on Acts takes Peter's words as a claim of fulfillment" and "Chrysostom's homilies on Acts" are plausible but unsourced in the file (no `s` key for Calvin or Chrysostom); add `calvin-acts` and a Chrysostom key or cut.

Fixed. book.json Numbering, the 2:28 and 3:1 notes, the Hebrew-and-Greek-texts section and the Orthodox card now say exactly which editions use which numbering (Rahlfs and the Greek Orthodox lectionary: 3:1-5 and 4:1-21; Swete's printout, the Vulgate and English: 2:28-32 and 3:1-21, the Vulgate checked on vulgate.org). The Greek chapter and verse (Rahlfs and Swete) are given in the notes at 2:23, 2:28, 2:32, 3:1, 3:4, 3:11, 3:13, 3:14, 3:21 and in the variants. The Orthodox "Joel 2:23-3:5" is explained as the Greek numbering of English 2:23-32 and kept as "among its readings". Brenton's numbering was not checked and is not claimed.
2. Partly fixed. Checked on Sefaria and added: Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Radak on the four locusts (new note 1:4), on the northern one (new note 2:20, with Sukkah 52a), on moreh (new note 2:23, Rashi only) and on the valley (3:2); Numbers Rabbah 15:25 (chapter 2 J). Mishnah Taanit 3:5 (arbeh and chasil among the troubles that call for an alarm) is added to chapter 1 J. Not verified, so not credited with any view: the Targum, Ephrem, Cyril, Theodoret, Chrysostom, Luther, Calvin on Joel, Pusey and the Peshitta. The J and X lens descriptions and the chapter 1-3 views say so plainly; cyril-twelve, theodoret-twelve, calvin-twelve, pusey-minor and cathcart-gordon-targum-minor are cut from book.json sources. The unsourced "Rashi and Ibn Ezra generally take the chapter as a real locust plague" is replaced by the checked statements.
3. Fixed. Chapter 2 J now says Sukkah 52a reads hatsefoni as tsafun, "hidden": the evil inclination hiding in a person's heart, among its seven names in the baraita (checked on Sefaria).
4. Fixed. Hubbard's conclusion could not be verified (a search found only that Hadjiev's replacement Tyndale volume sees little need for an early date), so he is removed from the early camp in the 1:1 note, book.json (Date, Early date row, audience s) and the joel topic's uncommon line, and hubbard-joel-amos is cut from the book's sources. "And by some Jewish tradition" is dropped from the early-date row; the 1:1 note says no Jewish authority for a ninth-century date was found and that Seder Olam gives a late pre-exilic date.
5. Fixed. The 1:17 note and variant now give the printout's first two words (afshu, purot) and say purot can be read parot "heifers", the best Hebrew support for the Greek, or perudot with a letter lost. The 3:21 variant and note say 4Q78 shows "their blood", the second "I cleared" and the final clause, with the first verb restored. The 3:3 Mur88 al/el is a new variant. book.json's scroll sentence is rewritten from the printout (3:8 is no longer called a reading; 2:11, 3:19 and the new variants are named). Scroll mentions added at 1:12 (4Q82 keeps the MT order), 2:2 (4Q82), 2:11, 2:13 (4Q78 preserves most of the creed; 4Q82 stops early), 2:17 (unpointed), 2:20 (4Q78 northern one; Mur88 "great things"), 3:4 (Mur88 agrees; 4Q82 spelling), 3:6 (4Q82 and Mur88) and 3:19 (4Q78 repeats "to a wilderness", the first occurrence restored).
6. Fixed. New variants: 1:12, 2:2, 2:20, 2:28 (Greek and Acts "from my Spirit"), 2:32 (Greek "those who bring good news"), 3:1 (Ketiv ashuv, Qere ashiv; Mur88's verb is restored, so it is not cited), 3:3 and 3:13 (redu; Vulgate "venite et descendite" checked). There are 16 variants now. Not made a variant: 3:6 (Greek and Hebrew agree in sense; only a 4Q78 spelling differs). The Plain of Acts 2:17-18 ("I will pour out my Spirit") hides the Greek apo; flagged for the Acts alignment pass.
7. Partly fixed. Lead decision: Joel 3:16 stays as the model and Amos 1:2 on disk now matches ("gives out his voice"); Joel 3:18 stays. The 3:18 note's misquote of Amos 9:13 is fixed to the Amos Plain on disk ("The mountains will drip with sweet wine", "all the hills will flow with it") and says Amos 9:13 is near, not at, the end of Amos. The note says the first clauses share verb, subject and object in the same Hebrew order (yitfu or vehittifu heharim 'asis) but differ in verb form, and the second clauses differ (telakhnah chalav, titmogagnah). The Hebrew order is the same in both, so it is the English order of Joel that hides the match. The 3:16 note says Amos 1:2 begins "He said:".
8. Partly fixed. In corrections/Jol.json the Valley of Jehoshaphat is unmapped in chapter 3 (the name is symbolic; the Kidron is a later tradition), and Philistia and Sheba (at Marib) are added with approximate points. The global points.json is not changed. The atlas review still has to look at them.
9. Fixed. Plain 1:3 is now "Tell your children about it. / Let your children tell their children, / and their children the next generation."; the 1:3 note explains the continued command.
10. Fixed. The 1:6 note says Proverbs 30:25-26 calls the ants and the rock badgers each "a people" and 30:27 says the locusts have no king (checked against the Proverbs Plain).
11. Fixed. The 1:11 note is rewritten: two roots (bosh, yavesh) with the same causative hovish, yavesh in 1:12 and 1:20, and the Plain's choices verse by verse.
12. Fixed. The 1:15 note drops "more than in any other short book" and names Zephaniah 1:7, 14, 18, 2:2-3 and Amos 5:18-20.
13. Fixed. The 1:15 note says why the Plain has "close" (the Isaiah 13:6 Plain) and "near" at 2:1 and 3:14 (as in Ezekiel 30:3 and Obadiah 15).
14. Fixed. Plain 2:2 is "A day of darkness and gloom, / a day of cloud and deep darkness. / ... a people, many and mighty." (Hebrew order, no added "comes").

(b) It also has "the early rain to set things right" (tsedaqah "by context" in the terms), with the Hebrew options in the note and variant; the entry in proposals/prophets/terms.json is updated to match.

21. Fixed. The 3:13 note says Isaiah 63:3 and Lamentations 1:15 use darakh (puroh for the press in Isaiah), so the image is shared and the verb is not.
