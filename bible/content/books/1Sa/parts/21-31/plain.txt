## David at Nob
21:1 David came to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest. Ahimelech was shaking as he went to meet David. "Why are you by yourself," he said to him, "with nobody along?"
21:2 David told Ahimelech the priest, "The king gave me a job to do. He said to me, 'No one may know anything about the job I am sending you on or the orders I have given you.' As for my young men, I sent them off to such-and-such a spot.
21:3 So what have you got here? Put five loaves of bread in my hand, or anything that is here."
21:4 The priest answered David, "I have no everyday bread here, only holy bread, and that only if your young men have stayed away from women."
21:5 David answered the priest, "Yes, women have been off limits to us, as in the past every time I have gone out. Even on an ordinary trip the young men keep their gear holy. So surely today what they carry will be holy."
21:6 So the priest gave him what was holy. The only bread on hand was the Presence Bread, taken from the LORD's presence that day so that warm bread could be set out in its place.
21:7 That same day one of Saul's servants was there, held at the sanctuary before the LORD. He was named Doeg, an Edomite, the head of Saul's shepherds.
21:8 David said to Ahimelech, "Is there no spear or sword here at your hand? The king's matter was pressing, so I did not take my sword or my other weapons along."
21:9 The priest said, "The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of Elah, is here. Look, it is wrapped in a cloak behind the ephod. If you will take it, take it. There is nothing else here." David said, "Nothing compares to it. Give it to me."
## David plays the madman in Gath
21:10 That day David got up and ran from Saul. He came to Achish king of Gath.
21:11 The servants of Achish said to him, "Isn't this David, king of the land? Didn't they dance and sing to each other about him, 'Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his tens of thousands'?"
21:12 David kept turning these words over in his mind, and he was badly frightened of Achish king of Gath.
21:13 So in front of them he hid his good sense, and in their grip he played the madman. He scratched marks on the doors of the gate and let his drool run down into his beard.
21:14 "You can see the man is out of his mind," Achish told his servants. "What made you bring him to me?
21:15 Do I lack crazy people, that you bring this one to rant at me? Should someone like this enter my house?"
## The cave of Adullam
22:1 David got away from there and fled to the cave of Adullam. His brothers and the whole family of his father heard, and they came down to join him there.
22:2 Every man in a tight spot, every man with a lender after him, and every man with a bitter heart came and joined him. He took command of them, some four hundred men in all.
22:3 David moved on from there to Mizpah of Moab. "Please let my father and mother come out and live with you," he asked the king of Moab, "until I find out what God will do for me."
22:4 He brought them into the presence of the king of Moab. They lived with the king all the days David was in the stronghold.
22:5 Gad the prophet told David, "You must not remain in the stronghold. Be on your way. Head for the land of Judah." David set out and came to the forest of Hereth.
## Saul kills the priests of Nob
22:6 News came to Saul that David had been spotted, along with his men. Saul was sitting in Gibeah beneath the tamarisk at the high place. His spear was in his hand, and all his servants stood near him.
22:7 "Hear me, Benjaminites!" Saul said to the servants who stood by him. "Is the son of Jesse going to hand out fields and vineyards to every one of you? Will he put all of you in charge of thousands and hundreds?
22:8 You have all ganged up on me! When my son made a pact with the son of Jesse, nobody whispered it in my ear. Not one of you cares about me enough to tell me. My son has set my own servant against me, to wait in ambush for me, as he is doing now."
22:9 Doeg the Edomite spoke up. He was standing there with Saul's servants. "I saw the son of Jesse," he said. "He came to Nob, to Ahimelech son of Ahitub.
22:10 Ahimelech consulted the LORD for him. He handed him supplies, and he handed him the sword of Goliath the Philistine."
22:11 The king sent men to call Ahimelech son of Ahitub, the priest, along with his whole father's house, the priests at Nob. Every one of them came to the king.
22:12 "Hear me, son of Ahitub," said Saul. "Here I am, my lord," he said.
22:13 Saul said to him, "You and the son of Jesse, why did you plot against me? You handed him bread and a sword. You consulted God for him. Now he has risen against me to wait in ambush, as he is doing this very day."
22:14 Ahimelech answered the king, "Among all your servants, who can be trusted like David? He is the king's son-in-law and head of your personal guard. Everyone in your household holds him in honor.
22:15 Is today the first day I have consulted God for him? Far from it! The king must not lay blame on his servant or on anyone in my father's house. Your servant did not know a thing about all this, not a small thing or a big one."
22:16 "You are going to die, Ahimelech," said the king, "you and all your father's house."
22:17 The king told the runners who stood around him, "Go around and put the priests of the LORD to death. Their hand is with David too. They knew he was on the run, and they never whispered it in my ear." But the king's servants refused to lift their hands against the priests of the LORD and cut them down.
22:18 Then the king said to Doeg, "You go around and attack the priests." Doeg the Edomite went around, and he himself attacked the priests. On that day he put to death eighty-five men, men who wore a linen ephod.
22:19 And Nob, the priests' town, he struck down with the edge of the sword: man and woman, child and nursing baby, ox, donkey and sheep, by the edge of the sword.
22:20 One son of Ahimelech son of Ahitub got away. Abiathar was his name, and he fled to follow David.
22:21 Abiathar reported to David that Saul had killed the LORD's priests.
22:22 David said to Abiathar, "I knew it on that day. Doeg the Edomite was there, and I knew he was bound to tell Saul. I have caused the loss of every life in your father's house.
22:23 Stay with me and have no fear. Whoever is hunting my life is hunting yours. With me you are under guard."
## David rescues Keilah
23:1 Word came to David: "The Philistines are at war with Keilah. They are stripping the threshing floors bare."
23:2 So David asked the LORD, "Shall I go? Shall I strike these Philistines?" "Go," the LORD told David. "Strike the Philistines, and you will rescue Keilah."
23:3 David's men said to him, "Even here in Judah we live in fear. What will it be like if we march to Keilah to face the battle lines of the Philistines?"
23:4 So David asked the LORD one more time. The LORD gave him an answer: "Up! Go down to Keilah. I am about to hand the Philistines over to you."
23:5 David went to Keilah with his men and made war on the Philistines. He led away their herds and struck them down in a great slaughter. That is how David rescued those who lived in Keilah.
23:6 When Abiathar son of Ahimelech ran away to David at Keilah, an ephod came down with him, in his hand.
23:7 News reached Saul that David had come to Keilah. "God has given him up into my hand," Saul said. "He has locked himself in, walking into a town with doors and a bar."
23:8 Saul summoned all the troops to the fight. They were to go down to Keilah and close in on David and his men.
23:9 David knew that Saul was forging this harm against him. "Bring the ephod over," he said to Abiathar the priest.
23:10 Then David prayed, "O LORD, God of Israel, your servant has heard it said for sure: Saul is looking to come to Keilah and wreck the town because of me.
23:11 Will the leading men of Keilah give me up to him? Is Saul coming down, just as your servant has heard? O LORD, God of Israel, please let your servant know." "He is coming down," said the LORD.
23:12 "And will the leading men of Keilah give me and my men up into Saul's hand?" David asked. "They will give you up," said the LORD.
23:13 David and his men, some six hundred of them, got up and left Keilah. They went wherever they could go. Word reached Saul that David had slipped away from Keilah, so he called off the march.
23:14 David lived out in the wilderness, in hideouts, and he settled in the hills of the wilderness of Ziph. All that time Saul was searching for him, yet God never put him into Saul's hands.
## Jonathan's last visit
23:15 David was in the wilderness of Ziph, at Horesh, when he became aware that Saul had marched out to hunt for his life.
23:16 Jonathan, Saul's son, set out and came to David at Horesh. He made David's hand strong in God.
23:17 "Have no fear," he said to him. "The hand of my father Saul will never reach you. You are the one who will reign over Israel, and I will be next to you, second in rank. Saul my father knows it too."
23:18 So the two made a covenant there before the LORD. David stayed on at Horesh, while Jonathan went back to his house.
## The Ziphites betray David
23:19 Then men of Ziph went up to Saul at Gibeah. "Did you know David is hiding out near us?" they said. "He is in the hideouts at Horesh, on the hill of Hakilah, which lies south of Jeshimon.
23:20 So whenever your heart longs to come down, O king, come down. Handing him over to the king is our job."
23:21 "The LORD bless you," Saul said, "for taking pity on me.
23:22 Go now and get things ready some more. Learn and look closely at the spot where his foot treads, and who has seen him there. People tell me he is extremely sly.
23:23 Look into every hiding place he uses and learn them all. Then come back to me when you are sure, and I will go along with you. If he is anywhere in the land, I will hunt him out among all the thousands of Judah."
23:24 They left and went off to Ziph before Saul. By then David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, down in the Arabah, south of Jeshimon.
23:25 Saul set out with his men to search. Someone told David, so he came down to the rock and lived on in the wilderness of Maon. Saul heard of it and went after David into the wilderness of Maon.
23:26 Saul was on this side of the mountain, and David with his men was on that side. David was rushing to escape from Saul, but Saul and his men were circling David and his men to seize them.
23:27 Just then a messenger reached Saul. "Hurry back!" he said. "The Philistines have swept into the land."
23:28 Saul turned away from going after David and went to face the Philistines. So people called that place Sela-hammahlekoth.
23:29 David went up from there and settled in the hideouts at En-gedi.
## David spares Saul in the cave
24:1 Saul returned from going after the Philistines, and people brought him word: "Look, David is out in the wilderness of En-gedi."
24:2 Saul picked out three thousand of the best men in all Israel and set off to hunt for David and his men on the face of the Ibex Rocks.
24:3 Along the road he reached the stone sheepfolds, and a cave was there. Saul stepped inside to relieve himself. Deep in the back of the cave sat David and his men.
24:4 "Here is the day!" David's men said to him. "The LORD said to you, 'I am going to put your enemy in your hand. Treat him however you see fit.'" David got up, and in secret he sliced off the edge of the robe Saul had on.
24:5 But later David's heart struck him, because he had cut the edge off Saul's robe.
24:6 He told his men, "May the LORD keep me far from doing this to my lord, the LORD's anointed, from reaching out my hand against him. For he is the LORD's anointed."
24:7 David tore into his men with these words and would not allow them to rise against Saul. Then Saul got up out of the cave and went down the road.
24:8 After that David got up too. He went out of the cave and shouted after Saul, "My lord the king!" Saul glanced back, and David bent low with his face to the earth and bowed down.
24:9 "Why do you pay attention to men's talk?" David said to Saul. "They say, 'Look, David is out to do you harm.'
24:10 Look! This very day your eyes have seen it. The LORD put you in my hand today in the cave. There was talk of killing you, but my eye took pity on you. I said, 'I will not stretch out my hand against my lord. He is the LORD's anointed.'
24:11 My father, look! Yes, look at the edge of your robe in my hand. I cut off the edge of your robe, and I did not kill you. Understand, then, and see: my hand holds no evil and no revolt. I have done you no wrong. Yet you stalk my life to take it away.
24:12 Let the LORD be judge between me and you. Let the LORD pay you back for me. My own hand will never be raised against you.
24:13 The ancient saying puts it this way: 'Out of the wicked comes wickedness.' My hand will never be raised against you.
24:14 Who is it the king of Israel has marched out after? Who is it you are chasing? A dead dog. One flea.
24:15 The LORD will be the judge. Let him decide between me and you. Let him look into it and argue my case, and win me justice by setting me free from your hand."
24:16 David finished speaking these words to Saul, and Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" Then Saul raised his voice and cried.
24:17 "You are in the right, not I," he told David. "You have paid me back with good, and I have paid you back with evil.
24:18 This day you have made it plain that you did right by me. The LORD handed me over to you, and you did not kill me.
24:19 Who ever finds his enemy and sends him off on a good road? May the LORD give you good in return for what you did for me this day.
24:20 And now, look, I know that you will certainly reign. The kingdom of Israel will stand firm in your hand.
24:21 So take an oath for me by the LORD: you will not cut off my offspring after me, and you will not erase my name from my father's house."
24:22 David gave Saul his oath. Saul went back to his house, while David and his men climbed up to the stronghold.
## Samuel dies
25:1 Then Samuel died. All Israel came together to mourn him, and they laid him in his grave at his house in Ramah. David packed up and went down to the wilderness of Paran.
## Nabal and Abigail
25:2 A man in Maon had his work in Carmel. The man was very well off: he had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats. It was the time when he was shearing his sheep in Carmel.
25:3 Nabal was the man's name, and Abigail his wife's. She was a woman of good sense and lovely to look at. He was hard and nasty in what he did. He came from Caleb's clan.
25:4 Out in the wilderness David heard that Nabal was at his sheep-shearing.
25:5 David sent off ten young men. "Go up to Carmel," he told them. "When you come to Nabal, ask him in my name how he is doing.
25:6 Say this: 'To life! Peace on you, peace on your house, and peace on everything that is yours.
25:7 I hear the shearers are at work for you. Your shepherds have been near us, and we never shamed them. They did not lose a thing all the days they were in Carmel.
25:8 Ask your own young men; they will tell you. Look kindly on these young men of mine, because we have come on a good day. Please give your servants, and your son David, whatever you can spare.'"
25:9 David's young men arrived and gave Nabal the whole message in David's name, and then they stood quietly.
25:10 But Nabal answered David's servants, "David? Who is he? Who is the son of Jesse? These days there are plenty of slaves running off, every one from his master.
25:11 Am I supposed to take my bread, my water, and the animals I butchered for my shearers, and hand them to men I don't know, from I don't know where?"
25:12 So David's young men headed back the way they came. They reached him and reported all these words.
25:13 "Buckle on your swords, every man," David told his men. Every man buckled on his sword, and David buckled on his own. Some four hundred men went up behind David, and two hundred stayed back by the gear.
25:14 Meanwhile one of the young men spoke to Abigail, Nabal's wife. "Listen," he said. "David sent messengers out of the wilderness to bless our master, and he flew at them screaming.
25:15 Those men treated us very well. We were never shamed, and we lost nothing in all the days we moved around with them out in the open country.
25:16 By night and by day they were a wall for us, all the days we were near them keeping the flocks.
25:17 So now, decide what you will do, and see to it. Disaster has been settled on for our master and all his house. And he is a worthless man; nobody can get a word in with him."
25:18 Abigail moved fast. She took two hundred loaves, two skins of wine and five sheep ready to cook. She took five seahs of parched grain, a hundred raisin clusters and two hundred fig cakes, and she put them on the donkeys.
25:19 "Go on in front of me," she said to her young men. "I'm coming right behind you." Her husband Nabal heard nothing of it from her.
25:20 She was riding on the donkey, going down hidden by the hillside, when suddenly David and his men came down toward her, and she ran into them.
25:21 David had just said, "So all my guarding of this man's things out in the wilderness was a waste. Nothing that belonged to him went missing, and he returns me evil in place of good.
25:22 May God do this to David, and more besides, if I leave a single male of Nabal's alive by morning light."
25:23 The moment Abigail saw David, she hurried down off the donkey. She threw herself face down in front of David and bowed to the earth.
25:24 Down at his feet she said, "Put the guilt on me, my lord, on me. Please, let your servant speak where you can hear, and listen to what your servant says.
25:25 My lord, please do not set your heart on this worthless man, Nabal. As his name is, so is he. Nabal is his name, and shameful folly is his companion. As for me, your servant, I never saw the young men my lord sent.
25:26 So now, my lord, I swear by the life of the LORD and by your own life. The LORD has held you back from walking into bloodguilt and from taking your rescue into your own hand. Now may your enemies, and all who look for ways to hurt my lord, end up like Nabal.
25:27 Here is a present, a blessing your servant has brought to my lord. Let it go to the young men who walk at my lord's heels.
25:28 Pardon the wrong your servant has done, I beg you. The LORD will certainly build my lord a house that stands firm, for my lord is fighting the battles of the LORD. No evil will be found in you, all your days.
25:29 A man has risen to chase you and hunt for your life. But my lord's life will be tied up safe in the bundle of the living, with the LORD your God. The lives of your enemies he will whirl and fling out from the cup of a sling.
25:30 The LORD will do for my lord every good thing he has spoken about you. He will appoint you ruler over Israel.
25:31 When that day comes, this will not become something that makes you stagger. It will not be a stumbling of heart for my lord that you poured out blood for nothing and saved yourself by your own hand. And when the LORD does good to my lord, remember your servant."
25:32 "The LORD God of Israel be blessed," David said to Abigail. "He sent you out today to meet me.
25:33 And blessed is your clear thinking, and blessed are you. Today you held me back from walking into bloodguilt and from rescuing myself with my own hand.
25:34 But by the life of the LORD, the God of Israel, who held me back from hurting you, I tell you this. If you had not rushed out to meet me, by the time morning broke Nabal would have had no male of his left."
25:35 David took from her hand what she had brought for him. "Go back up to your house in peace," he told her. "Look, I have heard what you said, and I am giving you what you asked."
25:36 Abigail came home to Nabal, and there he was, throwing a feast in his house fit for a king. Nabal's heart was merry inside him, and he was very, very drunk. She did not tell him a thing, small or big, until morning came.
25:37 In the morning, once the wine had worn off, his wife told Nabal everything. His heart died in his chest, and he turned to stone.
25:38 Ten days or so went by. Then the LORD struck Nabal down, and he died.
## David marries Abigail
25:39 David heard that Nabal had died. "Blessed is the LORD," he said, "who argued my case over the insult I took from Nabal's hand. He held his servant back from evil, and the LORD has turned Nabal's evil back onto his own head." Then David sent a message and spoke to Abigail about taking her as his wife.
25:40 His servants went to Abigail at Carmel. "David sent us to you," they told her. "He wants to take you as his wife."
25:41 She rose, then bowed low with her face to the earth. "Look, your servant is a slave girl," she said, "to wash the feet of my lord's servants."
25:42 Quickly Abigail got up and mounted a donkey. Five of her young women went along on foot behind her. She followed David's messengers, and she became his wife.
25:43 David had already taken Ahinoam from Jezreel, so both women became his wives.
25:44 Saul, meanwhile, had handed his daughter Michal, who was David's wife, to Palti son of Laish from Gallim.
## David spares Saul again
26:1 Men of Ziph came to Saul at Gibeah. "You know David is hiding out on the hill of Hakilah, across from Jeshimon?" they said.
26:2 Saul set out and went down into the wilderness of Ziph, taking three thousand of Israel's picked men, to hunt for David in the wilderness of Ziph.
26:3 Saul pitched camp on the hill of Hakilah, next to the road, across from Jeshimon. David was living out in the wilderness, and he saw that Saul had come into the wilderness after him.
26:4 So David sent out spies, and he found out for sure that Saul had come.
26:5 David got up and came near the spot where Saul was camped. He looked over the place where Saul slept, along with Abner son of Ner, his army commander. Saul was asleep in the middle of the camp ring, and the troops were bedded down all around him.
26:6 David turned to Ahimelech the Hittite and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother. "Which of you will go down with me to Saul, into the camp?" "I will," said Abishai. "I will go down with you."
26:7 That night David and Abishai crept up on the troops. There lay Saul, sound asleep in the camp ring. His spear was driven into the ground beside his head, and Abner and the soldiers lay around him.
26:8 "God has put your enemy in your hand today," Abishai said to David. "Now let me strike him with the spear, just once, right into the ground. I won't have to do it again."
26:9 David answered Abishai, "You must not kill him. Who has ever stretched out his hand against the LORD's anointed and gone unpunished?"
26:10 "By the life of the LORD," David went on, "the LORD will strike him, and no one else. Or his day will arrive, and he will die. Or he will go down to the battle and be carried off.
26:11 May the LORD keep me far from stretching out my hand against the LORD's anointed! Now, please, take the spear that stands at his head, and the jug of water, and let us get out of here."
26:12 David took the spear and the water jug from by Saul's head, and the two went off. Nobody saw. Nobody knew. Nobody woke. Every one of them was asleep, for the LORD's own deep sleep had dropped over them.
26:13 David went across to the far side and stood on the hilltop, a long way off, with a big gap between them.
26:14 He shouted to the army and to Abner son of Ner, "Abner! Won't you answer?" "Who are you," Abner shouted back, "to call out to the king?"
26:15 "Aren't you a real man?" David said to Abner. "Who in Israel compares with you? So why did you not keep watch over your lord the king? One of the people came in to do away with the king, your lord.
26:16 This thing you have done is no good. By the life of the LORD, you men are as good as dead, because you did not watch over your master, over the LORD's anointed. Now look! Where is the king's spear? Where is the water jug that stood at his head?"
26:17 Saul knew it was David's voice. "Is this your voice, my son David?" he called. "My voice it is, my lord the king," said David.
26:18 "Why is my lord running after his servant like this?" he asked. "What did I do? What evil is there in my hand?
26:19 So please, my lord the king, hear out your servant. If the LORD has set you on me, let him breathe in the scent of an offering. But if human beings are behind it, a curse on them in the LORD's presence! They have chased me out today, cut off from my portion in the LORD's inheritance, as if to say, 'Go, serve other gods.'
26:20 So don't let my blood spill onto the ground away from the LORD's face. The king of Israel has come out searching for one flea, the way a man chases a partridge over the hills."
26:21 "I have sinned," Saul said. "Return, my son David. I will never do you harm again, since my life was precious in your eyes this day. I have played the fool. I have gone very far astray."
26:22 David answered, "Look, the king's spear! Send one of the young men across to fetch it.
26:23 The LORD gives each man back what his rightness and his loyalty deserve. Today the LORD put you in my power, and I refused to stretch out my hand against the LORD's anointed.
26:24 Look, as your life counted for much in my eyes today, so may my life count for much in the LORD's eyes. May he snatch me out of every tight place."
26:25 Saul told David, "A blessing on you, my son David! What you set out to do, you will do, and you will win out." David went off on his road, and Saul returned to his place.
## David among the Philistines
27:1 David said in his heart, "Some day soon Saul's hand will sweep me away. My only good choice is to slip away into the land of the Philistines. Then Saul will give up hope of hunting me through all Israel's land, and I will get away from his hand."
27:2 David set out, and he crossed over with his six hundred men to Achish son of Maoch, the king of Gath.
27:3 David made his home with Achish in Gath, he and his men, every man with his family. David brought both his wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail the Carmelite, who had been Nabal's wife.
27:4 Word reached Saul that David had run off to Gath, and he stopped hunting for him.
27:5 "If you look on me kindly," David said to Achish, "please have me given a spot in one of the towns out in the countryside, and I will settle there. What is your servant doing living with you in the king's own city?"
27:6 That same day Achish handed him Ziklag. This is how Ziklag came to belong to the kings of Judah, as it still does today.
27:7 The time David spent in the Philistines' country came to a year and four months.
27:8 David would go up with his men and make raids on the Geshurites, the Girzites and the Amalekites. Those peoples had lived in that land from long ago, all the way toward Shur and on to the land of Egypt.
27:9 David would strike the land and leave no man or woman living. He carried off flocks and herds, donkeys, camels and clothing, and came back to Achish.
27:10 Achish would ask, "Whom did you raid today?" And David would answer, "The Negev of Judah," or "The Negev of the Jerahmeelites," or "The Kenite part of the Negev."
27:11 Man or woman, David let no one live to be brought back to Gath. "They could report on us," he reasoned, "and say, 'David did this.'" That was his way of working all the time he lived in the Philistines' country.
27:12 Achish believed David. "He has made himself stink to his own people, to Israel," he thought. "He will be my servant for good."
## The Philistines gather
28:1 In those days the Philistines brought their troops together for war, to fight Israel. Achish told David, "Know this for sure: you will march out with me in the camp, you and your men."
28:2 "Good," David said to Achish. "Now you will know what your servant will do." "Good," Achish replied to David. "Then I will make you the guard of my head for the rest of your days."
## Saul and the medium at Endor
28:3 By then Samuel was dead. All Israel had mourned him and buried him in Ramah, in his own town. Saul had removed the ghosts of the dead and the spirits that know things from the land.
28:4 The Philistines massed together, came, and pitched camp at Shunem. Saul called all Israel together, and they pitched camp on Gilboa.
28:5 Saul looked at the Philistine camp, and fear took hold of him. His heart shook hard.
28:6 Saul turned to the LORD with his question, but the LORD gave him no answer, whether through dreams, through the Urim or through the prophets.
28:7 So Saul told his servants, "Look for a woman for me who has a ghost at her call. I will go to her and ask through her." "There is a woman at Endor who has a ghost at her call," his servants told him.
28:8 Saul made himself unrecognizable by putting on different clothes. He set out with two men, and they reached the woman by night. "Use the ghost to read the future for me," he said, "and bring up the one I tell you."
28:9 "Look," the woman told him, "you know very well what Saul did. He wiped out the ghosts of the dead and the spirits that know things from the land. Why are you laying a snare for my life, to get me put to death?"
28:10 Saul gave her an oath by the LORD. "By the life of the LORD," he said, "no guilt will fall on you for this thing."
28:11 "Who is it I should bring up for you?" the woman said. "Samuel," he said. "Bring him up for me."
28:12 The woman saw Samuel, and she let out a loud shriek. "Why did you trick me?" she cried to Saul. "You are Saul!"
28:13 "Do not be afraid," the king told her. "What are you seeing?" "I see a divine being," the woman told Saul, "rising up out of the earth."
28:14 "What is his form?" he asked her. "It's an old man coming up," she said, "and he has a robe wrapped around him." Saul knew then that it was Samuel. He bent down with his face to the earth and bowed low.
28:15 Samuel spoke to Saul: "Why did you stir me up and bring me up here?" Saul answered, "I am in terrible straits. The Philistines are at war with me, and God has gone away from me. He does not answer me anymore, not through the prophets and not through dreams. So I called for you, to let me know what I should do."
28:16 "Then why ask me?" Samuel said. "The LORD has gone away from you and become your foe.
28:17 The LORD has done to you what he said he would, speaking through me. He has ripped the kingdom from your hand and handed it to your neighbor, to David.
28:18 You would not listen to the LORD's voice, and you did not pour out the heat of his anger on Amalek. That is why the LORD has done this to you this day.
28:19 And the LORD will give Israel too, along with you, into the hand of the Philistines. Tomorrow you and your sons will be with me. The camp of Israel as well the LORD will give into the Philistines' hand."
28:20 At once Saul dropped flat, his whole height stretched out on the ground. Samuel's words struck him with terror. There was no strength in him, since he had not eaten a bite of food that whole day or the whole night.
28:21 The woman came over to Saul and saw how terrified he was. "Listen," she said, "your servant did as you said. I put my life in the palm of my hand and heeded the words you spoke to me.
28:22 Now it's your turn, please: listen to your servant's voice. Let me put a piece of bread before you. Eat, so there will be strength in you for the road."
28:23 He would not. "I won't eat," he said. Then his servants pressed him, and the woman too, and he gave in to them. He rose off the ground and sat down on the bed.
28:24 The woman owned a calf fattened in the stall at the house. She hurried and slaughtered it. Then she took flour, worked it into dough, and baked flat bread with no yeast.
28:25 She served it to Saul and his servants, and they ate. Then they rose and walked away into that night.
## The Philistines send David away
29:1 The Philistines brought all their armies together at Aphek, while Israel was camped at the spring in Jezreel.
29:2 The rulers of the Philistines were passing by in units of a hundred and a thousand, and last of all came David and his men, passing by with Achish.
29:3 The Philistine officers said, "What are these Hebrews?" Achish answered the Philistine officers, "This is David, the servant of Saul, the king of Israel. He has been with me a good long time now, days and years. Since the day he deserted to me I have not found a thing wrong in him, to this very day."
29:4 The Philistine officers were furious with Achish. "Make the man go back," they told him. "Let him return to the place you put him. He is not going down with us to the fight. He could turn into our adversary in the middle of the battle. And how would this fellow get back into his master's good graces? Wouldn't it be with the heads of these men here?
29:5 Is this not David, the one they answered each other about in their dances, singing, 'Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his tens of thousands'?"
29:6 Achish called David over. "By the life of the LORD," he told him, "you are an upright man. In my eyes it would be good for you to march out and back with me in the camp. I have found nothing bad in you from the day you came to me until now. But in the eyes of the rulers, you are not good.
29:7 So turn around now and go in peace. Do not do anything that looks bad in the eyes of the Philistine rulers."
29:8 "What did I do?" David said to Achish. "From the day I came into your service up to today, what have you found in your servant, that I may not go and fight the enemies of my lord the king?"
29:9 Achish answered David, "I know it. In my eyes you are as good as an angel of God. Still, the Philistine officers have said, 'He shall not go up with us to the fight.'
29:10 So rise early in the morning, you and the servants of your master who came along with you. Go to the place where I stationed you, and do not take any evil word to heart, for in my eyes you are good. Rise early, and once there is light for you, go."
29:11 David rose early, he and his men, to head out in the morning and go back to Philistine land. The Philistines went on up to Jezreel.
## Ziklag burned
30:1 It took David and his men until the third day to reach Ziklag. By then the Amalekites had swept down on the Negev and on Ziklag. They had struck Ziklag and set it on fire.
30:2 They had taken prisoner the women who were in it, from the youngest to the oldest. They put no one to death. They drove them off and went their way.
30:3 David and his men came to the town and found it burned down. Their wives, their sons and their daughters had been carried off as prisoners.
30:4 David and the troops with him raised their voices and cried, until there was no strength left in them for crying.
30:5 Both of David's wives had been taken prisoner too: Ahinoam the Jezreelite, and Abigail the Carmelite, who had been Nabal's wife.
30:6 David was in a very tight spot, because the troops were saying he should be stoned. Every man's heart was bitter over his sons and his daughters. But David made himself strong in the LORD his God.
30:7 David told Abiathar the priest, Ahimelech's son, "Bring the ephod over to me, please." So Abiathar brought the ephod over to David.
30:8 David asked the LORD, "Should I go after this raiding band? Will I catch up with it?" "Go after it," the LORD told him. "You will certainly catch them, and you will surely rescue them all."
30:9 David went, with the six hundred men who were with him, and they reached the Besor wadi. There the ones being left behind stopped.
30:10 David pressed on in pursuit with four hundred men. Two hundred men halted, because they were too worn out to get across the Besor wadi.
30:11 Out in the countryside they came upon an Egyptian man and took him to David. They gave him bread, and he ate. They let him drink water.
30:12 They also gave him a slab of fig cake and two raisin clusters. When he had eaten, his spirit came back into him. He had gone three days and three nights without eating bread or drinking water.
30:13 "Whose man are you?" David asked him. "Where have you come from?" "I am a young Egyptian," he said, "slave to an Amalekite. Three days ago I fell sick, and my master left me lying.
30:14 We had made raids on the Kerethite Negev, on the land that is Judah's, and on the Negev of Caleb. Ziklag we set on fire."
30:15 "Will you guide me down to this raiding band?" David asked. "Give me your oath by God," he answered, "that you will not put me to death and will not hand me back to my master. Then I will guide you down to the band."
30:16 He brought David down, and there they were, sprawled across the face of the whole land. They were eating and drinking and holding a festival over all the huge haul they had seized from Philistine country and from the land of Judah.
30:17 David cut them down from twilight to the evening of the following day. No man among them got away, apart from four hundred young men who climbed onto camels and ran.
30:18 David snatched back all that Amalek had taken. David snatched back his two wives too.
30:19 Nothing of theirs was lost, from small things to big, from sons to daughters, from the spoil to everything the raiders had kept for themselves. David brought all of it home.
30:20 David also took every flock and herd. They drove these in front of the other animals and called out, "This is David's spoil!"
## Sharing the spoil
30:21 David came back to the two hundred men who had been too worn out to go on after him, the ones he had left at the Besor wadi. They came out to meet David and the troops with him. David walked up to them and asked how they were.
30:22 But every nasty, worthless man among those who had marched with David spoke up. "They didn't go with us," they said, "so we won't give them any of the spoil we took back. Each one can have his wife and his children. Let them take them and go."
30:23 "Brothers, don't act like this," David said, "not with what the LORD has given us. He kept us safe, and he put into our hand the band that came raiding against us.
30:24 Who would side with you on this? As the portion is for the one who goes down into the fight, so the portion will be for the one who stays by the gear. They will divide it equally."
30:25 From that day onward David made it a rule and a ruling for Israel, and so it is to this day.
30:26 When David got back to Ziklag, he sent part of the spoil to the elders of Judah, his friends. His message was: "Here is a blessing for you, out of the spoil taken from the enemies of the LORD."
30:27 It went to the people in Bethel, in Ramoth of the Negev and in Jattir;
30:28 to the people in Aroer, in Siphmoth and in Eshtemoa;
30:29 to the people in Racal, in the towns of the Jerahmeelites and in the towns of the Kenites;
30:30 to the people in Hormah, in Bor-ashan and in Athach;
30:31 to the people in Hebron; and to all the places David and his men had wandered through.
## The death of Saul
31:1 The Philistines were fighting Israel. Israel's men ran from the Philistines, and their dead lay fallen on Mount Gilboa.
31:2 The Philistines closed in on Saul and his sons. They cut down Saul's sons Jonathan, Abinadab and Malchishua.
31:3 The battle bore down heavily on Saul. The archers, the men with bows, found him, and he was in great anguish from the archers.
31:4 "Pull out your sword and stab me with it," Saul told his armor-bearer. "If you don't, these uncircumcised men will come and stab me and have their cruel fun with me." But his armor-bearer was too afraid. He would not. So Saul took hold of the sword and fell on it.
31:5 His armor-bearer saw that Saul had died. Then he also threw himself on his own sword, and died with him.
31:6 That is how Saul died that day, along with his three sons and his armor-bearer and all his men, all of them together.
31:7 The Israelites beyond the valley and beyond the Jordan saw that Israel's men had run, and that Saul and his sons were dead. They walked out of their towns and ran away, and the Philistines came in and settled there.
31:8 On the day after, the Philistines went out to plunder the bodies of the slain. They came upon Saul and his three sons lying where they fell on Mount Gilboa.
31:9 They hacked off his head and took off his armor. Then they sent word all around the land of the Philistines, to bring the good news to their idols' temples and to the people.
31:10 His armor they laid in the house of the Ashtaroth goddesses, and his corpse they fastened to the wall of Beth-shan.
## Jabesh repays Saul
31:11 Those living in Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul.
31:12 Every man of courage got up and walked all through the night. They took Saul's body, and the bodies of his sons, off the wall of Beth-shan. Then they came to Jabesh and burned them there.
31:13 They gathered up their bones and buried them beneath the tamarisk in Jabesh. Then they went without food for seven days.
