## Hannah's sorrow
1:1 A man lived in Ramathaim-zophim, in the hill country of Ephraim. His name was Elkanah son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephrathite.
1:2 He had two wives. One wife's name was Hannah, and the second one's name was Peninnah. Peninnah had children, while Hannah had none.
1:3 Every year this man would go up from his town to Shiloh, to bow down in worship and to sacrifice to the LORD of Armies. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were the LORD's priests there.
1:4 When the day came, Elkanah would sacrifice and hand out shares to his wife Peninnah and to every one of her sons and daughters.
1:5 But to Hannah he gave a single portion worth two, because he loved Hannah, even though the LORD had shut her womb.
1:6 Her rival taunted her cruelly to stir her up, since the LORD had shut her womb.
1:7 This is what he did each year. And each time she went up to the house of the LORD, the other wife taunted her in the same way. So Hannah cried and would not eat.
1:8 Her husband Elkanah said to her, "Hannah, what are you crying for? What keeps you from eating? Why are you so heavy-hearted? Am I not worth more to you than ten sons?"
## Hannah's prayer and vow
1:9 One time at Shiloh, after the eating and the drinking, Hannah got up. Eli the priest had his seat beside a doorpost of the LORD's temple.
1:10 Bitter in her soul, she prayed to the LORD, weeping and weeping.
1:11 She made a vow. "LORD of Armies," she said, "if you will really look at the misery of your servant and remember me, if you will not forget your servant, and will give your servant a male child, then I will give him to the LORD for his whole life. No razor will ever go up on his head."
1:12 She prayed on and on before the LORD, and Eli watched her mouth.
1:13 Hannah was speaking silently, to herself. Her lips were trembling, but no one could hear her voice, and Eli took her for a drunk woman.
1:14 Eli said to her, "How long are you going to keep this drunkenness up? Get rid of your wine!"
1:15 "No, sir," Hannah answered. "I am a woman whose spirit is hard pressed. Wine and strong drink I have not touched. I have poured my soul out before the LORD.
1:16 Do not count your servant as a worthless woman. It is out of all my distress and the taunting I suffer that I have been speaking up to now."
1:17 "Go in peace," Eli answered. "May the God of Israel give you what you asked him for."
1:18 "Let your servant be looked on kindly by you," she said. The woman went off, and she ate, and she no longer had that look on her face.
## Samuel is born
1:19 Early in the morning they got up and bowed in worship before the LORD, and then they set off back to their house in Ramah. Elkanah slept with his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.
1:20 When the time came round, Hannah conceived and had a son. She named him Samuel, since "I asked the LORD for him."
1:21 Then the man Elkanah and his whole household went up to offer the LORD the sacrifice of the year, and to pay what he had vowed.
1:22 Hannah did not go up, for she had told her husband, "Not until the boy is weaned. Then I will take him along, and he will appear in the LORD's presence and stay there for good."
1:23 Elkanah her husband told her, "Do whatever you think is good. Stay here till you wean him. Only may the LORD bring about what you have spoken." So the woman stayed behind and kept nursing her son until he was weaned.
1:24 As soon as she had weaned him, she brought him up with her. She took a bull three years old, an ephah of flour and a skin bottle of wine, and she brought him to Shiloh, to the LORD's house. The boy was just a little boy.
1:25 They slaughtered the bull, and then they took the boy to Eli.
1:26 "Excuse me, sir!" she said. "By your life, sir, I am that woman, the one who stood with you in this place to pray to the LORD.
1:27 This boy is what I prayed for, and the LORD has given me what I asked him for.
1:28 So I in turn have handed him over to the LORD. All the days of his life he is handed over to the LORD." And he bowed down there before the LORD.
## Hannah's song
2:1 Hannah prayed. She said: / My heart leaps for joy in the LORD; / my horn is raised high by the LORD. / My mouth is opened wide over my enemies, / because your rescue makes me glad.
2:2 No one is holy as the LORD is; / truly, there is none but you. / There is no rock like our God.
2:3 Stop your talk, your high and haughty talk; / let no boast come out of your mouth. / The LORD is a God who knows everything, / and he weighs what people do.
2:4 The warriors' bows are shattered, / while those who stumble wrap strength around them.
2:5 The well fed sell their work for bread, / and the hungry stop being hungry. / The childless woman bears seven, / and she who had many sons withers away.
2:6 The LORD kills and makes alive; / he takes down to Sheol, and he brings up.
2:7 The LORD makes people poor and makes them rich; / he puts them down, and he also raises them up.
2:8 He lifts the poor up out of the dust; / he raises the needy from the garbage heap / to give them a seat among nobles / and make a glorious throne theirs. / The earth's pillars belong to the LORD, / and he has set the world on them.
2:9 He keeps the feet of his loyal ones safe, / while the wicked are struck silent in the dark. / No one wins the day by his own strength.
2:10 As for the LORD, those who quarrel with him are smashed; / he thunders at them from the sky. / The LORD will judge the earth to its farthest ends. / He will grant power to his king / and raise high his anointed one's horn.
2:11 Elkanah went off to his house in Ramah. The boy went on serving the LORD under Eli the priest.
## The sons of Eli
2:12 Eli's sons were worthless men who did not know the LORD.
2:13 This was how the priests dealt with the people. When anyone offered a sacrifice, the priest's young man would come over as the meat was cooking, holding a fork with three prongs.
2:14 He would stab it into the pot, or the kettle, or the cauldron, or the pan. Whatever the fork pulled out, the priest kept for himself. They did this to all Israel when people came there to Shiloh.
2:15 Worse, before the fat was even turned to smoke, the priest's young man would come up to the one sacrificing and tell him, "Hand over meat for roasting, for the priest. He will not accept cooked meat from you, only raw."
2:16 And if the man answered him, "They must turn the fat to smoke first. Then take whatever your appetite wants," he would say, "No! You will give it now. If not, I'll take it by force."
2:17 The young men's sin was very great before the LORD, because the men despised the LORD's offering.
## Samuel grows up
2:18 Samuel, meanwhile, served in the LORD's presence, a boy with a linen ephod tied around him.
2:19 His mother would sew him a small robe and bring it up to him every year, when she came up with her husband to offer the sacrifice of the year.
2:20 Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife and say, "May the LORD repay you with children from this woman, in return for the one lent to the LORD." Then they would go back to their home.
2:21 For the LORD took note of Hannah. She became pregnant and had three sons and two daughters. And the boy Samuel grew up beside the LORD.
2:22 Eli had grown very old. He kept hearing of all the things his sons did to all Israel.
2:23 He said to them, "What are you doing these things for? Your evil doings are what I hear about from all these people.
2:24 Stop it, my sons! What I hear the LORD's people passing around is not a good report.
2:25 If a man sins against a man, God can judge between them. But if a man sins against the LORD, who will pray for him?" But they would not listen to their father's voice, because the LORD meant to put them to death.
2:26 And the boy Samuel kept growing up and doing well, in the eyes of the LORD and of people too.
## A man of God warns Eli
2:27 Then a man of God came to Eli. He told him, "The LORD says this: I revealed myself plainly, didn't I, to your ancestor's family when they were in Egypt, slaves in Pharaoh's household?
2:28 Out of all Israel's tribes I picked him to be a priest for me: to climb up to my altar, to send up incense in smoke, to carry an ephod before me. And I handed your ancestor's family every one of the Israelites' offerings by fire.
2:29 So why do you all kick at my sacrifice and my grain offering, the ones I ordered for my home? You give your sons more honor than me, as you fatten yourselves on the first and best of every offering my people Israel bring.
2:30 That is why this is the word of the LORD, the God of Israel: I did say your family and your ancestor's family would walk in my presence for all time. But now this is the LORD's word: Never! I will honor the ones who honor me, and the ones who scorn me will count for little.
2:31 Look, a time is coming when I will chop off your arm and the arm of your ancestor's family. No one in your house will live to be old.
2:32 There will not be an old man in your house for all time.
2:33 One man of yours I will not cut off from my altar; he will be left to wear out his eyes and make his heart ache. All the rest of your family will fall to men's swords.
2:34 This will be your sign, the thing that will come on your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas: on one day, the two of them will die.
2:35 Then I will raise up a faithful priest for myself, who will act on what is in my heart and soul. I will build him a house that stays faithful, and he will walk before my anointed one for all time.
2:36 Anyone left over from your family will come and bow low to him to get a bit of silver and a round of bread. He will say, 'Please attach me to one of the priestly groups, so I can eat a scrap of bread.'"
## The LORD calls Samuel
3:1 The boy Samuel served the LORD under Eli. The LORD's word was rare in those days; no vision was breaking out.
3:2 It happened at that time. Eli was lying down in his room. His eyes had started to go dim, and he could not see.
3:3 God's lamp was not yet out, and Samuel lay in the LORD's temple, where the ark of God stood.
3:4 The LORD called to Samuel, and he answered, "Here I am."
3:5 He ran over to Eli. "Here I am," he said. "You called me." "I didn't call," Eli said. "Lie down again." So he went and lay down.
3:6 Once more the LORD called, "Samuel!" Samuel got up, went to Eli and said, "Here I am. You called me." He answered, "I didn't call, my son. Lie down again."
3:7 Samuel had not come to know the LORD yet; no word of the LORD had yet been shown to him.
3:8 The LORD called Samuel again, for the third time. He rose, went to Eli and said, "Here I am. You called me." Now Eli saw that it was the LORD calling the boy.
3:9 Eli told Samuel, "Go, lie down. If he calls to you, then say, 'Speak, LORD. Your servant is listening.'" Samuel went back and lay down where he slept.
3:10 Then the LORD came and took his stand there. He called the same way as the other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" Samuel answered, "Speak. Your servant is listening."
3:11 The LORD told Samuel, "I am going to do a thing in Israel, and when people hear of it, both their ears will ring.
3:12 When that day comes, I will make good on all I said against Eli's house, start to finish.
3:13 I have told him I am about to judge his house for all time, for the wrong he knew of. His sons were cursing God, and he did not hold them back.
3:14 That is why I have sworn about Eli's family: no sacrifice or grain offering will ever make atonement for the guilt of Eli's house."
3:15 Samuel stayed lying down till morning, and then he opened up the doors of the LORD's house. But he was scared to report the vision to Eli.
3:16 Eli called Samuel. "Samuel, my son!" he said. "Here I am," he answered.
3:17 "What was the word he spoke to you?" Eli asked. "Please, don't keep it from me. God do this to you, and more besides, if you keep back from me one word of all he said to you."
3:18 Samuel told him all the words, keeping none of them back. Eli said, "He is the LORD. Let him do whatever looks good to him."
3:19 As Samuel grew, the LORD stayed with him. He did not let a single one of his words drop to the ground.
3:20 Everyone in Israel, from Dan all the way to Beersheba, knew that Samuel was proven trustworthy as a prophet of the LORD.
3:21 The LORD went on showing himself at Shiloh. At Shiloh the LORD made himself known to Samuel by the word of the LORD.
## The ark is captured
4:1 So what Samuel said reached all Israel. Israel marched out to meet the Philistines in battle and set up camp near Ebenezer, while the Philistines camped at Aphek.
4:2 The Philistines formed their lines against Israel. As the fighting spread out, Israel went down in defeat before the Philistines, who cut down about four thousand men in the battle lines out in the open field.
4:3 The army came back into camp, and Israel's elders said, "Why did the LORD strike us down today in front of the Philistines? Let's fetch the ark of the covenant of the LORD from Shiloh. Let it come into our camp and rescue us from our enemies' grip."
4:4 The people sent word to Shiloh, and men carried back from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of Armies, who sits on the cherubim as his throne. Hophni and Phinehas, the two sons of Eli, were there alongside the ark of God's covenant.
4:5 As the ark of the covenant of the LORD entered the camp, the whole of Israel let out a huge war cry, and the earth rang with it.
4:6 The Philistines heard the noise of the war cry. "What is the sound of this huge shout in the Hebrews' camp?" they said. Then they found out that the ark of the LORD had arrived in the camp.
4:7 Fear took hold of the Philistines, because they said, "A god has come into the camp." "We are done for!" they said. "Nothing like this has ever happened before, not yesterday or the day before.
4:8 We are done for! Who will rescue us from the power of these mighty gods? These are the very gods who struck Egypt down with plague after plague in the wilderness.
4:9 Get a grip on yourselves and act like men, Philistines, or you will end up serving the Hebrews the way they have served you. Act like men and fight!"
4:10 The Philistines fought. Israel went down in defeat, and they ran away, each man to his tent. The killing was enormous: thirty thousand of Israel's foot soldiers fell.
4:11 God's ark was captured. Eli's two sons died, Hophni and Phinehas.
## The death of Eli
4:12 A Benjaminite ran from the fighting and reached Shiloh on the same day. His clothes were ripped, and there was earth on his head.
4:13 He came, and there was Eli, sitting on his seat beside the road, keeping watch. His heart was shaking with fear for God's ark. The man went into town to give the news, and the whole town broke into a wail.
4:14 Eli heard the noise of crying. "Why is there such a commotion?" he said. The man came quickly and reported to Eli.
4:15 Eli was ninety-eight. His eyes stared without moving, and he could not see.
4:16 The man told Eli, "I'm the one who has come from the lines. I ran away from the lines today." Eli said, "How did things go, my son?"
4:17 The bringer of the news answered, "Israel ran from the Philistines. Worse, the army has been badly cut down. Worse still, your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead. And God's ark has been taken."
4:18 At the man's mention of God's ark, Eli toppled backward from his seat beside the gate. He broke his neck and died, since the man was old and heavy. For forty years he had judged Israel.
4:19 Phinehas's wife, Eli's daughter-in-law, was pregnant and about to deliver. She heard the report: God's ark was taken, her father-in-law was dead, and so was her husband. She crouched down and gave birth, because her labor pains suddenly turned on her.
4:20 As she lay dying, the women standing over her told her, "Don't be afraid. You've had a son!" She gave no answer and did not take it to heart.
4:21 She called the boy Ichabod. "Glory has gone into exile from Israel," she said, because God's ark had been taken, and because of her father-in-law and her husband.
4:22 "Glory has gone into exile from Israel," she said, "because God's ark is taken."
## The ark and Dagon
5:1 The Philistines had taken God's ark, and they carried it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.
5:2 The Philistines took up God's ark, brought it into the house of Dagon, and stood it next to Dagon.
5:3 Early the next morning the people of Ashdod got up, and look: Dagon lay face down on the ground in front of the LORD's ark! They lifted Dagon up and returned him to his spot.
5:4 The day after, they got up early in the morning, and look: Dagon was lying face down on the ground in front of the LORD's ark. Dagon's head and the palms of both his hands lay cut off on the doorsill. Only the main part of Dagon was left.
5:5 This is the reason the priests of Dagon, and everyone who goes into Dagon's house, do not walk on Dagon's doorsill in Ashdod, down to this day.
## The ark goes from town to town
5:6 The LORD's hand weighed down hard on the people of Ashdod. He ruined them and struck them with swellings, in Ashdod and all around it.
5:7 The men of Ashdod saw how it was, and they said, "The ark of Israel's God is not going to stay with us. His hand has been harsh on us and on our god Dagon."
5:8 They sent messages and gathered all the Philistine rulers to them. "What are we to do with the ark of Israel's God?" they asked. The answer came, "Let the ark of Israel's God be taken around to Gath." So they took the ark of the God of Israel around there.
5:9 Once they had taken it there, the LORD's hand came on the town with very great terror. He struck the men of the town, from the youngest to the oldest, and swellings broke out on them.
5:10 Then they sent God's ark on to Ekron. When God's ark arrived at Ekron, the Ekronites screamed, "They've carried the ark of Israel's God around to us, to kill me and my people!"
5:11 They sent and called together all the Philistine rulers and said, "Send the ark of Israel's God away. Let it go back to where it belongs, so that it won't kill me and my people." For a deadly terror had taken hold of the whole town. God's hand weighed down very hard there.
5:12 The men who did not die were struck with swellings, and the town's cry for help rose up to the sky.
## The ark comes back
6:1 The LORD's ark stayed in the Philistines' country for seven months.
6:2 The Philistines summoned their priests and fortune-tellers. "What are we to do about the LORD's ark?" they said. "Let us know what we should send with it when we send it home."
6:3 They said, "If you are sending away the ark of Israel's God, don't send it off empty-handed. You must pay him back with a guilt offering. Then you will get well, and you will find out why his hand would not leave you alone."
6:4 "What is the guilt offering we should send back to him?" they asked. The answer was, "To match the number of the Philistine rulers: five swellings made of gold and five mice made of gold. One plague came on all of them and on your rulers.
6:5 Make copies of your swellings and copies of your mice that are wrecking the land, and give honor to the God of Israel. Maybe he will make his hand lighter on you, on your gods and on your land.
6:6 Why make your hearts heavy, the way Egypt and Pharaoh made their hearts heavy? Once he had made sport of them, did they not let the people go, and off they went?
6:7 So now, go and build one new cart. Take two cows that are nursing calves and have never had a yoke on them. Hitch the cows to the cart, and bring their calves back home, away from them.
6:8 Take the LORD's ark and set it on the cart. Put the gold things you are paying back to him as a guilt offering into a chest at its side. Then send it off, and let it go its way.
6:9 Then keep an eye on it. If it goes up the road to its own land, to Beth-shemesh, then he is the one who has done us this great harm. If not, we will know that his hand did not touch us. It was an accident that came our way."
6:10 The men did just that. They took two nursing cows and hitched them to the cart, and they shut their calves in at home.
6:11 They set the LORD's ark on the cart, along with the chest and the gold mice and the copies of their swellings.
6:12 The cows headed straight down the road, the road to Beth-shemesh. They kept to the one main road, mooing as they walked, and never swerved right or left. The Philistine rulers walked behind them up to the edge of Beth-shemesh's land.
6:13 Beth-shemesh was out in the valley bringing in the wheat harvest. They raised their eyes, saw the ark, and were overjoyed to see it.
6:14 The cart rolled into the field of Joshua the Beth-shemeshite and came to a stop there. There was a big stone in that place. They broke up the cart's wood and offered the cows up to the LORD as a burnt offering.
6:15 The Levites lifted down the LORD's ark and the chest that went with it, the one holding the gold things, and set them on the big stone. On that day the men of Beth-shemesh offered up burnt offerings and sacrificed sacrifices to the LORD.
6:16 The five Philistine rulers watched, and that same day they went back to Ekron.
6:17 Here are the gold swellings that the Philistines paid back to the LORD as a guilt offering: for Ashdod one, for Gaza one, for Ashkelon one, for Gath one, for Ekron one.
6:18 The gold mice matched the number of all the Philistine towns that belonged to the five rulers, both walled cities and open villages. The count reached as far as the big stone where they set down the LORD's ark. That stone is still in the field of Joshua the Beth-shemeshite to this day.
6:19 But he struck at the men of Beth-shemesh for looking into the LORD's ark. Among the people he struck down seventy men. The people grieved, because the LORD had hit them with such a terrible blow.
6:20 The men of Beth-shemesh said, "Who is able to stand in front of the LORD, this holy God? To whom will it go up from us?"
6:21 So they sent messengers to the people living in Kiriath-jearim with this message: "The Philistines have brought the LORD's ark back. Come down here and carry it up to your place."
7:1 The men of Kiriath-jearim came and carried up the LORD's ark. They took it into the house of Abinadab on the hill and set apart his son Eleazar as holy to watch over the LORD's ark.
## Samuel at Mizpah
7:2 The ark settled in Kiriath-jearim, and from that day the time grew long. Twenty years went by, and the whole house of Israel longed and wept after the LORD.
7:3 Samuel told the whole house of Israel, "If you are coming back to the LORD with your whole heart, then remove the foreign gods and the Ashtoreths from your midst. Fix your hearts on the LORD and serve no one but him. Then he will rescue you from the Philistines' grip."
7:4 The Israelites removed the Baals and the Ashtoreths, and they served the LORD alone.
7:5 Samuel said, "Bring all Israel together at Mizpah, and I will pray for you to the LORD."
7:6 They came together at Mizpah. They drew up water and poured it out in the LORD's presence, and they went without food that day. There they said, "We have sinned against the LORD." Samuel judged the Israelites at Mizpah.
7:7 The Philistines heard that the Israelites had come together at Mizpah, and the Philistine rulers marched up against Israel. The Israelites heard, and the Philistines frightened them.
7:8 The Israelites said to Samuel, "Don't fall silent! Keep calling out for us to the LORD our God, so he will rescue us from the power of the Philistines."
7:9 Samuel took a lamb still nursing and offered it up whole to the LORD as a burnt offering. Samuel called out to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD answered him.
7:10 Samuel was still sending up the burnt offering when the Philistines came close to do battle with Israel. That day the LORD thundered over the Philistines with a great voice. He threw them into confusion, and they were struck down before Israel.
7:11 The men of Israel went out from Mizpah and chased the Philistines, cutting them down as far as the place below Beth-car.
7:12 Samuel took a single stone and placed it between Mizpah and Shen. He called it Ebenezer. "Up to this point the LORD has helped us," he said.
7:13 The Philistines were brought low. They did not come into Israel's land any more, and the LORD's hand was on the Philistines for as long as Samuel lived.
7:14 The towns that the Philistines had taken from Israel came back to Israel, from Ekron to Gath. Israel won back their lands from the Philistines' grip. And there was peace between Israel and the Amorites.
7:15 Samuel judged Israel for as long as he lived.
7:16 Every year he would travel around to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah, and he judged Israel at all these places.
7:17 Then he would come back to Ramah, because his house was there. There too he judged Israel, and he built an altar to the LORD in that place.
## Israel asks for a king
8:1 Once Samuel was old, he made his sons judges for Israel.
8:2 His oldest son's name was Joel, and his second son's name was Abijah. They judged at Beersheba.
8:3 His sons did not live the way he did. They went off chasing profit; they took bribes and bent justice.
8:4 All Israel's elders met together and went to see Samuel at Ramah.
8:5 "You have grown old," they told him, "and your sons do not live the way you do. So now, set up a king for us to judge us, like all the nations."
8:6 The thing looked evil to Samuel when they said, "Give us a king to judge us." He prayed to the LORD.
8:7 The LORD said to Samuel, "Listen to the voice of the people in all they say to you. For you are not the one they have turned down. Me they have turned down, so that I am not king over them.
8:8 They are treating you the same way they have acted all along, from the day I brought them up from Egypt to now: they left me and served other gods. Now they are doing it to you too.
8:9 So now, listen to their voice. But give them a clear warning, and tell them the ways of the king who will rule them."
8:10 Samuel passed on to the people asking him for a king everything the LORD had said.
8:11 He said, "Here is how the king who rules you will act. He will take your sons and put them to work for himself, on his chariots and among his horsemen, and they will run ahead of his chariot.
8:12 He will make them officers over thousands and officers over fifties for himself. Some will plow his fields and bring in his harvest. Some will make his weapons for war and the gear for his chariots.
8:13 Your daughters he will take to mix perfumes, to cook and to bake.
8:14 Your fields, your vineyards and your olive groves, the good ones, he will take and hand over to his servants.
8:15 He will take a tenth of your grain and your grapes and give it to his court officials and his servants.
8:16 Your male and female slaves, the best of your cattle, and your donkeys he will take and set to his own work.
8:17 A tenth of your sheep and goats he will take, and you will end up as his slaves.
8:18 On that day you will cry out because of your king, the one you picked for yourselves, and on that day the LORD will not answer you."
8:19 The people would not listen to Samuel's voice. "No," they said. "There is going to be a king over us.
8:20 We too will be like all the nations. Our king will judge us, and he will march out at our head and fight our wars."
8:21 Samuel listened to everything the people said and spoke it all again in the LORD's ears.
8:22 The LORD told Samuel, "Listen to their voice, and make a king for them." So Samuel said to the men of Israel, "Go home, each of you to his town."
## Saul looks for donkeys
9:1 There lived a Benjaminite man named Kish. He was the son of Abiel, son of Zeror, son of Becorath, son of Aphiah, son of a man of Benjamin, and he was a man of strength and wealth.
9:2 He had a son named Saul, a fine young man. Not one man among the Israelites was finer than he. From his shoulders up, he stood taller than all the people.
9:3 Some she-donkeys belonging to Kish, Saul's father, went missing. Kish told his son Saul, "Please take one of the young men along, and get going. Go search for the donkeys."
9:4 Saul crossed the hill country of Ephraim and crossed the land of Shalishah, with no success. They went across the land of Shaalim: nothing. He crossed the land of Benjamin, and they still found nothing.
9:5 They had come into the land of Zuph when Saul said to the young man with him, "Let's turn around and go home. Otherwise my father will forget the donkeys and get anxious about us."
9:6 He answered, "Listen. There is a man of God in this town, and people hold him in honor. Whatever he says always happens. Let's go there now. He may be able to tell us about the trip we set out on."
9:7 "All right, we go," Saul said to the young man, "but what will we bring the man? Our bags have run out of bread, and there is no present we can bring the man of God. What have we got?"
9:8 The young man spoke up again. "See here," he answered Saul, "I happen to have a quarter shekel of silver on me. I'll hand it to the man of God, and he will tell us our way."
9:9 In earlier times in Israel, a man going to ask God something would say, "Come on, let's go to the seer," for the one now called a prophet was called a seer back then.
9:10 "Well said," Saul told the young man. "Come on, let's go." And they headed to the town where the man of God lived.
9:11 On the way up the slope to the town, they came upon girls going out to get water. "Is the seer in this place?" they asked them.
9:12 "There is," they replied. "There, in front of you! Hurry now. He came into the town today, since the people have a sacrifice today on the high place.
9:13 When you get into town, you will catch him right away, before he climbs to the high place to eat. The people won't eat until he arrives, since it's his job to bless the sacrifice. After that the guests eat. So go on up. Right now you'll find him."
9:14 So they went up to the town. Just as they came into the middle of it, Samuel came out to meet them, going up to the high place.
## Samuel meets Saul
9:15 One day before Saul arrived, the LORD had opened Samuel's ear and told him,
9:16 "At this time tomorrow I am sending you a man from the territory of Benjamin. You are to anoint him as ruler over my people Israel. He will rescue my people from the Philistines' grip, for I have seen my people; their cry has come to me."
9:17 As soon as Samuel saw Saul, the LORD told him, "Here is the man I told you of. This one will rule my people."
9:18 Saul came up to Samuel in the middle of the gate. "Please tell me," he said, "where is the seer's house?"
9:19 Samuel answered Saul, "I am the seer. Walk up in front of me to the high place. You two will eat with me today. In the morning I'll let you go, and I'll tell you everything that is on your mind.
9:20 And the donkeys that went missing on you three days ago: don't give them a thought, because they have been found. And who is it that all Israel longs for? Isn't it you and your father's whole house?"
9:21 "I'm only a Benjaminite," Saul answered, "from the smallest of Israel's tribes, and my clan is the least important clan in all the tribe of Benjamin. Why are you talking to me like this?"
9:22 Samuel took Saul and his young man and led them into the dining room. He gave them a place at the head of the guests. There were about thirty men there.
9:23 Samuel told the cook, "Bring out the share I gave you, the one I said to keep by you."
9:24 The cook lifted up the thigh and what came with it and set it before Saul. Then Samuel said, "There, that is what was saved. Put it in front of you and eat, because it was kept for you for this set time, when I said I had invited the people." So Saul ate with Samuel that day.
9:25 They came down from the high place into the town. A bed was spread for Saul on the roof, and he lay down.
9:26 They got up early. As dawn was breaking, Samuel called up to Saul on the roof, "Up! I'll send you on your way." Saul got up, and the two of them, he and Samuel, went outside.
9:27 As they were walking down to the far end of the town, Samuel said to Saul, "Tell the young man to go on in front of us." He went on ahead. "But you, stop here for now, and I will let you hear the word of God."
## Samuel anoints Saul
10:1 Samuel took the flask of oil, poured it on Saul's head and kissed him. He said, "Has the LORD not anointed you as ruler over his people Israel? You will rule the LORD's people and rescue them from the power of the enemies around them. And this will be your sign that the LORD has anointed you ruler over his inheritance:
10:2 Today, as you go away from me, you will come upon two men by Rachel's grave in Benjamin's territory, at Zelzah. They will tell you, 'The donkeys you went searching for have turned up. Now your father has let the matter of the donkeys go, and he is anxious about you two. He keeps saying, "What am I to do about my son?"'
10:3 From there you will keep going farther, and you will come to the oak of Tabor. There three men will find you, on their way up to God at Bethel. One is carrying three young goats, one is carrying three round loaves of bread, and one is carrying a skin bottle of wine.
10:4 They will ask how you are and give you two loaves. Take them from their hands.
10:5 After this you will reach Gibeah of God, where the Philistines have a post. When you get there, to the town, you will run into a band of prophets on their way down from the high place. In front of them will be a harp, a hand drum, a flute and a lyre, and they will be prophesying.
10:6 The Spirit of the LORD will sweep down on you. You will prophesy along with them, and you will be turned into another man.
10:7 When these signs happen to you, do whatever the moment calls for, because God is with you.
10:8 Then go down to Gilgal before me. I will come down to you there to send up burnt offerings and to sacrifice sacrifices of peace offerings. You are to wait seven days, until I come to you, and then I will let you know what to do."
10:9 The moment Saul turned his back to go away from Samuel, God gave him another heart. And all these signs came true on that day.
10:10 They came there, to Gibeah, and there was a band of prophets coming to meet him. The Spirit of God swept down on him, and he prophesied among them.
10:11 Everyone who had known him before saw that here he was, prophesying with the prophets. People said to each other, "What has come over Kish's son? Is Saul among the prophets too?"
10:12 A man from there answered, "And who is their father?" That is how the saying began, "Is Saul among the prophets too?"
10:13 When he had finished prophesying, he went to the high place.
10:14 Saul's uncle asked him and his young man, "Where did you go?" "To look for the donkeys," he answered. "When we saw they were nowhere to be found, we went to Samuel."
10:15 "Please tell me," said Saul's uncle, "what did Samuel say to you?"
10:16 Saul answered his uncle, "He made it clear to us that the donkeys had been found." But about the matter of the kingdom, the thing Samuel had spoken of, he told him nothing.
## Saul chosen by lot
10:17 Samuel called the people together before the LORD at Mizpah.
10:18 He said to the Israelites, "The LORD, the God of Israel, says this: 'I am the one who brought Israel up out of Egypt. I rescued you from the power of the Egyptians and from the power of all the kingdoms that were crushing you.'
10:19 But today you have turned down your God, the one who rescues you from all your troubles and hardships. You told him, 'No! Set a king over us.' So now stand before the LORD tribe by tribe and clan by clan."
10:20 Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel forward, and the lot fell on the tribe of Benjamin.
10:21 He brought the tribe of Benjamin forward family by family, and the family of the Matrites was picked. Then Saul son of Kish was picked. They searched for him, but he could not be found.
10:22 So they asked the LORD again, "Has the man come here yet?" The LORD said, "Look, he is hiding among the baggage."
10:23 They ran and brought him from there. When he took his stand among the people, from his shoulders up he stood taller than all the people.
10:24 Samuel said to all the people, "Do you see the one the LORD has chosen? Among all the people there is no one like him." And all the people raised a shout. "Long live the king!" they said.
10:25 Samuel told the people the rules of the kingdom. He wrote them in a scroll and placed it before the LORD. Then Samuel dismissed all the people to their own houses.
10:26 Saul too went home to Gibeah, and with him went the fighting men God had touched in their hearts.
10:27 But some worthless men said, "How will this one rescue us?" They looked down on him and brought him no gift. Nahash, king of the Ammonites, had been harshly oppressing the Gadites and the Reubenites. He put out every right eye they had, and no one rescued Israel. Among the Israelites across the Jordan, not a man was left whose right eye Nahash king of the Ammonites had not put out. But seven thousand men had escaped from the Ammonites and had come to Jabesh-gilead.
## Saul rescues Jabesh-gilead
11:1 About a month later, Nahash the Ammonite marched up and camped against Jabesh-gilead. All the men of Jabesh said to Nahash, "Make a covenant with us, and we will be your servants."
11:2 Nahash the Ammonite answered them, "Here are my terms for a covenant with you: I put out every right eye you have. I will make it a disgrace laid on all Israel."
11:3 The elders of Jabesh told him, "Leave us alone for seven days, and let us send messengers through all the land of Israel. If no one rescues us, we will come out to you."
11:4 The messengers came to Saul's Gibeah and spoke these words in the people's hearing, and all the people burst out weeping.
11:5 Right then Saul came in from the field, following the oxen. "What's the matter with the people," Saul asked, "that they are crying?" They told him what the men of Jabesh had said.
11:6 As Saul heard these words, the Spirit of God swept down on him, and his anger blazed up hot.
11:7 He took a yoke of oxen and hacked them into pieces. He sent the pieces all through Israel's land by messengers, with this message: "Whoever does not come out after Saul and after Samuel, this is what will be done to his oxen." Then fear of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out like one man.
11:8 Saul counted them at Bezek. There were three hundred thousand Israelites and thirty thousand men of Judah.
11:9 They said to the messengers who had come, "Tell this to the men of Jabesh-gilead: Tomorrow, when the sun grows hot, rescue will come to you." The messengers went and reported it to the men of Jabesh, and they were glad.
11:10 So the men of Jabesh said, "Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you can do to us anything you think is good."
11:11 The next day Saul split the army into three groups. In the morning watch they came into the middle of the camp, and they struck down the Ammonites until the day grew hot. The ones left alive scattered so widely that you could not find two of them together.
11:12 The people said to Samuel, "Who were the ones saying, 'Is Saul going to be king over us?' Hand those men over, and we will put them to death."
11:13 But Saul said, "Not one man is to die today, because today the LORD has won a rescue in Israel."
11:14 Samuel told the people, "Come on, let's go to Gilgal and make the kingdom new there."
11:15 All the people went to Gilgal. There, before the LORD at Gilgal, they made Saul king. There they sacrificed peace offerings before the LORD, and there Saul and all the men of Israel celebrated with great joy.
