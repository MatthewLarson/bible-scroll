## Samuel steps aside
12:1 Samuel spoke to all Israel. "Look," he said, "I listened to you in everything you told me. I have put a king over you.
12:2 Now look: the king walks at your head. I am old, my hair is gray, and my sons are here among you. I have walked at your head from the time I was young until today.
12:3 Here I am. Testify against me before the LORD and before his anointed one. Whose ox did I take? Whose donkey did I take? Whom did I exploit? Whom did I crush? From whose hand did I take a bribe to look the other way? I will give it back to you."
12:4 "You did not exploit us," they said. "You did not crush us. Nothing at all ever passed from anyone's hand into yours."
12:5 Samuel said to them, "The LORD is a witness against you, and his anointed one is a witness today, that you have found nothing in my hands." "Yes, he is witness," they answered.
## The LORD's record and Israel's
12:6 Then Samuel said to the people, "The LORD is the one who appointed Moses and Aaron, and he brought your ancestors up from the land of Egypt.
12:7 So now, present yourselves, and I will take you to court before the LORD. I will lay out all the ways the LORD has done right by you and by your ancestors.
12:8 When Jacob went to Egypt, your ancestors cried out to the LORD. The LORD sent Moses and Aaron. They brought your ancestors out of Egypt and gave them a home in this place.
12:9 But they forgot the LORD their God. So he sold them into the power of Sisera, the commander of Hazor's army, and into the power of the Philistines and of the king of Moab, and these made war on them.
12:10 They cried out to the LORD and said, 'We have sinned. We abandoned the LORD and served the Baals and the Ashtoreths. Rescue us now from our enemies' grip, so that we may serve you.'
12:11 Then the LORD sent Jerubbaal, Barak, Jephthah and Samuel. He rescued you from the enemies all around you, and you lived secure.
12:12 But when you saw Nahash king of the Ammonites coming against you, you said to me, 'No! A king must rule over us!' And all the while the LORD your God was your king.
12:13 So now, here he is: the king you chose, the king you asked for. The LORD has put him over you as king.
12:14 If you will fear the LORD, serve him and listen to his voice, and not defy what the LORD says, then you and the king who rules you will be followers of the LORD your God.
12:15 But if you do not listen to the LORD's voice, and you defy what the LORD says, the LORD's hand will turn on you and on your ancestors.
## Thunder at harvest time
12:16 Even now, take your places and see it: the LORD is about to do a great thing right in front of you.
12:17 Isn't this the wheat harvest? I will call to the LORD, and he will send thunder and rain. Then you will know and see that you did a great evil in the LORD's eyes by asking for a king of your own."
12:18 Samuel called out to the LORD, and the LORD sent thunder and rain on that day. All the people were deeply afraid of the LORD and of Samuel.
12:19 All the people said to Samuel, "Pray for your servants to the LORD your God! Do not let us die! We have piled one more evil on top of all our sins: asking for a king of our own."
12:20 Samuel told the people, "Don't be afraid. You did do all this evil. Only, don't stop following the LORD. Serve the LORD with your whole heart.
12:21 Do not turn aside. That way leads after hollow things, which cannot help you or rescue you, because they are hollow.
12:22 The LORD will not walk away from his people. His great name is at stake. He took it upon himself to make you his own people.
12:23 And me? I will never sin against the LORD by giving up praying for you. I will teach you to walk the good and honest path.
12:24 Only fear the LORD. Serve him honestly, with your whole heart. Look how much he has done for you!
12:25 But if you keep doing evil, you and your king alike will be swept away."
## War with the Philistines
13:1 Saul was [...] years old when he became king. He ruled over Israel [...] two years.
13:2 Saul picked out three thousand men from Israel. He kept two thousand with him at Michmash and in the hills of Bethel, and a thousand stayed with Jonathan at Gibeah in Benjamin. The rest of the people he sent away, every man to his tent.
13:3 Jonathan struck down the Philistine post in Geba, and word of it reached the Philistines. Saul had the ram's horn sounded across the whole land, with the call, "Let the Hebrews hear!"
13:4 Word went out to all Israel: "Saul has struck the Philistine post, and now Israel stinks to the Philistines." The people were called out to follow Saul to Gilgal.
13:5 The Philistines massed for battle against Israel. They had three thousand chariots and six thousand horsemen, and foot soldiers like the sand on the seashore, too many to count. They came up and made camp at Michmash, east of Beth-aven.
13:6 The men of Israel could see they were in trouble; the troops were under heavy pressure. So the people hid themselves: in caves, in thorn bushes, among the cliffs, in underground rooms and in cisterns.
13:7 Hebrews went across the Jordan into Gad and Gilead country. Saul himself stayed at Gilgal, and the people who followed him shook with fear.
## Saul does not wait
13:8 For seven days Saul waited, until the time Samuel had fixed. But Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the people began drifting away from Saul.
13:9 Saul said, "Bring the burnt offering and the peace offerings over to me." Then he offered up the burnt offering.
13:10 Just then, as he finished the burnt offering, there was Samuel, coming! Saul went out to meet him and greet him with a blessing.
13:11 Samuel said, "What have you done?" "The people were drifting away from me," Saul answered. "You had not come within the days you set, and the Philistines were massing at Michmash.
13:12 I said to myself, 'The Philistines are about to march down on me here at Gilgal, and I have not yet pleaded with the LORD.' So I pushed myself to do it, and I offered the burnt offering."
13:13 "You have acted like a fool," Samuel said to Saul. "You have not obeyed what the LORD your God commanded you. Had you done so, the LORD would by now have set your kingship over Israel firm for all time.
13:14 But now your kingship will not last. The LORD has searched for a man to suit his own heart, and the LORD has appointed him ruler over his people, since you have not done what the LORD ordered you."
13:15 Samuel rose and left Gilgal, going up to Gibeah in Benjamin. Saul mustered the troops still with him: about six hundred men.
## No smiths in Israel
13:16 Saul and Jonathan his son, with the troops they had, took up position at Geba in Benjamin. The Philistines had their camp at Michmash.
13:17 The destroyers came out of the Philistine camp in three columns. One column headed down the road to Ophrah, toward the land of Shual.
13:18 A second column headed toward Beth-horon. The third took the road to the ridge above the Valley of Zeboim, facing the wilderness.
13:19 There was no smith to be found in the whole land of Israel. The Philistines had seen to it. "We don't want the Hebrews making swords or spears," they had said.
13:20 Every Israelite had to go down to the Philistines to get his plow blade, his hoe, his ax or his sickle sharpened.
13:21 The charge was a pim for plow blades and hoes, and a third of a shekel for forks and axes and for setting the point of an ox goad.
13:22 So when the day of battle came, not one of the people who followed Saul and Jonathan held a sword or a spear. Saul had them, and his son Jonathan, and no one else.
13:23 Meanwhile a Philistine post had moved out to hold the pass at Michmash.
## Jonathan climbs the cliff
14:1 The day came when Jonathan son of Saul said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come on, let's go across to the Philistine post over on the far side." He told his father nothing about it.
14:2 Saul sat at the far edge of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree in Migron, with about six hundred men around him.
14:3 Ahijah was there too, carrying the ephod. He was the son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod son of Phinehas, son of Eli, the LORD's priest at Shiloh. No one among the people knew Jonathan was gone.
14:4 In the pass that Jonathan was trying to cross to reach the Philistine post, a jagged crag rose on this side and another jagged crag on that side. One was called Bozez, the other Seneh.
14:5 The one crag rose like a column on the north side, in front of Michmash. The other was on the south side, in front of Geba.
14:6 Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come on, let's go across to the post of these uncircumcised men. Maybe the LORD will act for us. The LORD is not held back. He can save with many or with few."
14:7 His armor-bearer said to him, "Do everything your heart tells you. Turn that way if you like. I am with you; my heart is your heart."
14:8 "Here is the plan," said Jonathan. "We go across to those men and step out where they can see us.
14:9 If this is what they tell us: 'Hold still until we get to you,' then we stay put and do not climb up to them.
14:10 If instead they say, 'Come on up to us,' then up we go. The LORD has handed them over to us. That is the sign for us."
14:11 The two of them stepped into view of the Philistine post. "Look," the Philistines said, "Hebrews are coming out of the holes they hid in!"
14:12 The men of the post called down to Jonathan and his armor-bearer, "Come up to us, and we'll show you something!" "Follow me up," Jonathan told his armor-bearer. "The LORD has put them in Israel's power."
14:13 Jonathan scrambled up on hands and feet, and his armor-bearer came up behind him. Men went down in front of Jonathan, and his armor-bearer, following behind, finished them off.
14:14 The first slaughter that Jonathan and his armor-bearer made was about twenty men, inside a space about half a furrow long.
## The ground shakes
14:15 A shudder ran through the camp, through the field and through all the troops. The men at the post and the destroyers shook as well. The ground itself quaked. It was a trembling from God.
14:16 From Gibeah in Benjamin, Saul's watchmen saw it: the great crowd was melting away, going off this way and that.
14:17 Saul told the troops with him, "Count heads, and find out who has gone from us." They counted, and Jonathan was not there, nor his armor-bearer.
14:18 Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring the ark of God here." At that time the ark of God was with the Israelites.
14:19 Saul was still talking to the priest when the noise from the Philistine camp grew and grew. "Pull your hand back," Saul told the priest.
14:20 Saul and all his troops rallied and went into the fight. And look: every man's sword was against his own comrade. The confusion was enormous.
14:21 There were Hebrews who had sided with the Philistines in earlier days and had gone up with them into the camp from the country around. They too changed sides, to join the Israelites with Saul and Jonathan.
14:22 Up in the hills of Ephraim, the Israelites who had been hiding heard that the Philistines had fled. They too went hard after them in the battle.
14:23 So the LORD rescued Israel that day, and the fighting swept on past Beth-aven.
## Saul's oath
14:24 That day the men of Israel were hard pressed. Saul had made the people swear: "A curse on anyone who eats food before evening, until I take revenge on my enemies!" So not one of the people tasted food.
14:25 All the people of the land came into the forest, and there was honey lying out on the open field.
14:26 When the people came into the forest, there was the honey, dripping. But no one lifted his hand to his mouth, because the people were afraid of the oath.
14:27 Jonathan, though, had not heard when his father made the people swear. He held out the tip of the stick he had in his hand and dipped it into the comb of honey. When he brought his hand back to his mouth, his eyes lit up.
14:28 One of the troops spoke up. "Your father made the people swear," he said. "He told them, 'A curse on anyone who eats food today.'" And the people were worn out.
14:29 Jonathan said, "My father has stirred up trouble for the land. Just see how bright my eyes are, from one little taste of this honey!
14:30 If only the people had eaten their fill today of what they took from their enemies! Then the beating the Philistines took would have been far worse."
## Eating meat with the blood
14:31 They kept striking the Philistines that day all the way from Michmash to Aijalon, until the people were completely worn out.
14:32 The people swooped down on the spoil. They grabbed sheep, cattle and calves and slaughtered them right on the ground, and the people ate them, blood and all.
14:33 Someone told Saul, "Look! The people are eating meat, blood and all. That is a sin against the LORD." "You traitors!" Saul said. "Roll a big stone over to me today."
14:34 Then he said, "Scatter through the troops and tell them: 'Every man is to bring his ox or his sheep to me, kill it here and eat it. You must not sin against the LORD by eating meat with its blood.'" That night each one of the people led in his own ox and slaughtered it there.
14:35 Saul built an altar to the LORD; this was where he started building altars to the LORD.
## The lot falls on Jonathan
14:36 Saul said, "Let's go down after the Philistines tonight. We'll strip them bare until the morning light and not leave a man among them." "Do what looks good to you," they said. But the priest said, "Let's come close to God, right here."
14:37 Saul put the question to God: "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you put them in Israel's power?" No answer came from him that day.
14:38 Then Saul said, "All you cornerstones of the people, come over here. Find out what this sin is that happened today, and how.
14:39 By the life of the LORD, Israel's rescuer, even if the sin is in my son Jonathan, he must die for it." No one among all the people answered him.
14:40 He said to all Israel, "You be on one side. I and my son Jonathan will be on the other side." "Do what looks good to you," the people told Saul.
14:41 Saul said, "LORD God of Israel, why did you not answer your servant today? If the wrong lies in me or in my son Jonathan, LORD God of Israel, let Urim come out. If the wrong lies in your people Israel, let Thummim come out." Jonathan and Saul were taken by the lot, and the people went free.
14:42 "Now make it fall between me and Jonathan my son," Saul said. The lot took Jonathan.
14:43 "What did you do? Tell me," Saul said to Jonathan. Jonathan told him. "I had a taste, just a taste, of a little honey on the tip of the stick in my hand," he said. "Here I am, ready to die."
14:44 Saul said, "God do so, and more besides! You will die, Jonathan, you will surely die."
14:45 The people said to Saul, "Is Jonathan to die? He brought about this great rescue in Israel! Far from it! By the life of the LORD, not a single hair from his head will drop to the ground. What he did today, he did with God." So the people ransomed Jonathan, and he did not die.
14:46 Saul called off the chase and went back up, and the Philistines went home to their own territory.
## Saul's wars and family
14:47 Once Saul had taken hold of the kingship over Israel, he made war all around on all his enemies: on Moab, on the Ammonites, on Edom, on the kings of Zobah and on the Philistines. Everywhere he turned, he got the better of them.
14:48 He did mighty deeds. He struck down Amalek and set Israel free from those who looted it.
14:49 These were Saul's sons: Jonathan, Ishvi and Malchishua. He had two daughters. The older was named Merab, and the younger Michal.
14:50 His wife was Ahinoam, daughter of Ahimaaz. His army commander was Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle.
14:51 Kish was the father of Saul. Ner, the father of Abner, was a son of Abiel.
14:52 As long as Saul lived, the war with the Philistines was hard. Any time Saul spotted a powerful man or a man of courage, he drew him into his own ranks.
## The war on Amalek
15:1 Samuel said to Saul, "It was me the LORD sent to anoint you as king of his people Israel. So now, hear the sound of the LORD's words.
15:2 The LORD of Armies says this: 'I have kept count of what Amalek did to Israel. It set itself in Israel's path on the road up from Egypt.
15:3 Go now. Strike Amalek, and put all they have under the ban. Have no pity on them. Put to death man and woman, child and nursing baby, ox and sheep, camel and donkey.'"
15:4 Saul summoned the people and counted them at Telaim. There were two hundred thousand on foot, plus ten thousand men of Judah.
15:5 Saul went as far as the town of Amalek and lay in wait in the dry streambed.
15:6 Saul sent word to the Kenites: "Go! Leave! Get down and away from the Amalekites, or I will sweep you away with them. You treated all the Israelites with loyal love when they came up from Egypt." The Kenites pulled out from among Amalek.
15:7 Saul struck Amalek from Havilah on the way to Shur, which lies next to Egypt.
15:8 Agag king of Amalek he captured alive, but all the people he put under the ban by the edge of the sword.
15:9 Saul and the people took pity on Agag, and on the pick of the sheep and cattle, the second-year animals, the lambs and all that was good. Those they would not put under the ban. Whatever was cheap and worthless, that they put under the ban.
## Listening is better than sacrifice
15:10 The LORD's word came to Samuel:
15:11 "I regret making Saul king. He has turned his back on me, and he has not done what I said." Samuel was hot with anger, and all night long he cried out to the LORD.
15:12 Samuel rose early to go and meet Saul in the morning. Word came to Samuel: "Saul went to Carmel, and look, he is putting up a monument to himself! Then he moved on and went down to Gilgal."
15:13 Samuel came up to Saul. "Blessed are you by the LORD!" Saul said to him. "I have done what the LORD told me."
15:14 "And what," Samuel said, "is this sound of sheep in my ears, and this sound of cattle I keep hearing?"
15:15 "They brought them from the Amalekites," Saul said. "The people took pity on the pick of the flocks and herds, to offer them as sacrifices to the LORD your God. Everything else we put under the ban."
15:16 "Be quiet," Samuel said to Saul, "and I will tell you what the LORD spoke to me during the night." "Speak," he said.
15:17 Samuel said, "You may think yourself small, but aren't you head of Israel's tribes? The LORD anointed you as king over Israel.
15:18 He sent you on the road with orders: 'Go, and put the sinners, Amalek, under the ban. Fight them until they are finished.'
15:19 Why did you not listen to the LORD's voice? Why did you swoop down on the spoil and do what is evil in the LORD's eyes?"
15:20 Saul said to Samuel, "But I did listen to the LORD's voice! I went the way the LORD sent me. I brought back Agag king of Amalek, and I put Amalek under the ban.
15:21 It was the people who took from the spoil some sheep and cattle, the first and best of the banned things, to sacrifice to the LORD your God at Gilgal."
15:22 Samuel said, "Does the LORD take as much pleasure in burnt offerings and sacrifices / as in listening to the LORD's voice? / Look: listening is better than sacrifice, / paying attention better than the fat of rams.
15:23 Rebellion is the sin of fortune-telling, / and stubborn insistence is the evil of household gods. / You rejected the LORD's word, / so he has rejected you as king."
## The torn robe
15:24 "I have sinned," Saul said to Samuel. "I overstepped the LORD's order and your words. I was afraid of the people, and I listened to their voice.
15:25 Now please, carry away my sin. Turn back with me, so I can bow down before the LORD."
15:26 "I will not turn back with you," Samuel told Saul. "You rejected the LORD's word, so the LORD has rejected you. You are no longer to be king over Israel."
15:27 Samuel swung around to leave, and he caught at the corner of his robe, and it ripped.
15:28 Samuel said to him, "Today the LORD has ripped Israel's kingship off you and given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you.
15:29 Also, Israel's Enduring One does not lie and does not regret. He is not a human being, to have regrets."
15:30 "I have sinned," Saul said. "Even so, please give me honor now, before my people's elders and before Israel. Turn back with me, and I will bow down to the LORD your God."
15:31 Samuel turned and followed Saul back, and Saul bowed down before the LORD.
## The death of Agag
15:32 Samuel said, "Bring Agag king of Amalek over to me." Agag came to him in chains. Agag said, "Surely the bitter taste of death has passed."
15:33 Samuel said, "Your sword left women without children; / so your mother, of all women, will be left without a child." And Samuel hacked Agag to pieces before the LORD at Gilgal.
15:34 Samuel went off to Ramah. Saul went up to his own house at Gibeah of Saul.
15:35 Samuel never saw Saul again until the day he died, but Samuel mourned over Saul. And the LORD regretted having made Saul king over Israel.
## David anointed at Bethlehem
16:1 The LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you keep grieving over Saul? I myself have rejected him from ruling over Israel. Fill your horn with oil and go. I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, because I have seen a king for myself among his sons."
16:2 "How can I go?" Samuel asked. "Saul will hear, and he will kill me." The LORD said, "Take a heifer from the herd along with you. Say, 'I have come to offer a sacrifice to the LORD.'
16:3 Call Jesse to the sacrifice. Then I will make known to you what you are to do, and you will anoint the one I tell you to anoint for me."
16:4 Samuel did as the LORD said and went to Bethlehem. The town's elders hurried out to meet him, shaking. "Have you come in peace?" they said.
16:5 "Peace," he said. "I have come to offer sacrifice to the LORD. Make yourselves holy, and come with me to the sacrifice." Then he made Jesse and his sons holy, and he called them to the sacrifice.
16:6 As they arrived, he saw Eliab, and he said, "Surely the LORD's anointed stands here before him."
16:7 But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look at his face or how tall he stands. I have rejected him. It is not a matter of what a human sees. A human sees what is before the eyes, but the LORD sees the heart."
16:8 Jesse called Abinadab and walked him past Samuel. "This one too the LORD has not chosen," Samuel said.
16:9 Then Jesse walked Shammah past. "This one too the LORD has not chosen," Samuel said.
16:10 So Jesse paraded seven of his sons in front of Samuel. Samuel told Jesse, "The LORD has chosen none of these."
16:11 Samuel said to Jesse, "Is that the last of the boys?" "The smallest one is left," Jesse said, "but he's shepherding the flock." "Send and get him," Samuel told Jesse. "We will not take our places until he gets here."
16:12 Jesse sent and had him brought in. He had a reddish glow, lovely eyes and good looks. "Up! Anoint him," the LORD said. "He is the one."
16:13 Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him there among his brothers. And from that day on, the LORD's Spirit swept down on David. Samuel set out and went back to Ramah.
## David at Saul's court
16:14 The LORD's Spirit had turned away from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD kept terrifying him.
16:15 Saul's servants told him, "Look, please: an evil spirit from God is frightening you.
16:16 Our lord need only say the word to your servants here in front of you. We will find a man who knows how to play the lyre. Whenever the evil spirit from God is on you, he will play it with his hand, and things will go well for you."
16:17 Saul told his servants, "Pick out a good player for me, and bring him to me."
16:18 One of the young men spoke up. "Listen: I have seen one of Jesse's sons, the Bethlehemite. He knows how to play. He's a strong fighter and a soldier, good with words, handsome, and the LORD is with him."
16:19 So Saul sent messengers to Jesse: "Send me David your son, who is out with the flock."
16:20 Jesse loaded a donkey with bread, took a skin of wine and one young goat, and sent them to Saul by his son David.
16:21 David came to Saul and stood before him as his attendant. Saul came to love him deeply, and he became Saul's armor-bearer.
16:22 Saul sent word to Jesse: "Let David keep standing before me, please. He has won my favor."
16:23 So whenever God's spirit was on Saul, David picked up the lyre and played it. Saul could breathe again, things went well with him, and the evil spirit turned away from him.
## The Philistine champion
17:1 The Philistines brought their armies together for battle. They massed at Socoh, a town of Judah, and set up camp between Socoh and Azekah at Ephes-dammim.
17:2 Saul and the men of Israel came together too. They camped in the Valley of Elah and formed their battle line facing the Philistines.
17:3 The Philistines held the mountain on this side, Israel held the mountain on that side, and the valley ran between them.
17:4 Out from the Philistine camp stepped a champion named Goliath, from Gath. He stood four cubits and a span tall.
17:5 On his head was a bronze helmet. He was dressed in a coat of scales, and the coat weighed five thousand shekels of bronze.
17:6 Bronze shin guards covered his legs, and a bronze javelin hung between his shoulders.
17:7 His spear had a shaft like the beam on a weavers' loom, and its blade weighed six hundred shekels of iron. A man carrying his shield walked ahead of him.
17:8 He stood and called across to Israel's lines: "What are you doing, coming out to set up a battle line? I am the Philistine, and you are Saul's servants, aren't you? Choose a man of your own, and send him down to me.
17:9 If he is able to fight it out with me and strike me down, then we will be your slaves. If I get the upper hand and strike him down, then you will be our slaves, and you will serve us."
17:10 "Today I throw my insults at the ranks of Israel," the Philistine said. "Give me a man, and we will fight each other!"
17:11 Saul and all Israel heard what the Philistine said, and they were crushed in spirit and very much afraid.
## David comes to the camp
17:12 David was the son of that Ephrathite from Bethlehem in Judah whose name was Jesse. Jesse had eight sons. In the days of Saul, he was an old man, well on in years.
17:13 The three oldest sons of Jesse had gone; they had marched after Saul to the war. Of the three sons who went to war, the firstborn was Eliab, the next Abinadab and the third Shammah.
17:14 David was the smallest. The three big ones had gone after Saul,
17:15 and David used to come and go between Saul's service and shepherding his father's flock in Bethlehem.
17:16 Morning and evening the Philistine came near and planted himself there, forty days running.
17:17 Jesse said to his son David, "Please take your brothers this ephah of parched grain and these ten loaves of bread, and hurry them to the camp for your brothers.
17:18 These ten slices of cheese, take to the commander of their thousand. Check on how your brothers are, and get some token from them to bring back.
17:19 Saul is there, and they are, with all the men of Israel, in the Valley of Elah, fighting the Philistines."
17:20 David rose early in the morning, left the sheep with someone to watch them, picked up the load and went, as Jesse had told him. He came to the ring of the camp just when the army was marching out to its line, raising the battle shout.
17:21 Israel and the Philistines had set themselves in order, battle line facing battle line.
17:22 David handed over the gear he carried to the supply keeper and ran to the front line. When he got there, he asked his brothers if all was well.
17:23 He was still speaking with them when, look, the champion came up out of the Philistine lines. His name was Goliath, the Philistine from Gath, and he spoke the same words again. David heard them.
17:24 All the men of Israel, as soon as they saw the man, ran away from him in terror.
17:25 Someone in Israel said, "Have you seen this man who keeps coming up? He comes up to heap insults on Israel. Whoever strikes him down, the king will load him with riches. He will give him his daughter, and he will make his father's family free in Israel."
17:26 David asked the men standing by him, "The man who strikes down this Philistine and lifts this shame off Israel, what will he get? Who is this uncircumcised Philistine anyway, to insult the battle lines of the living God?"
17:27 The people gave him the same answer: "This is what the man who strikes him down will get."
17:28 Eliab, his big brother, heard him talking to the men, and Eliab flared up in anger at David. "What did you come down here for?" he said. "Who's looking after that little flock of sheep out in the wilderness? I know you, your pride and your bad heart. You came down to see the fighting!"
17:29 "What did I do now?" David said. "It was just talk, wasn't it?"
17:30 He turned away from Eliab toward another man and said the same thing, and the people answered him the way they had the first time.
17:31 People heard what David was saying and told it to Saul, who had him brought in.
## A shepherd's weapons
17:32 "No one's heart should sink because of him," David told Saul. "Your servant will go out and fight with this Philistine."
17:33 But Saul said to David, "You cannot take on this Philistine in a fight. You're just a lad, and he has been a soldier since he was a lad himself."
17:34 David said to Saul, "Your servant used to shepherd sheep for his father. A lion would come, or a bear, and grab a lamb out of the flock.
17:35 I would go out after it, hit it, and snatch the lamb out of its mouth. When it rose up against me, I would grab it by the beard, hit it and kill it.
17:36 Lion and bear alike, your servant has struck them down. And this uncircumcised Philistine will end up like one of them, since he has insulted the battle lines of the living God."
17:37 "The LORD saved me from the lion's claws and from the bear's claws," David said. "He is the one who will save me from this Philistine's hand." "Go, then," Saul told David, "and the LORD will be with you."
17:38 Saul put his own battle clothes on David. He set a bronze helmet on his head and clothed him in a coat of mail.
17:39 David buckled the sword on over the clothes and made an effort to walk, but he had never tried them out. "I can't walk in these," David told Saul. "I've never tried them out." And David pulled them off.
17:40 He took his stick in his hand. From the streambed he picked out five smooth stones for himself and dropped them into the shepherd's bag he had, the pouch. His sling was in his hand. Then he moved toward the Philistine.
## The sling and the stone
17:41 On came the Philistine, nearer and nearer to David, and the man with the big shield walked ahead of him.
17:42 The Philistine took one look at David and sneered at him. He was just a lad, with a reddish glow and good looks.
17:43 "Am I a dog," the Philistine said to David, "that you're coming at me with sticks?" Then the Philistine called down curses on David by his gods.
17:44 "Come on over to me," the Philistine told David, "and I'll feed your flesh to the birds in the sky and the beasts in the field."
17:45 David said to the Philistine, "You are coming at me with a sword, a spear and a javelin. I am coming at you in the name of the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel's battle lines, whom you have insulted.
17:46 This very day the LORD will hand you over to me. I will strike you, and I will take your head off you. And the corpses of the Philistine camp I will give today to the birds of the sky and the wild beasts of the earth. Then all the earth will know there is a God who belongs to Israel.
17:47 Everyone gathered here will learn that the LORD saves, and not by sword and spear. The battle belongs to the LORD, and he will put you in our power."
17:48 The Philistine rose and came on, closing in on David. David ran fast toward the battle line to meet the Philistine.
17:49 David put his hand into the bag and took a stone out of it. He slung it and hit the Philistine in the forehead. The stone sank deep into his forehead, and he fell face down on the ground.
17:50 That is how David got the better of the Philistine: with a sling and a stone. He struck the Philistine and put him to death, and David had no sword in his hand.
17:51 David ran up and stood over the Philistine. He took hold of the man's sword, pulled it out of its sheath and finished him off, and he cut off his head with it. When the Philistines saw their strongman dead, they fled.
17:52 Then the men of Israel and Judah rose up, raised the war cry and chased the Philistines all the way to Gath and to the gates of Ekron. The Philistine dead fell along the Shaaraim road, as far as Gath and Ekron.
17:53 The Israelites came back from their hot pursuit of the Philistines and stripped the Philistine camps.
17:54 David carried the Philistine's head to Jerusalem, but the man's gear he kept in his own tent.
## "Whose son is this?"
17:55 When Saul saw David go out to meet the Philistine, he asked Abner, the commander of the army, "Whose son is this lad, Abner?" And Abner said, "By your life, O king, I do not know."
17:56 The king said, "You go and ask whose son this young fellow is."
17:57 David was coming back from striking down the Philistine when Abner took him and led him in to Saul. The Philistine's head was in his hand.
17:58 Saul said to him, "Young man, whose son are you?" "The son of your servant Jesse, the Bethlehemite," David answered.
## Jonathan's covenant
18:1 By the time David had finished talking with Saul, Jonathan's life was tied up with David's life, and Jonathan loved him as much as his own life.
18:2 Saul took him in that day and did not allow him to go home again to his father's house.
18:3 Jonathan and David made a covenant, since Jonathan loved him as much as his own life.
18:4 Jonathan stripped off the robe he had on and gave it to David. He gave him his battle clothes too, down to his sword, his bow and his belt.
18:5 Wherever Saul sent him, David went out and handled things wisely. Saul set him over the fighting men. All the people thought it was good, and so did Saul's servants.
## Saul's jealousy
18:6 When they came home, as David came back from striking the Philistine, the women poured out of every town in Israel to meet King Saul. They sang and danced with tambourines, with joy and with three-stringed lutes.
18:7 The women sang in turns as they celebrated: "Saul has struck his thousands, / David his tens of thousands."
18:8 This burned Saul up. He took the song badly. "They gave David the tens of thousands," he said, "and they gave me the thousands. The only thing still missing for him is the kingship!"
18:9 Saul eyed David with suspicion from that day forward.
18:10 The very next day an evil spirit from God swept down on Saul, and he fell into a prophet's frenzy in the middle of the house. David was playing music with his hand, as he did every day, and the spear was in Saul's hand.
18:11 Saul threw the spear. "I'll strike David right into the wall," he said. David slipped away from him twice.
18:12 Saul was afraid of David. The LORD was with David, and from Saul he had turned away.
18:13 So Saul moved him out of his presence and made him a commander of a thousand. David led the people out and brought them in.
18:14 In all he undertook, David acted wisely and succeeded, and the LORD was with him.
18:15 Saul saw how very wisely he handled things, and he lived in dread of him.
18:16 But David was loved by all Israel and Judah, because he was the one leading them out and back.
## The king's daughters
18:17 Saul said to David, "Look: Merab, my older girl. She's yours to marry. Just be a man of courage for me and fight the LORD's wars." Saul was thinking, "Let my hand not be on him. Let it be the Philistines' hand on him."
18:18 But David said to Saul, "Who am I? Who are my people, my father's clan, in Israel, for me to be son-in-law to the king?"
18:19 When the time arrived to give Saul's daughter Merab to David, she was handed to Adriel the Meholathite as his wife.
18:20 Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David. People told Saul, and the news suited him.
18:21 "I'll give her to him," Saul said to himself. "She will be the bait to trap him, and the Philistines' hand will be on him." So Saul told David, "Through the second one, you will be my son-in-law today."
18:22 Saul gave his servants orders: "Have a quiet word with David. Tell him, 'The king is delighted with you, you know, and all his servants love you. So go ahead and marry into the king's family.'"
18:23 Saul's servants spoke those words into David's ears. David said, "Is being the king's son-in-law a light matter in your eyes? I am a poor man, and no one thinks much of me."
18:24 Saul's servants reported back to him: "David said such and such."
18:25 Saul said, "Here is what to say to David: 'The king has no wish for a bride-price, only for a hundred Philistine foreskins, to get revenge on the king's enemies.'" What Saul had in mind was to make David fall by the Philistines' hand.
18:26 His servants passed the words on to David, and the deal looked good in David's eyes: he could be the king's son-in-law. The time set had not yet run out.
18:27 David set out with his men and struck down two hundred of the Philistines. David brought their foreskins, and they were handed over to the king in full count, so that he could become the king's son-in-law. Then Saul gave him Michal his daughter as his wife.
18:28 Saul saw it, and he knew: the LORD was with David, and Michal, Saul's own daughter, loved him.
18:29 So Saul's fear of David grew even greater. Saul was David's enemy from then on.
18:30 The Philistine lords would march out to war, and whenever they marched out, David handled things more wisely than any of Saul's servants. His name came to be greatly valued.
## Jonathan speaks up for David
19:1 Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants about killing David. But Jonathan, Saul's son, took great delight in David.
19:2 Jonathan told David, "Saul my father is trying to kill you. So tomorrow morning, please, watch yourself. Stay somewhere secret and hide.
19:3 I'll go out and take my place by my father's side in the field where you'll be. I will talk about you with my father, and whatever I see, I'll let you know."
19:4 Jonathan put in a good word for David with Saul his father. "Let the king not sin against his servant David," he said. "David has not sinned against you. What he has done has served you very well.
19:5 He risked his life to strike down the Philistine, and the LORD worked a great rescue for all Israel. You saw it and were happy. So why would you sin against innocent blood, putting David to death for nothing?"
19:6 Saul paid attention to what Jonathan said, and he swore an oath: "By the life of the LORD, he shall not be killed."
19:7 Jonathan called David over and told him everything that was said. Then Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in Saul's presence as in the old days.
## Michal saves David
19:8 Then there was war again. David marched out to battle with the Philistines, struck them a great blow and put them to flight.
19:9 Then an evil spirit from the LORD came over Saul. He was sitting in his house, spear in hand, while David played music.
19:10 Saul tried to drive the spear through David into the wall. But David slipped out of his way, and the spear struck the wall. David fled and got away that night.
19:11 Saul sent messengers to David's house to keep watch on him and kill him in the morning. Michal his wife warned David: "Unless you get yourself away tonight, tomorrow you're a dead man."
19:12 Michal lowered David through the window, and off he went. He ran and got away.
19:13 Then Michal took the household god and put it in the bed. She set a mat of goat hair where the head would be and covered it with a cloak.
19:14 Saul sent messengers to seize David, but she said, "He's sick."
19:15 Saul sent the messengers to look at David for themselves. "Carry him up to me, bed and all," he said, "so I can kill him."
19:16 When the messengers went in, there lay the household god in the bed, with the goat hair mat at its head!
19:17 "Why have you fooled me like this?" Saul said to Michal. "You sent my enemy off, and he has slipped away." Michal answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go, or must I kill you?'"
## Saul among the prophets
19:18 So David fled to safety. He came to Samuel at Ramah and reported to him everything Saul had done to him. He and Samuel went off and lived at Naioth.
19:19 Someone told Saul, "David is at Naioth in Ramah."
19:20 Saul sent messengers to seize David. There they saw the company of prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing at their head. The Spirit of God came upon Saul's messengers, and they began prophesying as well.
19:21 When Saul heard, he sent a second group of messengers, and they prophesied as well. Saul tried again and sent a third group, and they prophesied as well.
19:22 At last he went to Ramah himself. He reached the big cistern at Secu and asked, "Where are Samuel and David?" "There, at Naioth in Ramah," someone said.
19:23 On his way there, to Naioth in Ramah, the Spirit of God came on him too. All the rest of the way he went prophesying, until he arrived at Naioth in Ramah.
19:24 He too tore off his clothes. He too prophesied before Samuel, and he dropped down and lay naked all that day and all that night. That is why people say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"
## A plan for the new moon
20:1 David ran from Naioth in Ramah, came and faced Jonathan. "What have I done?" he said. "What wrong is in me? What sin have I done against your father, that he is hunting for my life?"
20:2 "Far from it!" Jonathan told him. "You won't die. My father does nothing great or small without opening my ear to it. So why would my father keep this one thing hidden from me? It's not true."
20:3 David swore it again. "Your father knows for certain that I stand well with you," he said. "So he thinks, 'Jonathan must not find out, or it will hurt him.' But I swear by the life of the LORD and by your life: I am one step away from death."
20:4 "Anything you ask," Jonathan said to David, "I will do for you."
20:5 David said to Jonathan, "Tomorrow is the new moon, you know, and I really ought to sit with the king at the meal. Instead, send me off, and I will hide out in the field until the third evening.
20:6 If your father looks for me and finds me gone, tell him, 'David pleaded with me for leave to run over to Bethlehem, his own town. It's the time of the yearly sacrifice there for the whole clan.'
20:7 If his answer is 'Fine,' then all is well for your servant. If he burns with anger, be sure of this: he has settled on doing evil.
20:8 Deal with your servant in loyal love, for you brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD with you. But if I am guilty of anything, put me to death yourself. Why take me to your father?"
20:9 "Far be that from you!" Jonathan said. "If I learn for sure that my father has made up his mind to let evil fall on you, would I not tell you about it?"
20:10 David asked Jonathan, "Who will let me know? Or what if your father answers you roughly?"
## Covenant in the field
20:11 "Come on," Jonathan said to David. "Let's go out into the field." The two of them went out into the field.
20:12 Jonathan said to David, "By the LORD, the God of Israel! About this time tomorrow, or the day after, I will search out what my father thinks. If it looks good for David, won't I send to you then and open your ear?
20:13 If my father means to do you harm, and I do not open your ear and send you off safely on your way, then the LORD do so to Jonathan and more besides. The LORD be with you, the way he was with my father.
20:14 And if I am still living then, won't you show me the LORD's loyal love, so that I do not die?
20:15 Never cut your loyal love away from my family, even when the LORD has cut away David's enemies, every one, from the face of the ground."
20:16 So Jonathan cut a covenant with David's house: "The LORD will demand payment from the hand of David's enemies."
20:17 Jonathan had David swear once more, out of his love for him. He loved him with the love he had for his own life.
20:18 Then Jonathan told him, "Tomorrow is the new moon. They'll look for you, because your seat will be empty.
20:19 When you have waited a third day, go down quickly to the spot where you hid on the day of that business before, and stay beside the stone called Ezel.
20:20 I will send three arrows off to the side of it, as though I were aiming at a mark.
20:21 Then I will send the lad off: 'Go and find the arrows!' If I call to the lad, 'The arrows are this side of you, get them,' then come. You are safe; there is nothing to fear, by the life of the LORD.
20:22 But if I tell the boy, 'The arrows are out past you,' then go your way. The LORD is sending you off.
20:23 And this thing we have talked over, you and I: the LORD stands between me and you for all time."
## The empty seat
20:24 David hid himself in the field. The new moon came, and the king took his place at the meal to eat.
20:25 The king sat in his own seat, as he did every time, the seat against the wall. Jonathan rose, and Abner sat beside Saul. David's place stayed empty.
20:26 Saul did not say a word that day. "It's some accident," he thought. "He's not clean. That must be it, he's not clean."
20:27 The day after the new moon, the second day, came, and David's place was still empty. Saul asked Jonathan his son, "What's kept the son of Jesse from the table, yesterday and today both?"
20:28 Jonathan answered Saul, "David pleaded with me for leave to go to Bethlehem.
20:29 'Send me off, please,' he said. 'Our clan has a sacrifice in town, and my brother himself told me to come. So now, if you're kindly disposed to me, let me slip away and see my brothers.' That is the reason he hasn't come to the king's table."
20:30 Saul blazed up in anger at Jonathan. "You son of a twisted woman, a rebel!" he said to him. "I know you have picked the son of Jesse, don't I? You bring shame on yourself, and shame on the nakedness of your mother!
20:31 Every day the son of Jesse is alive on the earth, you and your kingship will never be secure. Now send and get him for me. He is a son of death!"
20:32 Jonathan answered Saul his father, "Why is he to die? What did he do?"
20:33 Saul threw the spear at him, to strike him down. So Jonathan knew it: his father had settled on killing David.
20:34 Jonathan rose from the table hot with anger. He ate no food that second day of the new moon, because he was hurting for David, and because his father had shamed him.
## The arrows
20:35 Next morning Jonathan went out into the field at the time agreed with David, taking a little lad along.
20:36 "Run, go find the arrows I'm about to shoot," he told his lad. As the lad ran, Jonathan shot the arrow to fly past him.
20:37 The lad got to the place of the arrow Jonathan had shot, and Jonathan shouted after the lad, "The arrow's out past you, isn't it?"
20:38 Jonathan kept shouting after the lad, "Fast! Hurry up! Don't stand there!" Jonathan's lad gathered up the arrows and came back to his master.
20:39 The lad had no idea of anything. Only Jonathan and David understood the matter.
20:40 Jonathan handed his weapons to the lad who was with him. "Off you go," he told him. "Carry these into town."
20:41 The lad went off, and David got up from where he was, on the south side. He threw himself face down on the ground and bowed three times. Each kissed the other, and each wept over the other, until David's weeping grew greatest.
20:42 Jonathan told David, "Go in peace. We two have sworn by the name of the LORD: 'The LORD will stand between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, for all time.'" David got up and went, and Jonathan went back into the town.
