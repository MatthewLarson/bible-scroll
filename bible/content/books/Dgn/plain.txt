## Diognetus asks about the Christians
1:1 I see, most excellent Diognetus, that you are extremely eager to learn about the Christians' reverence for God, and that you inquire about them with great clarity and care. What God do they trust, and how do they worship him, that all of them look down on the world and think nothing of death? Why do they not count as gods the ones the Greeks consider gods, and why do they not keep the superstition of the Jews? What is this deep affection they have for one another? And why has this new race or way of life entered human life now, and not earlier? I welcome this eagerness of yours. And I ask God, who gives us both the power to speak and the power to hear, to grant me to speak in such a way that, more than anything, you become better for having heard, and to grant you to hear in such a way that the one who spoke will not be sorry.
## Gods of stone and metal
2:1 Come, then. Clear your mind of all the reasonings that have taken hold of it beforehand. Put away the custom that has fooled you. Become like a new human being from the very start, since, as you yourself admitted, you are going to listen to a new word. Look not only with your eyes but also with your understanding: what substance and what form do they have, the ones you will call gods and think of as gods?
2:2 Is not one of them a stone, like the stone people walk on? Is not another bronze, no better than the vessels hammered out for our use? Another wood, already rotting? Another silver, which needs a man to guard it so that it will not be stolen? Another iron, eaten away by rust? Another clay, no finer to look at than the pot made for the lowliest use?
2:3 Are not all these made of matter that decays? Were they not hammered out with iron and fire? Did not a stonecutter shape one of them, a bronze-smith another, a silversmith another, a potter another? Before these men's skills pressed them into the shape each one has, was not each of them, and is it not still, open to being made over into something else? And the vessels now made of the same matter, if they happened to meet the same craftsmen, could they not turn out just like these?
2:4 And these things you now bow down to, could not human hands make them back into vessels like all the others? Are they not all mute? Are they not blind? Are they not without a soul? Are they not without feeling? Are they not unable to move? Are they not all rotting? Are they not all decaying?
2:5 You call these gods? You are slaves to these? You bow down to these, and you end up becoming just like them.
2:6 So you hate Christians for this, for not thinking these are gods?
2:7 For you think and suppose that you are praising them. But are you not treating them with far more contempt? Are you not far more mocking and insulting them, when you worship the stone and clay ones and leave them unguarded, but lock up the silver and gold ones at night and post guards beside them by day, so that they will not be stolen?
2:8 As for the honors you think you are offering them: if they can feel, you are really punishing them; and if they cannot feel, you show it up when you worship them with blood and the smoke of burning fat.
2:9 Let one of you put up with these things! Let someone allow them to be done to himself! Not one human being would willingly bear this punishment, because he has feeling and reason. But the stone bears it, because it feels nothing. So are you not proving that it cannot feel?
2:10 I could say many other things about why Christians are not enslaved to such gods. But if anyone thinks even this is not enough, I consider it pointless to say more.
## Sacrifices to the God who needs nothing
3:1 Next, I think you most want to hear why they do not revere God in the same way as the Jews.
3:2 Now the Jews, insofar as they keep away from the worship just described, are right to claim that they honor one God of all things and think of him as Master. But insofar as they bring him this service in the same way as those I have spoken of, they are badly wrong.
3:3 For the Greeks give proof of their folly by offering these things to objects that cannot feel or hear. And the Jews, who reckon that they are supplying them to God as if he needed them, ought rather to count it foolishness, not reverence for God.
3:4 For he who made heaven and earth and all that is in them, and who supplies all of us with what we need, could himself need none of the things that he himself provides to those who suppose they are giving them.
3:5 Those who think they are carrying out sacrifices to him with blood and the smoke of fat and whole burnt offerings, and honoring him with these honors, seem to me no different from those who show the same eagerness toward deaf idols. The one group gives to things that cannot share in the honor; the other thinks it is giving to the One who needs nothing.
## Foods, Sabbaths and new moons
4:1 As for their jumpy fear about foods, their superstition about the Sabbath, their bragging about circumcision, and the pretense of their fasting and new moons, these things are laughable and not worth a word. I do not think you need to learn about them from me.
4:2 Of the things God created for people to use, to accept some as well made and to turn down others as useless and unneeded: how is that not unlawful?
4:3 And to slander God, as if he forbade doing any good thing on the Sabbath day: how is that not ungodly?
4:4 And to boast that the cutting short of the flesh is proof that they are chosen, as if for this reason they were loved by God above others: how does that not deserve mockery?
4:5 And they keep close watch on the stars and the moon to observe the months and the days. They carve up God's plans and the changes of the seasons to suit their own whims, some into feasts and some into times of mourning. Who would take that as proof of reverence for God, and not far more of folly?
4:6 So I think you have learned well enough that Christians are right to keep away from the empty folly and deceit that all share, and from the fussing and the boasting of the Jews. But the mystery of their own reverence for God, do not expect to learn it from a human being.
## Citizens and strangers
5:1 For it is not by land, speech or customs that Christians differ from other people.
5:2 They have no cities of their own anywhere to live in; they speak no peculiar dialect; their life is not marked out as odd.
5:3 What they have learned was not found out by the inventiveness and worrying of busybodies, and they are not champions of a human doctrine, as some are.
5:4 They make their homes in Greek cities and in foreign ones, as each was allotted, and in clothing, food and all the rest of life they go along with the customs of the place. Yet the way their own citizenship is ordered, as they show it, is remarkable and, everyone agrees, beyond belief.
5:5 They live in their own homelands, but as resident aliens. They take part in everything as citizens, and they put up with everything as strangers. Every foreign land is their homeland, and every homeland is a foreign land.
5:6 Like all others, they marry; they have children; but their newborn children they do not throw away.
5:7 They set out a shared table, but not a shared bed.
5:8 They are in the flesh, but they do not live by the flesh.
5:9 They pass their days on earth, but they live as citizens of heaven.
5:10 They obey the laws that have been laid down, and by the way they live they outdo the laws.
5:11 They love everyone, and they are persecuted by everyone.
5:12 No one knows them, and yet they are condemned. They are put to death, and they are made alive.
5:13 They are poor, and yet they make many rich. They lack everything, and they have plenty of everything.
5:14 They are dishonored, and in their dishonor they are given glory. They are slandered, and they are shown to be in the right.
5:15 They are insulted, and they bless. They are treated with contempt, and they give honor.
5:16 For doing good they are punished as if they were wicked; under punishment they rejoice, as people given life.
5:17 The Jews make war on them as foreigners, and the Greeks persecute them. And those who hate them cannot say why they are their enemies.
## The soul of the world
6:1 To put it simply: what the soul is to a body, that is what Christians are in the world.
6:2 The soul is scattered through all the parts of the body, and Christians across the world's cities.
6:3 The soul lives in the body, but it is not from the body. And Christians live in the world, but they are not from the world.
6:4 The soul is invisible, and it is kept under guard in the visible body. Christians are known to be in the world, but their reverence for God stays out of sight.
6:5 The flesh hates the soul and makes war on it, though it has suffered no wrong, because it is kept from enjoying its pleasures. The world hates Christians too, though it has suffered no wrong, because they stand against its pleasures.
6:6 The soul loves the flesh that hates it, and the body's parts. And Christians love those who hate them.
6:7 The soul is shut up in the body, yet it is the soul that holds the body together. Christians are held in the world as in a prison, yet they are the ones who hold the world together.
6:8 The immortal soul lives in a mortal tent. And Christians live as resident aliens among things that perish, while they wait for the incorruption that is in heaven.
6:9 When the soul is treated harshly in food and drink, it becomes better. And Christians, when they are punished day after day, grow all the more in number.
6:10 God has stationed them at a post so great that they are not allowed to refuse it.
## God sent his Word
7:1 For, as I said, this was not some earthly discovery handed down to them. It is not a mortal idea that they think worth guarding so carefully. Nor is it a plan of merely human mysteries that has been put in their trust.
7:2 No, it was truly God himself, the Almighty, the Creator of all, the invisible God. He himself from heaven set among people the truth and the holy Word that cannot be grasped, and fixed them firmly in their hearts. He did not do it, as someone might guess, by sending people some attendant, or an angel, or a ruler, or one of those who manage earthly affairs, or one of those entrusted with governing things in heaven. He sent the very Craftsman and Creator of the universe himself. By him God created the heavens. By him he shut the sea within its own bounds. All the elements faithfully keep his mysteries. From him the sun has received the measures of the day's courses to keep. The moon obeys him when he commands it to shine at night. The stars obey him as they follow the course of the moon. By him all things have been put in order, given their bounds and made subject: the heavens and what is in the heavens, the earth and what is in the earth, the sea and what is in the sea, fire, air, the abyss, what is in the heights, what is in the depths, what is in between. This is the one he sent to them.
7:3 Was it, as a human being might reckon, to rule as a tyrant, with fear and terror?
7:4 Not at all. He sent him in forbearance and gentleness. As a king sends his son, he sent him as a king. He sent him as God. He sent him as a human being to human beings. He sent him to save and to persuade, not to force. For force has no place in God.
7:5 He sent him to call, not to hunt down. He sent him in love, not in judgment.
7:6 For he will send him to judge, and who will remain standing at his coming? ...
7:7 [Do you not see them] thrown to wild beasts to make them deny the Lord, and not overcome?
7:8 Do you not see others growing in number, all the more as more of them are punished?
7:9 None of this looks like human work. This is God's power. These are the proofs of his coming.
## No one knew God until he came
8:1 For before he came, who among human beings knew at all what God is?
8:2 Or do you accept the empty, silly talk of those "trustworthy" philosophers? Some of them said God is fire (they call God the very thing they are about to go into!), others water, others some other one of the elements that God created.
8:3 And yet, if any of these claims is acceptable, each one of the other created things could just as well be declared God.
8:4 But these are the wonder-tales and the trickery of sorcerers.
8:5 No human being has seen him or known him. But he has shown himself.
8:6 And he showed himself through faith, the only way anyone is allowed to see God.
8:7 For God, the Master and Creator of the universe, who made all things and set each in its order, was not only a lover of humankind but patient as well.
8:8 Indeed, he always was such a God, and he is, and he will be: kind and good and free from anger and true. And he alone is good.
8:9 He conceived a great thought, too great for words, and he shared it with his Child alone.
8:10 So as long as he kept his wise purpose to himself in a mystery and guarded it, he seemed to neglect us and not to care.
8:11 But when he revealed it through his beloved Child and made known what had been prepared from the beginning, he gave us everything at once: to share in his good gifts, and to see and to understand. Which of us would ever have expected these things?
## Why the Son came so late
9:1 So he had already planned everything in his own mind together with his Child. Yet up to the former time he let us be swept along by disorderly impulses, as we wanted, carried off by pleasures and desires. It was not that he took any delight in our sins; he was bearing with them. He did not approve of that time of wrongdoing; he was shaping the present time of righteousness. He did this so that we, proven by our own deeds in that former time to be unworthy of life, might now be counted worthy of it by God's kindness; and so that, having made it plain that on our own we could not enter the kingdom of God, we might be made able to by God's power.
9:2 But when our wrongdoing had come to its full measure, and it had been made completely plain that its wages, punishment and death, were waiting for it, then the time came that God had set to show at last his kindness and power. (Oh, God's overflowing kindness to humankind, and his love!) He did not hate us, or push us away, or hold our wrongs against us. He was patient; he bore with us. In mercy he himself took up our sins. It was he who handed over his own Son, a ransom on our behalf: the holy for the lawless, the blameless for the wicked, the righteous for the unrighteous, the incorruptible for the corruptible, the immortal for the mortal.
9:3 For what else had the power to cover our sins, except his righteousness?
9:4 In whom could we, lawless and ungodly as we were, be justified, except in the Son of God alone?
9:5 Oh, the sweet exchange! Oh, the workmanship no one can trace out! Oh, the good gifts no one expected! That the lawlessness of many should be hidden in one righteous man, and the righteousness of one should justify many who are lawless!
9:6 So in the former time he proved that our nature could not reach life, and now he has shown us the Savior, who is able to save even what could not be saved. By both he wanted us to trust in his kindness, to think of him as the one who raises us, father, teacher, counselor, doctor, mind, light, honor, glory, strength and life, and not to worry about clothing and food.
## Imitators of God
10:1 If you too long for this faith, and you receive first of all full knowledge of the Father ...
10:2 For God loved human beings. For their sake he made the world. To them he made subject everything on the earth. To them he gave reason, to them a mind. Them alone he allowed to look up to him. Them he formed from his own image. To them he sent his only Son. To them he promised the kingdom in heaven, and he will give it to those who have loved him.
10:3 And once you know him, what joy do you suppose will fill you? Or how will you love the one who loved you first like this?
10:4 And when you love him, you will become an imitator of his kindness. Do not be surprised that a human being can become an imitator of God. He is able to, when God wills.
10:5 For happiness is not found in ruling your neighbors by force, in craving more than the weaker have, or in being rich and pushing around those who have less. No one can imitate God in these things; they are foreign to his greatness.
10:6 But whoever takes up his neighbor's burden, whoever is stronger in something and wants to help the one who comes up short in it, whoever passes on to the needy what he has received from God and holds, and so becomes a god to those who receive it: that person is an imitator of God.
10:7 Then, while you are still on earth, you will see that God governs in heaven. Then you will begin to speak of God's mysteries. Then you will love and admire those who are punished because they will not deny God. Then you will condemn the deceit and the error of the world, when you come to know what it really is to live in heaven, when you think nothing of what passes for death here, when you fear the real death. That death is kept for those who will be condemned to the eternal fire, which will punish to the end those handed over to it.
10:8 Then the people who hold out for righteousness under the fire that lasts a short time will win your admiration, and you will count them blessed, once you know that other fire.
## A disciple of apostles
11:1 What I am saying is nothing strange, and I am not asking questions beyond reason. I became a disciple of apostles, and I am becoming a teacher of the nations. What has been handed down I pass on, as it deserves, to those who are becoming disciples of the truth.
11:2 For who that has been rightly taught and has come to love the Word does not seek to learn clearly the things the Word openly showed to his disciples? The Word appeared and revealed them to the disciples. He spoke boldly. Unbelievers did not understand him, but he explained things to the disciples, and they, counted faithful by him, came to know the Father's mysteries.
11:3 For this reason he sent the Word, to appear to the world. The people dishonored him; apostles preached him; the nations believed in him.
11:4 He is the one from the beginning. He appeared as new, was discovered to be old, and is always being born young in the hearts of the saints.
11:5 This is the one who always is, who today is reckoned as Son. Through him the church is made rich, and grace, spread out among the saints, is multiplied. This grace gives understanding, makes mysteries plain, announces the seasons, rejoices over the faithful, and is given to those who seek it: to those who do not break the pledges of faith or move the boundary stones of the fathers.
11:6 Then the fear of the Law is sung, the grace of the Prophets is known, the faith of the Gospels is made firm, the tradition of the apostles is kept, and the grace of the church leaps for joy.
11:7 If you do not grieve this grace, you will come to know what the Word says, through whom he wishes, when he wishes.
11:8 For whatever we were moved to speak out with toil by the will of the Word who commands us, we share with you, out of love for the things revealed to us.
## The two trees of paradise
12:1 When you have read these things and listened to them with care, you will know how much God gives to those who love him rightly. They become a paradise of delight. They make a tree grow up in themselves that bears every fruit and flourishes, and they are adorned with fruits of every kind.
12:2 For in this place a tree of knowledge and a tree of life have been planted. But the tree of knowledge does not kill; disobedience kills.
12:3 For what is written is not obscure: how God from the beginning planted a tree of knowledge and a tree of life in the middle of paradise, showing life through knowledge. Because the first people did not use it purely, they were stripped bare by the snake's deceit.
12:4 For there is no life without knowledge, and no safe knowledge without true life. That is why the two were planted side by side.
12:5 The apostle saw the force of this. He found fault with knowledge that is practiced without the truth of the command that leads to life, and he says, "Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up."
12:6 For whoever thinks he knows anything without knowledge that is true and borne out by his life does not really know. He is led astray by the snake, because he has not loved life. But whoever has come to know with fear, and seeks life, plants in hope, expecting fruit.
12:7 Let your heart be knowledge, and your life the true word, given room in you.
12:8 If you bear its tree and pick its fruit, you will always harvest the things longed for in God's presence, which the snake does not touch and deceit does not stain. And Eve is not corrupted, but a virgin is trusted.
12:9 And salvation is shown, and apostles are given understanding, and the Lord's Passover goes forward, and the seasons are brought together and set in harmony with the world, and the Word rejoices as he teaches the saints. Through him the Father is glorified. To him be the glory forever. Amen.
