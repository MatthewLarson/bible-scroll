# Hostile review: Philemon (Phm), one part, flat folder

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent. Content files were not edited. `node tools/build.js --check Phm` and `VERBOSE=1` both give 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

Philemon is strong work. The Plain English Translation is faithful to the Greek verse by verse, key terms match the rest of the site (partnership, set at rest, slave, fellow captive, God's holy people), and I found no invented source, quotation, fragment or scholar. The scholar and source claims I opened this session check out. The weak spots are all in the slavery traditions card, which is thinner and less even than the brief asked for, plus a few cross-references and one note (1:9). One card pointer is dangling, one denominational reading (Wesley) is misplaced, the variants file omits several real variants, and four hedged "I have not seen this" sentences break STYLE rule 2. The Plain English Translation has a few phrasings that sit close to the common modern versions, but none is a copy. There are no critical findings.

Findings: 0 critical, 5 major, 8 minor.

## Findings

### Major

**1. The slavery card (chapters.json, traditions, `top: slavery`) is uneven and softens what churches taught then.**
The brief asked for Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, Methodist, Baptist, Reformed, Lutheran, Anabaptist and Quaker, the 19th-century split, present teaching, "never soften what any church taught then." The card as written:
- mixes eras and kinds of authority without saying so: Chrysostom (4th c.) stands for "Orthodox", Luther (16th c.) for Lutheran, a Cambridge scholar for "Anglican", Wesley's note for Methodist, a 1995 resolution for Baptist, a 1992 catechism for Catholic;
- gives the Catholic row only the modern Catechism, with nothing on what the church or its members taught or did while slavery was lawful. The Ephesians card already carries *Gaudium et Spes* 27 and Gregory XVI's *In supremo* (source keys exist: `gaudium-et-spes`, `in-supremo`);
- gives the Baptist row a 1995 apology that says nothing about Philemon and repeats, nearly word for word, the SBC row of the Ephesians 6 card;
- has no Quaker and no Anabaptist row, though the Ephesians card has Quaker;
- has no modern Orthodox, Anglican or Lutheran church statement;
- says of Luther that his preface "does not treat the letter as a ruling on slavery". That is an unsourced claim about silence.
Several rows therefore answer "what did this church say about slavery" and not the card's own question, "what did they take Philemon to ask."
Fix: restate each row as (church or figure, date, what they took Philemon to ask or permit) using sources actually opened. Keep the Reformed row (Jones/Barnes), which does this well. Add Quaker (reuse the Ephesians card's keys). Add Catholic historical and Gregory XVI. Show the 19th-century splits only where sourced in a source you open. Drop or relabel the rows that cannot be tied to Philemon, and do not repeat the Ephesians Baptist sentence. State plainly where a row is one thinker and not a church.

**2. Wesley is misplaced and misdescribed (chapters.json card, Methodist row; notes.json 1:16 views note).**
Wesley's "not only pardoned, but gave him his liberty" is in his introduction to Philemon (Wesley Center Online, notes on Philemon), where it rests on identifying Onesimus with Ignatius's bishop of Ephesus. His note on 1:16 reads only "In the flesh - As a dutiful servant. In the Lord - As a fellow Christian." The 1:16 note puts Wesley under "A request for freedom" ("Wesley thought Philemon 'gave him his liberty'"), and the card says he reads the letter "as fulfilled by Onesimus's freedom". Neither says the claim comes from the bishop identification and not from the verse. Wesley's 1:22 note ("Given to you - Restored to liberty") is about Paul, not Onesimus.
Fix: say "in his introduction Wesley says Philemon 'not only pardoned, but gave him his liberty', and gives the Ignatius identification as his reason", and show his 1:16 gloss. Do not use him as an exegetical witness for the "freedom request" view.

**3. Dangling pointer to a Colossians slavery card (chapters.json deep dive "Philemon and slavery").**
The text says "see the card on Ephesians 6:5-9 and the one on Colossians 3:22-4:1." Colossians has no traditions card on slavery (its cards are son-of-god, suffering, baptism, sabbath, angels). Only the Ephesians 6 card exists. Fix: point to the Ephesians card only, or to the Colossians chapter's notes, and do not promise a card.

**4. The 1:9 note overstates the manuscript and edition facts and leaves the consensus unlabeled (notes.json, 1:9 "who is old").**
- It says "Every Greek manuscript and edition reads *presbytēs*." I could not confirm "every edition". A secondary report I found says Westcott-Hort accepted Bentley's emendation and that *presbeutēs* appears in the NA27 apparatus. I did not open WH or NA27 myself. State only what the project's word lists show, or check WH and NA28 in this session. Also say how each critical edition treats it (text, margin or apparatus), as the brief required.
- Lightfoot, the note's main source, says in the text itself that he still believes Paul speaks as "ambassador" and that the form *presbytēs* may be an alternative spelling of *presbeutēs*. The note gives this, but the Plain English Translation then commits to "who is old" with the single reason "'old' and 'prisoner' both fit an appeal." Lightfoot's own comment is that *presbytēs* and *desmios* "would suit either entreaty or command", so that reason does not separate the two readings.
- The note does not say which reading most modern commentaries and versions take. STYLE rule 3 wants the consensus labeled, with who dissents.
- Welborn's argument is described as "Paul was about sixty"; the abstract does say this, but it is an inference from demographic data, not a fact in the text.
Fix: add one plain sentence on the majority reading and who dissents (cite the editions you open). Keep the Eph 6:20 Plain English Translation quote as the model for the alternative. Give a better reason for "old", for example the Greek word itself and the paired "now ... also" in the literal.

**5. variants.json omits real variants and its witness lists are not checkable (variants.json; notes.json 1:1, 1:5).**
`texts/auto/Phm.json` and the La Parola data list differences that have no entry:
- **1:5** *pros* (NA28, SBL, TR, Byz) against *eis* (WH, Tregelles), a "meaning variant" that sets editions against each other. Note 1:5 itself discusses the *pros/eis* change of preposition and "some copies smooth the order to 'faith and love'", but there is no variant entry for either.
- **1:1** *apostolos* (D*, per the only apparatus-style listing I could open, a weak secondary page; 629 *apostolos desmios*; 323 and 945 *doulos*). Note 1:1 states the D* reading, and the date it gives for Claromontanus ("about 400 to 600") is loose.
- **1:6** *agathou* against *ergou* (F, G, Vulgate Clementine).
- **1:11** bracketed *kai* (NA27/28 in brackets; SBL text without).
- **1:13** word order *moi diakonē* against *diakonē moi*.
- **1:21** *ha* against *ho*; **1:23** *aspazetai* against *aspazontai*; **1:25** the long TR subscription.
Separately, I could not open an apparatus (NA28, UBS5, ECM or La Parola's data) for the witness lists in the entries that exist, so I cannot confirm them. The riskiest are the 1:6 list (P61, with a date of 600s-700s), the 1:12 corrector sigla, the 1:25 Amen list, and the P87 statement that it "ends without Amen." STYLE rule 2 requires a checked source for each.
Fix: add the missing entries (at least 1:1, 1:5, 1:6 and 1:11, which affect meaning), and check every witness against a source you open in the session. If a siglum cannot be checked, name only the editions the word list shows.

### Minor

**6. Hedges that STYLE rule 2 forbids (book.json spec; chapters.json spec box; notes.json 1:15 and 1:20 spec notes).** Four sentences say "I have not seen this ... in what I read", "I have not found this exact chain argued in what I read", "I have not seen it argued in the works I consulted." These are hedges about an unchecked detail. Also notes.json 1:6 "Jerome remarked ... (as Lightfoot reports from Jerome's commentary)": Lightfoot (line on *evidens*/*efficax*) does say it, so state it plainly. Fix: delete the novelty hedges and keep only the label "my own reading".

**7. Plain English Translation 1:15 loses the passive the note stands on.** The Plain English Translation reads "the reason he was away from you for a short time". The Greek is *echōristhē*, "he was separated", and the note is titled "'He was parted,' not 'he ran away'", with Chrysostom cited for the passive. A reader of the Plain English Translation cannot see that. Fix: "he was parted from you" (the term list and 1:15 gloss already suit it).

**8. Drift toward the common versions, judged in my head only.** 1:7 ("Your love has brought me great joy and comfort") follows the frame of a well-known modern wording ("Your love has given me great joy and encouragement"); 1:16 ("no longer as a slave, but as more than a slave: a much-loved brother") keeps the familiar sequence; 1:18 ("or does he owe you anything?") is close to the standard "or owes you anything." None is a copy and the Greek forces much of it, but each can be reworded without loss, e.g. 1:7 "I had great joy and was greatly encouraged by your love" restructured, 1:18 "or does he owe you something?"

**9. Gloss at 1:16 "in the flesh": "as people, in everyday life."** This decides the contested point (Callahan's blood tie against the everyday sphere) in the gloss, while the note correctly lays out both. Fix: a neutral gloss ("in this life") or none.

**10. Topic contributions.** (a) The brief asked for a `colossae` contribution; `content/proposals/nt/topics/colossae.json` now exists with empty sections and Phm's topics.json adds none (progress.md says it was held back for the Col core). `colossae` is also missing from book.json and chapter topic lists. (b) The `luke` contribution says Luke is among Paul's fellow workers "in prison"; the verse says only "my fellow workers" (only Epaphras is a "fellow captive"). (c) Brief topics `demas`, `adoption`, `jubilee` have no contribution (note only if a core exists).

**11. `slavery` topic labels one view "common."** `common: "Paul did not ask Philemon to free Onesimus, but asked him to treat him as a brother"` presents as the standard view what the book's own 1:16 and 1:21 notes show is divided (Lightfoot "trembling on his lips"; many read a hint at manumission). Fix: state "Paul does not tell Philemon to free him" as a fact of the text, and keep the interpretation (hint or not) in the views.

**12. Audience and settings.** (a) `audience.views` and the 1:8-16 setting list four occasion theories; Tolmie, whom the book cites, counts five (the asylum view is missing, and the habitual absentee, *erro*, is in the deep dive but not in the settings). (b) The 1:8-16 setting says "For the first hearers, a slave's return to his master's house normally meant punishment or at best the old place" with no source. (c) "The house church likely included free people and slaves ... sharing one meal" (audience.to; house-churches topic) is inference stated as likelihood. Fix: add the missing view, source or soften (b) and (c).

**13. Quotation hygiene.** (a) Lightfoot's "must in the end prove fatal to slavery" is quoted in the deep dive as "must prove fatal" with no ellipsis; restore the words. (b) The Digest 21.1.43.1 English in quotation marks is the writer's own translation and should be labeled as such. (c) The Muratorian "one to Philemon, one to Titus, and two to Timothy" is in quotation marks with no translator named; I did not open the Fragment. (d) Luther's preface is quoted only through Lightfoot's old English; the source key `luther-preface-philemon` implies the German preface was read. Say "as quoted by Lightfoot." (e) Jerome's and Theodore's prefaces defending the letter and Theodore's remark on "beloved" at 1:2 were not opened by me and are second-hand to the book as written; keep only if the writer opened them.
