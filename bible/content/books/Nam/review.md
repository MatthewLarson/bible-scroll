# Hostile review: Nahum (Nam), whole book

Reviewer: separate agent, did not write the book. Checked: plain.txt against `node tools/literal.js Nam 1-3` and `texts/orig/Nam.json`; notes (65), variants (25), glosses (20), chapters, book.json, topics, sources against the Plain of Exodus, Joel, Jonah, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, 2 Kings, Mark, Revelation and others; the Swete Greek (`tools/lxx.py Nah`), the DSS printout (`tools/dss.py Nam 1-3`) and `texts/auto/Nam.json`. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Nam`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The Plain is mostly sound, readable and honest about the hard Hebrew. Its main drift is a handful of lines that stay close to NIV/ESV sentence shape, plus one idiom ("Tighten your belt") that misleads. The scholarly apparatus is careful about scrolls: every "legible" or "restored" claim for Mur88 and 4Q82 that I checked against the printout is right. The Exodus 34:6-7 creed match is correct and the Jeremiah 30:12 and Joel 2:6 pairs are aligned. The weak parts are (1) several factual errors in notes (the gender of the "you" in 1:14, the root of 2 Kings 8:12, the "root" of "gospel", the cause of the Hebrew chapter break), (2) a note at 3:10 that misreads Hosea 13:16 and 2 Kings 8:12, (3) both traditions cards: wrong or unsupported source lines, an unequal tradition list, loaded questions, and the planned 1:15 card is missing, (4) lens descriptions that promise Targum, Talmud, Theodoret, Theodore and Luther material that never appears, and Jewish and patristic readings stated with no named source, and (5) a few unverified statements the writer should soften. No invented source or manuscript reading found. No critical finding.

Count: critical 0, major 11, minor 24.

## Findings

### Major

1. **Major. book.json chapters.json ch.1 `deep` "The addressee of 1:9-14", settings 1:9-1:14, notes.json 1:9 ("Who is speaking").** The notes say the "you" of 1:11-14 is feminine singular, and that 1:14 can go to Nineveh. The Hebrew of 1:14 is masculine: `עָלֶיךָ`, `מִשִּׁמְךָ`, `אֱלֹהֶיךָ`, `קִבְרֶךָ`, `קַלּוֹתָ` (all 2ms in `texts/orig/Nam.json`). 1:11-13 is feminine (`מִמֵּךְ`, `עִנִּתִךְ`, `אֲעַנֵּךְ`, `מֵעָלַיִךְ`, `מוֹסְרֹתַיִךְ`). Nineveh is feminine everywhere else (2:1 `פָּנַיִךְ`, 2:13 `אֵלַיִךְ`, 3:5). So 1:14 does not fit "Nineveh" in the usual gendered sense; it is addressed to a male figure (the king or the worthless counselor), and that is exactly the speaker-shift the brief asked the Plain not to guess at. Fix: state the gender change 1:11-13 (f.) to 1:14 (m.), drop "1:14 to Nineveh" from the "one path" and from the settings view "Nineveh in 1:9-11, 14", and offer the masculine "you" as evidence for the king.

2. **Major. notes.json 3:10 "The smashed children".** (a) "2 Kings 8:12 ... uses a different verb" is false: 2 Kings 8:12 has `תְּרַטֵּשׁ` (H7376), the same root as Nahum 3:10 `יְרֻטְּשׁוּ`, Hosea 13:16 and Isaiah 13:16; the Plain's "smash" matches in 2 Kings, Hosea and Nahum, and Isaiah 13:16 has "battered to pieces" for the same root. (b) "the prophets name this cruelty as wrong when done to Israel (Hosea 13:16; 2 Kings 8:12)" misreads both: in Hosea 13:16 it is the LORD's own judgment on Samaria ("Samaria will be held guilty"), and in 2 Kings 8:12 Elisha weeps at what Hazael will do but it is announced, not condemned as Hosea does. (c) "the same acts are then visited on Nineveh in the book's own logic" is not in the text: Nahum never says Nineveh's children are smashed. (d) "The text reports what Assyria did to Thebes": the Hebrew names no perpetrator (passive); "the prophets feared" has no source. This is the one place where the brief's rule (do not soften) is broken by commentary. Fix: correct the root, say plainly that Hosea 13:16 is God's judgment on Israel and so the same moral problem, mark Assyria as the usual inference, delete (c).

3. **Major. notes.json 1:15 "Nahum 1:15, Isaiah 52:7 and Romans 10:15" and topic good-news.** "The Plain's 'good news' renders the verb basar, the root of the Greek and English 'gospel'." Wrong: basar is Hebrew; Greek euangelion/euangelizomai is a Greek compound of eu + angelos, which the Septuagint uses to translate basar; English "gospel" comes from Old English god-spell and has no link to basar. Also "Romans 10:15 quotes Isaiah's Greek" is too strong: Paul has plural `τῶν εὐαγγελιζομένων [τὰ] ἀγαθά`, the Swete Isaiah 52:7 is singular (`ὡς πόδες εὐαγγελιζομένου ... ὡς εὐαγγελιζόμενος ἀγαθά`). Say "Paul's wording is a short adaptation close to the Greek of Isaiah". Same sentence is repeated in topics.json `good-news`. Fix in both.

4. **Major. chapters.json ch.1 `traditions` card (1:2-8).** (a) `dordrecht-1632` is defined in the Matthew sources as "articles 16-17" (the ban and shunning); the teaching on revenge is Article XIV "Of Revenge" (confirmed by the article list; GAMEO or the Wikipedia list shows XIV, and the article opens "As regards revenge ... the Lord Christ has forbidden"). The card therefore cites the wrong scope. Add a separate key for article XIV rather than reuse the key. (b) The card says both Schleitheim art. 6 and Dordrecht "draw from Deuteronomy 32:35 and Romans 12:19". Schleitheim art. 6 is about the sword and the magistrate and, as far as I know, does not cite Romans 12:19 or Deut 32:35; say only what the article says (Christians do not use the sword or take revenge), and cite the verses only if the article text has them. (c) Orthodox row: "Many Orthodox writers, following Isaac the Syrian" with no passage and no other Orthodox voice. Isaac teaches this (the Wensinck 1923 `Mystic Treatises` is real), but his homily must be named, and the card should say that other Orthodox writers and the liturgy keep the language of divine wrath. Isaac was a bishop of Nineveh only briefly and is a Church of the East saint whom the Orthodox also venerate: say that. (d) The Baptist row "repeats the Westminster wording" shows no difference; the row is a repeat of Reformed and should either be a real Baptist voice (for example the Baptist Faith and Message art. II, or Spurgeon) or be dropped. (e) The question "Is vengeance a worthy thing to say about God?" is loaded; use "How do the churches read the language of God's vengeance?". (f) The Anabaptist row answers a different question (Christian non-retaliation). Move it to the chapter 3 card, where it already is.

5. **Major. chapters.json ch.3 `traditions` card (3:1-7), and the missing 1:15 card.** (a) Catholic row: "The prophets' announcements of judgment on nations are God's acts, not a mandate for human violence" is cited to CCC 2308-2309, which are about war and lawful defense and do not say this. Either cite what the Catechism says (strict conditions for defense) and keep the Nahum reading as an unsourced inference, or find a real source (for example Dei Verbum on the prophets, or Aquinas ST II-II q.64). (b) The Reformed row ("Calvin reads Nahum as showing that God holds empires to account") does not answer the card's question about violence. (c) Orthodox and Baptist are missing, though the brief for this card named them; both have distinct and well-documented positions (the Orthodox Church does not teach a just-war doctrine as Catholic and Protestant churches do; Baptists are divided between pacifist and just-war voices). (d) The question "a book that celebrates a city's destruction" is loaded and so is topic `nonviolence` ("A book that celebrates"). (e) The brief planned a card on 1:15 (Romans 10:15, the herald as the church's preaching or as Christ), which is the one real conflict among the churches in this book's reception; it is absent, while progress.md says "3 traditions cards" and there are 2. Add the 1:15 card (Jerome and Cyril read the herald as Christ and the apostles; Calvin as Hezekiah's messenger or the gospel; check exact wording before quoting) or correct progress.md and say why.

6. **Major. plain.txt 3:7, last line, and 3:5 last two lines.** "Where could I find anyone to comfort you?" is NIV 3:7's last line ("Where can I find anyone to comfort you?") with two small word changes. 3:5 "and show the nations your nakedness / and the kingdoms your shame" follows the NIV clause by clause. The Hebrew is close to those English orders, but the brief says to check sentence shape, and a rebuild is possible: "Where can I look for people to comfort you?" and for 3:5 something like "I will let nations see you bare, and kingdoms see your disgrace" (keep `מַעֲרֵךְ` "nakedness" and `קְלוֹנֵךְ` "shame/dishonor" distinct; the Plain's Isaiah 47:3 uses "nakedness" and "disgrace").

7. **Major. plain.txt 2:1 "Tighten your belt!"** In modern American English "tighten your belt" means "economize"; a reader will read the idiom, not the soldier's girding of the loins (`חַזֵּק מָתְנַיִם`). The gloss ("get ready to fight") fixes the cause of nothing, it patches it. Use "Brace yourselves" or "Strap on your gear" (check that the term is not used for `אזר מתנים` elsewhere in the Plain).

8. **Major. book.json lens descriptions J and X vs. the notes.** The J lens says it reads Nahum through the Targum Jonathan, the medieval commentaries of Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Radak, and "the reading of Nahum with Jonah". The X lens names Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret and Cyril. Not one note quotes the Targum, the Talmud (brief: Megillah or Sanhedrin, "verify"), a haftarah, Radak, Luther, Theodore or Theodoret. Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Radak appear only as a bare list in chapters.json ("read the chapter as the fall of Assyria") with no passage. Jerome and Cyril are credited with readings ("1:15 as the preaching of the gospel"; "Nineveh repented under Jonah and then returned to its evil") with no work or section cited, and "Some read Nineveh's walls and gates as the world's defenses against the gospel" has no source at all. Fix: either add notes with named, checked passages (Targum on 1:15 and 3:8; Jerome and Cyril on 1:15; Calvin on 1:2 and 1:7; Luther's Nahum lectures if used) or cut the lens descriptions to what the book actually shows and say what was not checked.

9. **Major. notes.json 1:7 "The one comfort in the hymn".** "Calvin (on Nahum 1:7) said the prophet turns to comfort the faithful" and "Jewish commentators likewise read it as a word of shelter for Judah" are on the writer's unverified list and are given as fact with no passage. Calvin's lecture on 1:7 does speak of comfort for the godly after the threats, so the claim is likely right, but cite `calvin-twelve` with the lecture and verse, and name the Jewish commentator (Rashi? Radak?) or write "Jewish commentators are reported to ...". This verse is a place the brief asked for full treatment, and the note has no Greek: Swete's Greek 1:7 reads `χρηστὸς Κύριος τοῖς ὑπομένουσιν αὐτὸν` ("good to those who wait for him") and has no "stronghold" (`מָעוֹז`); 4Q82 and Mur88 are partly readable here and agree with the Masoretic text. That is a variant the brief would expect (see 15).

10. **Major. notes.json 1:15 (herald), chapters.json ch.1 J ("The herald of 1:15 is joined ... to Isaiah 52:7, the herald of redemption"), notes 1:15 "Jewish tradition ... has read the herald as a figure of redemption".** No source. The brief says "Messianic (the herald)" with a "check". Either cite the Targum (Nahum 2:1 in the Hebrew numbering), a midrash or Rashi, or write "reported by". The same applies to notes.json 1:11: "The commonest ancient answer is Sennacherib or his officer Rabshakeh". Sennacherib is Rashi's and Jerome's answer as I recall, but Rabshakeh is the writer's addition; cite or soften.

11. **Major. notes.json 3:5 "The woman shamed", chapters.json ch.3 `deep` "A woman, a prostitute, and what the text does", ch.3 settings 3:1-3:7 `why`.** The brief says neither soften nor defend 3:5-7. The Plain does neither. The commentary is mostly fair, but: (a) the critic and defender names are not matched to their positions: Ortlund (*Whoredom*, 1996) is a theological defense of the metaphor and not "a stock picture from ancient law and war"; Keefe (2001) is a feminist-social critic of Hosea, not a respondent to Weems; she cannot be listed as an answer "in a different way" without saying what she argues. Re-source each name to what the cited book says or drop the name. (b) The ch.3 settings `why` ("the hard images are those of the ancient world's treatment of captured cities") and the "Read as justice ... the book ends with the last word to the victims" reading are explanations that read as a defense. Add the critique alongside them (the speaker is God in the first person, "I will uncover", so the text gives the violence a divine author, which is the point of Weems and Yee). (c) For 3:13 the Plain says "Look at the people in your midst: they are women!" (`עַמֵּךְ`), but the note and book.json say "soldiers" and "army"; say that `'am` can mean troops or population and which one the Plain picked.

### Minor

12. **Minor. notes.json 1:2 "who takes vengeance".** "The verse repeats it four times in the Hebrew." The Hebrew has `נֹקֵם` three times in 1:2 (`וְנֹקֵם`, `נֹקֵם`, `נֹקֵם`); the Plain also has three. The variant at 1:9 says "three times" correctly.

13. **Minor. notes.json 1:15 numbering and variants.json 1:15.** "This is why the Hebrew Bible's chapter 2 begins at what English calls 1:15" is not supported: the Hebrew verse and chapter division is not caused by the Greek reading of `כֻּלּוֹ נִכְרָת`. Also book.json and the note say the Greek "puts the second half of English 1:15 at the start of Greek chapter 2"; in Swete, Greek 1:15 carries everything through "pass through you" and only `Συντετέλεσται, ἐξῆρται` (= "He is cut off completely") is Greek 2:1. Fix both.

14. **Minor. notes.json 1:3 "The creed turned" and 1:3 "by no means".** (a) The note says Joel and Jonah "take the first half" but does not list the changes from the Exodus Plain: the order of "compassionate and gracious" is reversed in Joel and Jonah ("gracious and compassionate"), "and faithfulness" is dropped, and "changes his mind about the harm" is added. (b) "swaps 'great in loyal love' for 'great in power'": the Plain of 1:3 is "his power is great", not "great in power", so the parallel with "great in loyal love" is invisible in English. (c) "follows 33 words of mercy": unverifiable word count; delete. (d) "quoted in the Twelve three times": add Micah 7:18-20 and Psalm 103, or say "most clearly". The creed match itself (`וְנַקֵּה לֹא יְנַקֶּה`, Plain "he will by no means leave guilt unpunished") is correct and equal to Exodus 34:7.

15. **Minor. variants.json, missing or incomplete entries.** The brief asked for a full entry at 1:4; there is none (the Greek `ὀλιγώθη ... ἐξέλιπεν` is in a note, not a variant). Also unrecorded: 1:7 (Greek, above); 1:3 and 1:5 Greek (`ἐν συντελείᾳ καὶ ἐν συνσεισμῷ`; `ἀνεστάλη ἡ γῆ`); 1:11 and 1:15 Greek for `בליעל` (`εἰς παλαίωσιν`, in 1:15); 3:11 `תְּהִי נְעֲלָמָה` / Greek `ἔσῃ ὑπερεωραμένη` ("despised"); 3:13 Mur88 `אל איביך` for the Masoretic `לאיביך` (marked as differing in the printout); the Ketiv/Qere items in `texts/auto/Nam.json` at 1:3 and 1:15 (the writer used only 2:5 and 3:3). Also say in book.json whether 4QXIIa, c and d (named in the brief) contain Nahum; the printout shows only Mur88 and 4Q82, so the claim "agree wherever their letters can be read" is true for those two only.

16. **Minor. variants.json 2:5 and 3:3.** "The Hebrew data this site draws on records a second spelling" is not an edition or witness. Name it (the Ketiv/Qere notes in the STEPBible/ETCBC data, `texts/auto`).

17. **Minor. variants.json 1:15 "plain" field and 1:10.** The 1:10 Greek reading has an empty "plain" field; give an English line or mark it as untranslatable. The 2:2 "en" gloss says "restores" while the Plain says "will bring back": the Hebrew `שָׁב` is perfect; say that the Plain reads it as a prophetic perfect.

18. **Minor. plain.txt consistency of repeated Hebrew.** 1:8 "put a total end" and 1:9 "make a full end" are the same `כָּלָה ... עשה` and should match (the repeated phrase is the point of the verse). `צָרָה` is "trouble" in 1:7 and "Distress" in 1:9. `אַדִּירִים` is "officers" in 2:5 but "great men" in 3:18, while note 2:5 says the word "returns" in 3:18. `גער` is "speaks sharply" in 1:4 while the Mark 4:39 Plain (quoted in the same note as the parallel) has "spoke sternly".

19. **Minor. plain.txt 1:8 "drive his enemies off into darkness".** `יְרַדֵּף` is "pursue/chase"; "drive off" changes the picture (pursue vs expel). Use "chase". Note the Greek reverses subject and object (darkness pursues).

20. **Minor. plain.txt and Jeremiah 13:26 pair.** The Plain of Jeremiah 13:26 has "strip off your skirts to your face" and Nahum 3:5 has "over your face" for the same `עַל פָּנַיִךְ`. The note says so but leaves the two English phrases different. Align (use "over your face" in both, or explain). The Jeremiah 30:12 pair ("your wound is severe", "fracture") and Joel 2:6 ("Every face loses its color") are aligned. Hosea 13:16, 2 Kings 8:12 and Nahum 3:10 all use "smash"; Isaiah 13:16 "battered to pieces" for the same root is not aligned and should be listed for the lead.

21. **Minor. notes.json 3:15 and the locust note.** `'arbeh` is "locust" here but "swarming locust" in Joel 1:4 and 2:25; the note says why only by pointing to Exodus. Decide: keep "locust" with a clear difference note or use "swarming locust" and fix the Plain at 3:15 and 3:17.

22. **Minor. notes.json 2:11 and 3:1 (quotation errors).** 2:11 says Isaiah 33:18 is "Where is the king?" The Plain has "Where is the clerk? Where is the man with the scales?". 3:1 quotes 1 Kings 13:30 as "Alas, my brother!"; the Plain has "Oh, my brother!" (the build did not catch this). Also the "five lion words" in P vs four in B (2:11).

23. **Minor. notes.json 2:3 "Pladot".** The title spells it "Pladot" and the note "peladot"; the explanation "steel (from a word for iron)" is garbled (the proposed "steel" is from a Persian/Arabic word for steel, `fulad`, and is debated). Fix.

24. **Minor. notes.json 2:7, 2:9.** `הֻעֲלָתָה` (hophal of `עלה`) means "was brought up/led up"; "carried off" is an interpretation that the note should call so. `תְּכוּנָה` as "storeroom" is not discussed beyond the variant; the usual senses are "supply" or "fixtures"; say why "storeroom".

25. **Minor. notes.json 1:13, 3:8 (facts).** Esarhaddon's Prism B (about 673 BC) listing Manasseh among 22 kings of Hatti is correct (ANET, 3rd ed., p. 291); cite the page, and mention Ashurbanipal's prism, which also lists him. "Josiah's reforms show Judah acting without Assyrian leave" is an inference; mark it. Josephus *Antiquities* 9.11.3 (about §239-242) does put Nahum under Jotham, "115 years" before Nineveh's fall; add the section number. The Babylonian Chronicle ABC 3 summaries (siege of about three months, Medes at Ashur in 614, Harran until about 609, Sin-shar-ishkun's death) match my knowledge of the text.

26. **Minor. notes.json 2:1 (Neco), topics.json josiah and fall-of-nineveh.** "Josiah died at Megiddo in 609 trying to stop Pharaoh Neco from marching to help Assyria (2 Kings 23:29)": the site's own 2 Kings 23:29 says only that Neco "marched up toward the king of Assyria" and Josiah "went out to meet him"; the motive is an inference. Write "probably", and cite 2 Chronicles 35:20-24 or the Chronicle for the Egyptian aid.

27. **Minor. notes.json 3:9 and topic sea.** "The Sea is a creature that obeys" in Israel and a rival in Canaan is too neat; Psalm 74 and 89 have Israel's own conflict with the sea. "Nahum chose the picture to answer the Assyrian claim that Ashur or Adad did" is an inference stated as fact: label it (spec) or say "may".

28. **Minor. notes.json 3:4 "Nineveh the prostitute".** "Here prostitution pictures trade and politics, not worship" is given as settled, but the same verse has "sorceries", and Spronk, O'Brien and others read the cultic side as well. Present the political-commercial reading as the usual one and give the other. Also check the quoted Revelation 18:23 and the claim "the Greek of Nahum has the same word (porneia)": correct (`πορνείας πόρνη`).

29. **Minor. book.json authorship vs note 1:1.** "Christian commentators from Jerome on have taken the author as ... a man of the people of Judah" contradicts note 1:1 where Jerome says Elkosh was a Galilean village and the *Lives of the Prophets* put it in Simeon. Fix.

30. **Minor. dating and Jonah (book.json lead, notes 1:3, topics nineveh, jonah).** "Jonah, a century or more earlier" and "Nahum, a century and a half later" read the Jonah story as an 8th-century event, but the site's own Jonah book.json gives the writing as post-exilic. Say "set a century or more earlier" everywhere (nineveh topic already does).

31. **Minor. chapters.json ch.3 H.** "the early-fig and women images are stock curses and taunts (compare the treaty curses studied by Hillers)": Hillers covers the "women" curse (and the 3:12 figs are not a treaty curse); say so.

32. **Minor. notes.json 1:12, 1:14 and 1:15 (voice).** The speaker of 1:14 is the LORD "commanded" (prophet speaking), but the Plain's quotation marks end at 1:13's close and reopen in 1:14 after "The LORD has issued an order about you:". That is consistent, but the book.json heading "Plots against the LORD" labels 1:9-15 for readers as if the plot were settled; keep the heading neutral ("Turning between Nineveh and Judah").

33. **Minor. notes.json 1:14 "Ashur-uballit II ... defeated about 609".** Harran fell in 610 (Ashur-uballit withdrew) and the allied attempt to retake it failed in 609. Say "last stand at Harran until about 609".

34. **Minor. notes.json 1:14 "Nineveh's temples held the images of Ashur ...".** Ashur's main temple was at Ashur; Nineveh's principal temples were of Ishtar and Nabu. Reword to "Assyria's gods, such as Ashur and the Ishtar of Nineveh".

35. **Minor. progress.md.** "3 traditions cards" should be 2 (or add the 1:15 card, finding 5). "Drift audit: 22 of 47 verses rebuilt" is at odds with this review's findings in 3:5, 3:7, 2:5, 2:3, 2:1 and 1:9.
