## Four young men taken to Babylon
1:1 In the third year of Jehoiakim's rule over Judah, King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon came to Jerusalem and put it under siege.
1:2 The Lord handed Jehoiakim king of Judah over to him, together with a share of the vessels from God's house. Nebuchadnezzar brought them to the land of Shinar, to the house of his god, and set the vessels in the treasury of that god's house.
1:3 The king told Ashpenaz, the head of his eunuchs, to bring in some of the Israelites, some from the royal family and some from the nobles.
1:4 They were to be young men with no blemish on them, good to look at, insightful in all wisdom, rich in knowledge, sharp in understanding, and fit to stand in the king's palace. Ashpenaz was also to teach them the writing and the language of the Chaldeans.
1:5 The king set aside a portion for them for every day, from the king's rich food and from the king's own wine. They were to be raised for three years, and after that they would attend the king.
1:6 Among them were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, men of Judah.
1:7 The head of the eunuchs gave them new names. Daniel he called Belteshazzar, Hananiah he called Shadrach, Mishael he called Meshach, and Azariah he called Abednego.
## Daniel will not defile himself
1:8 Daniel set his heart on not defiling himself with the king's rich food or with the king's own wine. So he asked the head of the eunuchs to keep him from being defiled.
1:9 God gave Daniel loyal love and compassion with the head of the eunuchs.
1:10 The head of the eunuchs told Daniel, "I am afraid of my lord the king, who has set your food and your drink. Why should he see you with gloomy faces next to the young men your own age? You would cost me my head with the king."
1:11 Then Daniel spoke to the overseer whom the head of the eunuchs had placed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah.
1:12 "Please put your servants to the test for ten days. Let plant food be given to us, and we will eat; let water be given, and we will drink.
1:13 Then let our faces be seen by you beside the faces of the young men who are eating the king's rich food. After that, do with your servants as you see fit."
1:14 He listened to them on this matter, and he tested them for ten days.
1:15 When the ten days were up, they looked well, and their bodies were fuller than those of all the young men who were eating the king's rich food.
1:16 From then on the overseer would carry off their rich food and their wine, and he gave them plant food instead.
## The king finds no one like them
1:17 God gave these four young men knowledge and insight in all writing and wisdom, and Daniel could make sense of every vision and of dreams.
1:18 When the time the king had named was over, the head of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.
1:19 The king talked with them, and out of all of them no one was found to equal Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. So they took their places before the king.
1:20 On every matter of wisdom and understanding that the king put to them, he found them ten times better than every magician and enchanter in all his kingdom.
1:21 Daniel was there until the first year of King Cyrus.
## The king's troubling dream
2:1 In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign, Nebuchadnezzar dreamed dreams. His spirit was stirred up, and sleep slipped away from him.
2:2 The king gave the word to call in the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans, so that they could tell the king his dreams. They came in and stood before him.
2:3 He said to them, "A dream has come to me, and it has shaken my spirit. I want to know the dream."
## The Chaldeans answer in Aramaic
2:4 The Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic: "O king, live forever! Tell the dream to your servants, and we will declare its meaning."
2:5 The king answered the Chaldeans, "My word is firm. If you do not make the dream and its meaning known to me, you will be hacked apart, and your houses will be made a dunghill.
2:6 But if you can declare the dream and its meaning, you will receive gifts, a prize and great honor from my presence. So declare to me the dream and its meaning."
2:7 A second time they answered, "Let the king tell the dream to his servants, and we will declare its meaning."
2:8 The king answered, "I know for sure that you are buying time, since you can see that my word is firm.
2:9 If you do not make the dream known to me, there is one decree for you. You have agreed together to tell me something false and crooked, to stall until the times change. So tell me the dream, and then I will know that you can declare its meaning to me."
2:10 "Nobody on earth can tell the king what he is asking," the Chaldeans answered the king, "because no king, however great and powerful, has ever asked a thing like this from any magician, enchanter or Chaldean.
2:11 What the king is asking is hard. No one else can tell it to the king, except the gods, and they do not live among mortal flesh."
2:12 At this the king flew into a rage, so furious that he gave the order to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.
2:13 The order went out, and the wise men were to be put to death. They also searched for Daniel and his friends, to kill them.
## Daniel prays, and the secret is shown
2:14 Then Daniel spoke back with good sense and tact to Arioch, chief of the king's executioners, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.
2:15 He asked Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the order from the king so harsh?" Arioch then explained the matter to Daniel.
2:16 Daniel went in and asked the king for an appointed time, so that he could give the king the meaning.
2:17 Then Daniel went to his house and told the matter to Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, his friends.
2:18 He told them to ask the God of the heavens for compassion about this secret, so that Daniel and his friends would not be killed along with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.
2:19 Then, in a vision of the night, the secret was uncovered for Daniel. Daniel blessed the God of the heavens.
2:20 Daniel spoke up and said: / "Let the name of God be blessed / from age to age, / for wisdom and power belong to him.
2:21 He is the one who changes the periods and the appointed times; / he removes kings and he sets kings up. / He gives wisdom to the wise / and knowledge to those who know how to understand.
2:22 He uncovers deep things and hidden things. / He knows what is in the dark, / and light makes its home with him.
2:23 To you, God of my fathers, / I give thanks and I praise you, / because you have given me wisdom and power. / Now you have let me know what we asked from you; / you have made the king's matter known to us."
## Daniel before the king
2:24 Because of this, Daniel went to Arioch, the man the king had put in charge of destroying the wise men of Babylon. He said to him: "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon! Take me in before the king, and I will tell the king the meaning."
2:25 In a hurry Arioch brought Daniel in before the king and said, "I have found a man among the people carried off from Judah, and he will make the meaning known to the king."
2:26 The king asked Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to tell me the dream that I saw, and what it means?"
2:27 Daniel answered before the king: "The secret the king is asking about cannot be declared to the king by wise men, enchanters, magicians or diviners.
2:28 But there is a God in heaven who uncovers secrets, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what is to be in the last days. Here is your dream and the visions of your head upon your bed:
2:29 As for you, O king, on your bed your thoughts rose to what will come after this, and the one who uncovers secrets has shown you what will be.
2:30 As for me, this secret was not uncovered to me because I have more wisdom than all the living, but so that the meaning would be made known to the king, and you would understand the thoughts of your heart."
## The statue
2:31 "You, O king, were watching, and look: a single great statue! That statue was huge, its shine was extraordinary, and it stood facing you. Its look was terrifying.
2:32 The head of that statue was fine gold. Its chest and its arms were silver, and its belly and its hips were bronze.
2:33 Its legs were iron, and its feet were part iron and part clay.
2:34 You kept watching until a stone was cut out, but not by hands. It hit the statue's feet, the ones of iron and clay, and smashed them.
2:35 At that, iron, clay, bronze, silver and gold were crushed all at once. They turned into chaff on a summer threshing floor, and the wind blew them away, leaving no place where they could be found. The stone that had hit the statue grew into a huge mountain, and it filled the entire earth."
## The meaning of the dream
2:36 "That was the dream. Now we will say its meaning before the king.
2:37 You, O king, are the king of kings. The God of the heavens has given you the kingdom, the might, the strength and the honor.
2:38 Wherever human beings live, and the animals of the field and the birds of the sky, he has put them into your hand and made you ruler over all of them. You are the head of gold.
2:39 After you another kingdom will rise, lower than yours, and a third kingdom after that, one of bronze, and it will rule over all the earth.
2:40 The fourth kingdom will be hard as iron, for iron smashes and grinds down everything. Like iron that pounds things into pieces, it will smash and grind down all these.
2:41 You saw that the feet and the toes were part potter's clay and part iron. That means a divided kingdom. Some of the firmness of iron will be in it, since you saw iron mixed with muddy clay.
2:42 The toes of the feet were part iron and part clay, so part of the kingdom will be strong and part of it will break easily.
2:43 You saw iron mixed with muddy clay: they will be mixed together by human seed. But they will not stick to each other, for iron and clay do not blend.
2:44 During the reigns of these kings the God of the heavens will raise up a kingdom that will never be ruined. Its rule will not be handed to another people. It will pound all these kingdoms to pieces and end them, and it alone will stand forever.
2:45 That is what it means that you saw a stone cut from a mountain, though not by hands, and it smashed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver and the gold. A great God has made known to the king what will be after this. The dream is sure, and its meaning can be trusted."
## The king honors Daniel
2:46 Then King Nebuchadnezzar threw himself face down and paid homage to Daniel. He ordered that gifts of grain and sweet-smelling incense be offered to him.
2:47 The king said to Daniel, "It is true: your God is the God of gods and the lord of kings, the one who uncovers secrets, since you were able to uncover this secret."
2:48 Then the king raised Daniel to a high rank and gave him many great gifts. He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief of the prefects over all the wise men of Babylon.
2:49 Daniel asked the king, and he put Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in charge of the work of the province of Babylon. Daniel himself was at the king's gate.
## The golden statue
3:1 King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold, sixty cubits tall and six cubits across. He stood it upright on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.
3:2 Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent out a call to bring together the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the advisers, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the other officials of the provinces. They were to come to the dedication of the statue King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
3:3 So the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the advisers, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the other officials of the provinces were gathered for the dedication of the statue King Nebuchadnezzar had set up. They stood in front of the statue he had set up.
3:4 A herald cried out with a strong voice: "This is what you are told to do, O peoples, nations and languages:
3:5 The moment you hear the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the triangle-lyre, the harp, the pipes and every other kind of music, you are to fall to the ground and worship the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.
3:6 Anyone who does not fall to the ground and worship will be thrown that same hour into the middle of a furnace of burning fire."
3:7 So at that very time, as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the triangle-lyre, the harp and every other kind of music, all the peoples, nations and languages fell down and worshiped the golden statue King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
## The Chaldeans accuse the Jews
3:8 At that time certain Chaldean men came forward and slandered the Jews.
3:9 They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "O king, live forever!
3:10 You yourself, O king, gave the order that every person who hears the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the triangle-lyre, the harp, the pipes and every other kind of music is to fall down and worship the golden statue,
3:11 and that whoever does not fall down and worship is to be thrown into the middle of a furnace of burning fire.
3:12 There are Jewish men whom you have put over the work of the province of Babylon: Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men have not given you a thought, O king. They do not serve your gods, and they do not worship the golden statue you have set up."
## The king's test
3:13 Then Nebuchadnezzar, shaking with rage and fury, ordered Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego to be brought. So those men were brought in before the king.
3:14 Nebuchadnezzar said to them, "Is it on purpose, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods and do not worship the golden statue I have set up?
3:15 So now: if you are prepared, at the moment you hear the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the triangle-lyre, the harp, the pipes and every other kind of music, to fall down and worship the statue I made, that is fine. But if you do not worship, in that same hour you will be thrown into the middle of a furnace of burning fire. Which god is there that can save you from my hands?"
3:16 Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar, we do not need to give you a reply about this matter.
3:17 If it comes to that, our God, whom we serve, is able to save us from the furnace of burning fire. And he will save us from your hand, O king.
3:18 And if he does not, O king, let this be known to you: we do not serve your gods, and we will not worship the golden statue you have set up."
## The furnace
3:19 Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the look of his face was changed toward Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. He gave the order to heat the furnace seven times hotter than anyone had thought right to heat it.
3:20 He told some mighty men of his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and throw them into the furnace of burning fire.
3:21 Then these men were tied up in their cloaks, their trousers, their caps and their other clothes, and they were thrown into the middle of the furnace of burning fire.
3:22 The king's order was so harsh, and the furnace so very hot, that the flame of the fire killed the men who lifted Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego up to it.
3:23 And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, fell, still bound, into the middle of the furnace of burning fire.
## The fourth figure
3:24 Then King Nebuchadnezzar was startled and rose quickly. He spoke to his counselors: "Weren't there three men that we threw, tied up, into the middle of the fire?" They answered the king, "Surely, O king."
3:25 He answered, "I am looking at four men, unbound, pacing in the middle of the fire, with no harm on them. The fourth one's appearance is like that of a son of the gods."
3:26 Then Nebuchadnezzar came up to the door of the furnace of burning fire and called out: "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, servants of the Most High God, come out and come this way!" Then Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego walked out of the middle of the fire.
3:27 The satraps, the prefects, the governors and the king's counselors crowded around and looked at these men. The fire had had no power over their bodies; the hair on their heads was not scorched, their cloaks had not changed, and not even the smell of fire had come on them.
## The king blesses their God
3:28 Nebuchadnezzar spoke: "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who sent his angel and saved his servants. They trusted in him, they disobeyed the king's order, and they gave up their bodies rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.
3:29 So from me an order is given: any people, nation or language that says anything wrong against the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego will be hacked apart, and its house will be made a dunghill, because there is no other god who can rescue like this."
3:30 Then the king made Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego prosper in the province of Babylon.
