# Hostile review: Daniel 1-3 (part 01-03) and book.json

Reviewer notes: read plain.txt, notes, variants, glosses, chapters, topics and sources of the part, and book.json. Checked against texts/orig/Dan.json, texts/auto/Dan.json, `tools/dss.py Dan 1|2|3`, `tools/lxx.py Dan|Dat 1-3` (Swete's print only), the writer's literal lines and drift audit in .scratch/Dan-01-03, content/terms.json and the prophets proposals, and web checks (Calvin, the Dositheus Confession, Pesachim 118a, Maimonides, Susanna's position). `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Dan/01-03` gives 2 errors and 0 warnings. Both errors are in other books (Ezk 20-24 `galambush-jerusalem`; Psa 138-150 `etcbc-dss`), not in this part.

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly honest part. The dating debate in book.json is fair and names its holders. The canon card has six church answers that match each church's own text where I could check (Westminster 1.3, Belgic art. 6, Article VI, the Dositheus Confession Decree 4, the Luther Apocrypha heading, the 1689 Confession). Qumran claims are mostly accurate. The Plain English Translation is largely rebuilt from the Hebrew and Aramaic. The problems are these. A lead decision was not applied ("the God of the heavens"). Several witness claims are wrong, overstated or incomplete: two real Qumran/Greek variants are missing, the Old Greek is left out of the 2:20 variant, and the Greek attributions in four notes are swapped or lumped. One Talmud report (Pesachim 118a) is wrong. A few verses still carry NIV/ESV sentence shape (3:25, 3:26, 3:17, 2:28, 2:31). The "Old Greek twelfth year" is sourced only to "the commentaries". Nothing is invented from nothing and no reader-facing text says "from memory", so I rate no finding critical. Counts: 0 critical, 9 major, 15 minor.

## Major

1. **Lead decision not applied: "the God of heaven" (major).** plain.txt 2:18, 2:19, 2:37, 2:44 say "the God of heaven". The Lead decision in brief-dan.md requires "the God of the heavens", as in Ezra and Nehemiah. The Aramaic is `'elah shemayya`, and the writer's own literal line has "heavens". notes.json 2:18 says "Daniel's Plain English Translation follows the site's term 'the God of heaven'", and proposals/prophets/terms.json (row for `'elah shemaya`) records the same. Variant 2:18 `plain` text and the setting at 2:27-45 repeat it. Fix: change the four verses, rewrite the 2:18 note and the term row, and keep "a God in heaven" at 2:28 (singular `bishmayya`). 2:28 and 3:26 are fine.

2. **Variant 2:20: the Old Greek is omitted, so "only one copy" is false, and the note is garbled (major).** 4Q112 (4QDan-a) reads `shemeh di 'elaha «rabba»` (rabba is visible, not bracketed). Swete's Old Greek 2:20 reads "the name of the great Lord (kyriou tou megalou) be blessed". Theodotion has only "the name of God". So two witnesses, one Hebrew-side and one Greek, have "great". The `why` says "Only one copy has the longer text", which is not so. The `b` says "'great' also appears in the next lines of the same hymn ... ('a great God' in 2:45)". 2:45 is not in the hymn (2:20-23), and it is the end of Daniel's interpretation. Fix: add the Old Greek to the second reading with a note that Theodotion and the MT lack it. Rewrite the `why` (two independent witnesses; the MT may have lost it, or both may have added a common epithet; the Plain English Translation still follows the MT). Delete the garbled sentence about 2:45.

3. **Two real Qumran/Greek variants are missing (major).** (a) 2:40: dss.py shows 4Q112 with two extra words after `we-tera'` (marked «kl» «'r'», "all the earth"), which agrees in sense with the Old Greek "all the earth will be shaken" (Swete). The MT and Theodotion lack it. No variant is given. (b) 2:31: the MT has `ziveh` "its brightness", the source of the Plain English Translation's "its shine". 4Q112 reads «`hazwoh`» "its appearance" (visible), and both Greek forms have `prosopsis`/`horasis`, "appearance". This is a real MT/4QDan-a/Greek split. Fix: add both entries. List 4Q112 on each. Say that the 4Q112 words are visible, not restored. Brief rule: "never say the Greek supports a reading until you checked" applies, so check that the Greek wording comes from `hzwh`.

4. **Pesachim 118a is misreported (major).** notes.json 3:25 says "in the Talmud Gabriel and another angel offer to cool the furnace (Pesachim 118a)". chapters.json ch. 3 J says "the Talmud has angels offer to cool the furnace". topics.json `son-of-god` `uncommon` says "the fourth man was the angel Gabriel (rabbinic stories)". In the Pesachim passage, as I found it, the angel in charge of hail (`sar shel barad`) offers to cool the furnace and Gabriel objects and proposes to make it cold inside and hot outside; God then speaks of rescuing the righteous himself. (The web sources I could reach quote it second-hand; check Sefaria directly.) It does not identify the fourth figure as Gabriel, and it does not say the fourth figure was an angel. The medieval Jewish angel reading comes from commentators (Rashi, Saadia, as reported by Lange). Fix: correct the note and the J view, delete the Gabriel claim from `son-of-god` (or find a real source), and cite the commentators by Sefaria if they are to stand for the Jewish angel reading.

5. **Greek attributions wrong or lumped in four notes (major).** Checked with lxx.py:
   - Note 3:21: "Theodotion's Greek has 'shoes' and 'tiaras' and the Old Greek similar." It is the other way round. The Old Greek (Swete) has `hypodemata` "shoes" and `tiaras`. Theodotion has `sarabara` (a loanword, "trousers"), `tiarais` and `periknemisi` "leggings".
   - Note 3:14: "The Greek has 'Why, Shadrach ...?'". That is the Old Greek (`Dia ti`). Theodotion reads `Ei alethos`, "Is it true?", the KJV sense, which is exactly the point the note is arguing. Name both.
   - Note 3:1/Dura: "In the Greek versions the name is spelled Deira." Only Theodotion has `Deeira`. The Old Greek renders the word by sense (`pedio tou peribolou`, "the plain of the enclosure/wall"), which supports the note's "wall" meaning and should be said.
   - Note 1:12: "The Greek has 'pulse' (ospria)". This is the Old Greek. Theodotion has `spermata`, "seeds".
   Brief: "always say which Greek".

6. **Old Greek 2:1 "twelfth year" rests on "the commentaries" (major).** variants.json 2:1 and notes 1:5 attribute "twelfth" to "the commentaries" and to P967/Syro-Hexapla "as reported", with `s` keys collins-daniel and ziegler-daniel. The Lead decision allows "reported by <named source>" only. Swete's Chisianus print reads "second" (confirmed), so the writer's treatment of the print is right, but the claim about P967 and the Syro-Hexapla is not checked and not pinned to a page. Fix: name the report (for example "reported by Collins, Daniel, on 2:1, and in Ziegler's apparatus") after verifying it in the book, or cut the second reading to a one-line note. Also make the `w` field of the first reading say the print is one manuscript whose text may be corrected toward Theodotion (do not guess why).

7. **NIV/ESV sentence shape survives in several verses (major, overall).** The audit says 3:17 and 3:25-26 were "kept where Aramaic forces it", but the Aramaic does not force the following:
   - 3:25 "Look! I see four men ... walking about in the middle of the fire ... And the fourth looks like a son of the gods" follows the NIV's "Look! I see four men walking around in the fire ... the fourth looks like a son of the gods". The Aramaic has `chaze ... ve-rev'eh di rivi'aah dameh`, "and the appearance of the fourth is like". Rebuild, for example: "Now I see four men, untied, pacing in the middle of the fire. They are not harmed. The fourth one has the look of a son of the gods."
   - 3:26 "drew near to the opening of the furnace of burning fire and spoke: 'Shadrach ... servants of the Most High God, come out! Come here!'" is close to the NIV (approached the opening ... come out! Come here!). `tar'a` is "door", and the writer's own gloss says "the door of the furnace" for "opening". Use "door".
   - 3:17 "If that is the case, our God, whom we serve, is able to save us from the furnace of burning fire, and he will save us from your hand, O king" has the ESV/KJV frame (If it be so ... is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace; and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king). `hen ithay` is a hard clause (see the note), and the Plain English Translation's "If that is the case" is a smoothing of "if it be so". Try the existential reading the variant offers, or break the sentence.
   - 3:18 "And if he does not, you should know, O king, that we do not serve your gods" echoes the ESV "be it known to you, O king".
   - 2:28 "the visions that passed through your head as you lay on your bed" has the NIV's added verb "passed through". The Aramaic has no verb: "your dream and the visions of your head upon your bed are this". Also "what will happen" (2:28) is the NIV's phrase for `mah di lehe'`.
   - 2:31 "there before you stood one great statue" echoes the NIV "there before you stood a large statue".
   - 1:2 still follows the ESV's two-sentence chain ("into his hand, with some of the vessels ... He brought them to the land of Shinar, to the house of his god").
   - Lesser: 1:20 ("On every question that called for wisdom ... he found them ten times better than every magician and enchanter") tracks the NIV; 2:3 is the ESV's "I have had a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream" plus two words.
   Not a copy of whole verses, so I do not call it critical; the sentence shape is the problem the brief names.

8. **Witness evidence overstated in four variants (major).**
   - Variant 3:1: "the Aramaic copy at Qumran has no date at this point". In dss.py 4Q112 3:1 starts with a restored `[Nebuchadnezzar]` at the head of the fragment, so whatever stood before it is lost and an absent date cannot be shown. Reword to "4Q112 begins with the king's name; whether a date stood before it cannot be seen", or cite DJD 16 for the editors' judgment.
   - Variant 3:25: 1QDan-b "'like a son of' is preserved". In 1Q72 only `lebar` is visible; `dama` ("like") and `'elahin` are restored. Say so.
   - Variant 2:28: `orig` prints 4Q112's restored words (`malka`, `chelmakh`) without brackets, and the `plain` of that reading uses "This is your dream". Mark restored words in the `orig` and `en` (only `le'almin` and `chayyi` are visible), as the rest of the apparatus does. Also say what the short text drops (`vechezwe re'shakh`). The `b` sentence "if confirmed in the edition" is a hedge on data the writer already has; check DJD 16 or drop the sentence.
   - Note 2:4: 1QDan-a "keeps the break at the same point". In dss.py `aramit` itself is in brackets (restored); what is visible is `malka le'almin` running on. Say that.
   - Also `s` keys: the readings come from the ETCBC (Abegg-Bowley-Cook) transcription, not from *The Dead Sea Scrolls Bible* (`abegg-dssb`). 1QDan-a/b are published in DJD 1, not DJD 16 (`djd16` is the Cave 4 volume). Add or reuse a key for the transcription.

9. **Susanna variant lumps the wrong witnesses (major).** variants.json 1:1 reading 2 says "Greek Daniel (Theodotion and the Old Greek) in manuscripts and editions that put Susanna first; the Vulgate puts it last". The Old Greek's one early witness, P967, puts Susanna at the end (after Bel). The sources I found put Susanna before Daniel in the Theodotion manuscript tradition (Vaticanus, as reported), directly after Daniel in the Syro-Hexapla, and last in the Vulgate (ch. 13). The body (`b`) gets P967 right ("reported to put it after the main text"), so the `w` field contradicts it. Fix: split into the positions, each with its witness, and mark each "reported by" with the source. The brief's own phrasing ("before 1:1 in Theodotion's order as printed in the Vulgate tradition as ch. 13") is muddled, so do not copy it.

## Minor

10. **Plain English Translation 1:4 "taught in every kind of wisdom".** `maskilim bekhol chokhmah` is "having insight in all wisdom", not "taught". The term row `maskilim` in proposals/prophets/terms.json joins this root to 1:17 (`haskel`, which the Plain English Translation does render "insight") and to 11:33-35 and 12:3. The Plain English Translation loses the link at the one place where the book first uses the root. Use "insightful in all wisdom" or "skilled in all wisdom with insight".

11. **Small additions or inconsistencies in the Plain English Translation.** 2:6 "will come to you from my hand": the Aramaic is `min qodami`, "from before me". 2:9 "You have all agreed": no "all". 2:45 "a stone come loose from the mountain, though no hands cut it": `'itgezeret` is "was cut" (2:34 says "cut out"), so "come loose" contradicts the Plain English Translation itself. 2:46 "paid homage" for `segid` vs "worship" at 3:5 and following: the term row defends this, and the note 2:46 says so, but the Plain English Translation hides the repeated verb, which is the whole point of the contrast; at least repeat the Aramaic `segid` in the 2:46 gloss.

12. **Hebrew and form errors.** Variant 1:16 prints `zero'anim` with the pointing zĕro'ānîm. The text (texts/orig, WLC) at 1:16 is `zer'onim` (זֵרְעֹנִים), and at 1:12 the word is `hazzero'im` (הַזֵּרֹעִים). Note 12 (at 1:12) shows the 1:16 form (`זֵרְעֹנִים`) labelled `zero'im`. Fix both and the transliterations. Note 17 (2:1) `orig` pointing is also off (the WLC has `וּשְׁנָתוֹ נִהְיְתָה`). Note 43 gives `qitharos`; the Ketiv/Qere is `qitaros` / `qatros`.

13. **Ketiv/Qere and oldest-scroll readings not listed.** texts/auto marks 2:4 (Qere singular `'avdakh`, Ketiv plural; the Plain English Translation takes the plural, and 1Q71 has the plural), 2:22 (Qere `nehora`, Ketiv `nehira`; 4Q112 reads `nehira`), and 3:12, 3:18 (`le-'elahakh` Qere "gods"; the Plain English Translation "your gods" follows it but the writer's literal line has "god"). None is in the variants or notes. 1Q71 also preserves `'azda` at 2:5 and should be named in the note at 2:5 as the oldest witness. Also the Old Greek's plus at 1:20 (the king "glorified them and made them rulers") is not noted, nor Old Greek/Theodotion 3:30 (`3:97`) "over the whole land" / "over all the Jews" (only Theodotion is given).

14. **Dangling cross-references.** notes 1:2 and 3:1 send readers to "the book's opening pages for a theory about Shinar and the plain of Dura". book.json has no such theory; it sits as a `spec` box in the chapter 1 page ("Claude's theory: Daniel opens in Shinar"). Fix the pointers. In that box, "the only place in the prophetic books that sends the temple vessels to Shinar" is a claim that needs a check against 2 Chronicles 36:7 / Jeremiah 27:16-22 / Zechariah 5:11 (Zechariah has an ephah, not vessels, so the claim holds narrowly), and "the same word, `biq'ah`" is Hebrew in Genesis 11:2 and Aramaic `biq'a` in Daniel 3:1. The note at 3:1 "sixty and six echo Babylon's base-sixty counting" is unlabeled speculation; mark it or cut it.

15. **Maimonides quotation and translation.** book.json quotes "the nation desired to place the book of Daniel among the Hagiographa" (Guide 2.45). That wording is Friedländer's translation; the source key `maimonides-guide` names Pines (1963). Either quote Pines or change the key. "One degree below prophecy" is also loose: Maimonides says Daniel's dream-vision stands one degree below the prophetic dream-form quoted from Numbers 12:6, and puts the Hagiographa in the holy-spirit class.

16. **book.json sentence broken.** Section "When and by whom": "The book sets stories in the sixth century, Outside the book, Daniel is called a prophet in Matthew 24:15 ..." is a comma splice with a missing clause. Also "Jesus speaks of 'the prophet Daniel' (Matthew 24:15)" in the authorship list and "(Matthew 24:15; Mark 13:14)" in "Daniel in the New Testament": the words "spoken of through the prophet Daniel" are in Matthew only (Mark's earliest manuscripts lack them). Say so.

17. **Missing settings.** The settings cards leave out speeches and acts: the head of the eunuchs' fear (1:10), the king's honoring and homage (2:46-49), the king's challenge at 3:13-15 (the "which god can save you" scene is covered only in a note), and the king's recognition at 3:24-27. 1:12-13 and 3:16-18 are good. Also, at 3:9-12 the `readers` cite 1 Maccabees 1:34 and 2:15-26 as "informers"; 1:34 is the garrison and 2:15-26 the king's officers at Modein, not informers. Chapter 1 `views` J says "the Midrash makes Daniel's food test a model of keeping the commandments in exile" with no named midrash, and X says "some churches treat the passage as a call to fasting" with no church named. Name them or remove them.

18. **Topics.** `angels`: "the only angel in the first half of Daniel before Gabriel and Michael" cites 6:22 in the same paragraph (also in the first half) and ignores the watchers of 4:13. Reword to "the first angel in the book". `four-kingdoms` core is only a restatement of the chapter 2 views with one source; it names no Jewish medieval commentator (Rashi, Ibn Ezra) and no Orthodox or Luther reading, which the brief lists. Add them from Sefaria and the Luther preface, or state that part 04-07 will.

19. **Terms.** Plain English Translation 1:9 "favor and mercy": the note says `hesed` is "favor" and `rachamim` is "mercy". The site's term `hesed` is "loyal love" and the prophets term `rachamim` is "compassion". Plain English Translation 2:18 "mercy" for Aramaic `rachamin` has the same problem. Either use the site terms or add a term row saying why Daniel differs.

20. **Glosses.** 1:5 "rich food" glossed as "royal gourmet meals" is anachronistic and a little silly; use "food from the king's own table". 3:2 gloss for "satraps" ("governors of the large regions") collides with the Plain English Translation's separate "governors" in the same verse. 2:46 "sweet-smelling incense" glossed "perfumed smoke offered to a god" is fine. The 3:19 "seven times hotter" gloss ("as hot as it could possibly be made") overstates; the Aramaic says one seven over what was fit.

21. **Traditions card, fine points.** The six answers check out. (a) Catholic: Trent does not name the additions; the card says "as they stood in the old Latin Vulgate", which is accurate, but say plainly that the naming is by the Vulgate's contents. (b) Lutheran is sourced to Luther's Bible and preface only, and no Lutheran confession is cited; the Book of Concord does not list the books, so say that. (c) Orthodox: add that the Synod of Jerusalem (Decree 4) lists "the history of the Dragon" and "the history of Susanna" (I confirmed these, but not the Song of the Three, which Dositheus does not list by name; the card says "Susanna and the story of the Dragon" and is right); the Song and Prayer come in through liturgy and the Septuagint text, as the card says. (d) Anglican: also cite the Benedicite in the 1662 BCP, which the card does.

22. **Hippolytus and Calvin on 3:25.** Calvin: confirmed (the king spoke as of an angel; Calvin also says the angel's help is fulfilled in Christ). Hippolytus: the surviving catena excerpt addresses Nebuchadnezzar about "the Son of God"; the note should say it is "as reported in the catena" and that the authorship of that excerpt is debated. The brief also named Justin Martyr and Augustine as holders of the Christ reading; the note names only Hippolytus. Either add them from a checked source or leave them out and say two named holders are given. Jerome (angels, with Symmachus' "sons of the gods"; a type of Christ) is a better-attested early witness than Justin here and should be added.

23. **Sources.** The `s` key `abegg-dssb` and `djd16` (item 8). Source `day-daniel-vt` and the matching proposal text say "The Daniel 01-03 writer remembers the article as ...": the citation is correct (Day, VT 30 (1980) 174-184), but the proposals file is lead-facing, so reword to "cited as" so no one reads "remembers" as a memory-only source.

24. **Dating debate and lenses (checked, no defect).** Both views are stated in their strongest form and named: Young, Baldwin, Archer, Hasel, Kitchen, Millard on one side; Porphyry, Collins, Hartman and Di Lella, Seow, Newsom on the other; Goldingay and Lucas as mediators. 4QDan-c/a dates are given "so Ulrich, as reported by Flint", which is allowed. Josephus (Ant. 11.337), Bava Batra 14b/15a and Megillah 3a are accurate. Lens descriptions say how each lens reads both datings. No change needed except the sentence in item 16.

25. **Reading level and style.** No em-dashes, no "from memory" in reader-facing text (the grep hits are "recalls"). Poetry lines in 2:20-23 are kept. A few notes run long and use "the verdict:" (note 2:31) oddly; change to "Summary:". The Plain English Translation 3:5/3:7 omit `sumponya` at 3:7 as the Aramaic does; correct.
