## The king's letter
4:1 King Nebuchadnezzar, to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth: May your peace grow greater!
4:2 It has seemed good to me to tell about the signs and wonders that the Most High God has done for me.
4:3 How great are his signs! / How mighty his wonders! / His kingdom lasts forever, / and his rule goes on from one generation to the next.
## The dream of the great tree
4:4 I, Nebuchadnezzar, lived at home without a worry, fresh and green in my palace.
4:5 I saw a dream, and it frightened me. The pictures that came as I lay in bed, the visions in my head, alarmed me.
4:6 So I gave this order: all the wise men of Babylon were to be brought in to me, so that they could tell me what the dream meant.
4:7 The magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners came in. I described the dream to them, but not one of them could make its meaning known to me.
4:8 Last of all, Daniel came in before me. His name is Belteshazzar, matching the name of my god, and a spirit of the holy gods is in him. I told him the dream:
4:9 "Belteshazzar, head of the magicians, I know that a spirit of the holy gods is in you and that no secret is too much for you. These are the visions I saw in my dream. Tell me what they mean.
4:10 "This is what I saw in my head as I lay in bed. I was looking, and there stood a tree in the middle of the earth, very tall.
4:11 The tree became huge and strong, with its top touching the sky. People could see it from the far ends of the whole earth.
4:12 Its leaves were lovely and its fruit was abundant; it held food for everyone. Wild animals rested in its shade, birds of the sky nested in its branches, and all living things ate from it.
4:13 "As I kept watching in the visions of my bed, a watcher, a holy one, came down from heaven.
4:14 He shouted with a loud voice: / 'Cut down the tree and lop off its branches! / Strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit! / Let the animals run from under it, / and the birds from its branches!
4:15 Yet leave the stump with its roots in the ground, / bound with iron and bronze, / in the grass of the field. / Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, / and let his share be with the animals in the plants of the earth.
4:16 Let his human heart be changed, / let the heart of an animal be given to him, / and let seven periods pass over him.
4:17 The watchers decreed this sentence; / the holy ones spoke the verdict. / So all who live will know / that the Most High is ruler over the kingdom of humankind. / He hands it to anyone he pleases / and can set the least of people over it.'
4:18 "This is the dream I saw, I, King Nebuchadnezzar. Now you, Belteshazzar, tell me its meaning. All the wise men of my kingdom have failed to show me what it means. You are able, for a spirit of the holy gods is in you."
## Daniel explains the tree
4:19 Then Daniel, called Belteshazzar, was struck silent for about an hour, and his thoughts terrified him. The king said, "Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its meaning frighten you." Belteshazzar replied, "My lord, may the dream belong to those who hate you, and its meaning to your enemies.
4:20 The tree that you saw, which grew huge and strong, whose top touched the sky and which the whole earth could see,
4:21 whose leaves were lovely and whose fruit was abundant, with food for everyone in it, under which the wild animals lived, and in whose branches the birds of the sky nested:
4:22 that tree is you, O king. You have grown large and strong. Your greatness has grown and reached the sky, and your rule reaches to the ends of the earth.
4:23 "The king saw a watcher, a holy one, coming down from heaven and saying, 'Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump with its roots in the ground, bound with iron and bronze, in the grass of the field. Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, and let his share be with the animals of the field, until seven periods pass over him.'
4:24 This is the meaning, O king, and it is the decree of the Most High that has come upon my lord the king:
4:25 You will be driven out from among people, and your home will be with the animals of the field. You will be fed grass like oxen, and the dew of heaven will drench you. Seven periods will pass over you, until you know that the Most High is ruler over the kingdom of humankind and gives it to whomever he pleases.
4:26 The order to leave the stump of the tree's roots means that your kingdom will stay yours, once you know that Heaven rules.
4:27 So, O king, take my advice. Break with your sins by doing what is right, and with your wrongs by showing mercy to the poor. Then your good fortune may last longer."
## The king is humbled
4:28 All of this happened to King Nebuchadnezzar.
4:29 Twelve months later he was walking on the roof of the royal palace in Babylon.
4:30 The king spoke up and said, "Is this not Babylon the Great? I built it to be a royal house, by the strength of my own might, to honor my majesty!"
4:31 The words were still on the king's lips when a voice fell from heaven: "King Nebuchadnezzar, this is said to you: the kingdom has left you.
4:32 You will be driven out from among people, and your home will be with the animals of the field. You will be fed grass like oxen, and seven periods will pass over you, until you know that the Most High is ruler over the kingdom of humankind and gives it to whomever he pleases."
4:33 In that very hour the word came true for Nebuchadnezzar. He was driven out from among people and ate grass like oxen, and the dew of heaven drenched his body, until his hair grew like an eagle's and his nails like a bird's.
## The king praises the Most High
4:34 When those days were over, I, Nebuchadnezzar, raised my eyes to heaven, and my reason came back to me. I blessed the Most High, and I praised and honored the one who lives forever: / His kingdom lasts forever, / and his rule goes on from one generation to the next.
4:35 Everyone who lives on the earth is counted as nothing, / and he does as he pleases / with the heavenly army and with those who live on the earth. / No one can slap his hand away / and ask him, "What have you done?"
4:36 At that same time my reason came back to me, and my glory, majesty and splendor came back with it, for the honor of my kingdom. My advisers and nobles came looking for me. I was set up again over my kingdom, and even more greatness was added to me.
4:37 So now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise, lift up and glorify the King of heaven. Everything he does is true, and his ways are just. Those who walk in pride he is able to humble.
## Belshazzar's banquet
5:1 King Belshazzar gave a great banquet for a thousand of his nobles, and he drank wine in front of them all.
5:2 Under the influence of the wine, Belshazzar gave an order: bring the gold and silver vessels that his father Nebuchadnezzar had carried off from the temple in Jerusalem. The king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines were going to drink from them.
5:3 So they brought in the gold vessels that had been taken from the temple, the house of God in Jerusalem, and the king drank from them with his nobles, his wives and his concubines.
5:4 As they drank wine, they sang the praises of their gods, made of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone.
## The writing on the wall
5:5 Fingers of a human hand came out in that same hour and began to write on the plaster of the palace wall, facing the lampstand. The king watched the palm of the hand as it wrote.
5:6 The king's color changed, and his thoughts terrified him. The joints of his hips gave way, and his knees knocked against each other.
5:7 The king shouted for the enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners to be brought in. He told the wise men of Babylon, "Any person who can read this writing and make its meaning known to me will wear purple, with a gold chain on his neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom."
5:8 Then all the king's wise men came in, but none of them could read the writing or tell the king what it meant.
5:9 King Belshazzar was badly frightened. His color changed again, and his nobles were at a loss.
## The queen speaks
5:10 The queen heard what the king and his nobles were saying and came into the banquet hall. She spoke: "O king, live forever! Do not let your thoughts frighten you, and do not let your color change.
5:11 There is a man in your kingdom who has a spirit of the holy gods in him. In your father's day, clear sight, insight and wisdom like the wisdom of the gods were found in him. King Nebuchadnezzar, your father, set him over the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners. Your father, the king, did this
5:12 because a remarkable spirit, with knowledge and insight, was found in Daniel, the man the king named Belteshazzar: he can interpret dreams, declare the answers to riddles and untie knots. Now call for Daniel, and he will tell you what it means."
## Daniel before the king
5:13 So Daniel was brought in to the king. The king said to him, "Are you that Daniel, one of the people of the exile from Judah, whom my father the king brought out of Judah?
5:14 I have heard of you that a spirit of the gods is in you, and that clear sight, insight and remarkable wisdom were found in you.
5:15 Just now the wise men and the enchanters were brought to me to read this writing and tell me what it means, but they were not able to explain the matter.
5:16 But I have heard of you that you can give interpretations and untie knots. Now if you are able to read the writing and make its meaning known to me, you will wear purple, with a gold chain on your neck, and rule as third in the kingdom."
5:17 Daniel answered in front of the king, "You may keep your gifts, and give your rewards to someone else. Still, I will read the writing for the king and tell him what it means.
## Daniel's rebuke
5:18 As for you, O king, the Most High God gave the kingdom, greatness, honor and majesty to your father Nebuchadnezzar.
5:19 Because of the greatness he gave him, all peoples, nations and languages shook with fear before him. He killed anyone he wanted to kill and let live anyone he wanted to let live. He raised up anyone he wanted to raise, and brought down anyone he wanted to bring down.
5:20 But when his heart grew proud and his spirit hardened into arrogance, he was brought down from his royal throne, and his honor was taken away.
5:21 He was driven out from among human beings, and his heart was made to match the animals'. He lived with the wild donkeys, and was fed grass like an ox, and the dew of heaven drenched his body, until he learned that the Most High God is ruler over the kingdom of humankind and puts over it whomever he pleases.
5:22 And you, Belshazzar, his son, have not made your heart humble, even though you knew all this.
5:23 You set yourself up against the Lord of heaven. You had the vessels of his house brought to you, and you drank wine from them, you and your nobles, your wives and your concubines. You sang praises to gods of silver and gold, bronze, iron, wood and stone, gods that cannot see or hear or know anything. But the God who holds your every breath in his hand, and to whom all your paths belong, you did not honor.
5:24 That is why the hand was sent from his presence, and this writing was set down.
## Mene, Tekel, Peres
5:25 This is the writing that was set down: Mene, mene, tekel, and parsin.
5:26 This is what the words mean. Mene: God has counted out your kingdom and has finished it.
5:27 Tekel: you were put on a scale and found lacking.
5:28 Peres: your kingdom has been divided and handed to the Medes and Persians."
5:29 Then Belshazzar commanded it, and Daniel was dressed in purple with a gold chain around his neck, and they announced that he would be third ruler in the kingdom.
5:30 That same night Belshazzar, the Chaldean king, was killed.
5:31 Darius the Mede received the kingdom when he was about sixty-two years old.
## The decree against prayer
6:1 Darius decided to appoint one hundred twenty satraps to be over the whole kingdom.
6:2 Over them were three overseers, and Daniel was one of them. The satraps were to report to these three, so the king would not suffer any loss.
6:3 Daniel soon outshone the other overseers and the satraps, because a remarkable spirit was in him. The king planned to put him in charge of the entire kingdom.
6:4 So the overseers and satraps kept looking for something to use against Daniel in his work for the kingdom. But they could find no grounds and no corruption, because he was faithful. No neglect and no wrongdoing could be found in him.
6:5 Those men said to one another, "We will never find a charge against this Daniel unless we find one in connection with the law of his God."
6:6 Then the overseers and satraps crowded in on the king and said to him: "King Darius, live forever!
6:7 All the overseers of the kingdom, with the prefects, satraps, counselors and governors, have agreed on this: the king should issue a decree and put a firm ban in force. For thirty days, anyone who makes a request of any god or any person, except you, O king, is to be thrown into the lions' den.
6:8 Now, O king, set the ban in place and sign your name to the document, so that it cannot be altered. That is the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be repealed."
6:9 So King Darius signed the document and the ban.
## Daniel prays
6:10 Daniel learned that the document had been signed. He went into his house. In his upstairs room he had windows that opened toward Jerusalem. Three times a day he knelt, prayed and gave thanks before his God, just as he had always done.
6:11 Then those men came bursting in, and they found Daniel making a request and begging for mercy before his God.
6:12 They went to the king and spoke about the king's ban: "Did you not sign a ban that for thirty days anyone who makes a request of any god or any person, except of you, O king, is to be thrown into the lions' den?" The king answered, "The matter stands firm, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be repealed."
6:13 Then they said to the king, "Daniel, one of the people of the exile from Judah, takes no notice of you, O king, or of the ban you signed. Three times a day he says his prayer."
6:14 When the king heard the matter, he was greatly upset. He set his mind to rescue Daniel, and until the sun went down he kept struggling to free him.
6:15 Then those men crowded in on the king and said, "Know, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians that no ban or decree the king establishes can be changed."
## The lions' den
6:16 So the king gave the command, and they brought Daniel and threw him into the lions' den. The king said to Daniel, "Your God, whom you serve without ceasing, will himself rescue you."
6:17 A single stone was brought and laid over the mouth of the den. The king sealed it with his own signet ring and the rings of his nobles, so that the decision about Daniel could not be altered.
6:18 Then the king went back to his palace and spent the night without food. Nothing to amuse him was brought in, and sleep ran from him.
6:19 At the first light of dawn the king got up and went in a hurry to the lions' den.
6:20 Coming close to the den, he called to Daniel in a voice full of pain: "Daniel, servant of the living God! Your God, whom you serve without ceasing, was he able to rescue you from the lions?"
6:21 Daniel answered the king: "O king, live forever!
6:22 My God sent his angel to shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me, since innocence was found in me before him. Before you also, O king, I have done no harm."
6:23 The king was delighted and ordered Daniel lifted out of the den. Daniel was lifted out, and no injury at all was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.
6:24 At the king's command, the men who had slandered Daniel were brought and thrown into the lions' den, they and their sons and their wives. They had not reached the floor of the den when the lions seized them and crushed all their bones.
## Darius's decree
6:25 Then King Darius wrote to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth: May your peace grow greater!
6:26 I issue this decree: in every part of my kingdom's rule, people must tremble and fear before the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, and he endures forever. His kingdom will never be destroyed, and his rule will last to the end.
6:27 He saves and rescues. He does signs and wonders in heaven and on earth. He is the one who saved Daniel from the power of the lions.
6:28 So this Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and during the reign of Cyrus the Persian.
## Four beasts from the sea
7:1 In the first year of Belshazzar, king of Babylon, Daniel had a dream, and the visions of his mind came to him as he lay in bed. He wrote the dream down and told the main points of it.
7:2 Daniel began: "In my vision during the night I kept looking, and there! The four winds of heaven were whipping up the great sea.
7:3 Four great beasts climbed up out of the sea, each one unlike the rest.
7:4 The first looked like a lion, but it had an eagle's wings. I kept watching until its wings were pulled out. Then it was raised from the ground and set upright on two feet like a person, and it was given a human heart.
7:5 Then there was another beast, a second one, that looked like a bear. It was raised on one side, with three ribs in its mouth between its teeth. And they told it: 'Get up! Eat your fill of meat!'
7:6 After this I kept looking, and there was another beast, like a leopard. It had four wings like a bird's on its back and four heads, and rule was given to it.
7:7 After this, in the visions of the night, I watched, and there was a fourth beast, fearful and frightening and enormously strong. Its teeth were of iron, large ones. It devoured and crushed, and it stamped the remainder with its feet. It differed from every beast before it, and it had ten horns.
7:8 I was studying the horns when another horn, a small one, pushed up among them. Three of the first horns were pulled out by their roots in front of it. This horn had eyes like a human's eyes and a mouth that spoke big words.
## The court and the one like a son of man
7:9 "I kept looking / until thrones were placed there, / and the Ancient of Days took his seat. / His clothing was white, like snow, / and the hair of his head was like clean wool. / His throne was flames of fire, / and fire kept burning in its wheels.
7:10 A stream of fire / poured out and came from his presence. / Thousands upon thousands waited on him, / and tens of thousands of tens of thousands were standing before him. / The court sat, / and books were opened.
7:11 Then I looked again, because of the sound of the big words the horn was speaking. I kept looking until the beast was killed, its body wiped out and given to the blazing fire.
7:12 The other beasts lost their rule, but they were allowed to live on for a season and a time.
7:13 "I kept looking in the night visions, / and there, coming with the clouds of heaven, / was one like a son of man. / He came to the Ancient of Days, / and they brought him near before him.
7:14 To him were given rule and honor and a kingdom, / so that he would be served by all peoples, nations and languages. / His rule is a rule that lasts forever and does not pass away, / and his kingdom will never be ruined.
## Daniel asks for the meaning
7:15 "My spirit was troubled, I, Daniel, in the sheath of my body, and the visions in my head alarmed me.
7:16 I went up to one of those standing by and asked him for the true meaning of all this. He answered, and he made the meaning of these things known to me.
7:17 'These great beasts, the four of them, are four kings who will come up from the earth.
7:18 But the holy ones of the Most High will receive the kingdom and hold it forever, for ever and ever.'
## The fourth beast and the little horn
7:19 "Then I wished to be certain about the fourth beast, which differed from all the rest. It was terrible beyond measure, with teeth of iron and claws of bronze; it devoured, shattered, and stamped the remainder with its feet.
7:20 I also asked about the ten horns on its head, and about the other horn that came up and made three fall before it: the horn that had eyes and a mouth speaking big words, and whose appearance was greater than its companions'.
7:21 As I watched, this horn made war on the holy ones and prevailed over them,
7:22 until the Ancient of Days came. The judgment went to the holy ones of the Most High, and the appointed time arrived, and the holy ones took possession of the kingdom.
7:23 "This is what he said: / 'The fourth beast / is a fourth kingdom that will be on the earth, / different from all the kingdoms. / It will consume all the earth, / tread it down and smash it.
7:24 The ten horns: / ten kings will stand up from this kingdom, / and after them another will stand up. / He will be unlike the earlier ones / and will bring three kings low.
7:25 He will speak against the Most High, / wear out the holy ones of the Most High, / and think to change the appointed times and the law. / They will be handed over to his power / for a time, times and half a time.
7:26 Then the court will sit, / and his rule will be removed, / wiped out and destroyed for good.
7:27 The kingdom, the rule / and the greatness of all the kingdoms under all heaven / will be given to the people of the holy ones of the Most High. / His kingdom is a kingdom that lasts forever, / and every power will serve him and obey him.'
7:28 This is where the account ends. As for me, Daniel, my thoughts terrified me, and my color changed, but I guarded the matter in my heart."
