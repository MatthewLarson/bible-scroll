# Hostile review: Daniel 4-7 (part 04-07)

Reviewer: separate agent, not the writer. Checked: all 124 verses of plain.txt against `texts/orig/Dan.json` and `node tools/literal.js Dan 4-7`; the 13 variants against `tools/dss.py` (4Q112, 4Q113, 4Q115) and `tools/lxx.py` (Dan = Swete's Old Greek from Chisianus, Dat = Theodotion); the 97 notes, 52 glosses, 4 chapter pages and 28 topic contributions; the Rashi, Talmud and midrash citations on Sefaria; the card against the Lead decisions in `tools/brief-dan.md`. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Dan/04-07` reports 0 errors and 0 warnings for Dan itself. The "2 errors" it prints are in other books (Ezk 20-24 `galambush-jerusalem`; Psa 138-150 `etcbc-dss` vs Lam) and are not this part's.

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly honest part. The Aramaic is translated faithfully, no number or name is wrong, the Qumran claims are accurate (no restored word is counted; 4Q115's damaged 4:16 is correctly not cited), the Old Greek claims check out against Swete, the Bel and the Dragon variant is labeled with who accepts it, no reader-facing text says "from memory", and Hagigah 14a, Sanhedrin 38b and 98a, Rashi on 7:4, 5, 8, 9, 13, 25 and 4:27 and the Yosippon note at 5:1 all say what the notes say. The problems are these. Chapter 7 has several lines that sit too close to the ESV or NIV, one of them almost word for word (7:10). Four checkable claims are wrong or unsupported: the midrash scheme at 7:17, "Rashi ... before Vespasian" at 7:23, the winged lions on the Processional Way, and the Luther line on the card. One attribution to Collins cannot be verified. The son of man material is nearly neutral but two sentences take a side. Two settings cards contain statements that contradict the text. No finding is critical (no invented source, reading or fragment). Counts: **critical 0, major 11, minor 17**.

## Major

### 1. Drift: Daniel 7:10 is the ESV's sentence (major)
`plain.txt` 7:10: "A thousand thousands served him; ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him." This matches the ESV's clause almost word for word ("a thousand thousands served him ... ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him"); the NIV and KJV share "ten thousand times ten thousand ... stood before him". Literal (`literal.js`): "a thousand of thousands they were ministering to him / and a myriad of myriads before him they were standing." Also in 7:10: "The court took its seat, and the books were opened" has the NIV's cadence ("The court was seated, and the books were opened"); "A river of fire flowed out from before him" keeps the NIV's "river of fire ... coming out from before him". The gloss "ten thousand times ten thousand" and the note anchor repeat the phrase.
Fix: rebuild the verse from the literal line, for example "Thousands upon thousands waited on him, and tens of thousands of tens of thousands stood in his presence" (keep the Rev 5:11 echo noted in the note, but check the build's quote warning). Rework "court took its seat" ("the judges sat down" is already in the gloss; use "Court was held" or "the court sat") and the river line ("A stream of fire poured out from his presence").

### 2. Drift: other chapter-7 phrases that are the NIV's or ESV's own wording (major, as a group)
- 7:2 "the four winds of heaven were churning up the great sea": "churning up" is the NIV's coinage for *megichan* ("were stirring up"; ESV "stirring up"). The anchor, the chapter-7 summary and a gloss ("churning up", 7:2) all repeat it. Use "stirring up" or "whipping".
- 7:9 "its wheels were burning fire": the ESV's last clause, verbatim. "put in place" is the NIV's "set in place". Literal: "wheels its fire burning".
- 7:22 "judgment was given in favor of the holy ones of the Most High ... the time arrived when": this is the NIV's choice for the contested *dina yehav le-* ("in favor of the holy people of the Most High ... the time came when"). The note at 7:22 admits the phrase is debated ("for" or "to") and then follows the NIV's resolution. At least reword ("the verdict went to the holy ones") and say in the note that the NIV and others take the same side.
- 7:24 "As for the ten horns, ... ten kings will rise out of this kingdom, and after them another will rise. He will be unlike the ones before him": the ESV's sequence ("As for the ten horns, out of this kingdom ten kings shall arise, and another shall arise after them; he shall be different from the former ones"). "As for" is not in the Aramaic.
- 7:19-20 follow the NIV's frame (a "wanted to know" clause, "iron teeth and bronze claws, that ... crushed ... trampled ... whatever was left"; "the other horn that came up, in front of which three fell"), 7:23 follows the NIV's "devour the whole earth, trampling it down and crushing it", 7:25 the ESV's "speak words against the Most High". The Aramaic partly forces this order, but 7:19, 7:20 and 7:23 should be recast with new verbs and a changed clause order.
- Outside ch. 7: 5:12 "interpret dreams, explain riddles" (NIV), 6:10 (NIV order: went home, upstairs room, windows opened toward Jerusalem, three times a day, knelt, prayed, gave thanks, as before), 6:22 (NIV order: sent his angel, shut the mouths of the lions, not hurt me, found innocent), 6:17 (NIV: "so that ... situation might not be changed"), 4:30 (ESV/NIV: "built ... as a royal ... by my mighty power ... glory of my majesty"), 5:27 "weighed on the scales" (NIV). These are shorter echoes; recast the verbs where the Aramaic allows.

### 3. 7:13 variant and note: attribution to "Collins" is unverified and probably wrong (major)
`variants.json` 7:13 `b`: "Some scholars see a slip (Greek heos ... hos); others (Collins among them) treat it as a possible rereading by a Jewish translator." A web check turned up Adela Yarbro Collins (with Jeansonne) as holding the single-scribal-slip view, which is the solution the Göttingen edition (Munnich/Ziegler) adopts; Lust and Stuckenbruck argue for a different Vorlage, and Reynolds (Biblica 89, 2008) for a deliberate reading. I could not confirm what John J. Collins's Hermeneia says. This was on the lead's "verify or demand removal" list.
Fix: drop "Collins among them". State only what the sources named show: the Göttingen editors (Ziegler; Munnich in the 2nd ed.) treat it as a slip from *heos*; Lust and Stuckenbruck argue for a different Vorlage or a deliberate rereading; Reynolds argues the Old Greek makes the figure resemble the Ancient of Days. Note also that neither Chisianus nor P967 can be called "other witnesses" without a source: "other witnesses ... reported ... by Collins" should be replaced by "P967 (reported by the editors; not read here)".

### 4. Traditions card, Lutheran row: the Luther line is wrong or evasive (major)
`chapters.json` ch. 7 `traditions`, Lutheran: "Luther's Preface to Daniel reads the book as a prophecy of the course of the empires down to Rome." Secondary sources I found say Luther's Preface to Daniel (1529/1530) takes the little horn of ch. 7 to be Mohammed or the Turk, while keeping the pope as Antichrist elsewhere (Dan 8, 11; the Smalcald Articles). The brief's assumption that Luther and Calvin read the horn as the papacy is therefore not safe either. The sentence as written avoids the point and sits beside a Smalcald quotation that has no tie to Daniel 7, so the reader will think Lutherans read Daniel 7:8 as the pope.
Fix: either quote Luther's preface exactly from LW 35:294-316 (it will show the horn as the Turk, an honest and interesting difference between Reformation readings) or delete the preface sentence and say only that the Smalcald Articles name the pope as Antichrist (II.IV) without a tie to Daniel 7. Check the date: the global source says 1530 "expanded 1541", the secondary sources say 1529. Note in the 7:24 note that the Reformers did not agree on the horn.

### 5. 7:17 and 7:5 notes put the midrashim in the wrong scheme (major)
`notes.json` 7:17: scheme (B) "Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome ... and in the rabbinic midrashim Leviticus Rabbah 13:5 and Genesis Rabbah 2:4". 7:5: "In the scheme Babylon-Media-Persia-Greece the bear is Media (Collins; Rowley; Leviticus Rabbah 13:5)". I read both on Sefaria. Leviticus Rabbah 13:5 reads Daniel 7: lion = Babylon, bear = Media (Rabbi Yohanan's reading of the word), leopard = Greece, fourth = Edom. Genesis Rabbah 2:4: tohu = Babylon, bohu = Media, darkness = Greece, the deep = the wicked kingdom (Edom, later Rome); Daniel is not cited in the body text. Neither has Persia, so neither is scheme A (which needs Persia as the third) nor scheme B (Medo-Persia as one). It is a third, rabbinic scheme: Babylon, Media, Greece, Edom/Rome.
Fix: name the rabbinic scheme as its own line in the 7:17 note, remove Leviticus Rabbah from the Collins/Rowley list in 7:5, and say that Genesis Rabbah 2:4 reads Genesis 1:2, not Daniel 7. This also affects the `four-kingdoms` topic, which should not imply the midrash supports "Medo-Persia".

### 6. 7:23 note: "Rashi names Rome and says that the ten kings reigned before Vespasian" is not in Rashi (major)
Rashi on Daniel 7:24 (Sefaria, Judaica Press) only says the ten horns are the explanation and that the last king "is Titus"; it does not name Rome or Vespasian. What Rashi says about 7:7 I could not see in the pages I fetched; if the claim comes from his 7:7 comment, give the verse and the words. Rashi on 7:4 (Babylon), 7:5 (Persia; ribs as three provinces or three kings Cyrus, Ahasuerus, Darius), 7:6 (Antiochus/Greece, four rulers after Alexander), 7:8 (Titus), 7:9, 7:13 (King Messiah), 7:25 ("an obscure end", holy ones are Israel) are all accurate.
Fix: restate to "Rashi says the last king is Titus (on 7:24)" or cite the exact comment. Also correct chapters.json ch. 7 `J` and the 7:9 note ("Rashi says one is for judging the nations and one for mercy to Israel") to the verified wording: the second throne is for charity or mercy toward Israel; keep.

### 7. 7:4 note: winged lions on the Processional Way (major, factual)
"Winged lions also stand along Babylon's Processional Way (the lion is the animal of the goddess Ishtar)." The lions on the Processional Way are striding, wingless glazed-brick lions; the Ishtar Gate carries bulls and *mushhushshu* dragons. Winged lions are Assyrian and Persian. This is a physical claim the reader will carry away.
Fix: "Striding lions in glazed brick line Babylon's Processional Way (the lion was Ishtar's animal); winged lions are better known from Assyrian and Persian art." Also soften "Jews and Christians have always read" (p field) and "Everyone, ancient and modern, agrees" (b): say "all the standard readings agree".

### 8. Son of man: two sentences take a side (major, neutrality)
- `topics.json` `son-of-man` `common`: "Daniel 7:13 is the Old Testament source of the Gospel title 'the Son of Man', whatever the identity of the figure in Daniel." This is exactly the point the chapter-7 deep note calls "debated (Casey, Dunn, Wright)" (title from Dan 7, from 1 Enoch 46-71, or from the Aramaic idiom). Change to "Many scholars and the Gospels' own usage (Mark 14:62) link the title to Daniel 7:13; Casey argues it is an ordinary idiom."
- `notes.json` 7:13 "'Like a human being', not yet a title" and the topic body "it is a description, not yet a title": the strongest form of the consensus, but stated flatly on a verse where the Christian reading (Mark 14:62, Revelation 1:13) treats it as already the figure. Label it as the scholarly consensus on the Aramaic phrase and say who reads it as a title or a person already (Boyarin; the Christian tradition). The kind `com` is also a question: if `com` means a common misreading, this note makes the Christian reading a misreading.
- Readings listed: Messiah (Rashi, Sanhedrin 98a), angel Michael (Collins), second divine figure (Boyarin), collective (Noth, Casey, Dunn, Wright), Jesus. Missing: Gabriel (named in the brief), and the Jewish medieval reading of the figure as Israel (Ibn Ezra is commonly cited; verify on Sefaria before adding, and Saadia). As listed, the Jewish side is only "Messiah". Item (5) "the Christian tradition" overlaps (1); say so.
- Verified and fine: Rashi on 7:13 ("That is the King Messiah"), Sanhedrin 98a (clouds if worthy, donkey if not), 38b and Hagigah 14a (Akiva: one throne for God and one for David; Yose objects).

### 9. Variants missing: the Old Greek's "on the clouds" and the alms reading (major for the first)
- 7:13: Swete's Old Greek reads *epi ton nepheion* "on the clouds" where Theodotion and the Aramaic have *meta* "with". This is the very difference Matthew 26:64 (*epi*) and Mark 14:62 (*meta*) show, and the entry on 7:13 says nothing about it though it is a one-line add to the existing entry. The note at 7:13 "How the New Testament uses this verse" should say it.
- 4:27: Theodotion (Swete Dat 4:24) and the Old Greek (Swete 4:24) both have *eleemosynais*; the note credits "the Old Greek" and the Vulgate only. Add a variant (Aramaic *tsidqah* "righteousness"; both Greeks "alms"), since the card of readings turns on it, and reword the note.
- Others worth an entry or a sentence in an existing one: the Old Greek of 4:34-37 (long ending) is covered; 7:1 (Old Greek "reigning ... of the land of Babylonia"); 7:18 (Theodotion/Old Greek omit "forever and ever" doubling), and P967's order (chs 7-8 before 5-6) is only in the 7:1 note, as "reported by Collins" which is right but should be in the chapter-4 witness list too.

### 10. Chapter settings: two cards contradict the text (major)
- ch. 6, `v: 6:6-9`: "They hide that Daniel is the target and that the law cannot be undone". The officials themselves tell the king the law cannot be repealed (6:8, again at 6:15). Rewrite: they hide that Daniel is the target and press the king with the law that cannot be undone.
- ch. 6, `v: 6:21-22`: "He makes clear that he had not broken the king's law against the king, only the officials' ban against God." Daniel did break the king's ban; the text has him say "before you also ... I have done no harm". Rewrite: he claims innocence before God and says he has done the king no harm, while not denying that he prayed.
- ch. 7 `readings[0]`: "The critical majority and many conservatives for chapter 8 read the four beasts as Babylon, Media, Persia and Greece." The clause "many conservatives for chapter 8" is nonsense here (it concerns the little horn of ch. 8). Rewrite so the Antiochus reading is held "by the critical majority, and some conservatives for the little horn of chapter 8 (Calvin, Hengstenberg per the brief)".
- Missing settings: 5:13-16 (the king's offer), 5:29-31, 6:14-20 (the king's night). The brief asks a card for every speech.

### 11. Traditions card sourcing (major)
Rows that are not sourced to the church's own text:
- Catholic: "Catholic scholars today commonly teach the historical reading of Antiochus IV" cites only `ccc`, which says nothing of the kind. Source it (for example the New American Bible notes, or Collins as a Catholic scholar) or remove. The Ribera and Bellarmine sentence has no source key either (Bellarmine, De Romano Pontifice 3.2-3 per the brief; add a key).
- Orthodox: sourced only to Hippolytus and Cyril, both fathers. That is not Orthodox church teaching, and "following Hippolytus" is an inference. Say "no Orthodox synod has ruled; the fathers read ..." and cite an Orthodox text (the Orthodox Study Bible notes or a liturgical or patristic reception) or flag as unsourced.
- Baptist: "Many Baptists today read the horn as a future Antichrist" has no source.
- Reformed: the Westminster 25.6 text and the 1788 American revision (Synod of May 1788; it dropped the Antichrist clause and kept the denial that the pope is head) check out. The Savoy Declaration 1658 (the brief names it) is missing from the card and from Congregational voices.
- Missing from the brief: the Council of Moscow (1666-67) and the debate on depicting the Ancient of Days as the Father (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant iconography); the brief makes it optional "if verified", so state in the progress file that it was left out.

## Minor

12. **Leviticus Rabbah / Genesis Rabbah / Sanhedrin 38b names.** Chapter 7 `J` and the 7:9 note call the objector "Rabbi Yose the Galilean" for both Hagigah 14a and Sanhedrin 38b. Hagigah 14a has "Yose HaGelili"; Sanhedrin 38b has "Rabbi Yose" only. Reword ("Hagigah 14a names him Yose the Galilean").
13. **Josephus on 4:16.** The 4:16 variant (`s: josephus-ant`) and note say "Josephus also gives (years)". I could not confirm Antiquities 10.10.6 from a text. Write "reported by" a named source or check the Loeb text and give the section.
14. **Collins on P967.** The 7:1 note says "Papyrus 967 ... as Collins reports, puts chapters 7-8 before 5-6". The order is standard (1-4, 7-8, 5-6, 9-12, then Susanna and Bel) and can stay, but the note's last clause ("so some Greek readers also saw the order as an issue") is a guess. Cut or label as inference.
15. **Qumran dates.** Chapter 4 witness list: "The editors (Ulrich and others in DJD 16) date the copying to the second or first century BC." The brief says to report editors' dates only as "reported by Ulrich/Flint". Rewrite "reported by Ulrich (DJD 16)" or give the numbers from DJD. Also: the global `djd16` source key lists only Proverbs to Qoheleth scrolls, not the Daniel scrolls; the Daniel scrolls are in DJD 16 too (add 4QDan-a to -e in the definition). The `swete-lxx` global definition says "Codex Vaticanus", but the variants use Chisianus for the Old Greek of Daniel; correct the definition (a shared file, so propose it).
16. **5:12 variant `orig`.** The reading line "... יקרא ופשרה יהחוה" suggests 4Q113 preserves "and its interpretation he will declare" as well. In the transcription 4Q113 preserves only *yqr'* (its preceding word is partly restored) and 4Q112 preserves *ufshrh yhhwh* but has *wktb' yqr'* restored. Say which scroll has which word.
17. **4:2 note.** "Daniel 4 and Daniel 6 are the only places in the Bible where a foreign king's letter praises Israel's God in this form" is overstated (Cyrus's decree, Ezra 1:2-4; Artaxerxes, Ezra 7:12-26; the 2 Chronicles 36:23 version). Change to "the only royal letters that tell of a rescue or a humbling by Israel's God".
18. **4:26 note.** "Daniel 4:26 is the only place in the Bible where 'Heaven' alone is a way of saying God" and then cites Luke 15:18, 21 and 1 Maccabees. Say "in the Hebrew Bible".
19. **5:12 note** says "the king and the queen both also call him by his Hebrew name (5:13)". Only the king does so at 5:13; the queen says "Daniel" at 5:12. Also 4:4: Psalm 73:12 uses a different word (*shalev*) from Aramaic *shelah*; 4:35's "Jeremiah 48:40" (an eagle for Moab) is not about Babylon by name; 6:10 note: *modeh* is the Hebrew form, the verb in 6:11 is *moda*.
20. **5:31 note.** Whitcomb distinguishes Ugbaru (the general who took the city and died) from Gubaru (governor), and identifies Darius with Gubaru; the note writes "Gubaru, or Ugbaru" as one man. Add the distinction (Beaulieu and others take them as one).
21. **Term consistency.** *ruach yatira* is "remarkable spirit" in 5:12 and "outstanding spirit" in 6:3; use one.
22. **Glosses.** 7:27 "God's faithful people" decides the holy-ones question that the 7:18 gloss ("God's holy people or angels?") and the note leave open; use the same two-way gloss. 4:37 "bring down a peg" is slang and not a gloss of "humble". 7:25 "probably years" conflicts with the ch. 4 insistence that *iddan* is not stated; say "three and a half periods (see the note)". 7:9 "God, pictured as a very old and wise judge" is fine but should not close the Old Greek question.
23. **Style.** 7:6 "four bird's wings" (should be "four wings like a bird's"); 7:15 "My own spirit, Daniel's, was distressed" is awkward ("My spirit was troubled, I, Daniel, ..." in the Aramaic order); 7:21 "was beating them" weakens *yakhelah* "prevailed over them"; 5:28 "split in two" adds "in two" (Aramaic *peres* is "divided"); 5:2 "With the taste of the wine on him" is unclear for *bi-ta'am chamra* ("under the influence of the wine" in the gloss is better as the Plain English Translation).
24. **Prayer of Nabonidus.** The 4:33 note says "a Jewish exorcist"; `works.json` says "a Jewish diviner". Match the works entry and check the copy date it gives (about 75-50 BC) if the note quotes one.
25. **Chapter 4 `F` view.** The Jehovah's Witnesses count (607 BC to AD 1914) and the Adventist/historicist "2,520 years" have no source key. Add `jw-insight` or similar from the existing key list, or label as "reported by".
26. **Chapter 7 `X`.** "Many Christian scholars who date the book late still read it as pointing to Christ" has no name. Name one or cut.
27. **Topics.** `persia`: "which fits the book's scheme in which a Median kingdom comes between Babylon and Persia" states the critical reading as fact; add "on the critical reading". `ancient-of-days`: the Canaanite *ab shnm* parallel is "likely source" in the note; say "proposed by Emerton, Day and others". `throne` says "justice and mercy (Rashi)"; the Talmud's pairing is "judgment and righteousness (charity)"; Rashi's is "judgment of the nations and charity for Israel".
28. **Bel and the Dragon variant.** Accurate and properly labeled, with the work named "Additions to Daniel" (`works.json` `additions-to-daniel`, tier dc). Two points: (a) the Plain English Translation field holds a 140-word summary, so the reader sees a block that looks like Plain English Translation text; keep it but move the "in summary" prefix into the `w` label; (b) the first reading's `w` ("Theodotion and the Old Greek give Bel and the Dragon at the end") should add that in P967 Bel and Susanna follow ch. 12, as reported by the editors; the brief asks for both positions to be given.
