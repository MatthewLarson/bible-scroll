## Cyrus Sends the Exiles Home
1:1 In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, to fulfill the LORD's word from Jeremiah's mouth, the LORD roused the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia. He sent a proclamation through his whole kingdom, and in writing as well:
1:2 "Cyrus king of Persia says this: All the kingdoms of the earth the LORD, the God of the heavens, has given me. He has appointed me to build him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.
1:3 Who among you is from all his people? May his God be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of the LORD, the God of Israel. He is the God who is in Jerusalem.
1:4 And everyone who is left, in any of the places where he lives as a stranger, the people of his place are to help him with silver, gold, goods and livestock, along with a freewill offering for God's house in Jerusalem."
1:5 Then the heads of families of Judah and Benjamin rose up, with the priests and the Levites: all those whose spirit God had roused to go up and build the LORD's house in Jerusalem.
1:6 All the people around them backed them with things made of silver, with gold, with goods, with livestock and with costly gifts, besides everything that was given freely.
## The Temple Vessels Given Back
1:7 King Cyrus also took out the vessels belonging to the LORD's house, the ones Nebuchadnezzar had carried off from Jerusalem and put in his gods' house.
1:8 Cyrus king of Persia brought them out through Mithredath the treasurer, and Mithredath counted them over to Sheshbazzar, the leader of Judah.
1:9 This was their count: 30 gold basins, 1,000 silver basins, 29 knives,
1:10 30 gold bowls, 410 silver bowls of a second kind, and 1,000 other vessels.
1:11 The vessels of gold and silver came to 5,400 in all. Sheshbazzar took all of them with him as the exile was brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem.

## Families Without Proof
2:59 These are the ones who came up from Tel-melah, Tel-harsha, Cherub, Addan and Immer. They could not tell which fathers' house they came from or what their line was, to show whether they were from Israel:

## The Whole Assembly
2:64 Taken together, the whole assembly was 42,360,

2:69 Each as he was able, they put into the treasury for the work 61,000 drachmas of gold, 5,000 minas of silver and 100 robes for priests.
2:70 The priests, the Levites, some of the people, the singers, the gatekeepers and the temple servants made their homes in their towns. All Israel lived in their towns.
## The Altar Rebuilt
3:1 The seventh month arrived. The Israelites were in their towns, and the people gathered at Jerusalem, all together as one.
3:2 Then Jeshua son of Jozadak rose up with his brothers the priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel with his brothers. They built the altar of Israel's God, so that they could offer burnt offerings on it, following what is written in the law of Moses the man of God.
3:3 They set the altar firmly on its old base, because dread of the peoples of the lands was on them. On it they offered the LORD burnt offerings, those of morning and evening.
3:4 They held the Feast of Booths just as it is written. They offered burnt offerings every day, the number set by the rule for each day.
3:5 After this they offered the regular burnt offering, and the offerings for the new moons and for all the LORD's holy set feasts, and those of everyone who brought the LORD a freewill offering.
3:6 Starting on the first day of the seventh month, they made burnt offerings to the LORD, even though the LORD's temple had not yet been founded.
3:7 They gave money to the stonecutters and the woodworkers, and food, drink and oil to the people of Sidon and Tyre, to bring cedar wood from Lebanon to the sea at Joppa, by the permission Cyrus king of Persia had given them.
## The Foundation Laid
3:8 In the second year after they came to God's house in Jerusalem, in the second month, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, Jeshua son of Jozadak and the rest of their brothers made a start, with the priests, the Levites and all who had come from the captivity to Jerusalem. They appointed the Levites twenty years old and up to oversee the work on the LORD's house.
3:9 Jeshua, with his sons and his brothers, and Kadmiel and his sons, the sons of Judah, stood up together to oversee those doing the work on God's house. So did the sons of Henadad, with their sons and their brothers, the Levites.
3:10 When the builders founded the LORD's temple, they stationed the priests, dressed in their robes, with trumpets, and the Levites of Asaph's family with cymbals, to give the LORD praise as David king of Israel had arranged.
3:11 They sang in answer to one another, praising and thanking the LORD: "For he is good, for his loyal love over Israel is forever." All the people raised a great shout as they praised the LORD, because the LORD's house had been founded.
3:12 Many of the priests, the Levites and the heads of families were old enough to have seen the first house. When this house was founded before their eyes, they wept out loud. Many others raised their voices in shouts of joy.
3:13 No one could tell the glad shouting apart from the weeping of the people, because the people shouted so loudly that the noise carried far away.
## Builders Turned Away
4:1 Judah and Benjamin's enemies heard that the people of the exile were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel.
4:2 So they came to Zerubbabel and to the heads of families and said to them, "Let us build with you. Like you, we seek your God. We have offered sacrifices to him from the time of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us up here."
4:3 But Zerubbabel, Jeshua and the rest of the heads of families of Israel told them, "It is not for you and us to build a house for our God. We ourselves, together, will build it for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, the king of Persia, commanded us."
4:4 From then on the people of the land worked to break the courage of the people of Judah. They frightened them away from building.
4:5 They paid advisers to work against them and spoil their plans, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia and on until the reign of Darius king of Persia.
## Letters of Accusation
4:6 In the reign of Ahasuerus, at the start of his reign, they put an accusation in writing against those living in Judah and Jerusalem.
4:7 And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel and the rest of his colleagues wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The writing of the letter was in Aramaic script, and it was translated into Aramaic.
4:8 Rehum the chief official and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter to King Artaxerxes against Jerusalem. It went like this.
4:9 Then: Rehum the chief official, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their colleagues, the judges, envoys and officials, the Persians, the men of Erech, of Babylon and of Susa, that is, the Elamites,
4:10 and the other peoples whom Osnappar, the great and honored, took into exile and settled in the city of Samaria and the rest of Beyond the River. And now:
4:11 Here is a copy of the letter they sent him. "To Artaxerxes the king, from your servants, the men of Beyond the River. And now:
4:12 Let the king know this: the Jews who came up to us from you have come to Jerusalem. They are building the rebellious and evil city. They have finished the walls and are repairing the foundations.
4:13 Now let the king know that if this city is built and its walls are finished, no tribute, tax or duty will be paid, and in the end it will do harm to the kings.
4:14 Now, because we eat the palace's salt, and it is not right for us to see the king put to shame, we are sending word to inform the king,
4:15 so that a search may be made in your fathers' record book. You will find out from the record book that this city is a city that rebels and does harm to kings and provinces. Revolt has been made inside it from days long past. That is the reason this city was destroyed.
4:16 We are informing the king that if this city is built and its walls are finished, then as a result you will have no share left in Beyond the River."
## The King Stops the Work
4:17 The king sent back word: "To Rehum the chief official, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their colleagues who live in Samaria and the rest of Beyond the River: Peace. And now:
4:18 The letter you sent us has been read out in my presence and explained.
4:19 I gave an order, and a search was made. They found that this city has risen up against kings since long ago, and that rebellion and revolt have been carried on in it.
4:20 There were mighty kings over Jerusalem who ruled all Beyond the River, and tribute, tax and duty were paid to them.
4:21 So now give an order to stop these men, so that this city is not built until I give the order.
4:22 Take care not to be careless in this. Why should harm grow, to the injury of kings?"
4:23 The moment the copy of the letter from Artaxerxes the king was read aloud to Rehum, Shimshai the scribe and their colleagues, they hurried to Jerusalem, to the Jews, and made them stop with force and might.
4:24 Then the work on God's house in Jerusalem stopped. It stayed stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.
## The Prophets Stir the Builders
5:1 Now the prophets, Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo, brought prophecies to the Jews living in Judah and Jerusalem, in the name of Israel's God, who was over them.
5:2 Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak got up and began to build God's house in Jerusalem. God's prophets were with them, helping them.
5:3 At that time Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, and Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues came to them and said, "Who issued you an order to build this house and complete these fittings?"
5:4 Then we said to them, "What are the names of the men who are building this building?"
5:5 But their God kept his eye on the elders of the Jews. No one stopped them while a report went to Darius and until a letter about it came back.
## Tattenai Writes to Darius
5:6 Here is a copy of the letter sent to Darius the king by Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and his colleagues, the officials in Beyond the River.
5:7 The report they sent him had this written in it: "To Darius the king: all peace.
5:8 Let the king know that we went into the province of Judah, to the great God's house. Its walls are going up with great blocks of stone, and beams are being set into them. This work is being done with care and is going well in their hands.
5:9 So we questioned those elders. We asked them, 'Who issued you an order to build this house and complete these fittings?'
5:10 We asked for their names too, so that we could let you know and write down the names of the men who lead them.
5:11 This is the answer they gave us: 'We are servants of the God of the heavens and the earth. We are building the house that stood built for many years before this time. A king of Israel, a great one, built it and completed it.
5:12 But because our ancestors angered the God of the heavens, he handed them over to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean. He tore down this house and carried the people away to Babylon.
5:13 Yet in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, King Cyrus gave an order to build this house of God.
5:14 King Cyrus also took out of the temple in Babylon the gold and silver vessels of God's house, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to the temple in Babylon. They were given to Sheshbazzar, as he was called, the man he had made governor.
5:15 He said to him: Take these vessels and go. Store them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let God's house be built on its site.
5:16 So this Sheshbazzar came. He laid the foundations of God's house in Jerusalem, and from then until now it has been under construction. It is still not finished.'
5:17 Now, if the king thinks it good, let a search be made in the king's treasure house there in Babylon, whether an order was given by King Cyrus to build this house of God in Jerusalem. Then let the king send us his wishes on this matter."
## Cyrus's Scroll Found
6:1 Then King Darius gave an order, and they searched in the house of records, where the treasures were stored there in Babylon.
6:2 In Ecbatana, the fortress in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and this was written in it: "For the record:
6:3 In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus gave an order about God's house in Jerusalem: The house is to be built as a place where sacrifices are offered, and its foundations are to be kept. It is to be sixty cubits high and sixty cubits wide,
6:4 with three courses of large stones and one course of new timber. The cost is to be given out of the king's house.
6:5 Also, let the gold and silver vessels of God's house, which Nebuchadnezzar took from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon, be given back. Let them go back to the temple in Jerusalem, each to its place, and you are to set them in God's house."
## Darius Orders the Work Done
6:6 "Now, Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues, the officials in Beyond the River: keep away from there.
6:7 Do not interfere with the work on this house of God. The Jews' governor and the Jews' elders are to build this house of God on its site.
6:8 I also issue an order on how you are to act toward these Jewish elders in building this house of God. Out of the king's funds, from the tribute of Beyond the River, the expenses are to be given to these men with care, so that they are not held up.
6:9 Whatever they need, young bulls, rams and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of the heavens, wheat, salt, wine and oil, as the priests in Jerusalem ask, must be provided to them each day, with no lapse,
6:10 so that they may offer sacrifices of soothing smell to the God of the heavens and pray for the life of the king and of his sons.
6:11 I also give an order: if anyone changes this decree, a beam is to be pulled from his house, and he is to be lifted up and impaled on it. His house is to be made a dunghill for this.
6:12 May the God who has made his name live there bring down every king and nation that reaches out a hand to alter it or wreck this house of God in Jerusalem. I, Darius, give the order. Let it be done with care."
## The House Finished
6:13 Then Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues did with all care just as King Darius had sent word.
6:14 The elders of the Jews went on building and doing well, as Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied. They built and finished it by the order of the God of Israel and by the order of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia.
6:15 This house was completed on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.
6:16 The Israelites, the priests, the Levites and the rest of the people of the exile joyfully held the dedication of this house of God.
6:17 For the dedication of this house of God they offered 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and 12 male goats as a sin offering for all Israel, matching the number of Israel's tribes.
6:18 They installed the priests by their divisions and the Levites by their groups, to serve God in Jerusalem, according to what is written in the book of Moses.
## Passover Kept
6:19 The people of the exile kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.
6:20 The priests and the Levites had made themselves pure together, so that every one of them was clean. They slaughtered the Passover for all the people of the exile, for their brothers the priests, and for themselves.
6:21 The Israelites who had come back from the exile ate it, along with everyone who had separated himself to them from the uncleanness of the nations of the land, to seek the LORD, the God of Israel.
6:22 With joy they kept the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days. For the LORD had made them glad and had turned the Assyrian king's heart toward them, to give their hands strength for the work on the house of God, Israel's God.
