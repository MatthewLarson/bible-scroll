## In praise of Eleazar
7:1 Our father Eleazar's reason was like a superb pilot. It steered the ship of godliness across the sea of the passions.
7:2 The tyrant's threats battered it, and the towering waves of the tortures swept over it,
7:3 but in no way did it turn the tiller of godliness aside, until he sailed into the harbor of victory over death.
7:4 A city under siege, attacked by many kinds of siege engines, never held out the way that all-holy man did. They burned his holy soul with torments and racks, yet he shook his besiegers, through the reason that stood as a shield over godliness.
7:5 Father Eleazar set his mind out like a sheer headland jutting into the sea, and he broke the raging surf of the passions around it.
7:6 O priest worthy of the priesthood, you did not defile your holy teeth. Your stomach held reverence for God and purity, and you did not give it a share in the eating of defiling food.
7:7 O you who lived in tune with the law, philosopher of a divine life!
7:8 That is what they must be like, those who work at the law with their own blood and shield it with noble sweat in sufferings that go to the point of death.
7:9 You, father, by your endurance made our keeping of the law firm and glorious. You praised holiness in solemn words, and you did not tear it down. By your deeds you made the words of philosophy believable.
7:10 O old man stronger than the tortures! O elder tougher than the fire! O greatest king over the passions, Eleazar!
7:11 Our father Aaron took up the censer like a weapon, ran through the packed crowd of the people, and defeated the angel who was burning them.
7:12 In the same way Eleazar, Aaron's descendant, was melted down by the fire, yet his reason was not turned.
7:13 And here is the most wonderful thing. He was an old man. The strength of his body was already slack, his flesh hung loose, and his sinews were worn out. Yet he became young again
7:14 in the spirit of his reason, and with a reason like Isaac's he made the many-headed rack powerless.
7:15 O blessed old age! O revered gray head! O life lived by the law, brought to its finish by the faithful seal of death!
## Godly reason is the ruler
7:16 So if an old man looked down on tortures that ended in death, for the sake of godliness, then everyone must grant that godly reason is the leader of the passions.
7:17 Some might say, perhaps, that not everyone rules the passions, because not everyone has a sensible reason.
7:18 But only those who care for godliness with all their heart can rule the passions of the flesh.
7:19 They are the ones who believe that they do not die to God. Like our patriarchs Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, they live to God.
7:20 So it is no argument against this that some people seem to be ruled by their passions. The cause is that their reason is weak.
7:21 Think of someone who lives in a godly way by the whole rule of philosophy and has put his trust in God,
7:22 and who knows that to endure any hardship for the sake of virtue is a blessed thing. How could such a person fail to rule the passions through godliness?
7:23 For only the wise and self-restrained man is the brave lord over the passions.
7:24 That is why even young boys, living as philosophers by the reason of godliness, have overcome still harsher instruments of torture.
## Seven brothers before the tyrant
8:1 The tyrant had been beaten for all to see at his first try: he could not force an old man to eat defiling food.
8:2 So, very much in the grip of passion, he ordered others from the young Hebrews to be brought. If they would eat defiling food, they were to be let go after eating. If they refused, they were to be tortured more harshly.
8:3 When the tyrant had given these orders, seven brothers were brought in, together with their aged mother. They were handsome, modest, well-born and graceful in every way.
8:4 The tyrant saw them standing around their mother, who was in the middle, as in a chorus. He was taken with them. Struck by their good looks and their noble bearing, he smiled at them, called them closer and said,
8:5 "Young men, I feel kindly toward you, and I admire the good looks of each one of you. I think highly of so large a band of brothers. So I not only advise you not to rage with the same madness as the old man tortured before you;
8:6 I also urge you to give in to me and enjoy my friendship. For I can punish those who disobey my orders, and I can just as well do good to those who obey me.
8:7 So trust me, and you will be given high posts over my affairs, if you turn your backs on the ancestral rule of your way of life as a people.
8:8 Take part in Greek life, change your diet, and enjoy yourselves while you are young.
8:9 But if your disobedience makes me angry, you will force me to put every one of you to death with terrible punishments, through the tortures.
8:10 So take pity on yourselves. Even I, your enemy, feel sorry for you, for your youth and your good looks.
8:11 Won't you think this over? If you disobey, nothing is waiting for you but to die on the rack."
8:12 As he said this, he ordered the instruments of torture to be set out in front of them, to persuade them through fear as well to eat defiling food.
8:13 The guards set out wheels and joint-wrenchers, racks and bone-crushers, catapults and kettles, griddles and thumbscrews, iron hands and wedges, and bellows for the fire. Then the tyrant spoke up again:
8:14 "Lads, be afraid. The Justice you worship will be merciful to you if you break the law because you were forced."
8:15 But they heard his enticements and saw the terrible things, and not only were they not afraid; they even argued philosophy back at the tyrant, and by their sound reasoning they brought his tyranny down.
## What cowards would have said
8:16 And yet let us think it through. If some of them had been cowardly and unmanly, what words would they have used? Would it not be these?
8:17 "How miserable we are, and how very foolish! The king is calling on us and inviting us to his kindness. Should we not obey him?
8:18 Why do we cheer ourselves with empty wishes and dare to disobey when disobeying means death?
8:19 Brothers, shouldn't we be afraid of the instruments of torture? Shouldn't we think about the tortures he threatens, and run from this empty vanity, this boasting that will destroy us?
8:20 Let us pity our own youth, and feel for our mother's old age.
8:21 And let us take to heart that if we disobey we will die.
8:22 Divine Justice too will forgive us if we fear the king because we have to.
8:23 Why are we taking ourselves out of this sweetest life and robbing ourselves of this sweet world?
8:24 Let us not fight against necessity, and not seek empty glory from our own torture.
8:25 The law itself would not want to put us to death because we were afraid of the instruments of torture.
8:26 Where does such a love of quarreling come from, that has sunk so deep into us? Why does a steadfastness that kills please us, when we could live untroubled by obeying the king?"
8:27 But the young men about to be tortured said none of these things. They did not even think them.
8:28 For they looked down on the passions and were sovereign over their pains.
8:29 So as soon as the tyrant had finished urging them to eat defiling food, all of them together, with one voice, as though from one soul, said,
## The brothers' answer
9:1 "Why are you waiting, tyrant? We are ready to die sooner than break the commandments of our fathers.
9:2 We would rightly be ashamed before our ancestors if we did not take obedience to the law and knowledge as our counselor.
9:3 Tyrant, counselor of lawbreaking, do not hate us and then show us more pity than we show ourselves.
9:4 For we think your pity, which would save us by breaking the law, is harder to bear than death itself.
9:5 You want to scare us with threats of death under torture. Did you not learn your lesson from Eleazar just a little while ago?
9:6 If the old men of the Hebrews endured tortures for godliness and died, it would be even more right for us young men to die. We look down on the tortures you force on us, which our old teacher also defeated.
9:7 So go ahead and test us, tyrant. If you take our lives for godliness, do not imagine that your torture does us harm.
9:8 For through this hardship and endurance we will carry off the prizes of virtue.
9:9 But you, for your foul murder of us, will have to hold out under an eternal torture by fire from divine Justice, the full penalty you have earned."
## The first brother
9:10 When they had said this, the tyrant was bitter with them, as one is with the disobedient, and furious too, as one is with the ungrateful.
9:11 So the floggers, under orders, led out the oldest. They tore his tunic apart and tied his hands and arms with straps, one on either side.
9:12 They whipped him until they were tired out and got nowhere. Then they threw him up onto the wheel.
9:13 Stretched around it, the noble young man had his joints pulled out.
9:14 As every limb was being broken, he accused the tyrant, saying,
9:15 "Foulest tyrant, enemy of heavenly Justice, savage-minded! You are not abusing me like this because I murdered anyone or because I was ungodly, but because I defend the divine law."
9:16 The guards said, "Say you will eat, and you will be rid of the tortures."
9:17 But he said, "Your methods are not strong enough, you defiled servants, to break my reason. Cut off my limbs, burn my flesh, twist my joints.
9:18 I will prove to you through every one of these tortures that the children of the Hebrews, and they alone, cannot be conquered when virtue is at stake."
9:19 As they said this, they spread more fire under him, and, stirring up the wheel, they stretched him on it even tighter.
9:20 The wheel was stained with blood all over. The heap of coals was put out by the drops of fluid from his body, and his flesh ran around the axles of the machine.
9:21 His bony frame was already melting apart, yet the great-hearted young man, of Abraham's stock, did not groan.
9:22 Instead, as though the fire were changing him into incorruption, he endured the racking nobly.
9:23 "Imitate me, brothers," he said. "Never desert me, like soldiers leaving their post. Do not swear off our brotherhood of courage. Fight a holy and noble campaign for godliness.
9:24 Through it may the just providence of our fathers become merciful to the nation and punish this accursed tyrant."
9:25 When he had said this, the saintly young man broke off his life.
## The second brother
9:26 Everyone marveled at his steadfast soul. Then the guards brought the one next in age after the first. They put on iron hands with sharp claws and bound him to the instruments and the catapult.
9:27 They asked him whether he would eat before he was tortured, and they heard a noble resolve.
9:28 Then, starting from the tendons, these leopard-like beasts dragged off with the iron hands all the flesh as far as his chin, and the skin of his head. He held out under the crushing pain and said,
9:29 "How sweet is every kind of death for the godliness of our fathers!" And he said to the tyrant,
9:30 "Most savage of all tyrants, don't you think you are the one under worse torture right now? You are watching the proud reason of your tyranny lose to our endurance for godliness.
9:31 For the pleasures that virtue brings make my pain lighter.
9:32 But you are tortured by the threats of your ungodliness. And you will not escape the punishments of God's anger, most foul tyrant."
## The third brother
10:1 After the second had held out through his glorious death, they brought the third. Crowds of people kept begging him to taste it and be saved.
10:2 But he cried out, "Don't you know that I come from the same father's seed as those who died? The same mother bore me, and I was raised on the same teachings.
10:3 I do not swear off the noble kinship of my brothers.
10:4 So if you have any instrument of punishment, bring it to my body. You will not touch my soul, not even if you want to."
10:5 His bold words made them furious. They took joint-wrenching tools and put his hands and feet out of joint. They levered his limbs out of their sockets and pulled him apart.
10:6 They wrenched around his fingers and arms, his legs and his elbows.
10:7 Since they could not choke him in any way, they peeled off his skin, fingertips and all, and stripped off his scalp the way the Scythians do. Then they took him straight to the wheel.
10:8 On it his spine was pulled apart, bone by bone, and he saw his own flesh ripped to shreds around him and drops of blood streaming from his insides.
10:9 As he was about to die, he said,
10:10 "We suffer these things, most foul tyrant, because of the training and the virtue that come from God.
10:11 But you, for your ungodliness and your bloodshed, will hold out under tortures that never end."
## The fourth brother
10:12 When he too had died as a brother should, they dragged in the fourth, saying,
10:13 "Do not go mad with the same madness as your brothers. Obey the king and save yourself."
10:14 But he said to them, "Your fire is not so much fiercer against me that I will turn coward.
10:15 By the blessed death of my brothers, by the eternal ruin of the tyrant, and by the glorious life of the godly, I will not deny our noble brotherhood.
10:16 Dream up tortures, tyrant. Through them you will find out that I am the brother of the men you tortured before me."
10:17 Hearing this, Antiochus, the bloodthirsty, the murderer, the all-defiled, ordered his tongue to be cut out.
10:18 But he said, "Even if you take away the organ of my voice, God hears the silent too.
10:19 Look, my tongue is hanging out. Cut it. Doing this will not cut out our reason's tongue.
10:20 We are glad to lose the limbs of our body for God.
10:21 But God will soon come after you, for you are cutting out a tongue that sang hymns to God."
## The fifth brother
11:1 When this one too died, mangled by the tortures, the fifth leaped forward, saying,
11:2 "I am not going to beg off, tyrant, from torture for the sake of virtue.
11:3 I came forward on my own. Kill me too, and you will owe heavenly Justice the punishment for still more crimes.
11:4 You hater of virtue, hater of humankind, what have we done, that you ravage us like this?
11:5 Or does it seem wrong to you that we are godly toward the Creator of all and live by his law that teaches virtue?
11:6 These are things that call for honors, not for tortures,
11:7 if you had any feeling and cared for people, and if you had hope of salvation from God.
11:8 But see: as it is, you are a stranger to God, and you make war on those who are godly toward God."
11:9 He was still speaking like this when the guards bound him and hauled him to the catapult.
11:10 They bound him to it on his knees and fastened his knees down with iron clamps. Then they bent his back down over the wheel's wedge. Around it he was bent all the way back over the wheel like a scorpion, and he was pulled limb from limb.
11:11 So, his breath squeezed and his body choking, he said,
11:12 "Fine favors, tyrant, fine favors you give us against your will, letting us show by nobler sufferings our steadfastness toward our law."
## The sixth brother
11:13 When this one too had died, the sixth was brought in, a young lad. The tyrant asked him whether he wanted to eat and be let go, and he said,
11:14 "In age I am younger than my brothers, but in mind I am as old as they are.
11:15 For we were born and raised for the same things, and we must also die for the same things alike.
11:16 So if you think it right to torture those who will not eat defiling food, torture me."
11:17 Once he had said this, they took him over to the wheel.
11:18 They stretched him out on it with care and pulled his back apart, and he was burned from below.
11:19 They heated sharp spits red-hot and put them to his back. They pierced his sides and burned his insides through.
11:20 While he was being tortured he said, "O saintly age, in which so many brothers have been called to a training ground of pain for godliness, and we have not been beaten!
11:21 For godly understanding, tyrant, cannot be conquered.
11:22 Armed with a noble goodness, I too will die alongside my brothers,
11:23 and I too will bring a great avenger down on you, you inventor of new tortures, enemy of the truly godly.
11:24 We six boys have brought down your tyranny.
11:25 You could not talk our reason around, and you could not force us into eating defiling food. Is that not your defeat?
11:26 Your fire is cold to us, your catapults cause no pain, and your force has no power.
11:27 For the guards who stand over us serve not a tyrant but the divine law. That is why our reason cannot be conquered."
## The youngest brother
12:1 This one too was thrown into a kettle and died a blessed death. Then the seventh came up, the youngest of them all.
12:2 The tyrant felt sorry for him, though the boy's brothers had reviled him terribly.
12:3 Seeing him already in chains, he had him brought closer and tried to talk him round, saying,
12:4 "You see how your brothers' folly ended. They were racked to death for their disobedience. If you do not obey, you too will be tortured, poor wretch, and die before your time.
12:5 But if you obey, you will be my Friend, and you will run the affairs of the kingdom."
12:6 As he urged this, he sent for the boy's mother. He meant to show her pity, robbed as she was of so many sons, and so to spur her to make the one left alive obedient, for his own safety.
12:7 But his mother spurred him on in the Hebrew tongue, as we will tell a little later,
12:8 and he said, "Untie me. Let me speak to the king and all his Friends with him."
12:9 They were overjoyed at the boy's promise and quickly untied him.
12:10 He ran up close to the griddles and said,
12:11 "Unholy tyrant, most ungodly of all the wicked! You received your good things and your kingdom from God. Were you not ashamed to kill his servants and to rack those who train themselves in godliness?
12:12 For this, divine Justice is storing you up for a fiercer and eternal fire and for tortures that will not let you go for all eternity.
12:13 Were you not ashamed? You are human, you savage beast, and yet you cut out the tongues of people who share your feelings and were made from the same elements, and you abused and tortured them like this.
12:14 They died nobly and so fulfilled their godliness toward God.
12:15 But you will wail miserably for killing the champions of virtue for no reason."
12:16 So he too, about to die, said,
12:17 "I will not desert my brothers' witness.
12:18 I call on the God of my fathers to be merciful to my people.
12:19 But you he will punish, both in this present life and when you are dead."
12:20 When he had prayed this, he threw himself down onto the griddles, and so he gave back his life.
