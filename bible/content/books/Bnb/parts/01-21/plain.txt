## Greetings
1:1 Greetings, sons and daughters, in the name of the Lord who loved us, in peace.
## Joy over their gift
1:2 God's decrees toward you are great and rich. So I am glad beyond measure, more than I can say, over your blessed and glorious spirits. That is how deeply planted in you is the grace of the spiritual gift you have received.
1:3 That is why I congratulate myself all the more, and hope to be saved. For I truly see among you the Spirit poured out on you from the Lord's rich spring. Seeing you, as I had longed to, left me amazed.
1:4 So I am sure of this, and I know it in myself: since I spoke among you, I understand a great deal, because the Lord has traveled with me on the way of righteousness. And I too am fully driven to this: to love you more than my own life. For great faith and love live in you, in the hope of his life.
1:5 So I worked it out. If I take care to share with you some part of what I received, serving such spirits as yours will bring me a reward. So I have hurried to send you a small part of it, so that along with your faith you may have perfect knowledge.
## Three teachings of the Lord
1:6 There are three teachings of the Lord. The hope of life is the beginning and end of our faith. Righteousness is the beginning and end of judgment. Love, shown in gladness and joy, is the testimony of the works of righteousness.
1:7 For the Master has made known to us, through the prophets, what is past and what is present. He has also given us a first taste of what is to come. And as we see these things coming true one by one, just as he said, we ought to bring a richer and a higher offering in the fear of him.
1:8 But I will point out a few things, not as a teacher, but as one of you. They will make you glad in these present times.
## Evil days
2:1 The days are evil, and the one who is at work has the power himself. So we ought to watch ourselves and search out the Lord's decrees.
2:2 Fear and endurance are the helpers of our faith. Patience and self-control fight on our side.
2:3 While these stay pure in what concerns the Lord, wisdom, understanding, insight and knowledge rejoice with them.
## Not sacrifices
2:4 For he has made it plain to us through all the prophets that he needs no sacrifices, no burnt offerings and no offerings. In one place he says:
2:5 "What do I want with all your sacrifices?" says the Lord. "I am full of burnt offerings. I do not want the fat of lambs or the blood of bulls and goats, not even when you come to appear before me. Who asked you to bring these things in your hands? You will not walk on my courtyard again. If you bring fine flour, it is useless. Incense is disgusting to me. Your new moons and your Sabbaths I cannot stand."
2:6 So he did away with these things, so that the new law of our Lord Jesus Christ, which has no yoke of force, would have an offering not made by human hands.
2:7 And again he says to them: "Did I command your fathers, when they came out of the land of Egypt, to bring me burnt offerings and sacrifices?
2:8 No, this is what I commanded them: None of you is to keep a grudge in his heart against his neighbor, and do not love a false oath."
## A crushed heart
2:9 So, since we are not without understanding, we ought to grasp the kind purpose of our Father. He is speaking to us. He wants us not to go astray as they did, but to seek how we may bring our offering to him.
2:10 So this is what he says to us: "A sacrifice to the Lord is a crushed heart. A fragrant smell to the Lord is a heart that gives glory to the one who formed it." So, brothers, we ought to be careful and exact about our salvation, so that the evil one does not slip error in among us and sling us away from our life.
## Not fasting
3:1 So again he says to them about these things: "Why do you fast for me," says the Lord, "so that today your voice is heard in shouting? This is not the fast I chose," says the Lord, "not a man humbling his soul.
3:2 Even if you bend your neck like a ring, put on sackcloth and spread ashes under you, not even then will you call it a fast I accept."
3:3 But to us he says: "Look, this is the fast I chose," says the Lord. "Loosen every bond of wrongdoing. Untie the knots of contracts forced on people. Set the crushed free, and tear up every unjust contract. Break your bread for the hungry. If you see someone naked, clothe him. Bring the homeless into your house. If you see someone lowly, do not look down on him, nor on anyone of your own flesh and blood.
3:4 Then your light will break out like the dawn, and your clothes will quickly spring up. Righteousness will go ahead of you, and the glory of God will wrap around you.
3:5 Then you will cry out, and God will hear you. While you are still speaking, he will say, 'Look, I am here,' if you take away from among you the bond, the pointing hand and the word of grumbling, and if you give your bread to the hungry with all your heart and show mercy to a soul brought low."
## Shown to us beforehand
3:6 So, brothers, the patient one saw beforehand that the people he prepared in his Beloved would believe in purity. And so he made everything plain to us beforehand, so that we would not be shipwrecked by joining their law like newcomers.
## The complete stumbling block
4:1 So we must look closely into the present times and search out what is able to save us. Let us run completely away from all the works of lawlessness, so that the works of lawlessness do not overtake us. And let us hate the error of the present time, so that we may be loved in the time to come.
4:2 Let us not give our souls free rein to run with sinners and wicked people, or we will become like them.
4:3 The complete stumbling block has come near, the one about which it is written, as Enoch says. For this is why the Master has cut short the times and the days: so that his Beloved may hurry and come into his inheritance.
4:4 And the prophet also says this: "Ten kingdoms will reign on the earth. And after that a small king will rise up, who will bring down three of the kings at one stroke."
4:5 Daniel says the same about the same one: "And I saw the fourth beast, evil and strong and fiercer than all the beasts of the sea. Ten horns sprang up from it, and from them a little horn, an offshoot. And it brought down three of the great horns at one stroke."
## Whose is the covenant?
4:6 So you ought to understand. And I ask you this too, as one of you, who loves each one and all of you more than my own life: pay attention to yourselves now. Do not be like some people, who pile up sins by saying, "The covenant is theirs and ours."
4:7 Yes, it is ours. Those people, though, lost it once and for all in the following way, when Moses had only just received it. For the Scripture says: "And Moses was on the mountain fasting forty days and forty nights. And he received the covenant from the Lord, tablets of stone written by the finger of the Lord's hand."
4:8 But they turned to idols and lost it. For this is what the Lord says: "Moses, Moses, go down quickly, because your people, whom you brought out of the land of Egypt, have acted lawlessly." And Moses understood, and he threw the two tablets out of his hands. And their covenant was smashed, so that the covenant of Jesus the Beloved might be sealed into our hearts in the hope that faith in him gives.
## Stand firm in the last days
4:9 I want to write many things, not as a teacher, but as fits someone who loves you and will not hold back anything we have. So I have hurried to write, I who am the lowliest of your servants. So let us pay attention in the last days. For the whole time of our faith will do us no good unless now, in the lawless time and among the stumbling blocks to come, we stand firm, as fits sons of God, so that the Black One may not slip in.
4:10 Let us run from everything empty. Let us hate completely the works of the evil way. Do not slip away and keep to yourselves, as though you were already made right with God. Come together in one place instead, and search out together what is good for everyone.
4:11 For the Scripture says: "Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and clever in their own sight." Let us become spiritual. Let us become a perfect temple for God. As far as it is up to us, let us think deeply on the fear of God and strive to keep his commandments, so that we may find joy in his decrees.
4:12 The Lord will judge the world without favoritism. Each one will receive what matches what he has done. If he is good, his righteousness will go ahead of him. If he is evil, the wages of evil will be in front of him.
4:13 So let us never relax as people who are already called and doze off in our sins. Otherwise the evil ruler will take power over us and push us out of the Lord's kingdom.
4:14 And consider this too, my brothers. You see that after such great signs and wonders were done in Israel, they were still abandoned. So let us pay attention, so that we are not found to be, as it is written, "many called, but few chosen."
## Why the Lord suffered in the flesh
5:1 For this is why the Lord endured handing over his flesh to destruction: so that we would be made clean by the forgiveness of sins, which comes by the blood of his sprinkling.
5:2 For some things written about him concern Israel, and some concern us. It says this: "He was wounded for our lawless deeds and made weak for our sins. By his welts we were healed. Like a sheep he was taken off to slaughter, and like a lamb that makes no sound in front of the one who shears it."
5:3 So we ought to give the Lord thanks beyond measure. He has made known to us what is past, and made us wise in what is present, and we are not without understanding about what is to come.
5:4 And the Scripture says: "Not unjustly are nets spread for birds." This means that a person deserves to perish who has the knowledge of the way of righteousness, and yet keeps himself on the way of darkness.
5:5 And there is this too, my brothers. The Lord endured suffering for our lives, though he is Lord of the whole world, the one to whom God said at the founding of the world, "Let us make humankind in our image and likeness." How, then, did he endure suffering at the hands of human beings?
5:6 Learn this. The prophets had their gift of grace from him, and they prophesied about him. And because he had to be revealed in the flesh, he endured it, to put an end to death and to show the resurrection from the dead.
5:7 He did it to keep the promise made to the fathers, and, while he was on earth and preparing the new people for himself, to show that he himself will raise the dead and then judge.
5:8 What is more, he taught Israel and did such great wonders and signs, and he preached to them and loved them greatly.
5:9 And when he chose his own apostles, who were to preach his good news, they were lawless beyond every kind of sin. He chose them to show that he did not come to call righteous people, but sinners. Then he revealed that he was the Son of God.
5:10 For if he had not come in the flesh, people could never have been saved by looking at him. For when they look at the sun, which will one day cease to be and is the work of his hands, they cannot look straight into its rays.
5:11 So the Son of God came in the flesh for this purpose: to bring to its full total the sins of those who had persecuted his prophets to death.
## Struck by his own
5:12 So this is why he endured it. For God says that the wound in his flesh came from them: "When they strike their own shepherd, then the sheep of the flock will be lost."
5:13 But he chose to suffer in this way. For he had to suffer on a tree. For the one who prophesies about him says: "Spare my life from the sword," and, "Nail my flesh, because gangs of evildoers have risen up against me."
5:14 And again he says: "Look, I have given my back to the whips and my cheeks to blows. And I have set my face like solid rock."
## The stone
6:1 So when he gave the commandment, what does he say? "Who wants to take me to court? Let him stand against me. Or who wants to win his case against me? Let him come near the Lord's servant.
6:2 Woe to you! For all of you will wear out like a garment, and the moth will eat you up." And again, since he was set like a strong stone for crushing, the prophet says: "Look, I will lay in the foundations of Zion a stone of great price, chosen, a cornerstone, held in honor."
6:3 Then what does he say? "And whoever hopes in him will live forever." Is our hope, then, in a stone? Of course not! He says this because the Lord set his flesh in strength. For he says, "And he set me like solid rock."
6:4 And again the prophet says: "A stone that the builders threw out has become the head of the corner." And again he says: "This is the great and wonderful day that the Lord has made."
6:5 I am writing to you very simply so that you will understand. I am the lowliest servant of your love.
6:6 So what does the prophet say again? "A gang of evildoers surrounded me. They circled around me like bees around a honeycomb," and, "For my clothing they cast lots."
6:7 So because he was going to be revealed in the flesh and to suffer, his suffering was revealed beforehand. For the prophet says about Israel: "Woe to their soul! For they have made an evil plan against themselves. They said, 'Let us tie up the righteous man, because he is a nuisance to us.'"
## The good land
6:8 What does the other prophet, Moses, say to them? "Look, this is what the Lord God says: Go into the good land that the Lord promised with an oath to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and take it as your inheritance, a land flowing with milk and honey."
6:9 But what does knowledge say? Learn this. "Hope," it says, "in Jesus, who is going to be revealed to you in the flesh." For a human being is earth that suffers. For Adam was formed from the face of the earth.
6:10 So what does he mean by "into the good land, a land flowing with milk and honey"? Our Lord be blessed, brothers! He has placed in us wisdom and insight into his secrets. For the prophet speaks a parable of the Lord. Who will understand it, except someone wise and knowing who loves his Lord?
## A new creation
6:11 So since he made us new by the forgiveness of sins, he made us into another type of person, so that we have the souls of children, as though he were forming us all over again.
6:12 For the Scripture is speaking about us when it says to the Son: "Let us make humankind in our image and likeness. And let them rule over the wild animals of the earth, the birds of the sky and the fish of the sea." And when the Lord saw how well we were formed, he said: "Grow and multiply and fill the earth." These words were spoken to the Son.
6:13 Again I will show you how he speaks to us. In the last times he made a second creation. And the Lord says: "Look, I make the last things like the first." That is why the prophet announced: "Go into a land flowing with milk and honey, and rule over it."
6:14 So you see, we have been formed anew, as he says again in another prophet: "Look," says the Lord, "I will take out of them (that is, out of those whom the Spirit of the Lord foresaw) their hearts of stone, and I will put in hearts of flesh." For he was going to be revealed in the flesh and to live in us.
6:15 For the dwelling place of our heart, my brothers, is a holy temple for the Lord.
6:16 For the Lord says again: "And how shall I appear before the Lord my God and be glorified?" He says: "I will praise you in the assembly of my brothers, and I will sing to you in the middle of the assembly of the saints." So we are the ones he has brought into the good land.
## Milk and honey
6:17 What, then, do the milk and the honey mean? A baby is first given life by honey, and then by milk. So in the same way we too, given life by faith in the promise and by the word, will live and rule over the earth.
6:18 We already said above: "And let them grow and multiply and rule over the fish." Who, then, is able to rule over animals or fish or the birds of the sky now? For we ought to see that ruling takes authority, so that the one who gives commands is master.
6:19 So if this is not happening now, he has told us when it will: when we ourselves are made perfect and become heirs of the Lord's covenant.
## The fast and the vinegar
7:1 So understand, children of gladness: the good Lord showed us everything beforehand, so that we may know whom we ought to praise and thank for everything.
7:2 So the Son of God, who is Lord and is going to judge the living and the dead, suffered so that his wound would give us life. Then let us believe that the Son of God could not have suffered except for our sake.
7:3 And besides, when he was crucified, he was given vinegar and gall to drink. Listen to how the priests of the temple showed this beforehand. The Lord gave a written commandment: "Whoever does not fast the fast will be cut off by death." He commanded this because he himself was going to offer the vessel of his spirit as a sacrifice for our sins, so that the figure set up in Isaac, who was offered on the altar, would be fulfilled.
7:4 So what does he say in the prophet? "And let them eat of the goat that is offered at the fast for all the sins." Pay close attention: "And let the priests alone, all of them, eat the gut unwashed, with vinegar."
7:5 Why? "Since you are going to give me gall mixed with vinegar to drink, when I am about to offer my flesh for the sins of my new people, you alone are to eat, while the people fast and mourn in sackcloth and ashes." This was to show that he had to suffer at their hands.
## The two goats
7:6 Pay attention to what he commanded: "Take two goats, fine ones and alike, and offer them. And let the priest take one of them as a burnt offering for sins."
7:7 But what are they to do with the other one? "The other one," he says, "is cursed." Pay attention to how the figure of Jesus is revealed.
7:8 "And all of you, spit on it and stab it, and wrap the scarlet wool around its head, and in this way let it be driven into the wilderness." And when this is done, the man who handles the goat leads it into the wilderness. He takes off the wool and puts it on a bush called the rachel. We are used to eating its shoots when we find them in the countryside. So the fruit of the rachel alone is sweet.
7:9 What, then, does this mean? Pay attention. "One goes on the altar, and the other is cursed." And why is the cursed one crowned? Because on that day they will see him wearing the long scarlet robe around his flesh. And they will say, "Isn't this the one we once crucified, when we despised him, stabbed him and spat on him? Truly this was the man who said then that he was the Son of God."
7:10 How is he like that goat? This is why the goats are alike, fine and equal: so that when they see him coming then, they will be astonished at how much he is like the goat. So you see here the figure of Jesus, who was going to suffer.
## The wool among the thorns
7:11 And why do they put the wool in the middle of the thorns? It is a figure of Jesus set up for the church. Whoever wants to take up the scarlet wool must suffer many things, because the thorn is fearsome, and only through hardship can he get hold of it. "So," he says, "those who want to see me and lay hold of my kingdom must receive me through hardship and suffering."
## The heifer
8:1 And what do you think this is a figure of? Israel was commanded that men whose sins are complete should offer a heifer, slaughter it and burn it. Then children were to gather up the ashes, put them in jars, and tie the scarlet wool and the hyssop around a piece of wood (see again the figure of the cross, and the scarlet wool). And so the children were to sprinkle the people one by one, so that they would be made clean from their sins.
8:2 Understand how simply he speaks to you. The calf is Jesus. The sinful men who offer it are the ones who brought him to be slaughtered. After that, there are no more such men, and no more glory for sinners.
8:3 The children who sprinkle are those who brought us the good news of the forgiveness of sins and the cleansing of the heart. He gave them authority over the good news so that they would preach it. (There are twelve of them as a witness to the tribes, because Israel has twelve tribes.)
8:4 And why are there three children who sprinkle? As a witness to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, because they are great in God's sight.
8:5 And why is the wool on the tree? Because the kingdom of Jesus is on the tree, and because those who hope in him will live forever.
8:6 And why are the wool and the hyssop together? Because in his kingdom there will be evil and filthy days, and in them we will be saved. For whoever has pain in his flesh is healed through the grime of the hyssop.
8:7 And so the things that happened in this way are clear to us, but dark to them, because they did not listen to the Lord's voice.
## The circumcision of the ears and heart
9:1 For again he speaks about the ears, and how he circumcised our heart. The Lord says in the prophet: "As soon as their ears heard, they obeyed me." And again he says: "Those far away will hear clearly, and they will know what I have done." And: "Circumcise your hearts," says the Lord.
9:2 And again he says: "Hear, Israel, for this is what the Lord your God says." And again the Spirit of the Lord prophesies: "Who is the one who wants to live forever? Let him listen closely to the voice of my servant."
9:3 And again he says: "Hear, you heavens! Listen, earth! For the Lord has spoken these things as a witness." And again he says: "Hear the Lord's word, you rulers of this people." And again he says: "Hear, children, the voice of one shouting in the wilderness." So he circumcised our hearing, so that we would hear the word and believe.
9:4 But the circumcision they have trusted in has been done away with. For he said that circumcision was not to be done in the flesh. But they went against it, because an evil angel was tricking them.
9:5 He says to them: "This is what the Lord your God says" (here I find a commandment): "Do not sow among thorns. Be circumcised for your Lord." And what does he say? "Circumcise your hard hearts, and do not harden your neck." Take this again: "Look," says the Lord, "all the nations are uncircumcised in their foreskin, but this people is uncircumcised in heart."
## Others who circumcise
9:6 But you will say, "Surely the people were circumcised as a seal!" But every Syrian and Arab is circumcised too, and all the priests of idols. So do they also belong to their covenant? Why, even the Egyptians practice circumcision!
## The three hundred and eighteen
9:7 So learn richly about everything, children of love. Abraham, who first gave circumcision, looked ahead in the Spirit to Jesus when he circumcised. He received the teachings of three letters.
9:8 For it says: "And Abraham circumcised eighteen and three hundred men of his household." What, then, was the knowledge given to him? Learn this. He names the eighteen first, then leaves a space and says three hundred. In eighteen, I is ten and H is eight: there you have Jesus. And because the cross, in the shape of T, was going to bring grace, he also says three hundred. So he shows Jesus in the two letters, and the cross in the one.
9:9 He knows this, the one who put in us the gift of his teaching, planted deep within. No one has learned a truer lesson from me. But I know that you are worthy of it.
## The food laws
10:1 Now when Moses said, "You must not eat a pig, or an eagle, or a hawk, or a crow, or any fish that has no scales on it," he received three teachings in his understanding.
10:2 What is more, he says to them in Deuteronomy: "And I will set out my decrees before this people as a covenant." So God's commandment is not about not eating. Moses spoke in the Spirit.
10:3 So this is why he spoke of the pig. "You must not be joined," he says, "to people who are like pigs." That is, when they live in luxury, they forget the Lord. But when they are in need, they recognize the Lord. It is just like the pig: when it eats, it does not know its master, but when it is hungry, it squeals, and once it is fed, it goes quiet again.
10:4 "Nor are you to eat the eagle, or the hawk, or the kite, or the crow." "You must not be joined," he says, "or become like such people." They do not know how to get their food by work and sweat. Instead, in their lawlessness they seize what belongs to others. They watch closely, walking about as if in purity, and look around for someone to strip bare in their greed. These birds are just like that. They alone do not get their own food, but sit idle and look for how they can devour the flesh of others. They are a plague in their wickedness.
10:5 "And you are not to eat," he says, "the moray eel, or the octopus, or the cuttlefish." "You must not," he says, "cling to such people and become like them." They are ungodly to the end and already condemned to death. Just so, these fish alone are cursed. They swim in the deep water, not swimming freely like the others, but living on the ground at the bottom of the deep.
## The hare, the hyena and the weasel
10:6 "But you are not to eat the hare either." Why? "You must not," he says, "become one who abuses boys, or become like such people." For the hare adds to its rear opening every year. It has as many holes as the years it has lived.
10:7 "But you are not to eat the hyena either." "You must not," he says, "become an adulterer or a seducer, or become like such people." Why? Because this animal changes its nature every year: at one time it becomes male, and at another female.
10:8 And he was right to hate the weasel too. "You must not," he says, "become like the people we hear of who do lawless things with the mouth because of their impurity. And you must not be joined to impure women who do lawless things with the mouth." For this animal conceives through its mouth.
## What Moses meant
10:9 So Moses received three teachings about foods and spoke in this way in the Spirit. But they took them, as the flesh desires, as if they were about eating.
10:10 And David receives knowledge of the same three teachings, and says: "Happy is the person who does not walk by the advice of the wicked," as the fish move in darkness down in the depths, "does not stand on the road of sinners," as those who seem to fear the Lord sin like the pig, "and does not sit in the seat of pests," as the birds sit waiting for prey. Now you have the whole teaching about food as well.
10:11 Again Moses says: "Eat every animal that has a split hoof and chews the cud." What does he mean? The animal that takes its food knows the one who feeds it, and it rests in him and seems glad. He spoke well, looking at the commandment. What, then, does he mean? Cling to those who fear the Lord, to those who think deeply in their hearts on the special meaning of the word they received, to those who speak and keep the Lord's decrees, to those who know that thinking deeply on it is a work of gladness, and who chew the cud of the Lord's word. And what does the split hoof mean? That the righteous person walks in this world and also waits for the holy age. See how well Moses made laws!
10:12 But how could they understand or grasp these things? We, though, have understood the commandments rightly, and we speak of them as the Lord wanted. That is why he circumcised our hearing and our hearts, so that we would grasp these things.
## The water
11:1 Now let us look into whether the Lord took care to reveal beforehand something about the water and about the cross. About the water, it is written of Israel that they would not accept the baptism that brings forgiveness of sins, but would build something for themselves.
11:2 For the prophet says: "Be astonished, sky, and let the earth shudder even more at this. For this people has done two evil things. They have abandoned me, the spring of life, and dug for themselves a pit of death.
11:3 Is my holy mountain Sinai a deserted rock? For you will be like the young of a bird, fluttering about when their nest is taken away."
11:4 And again the prophet says: "I will go ahead of you and level the mountains. I will smash gates of bronze and break bars of iron. And I will give you treasures that are dark, hidden and unseen, so that they may know that I am the Lord God."
11:5 And: "You will live in a high cave of strong rock." And: "His water will not fail. You will see a king in glory, and your soul will think deeply on the fear of the Lord."
11:6 And again he says in another prophet: "And the one who does these things will be like a tree set out by channels of water, that will give its fruit in its season. Its leaf will not fall off, and all that he does will turn out well.
11:7 Not so the wicked, not so! They are like the dust that the wind blows off the face of the earth. That is why the wicked will not rise up in the judgment, nor sinners in the council of the righteous. For the Lord knows the road of the righteous, but the road of the wicked will be lost."
## Water and cross together
11:8 Notice how he described the water and the cross together. For this is what he means: Happy are those who put their hope in the cross and went down into the water. For he speaks of the reward "in its season": "Then," he says, "I will pay it." But what he says now, "its leaf will not fall off," means this: every word that comes out of your mouth in faith and love will turn many to God and give them hope.
11:9 And again another prophet says: "And the land of Jacob was praised above every land." This means that he gives glory to the vessel of his Spirit.
11:10 Then what does he say? "And there was a river flowing on the right, and beautiful trees grew up out of it. And whoever eats from them will live forever."
11:11 This means that we go down into the water loaded with sins and filth, and come up bearing fruit in the heart, with fear, and with hope in Jesus in the Spirit. "And whoever eats from them will live forever" means this: "Whoever hears these things spoken," he says, "and believes them, will live forever."
## The cross
12:1 In the same way, he describes the cross again in another prophet, who says: "And when will these things be fulfilled?" The Lord says: "When a tree is bent down and stands up again, and when blood drips from a tree." There again you have something about the cross, and about the one who was going to be crucified.
12:2 And again he speaks to Moses, when Israel was under attack by foreign peoples. He wanted to remind them, while they were under attack, that they had been handed over to death because of their sins. So the Spirit speaks into the heart of Moses, telling him to make a figure of the cross and of the one who was going to suffer. For, he says, unless they hope in him, war will be made on them forever. So Moses piles up weapons one on another in the middle of the battle. He stands higher than all of them and stretches out his hands, and so Israel began winning again. Then, whenever he lowered them, they were being killed.
12:3 Why? So that they would know that they cannot be saved unless they hope in him.
12:4 And again he says in another prophet: "All day long I have spread out my hands to a disobedient people, who speak against my righteous way."
## The bronze snake
12:5 Again Moses makes a figure of Jesus: that he had to suffer, and that the one they would think they had destroyed will himself give life. He did this in a sign, while Israel was falling. (For the Lord made every kind of snake bite them, and they were dying, since the transgression came about through the snake in Eve.) He did it to convince them that because of their transgression they will be handed over to the distress of death.
12:6 What is more, Moses himself had commanded, "You are not to have any cast or carved image as a god." Yet he himself makes one, to show a figure of Jesus. So Moses makes a bronze snake and sets it up in honor, and he calls the people with a public announcement.
12:7 So they came together and begged Moses to offer a prayer for them, that they would be healed. And Moses said to them, "Whenever one of you is bitten, let him come to the snake that lies on the tree. Let him believe and hope that, though it is dead, it can give life. And he will be saved at once." And that is what they did. Here again you have the glory of Jesus: all things are in him and for him.
## The name Jesus
12:8 Again, what does Moses say to Jesus son of Nun, when he gave him this name, since he was a prophet? He did it so that all the people would listen to him alone. For the Father reveals everything about his Son Jesus.
12:9 So Moses says to Jesus son of Nun, after he gave him this name, when he sent him to spy out the land: "Take a book in your hands and write what the Lord says: that in the last days the Son of God will cut off the whole house of Amalek by the roots."
## Son of David?
12:10 See again: it is Jesus, not a son of man but the Son of God, revealed in a figure and in the flesh. So since they are going to say that Christ is David's son, David himself prophesies, because he fears and understands the error of sinners: "The Lord said to my Lord, 'Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies down as a footstool for your feet.'"
12:11 And again Isaiah says this: "The Lord said to my Christ, the Lord, whose right hand I have taken hold of, that nations would obey him, and I will break apart the strength of kings." See how David calls him Lord, and does not call him son.
## Which people is the heir?
13:1 But let us see whether this people is the heir, or the first one, and whether the covenant is for us or for them.
13:2 So hear what the Scripture says about the people: "Isaac prayed for Rebekah his wife, because she could not have children. And she became pregnant. Then Rebekah went out to ask the Lord, and the Lord said to her: 'Two nations are in your belly, and two peoples in your womb. One people will rise above the other, and the greater will serve the lesser.'"
13:3 You ought to see who Isaac is, and who Rebekah is, and of whom he has shown that this people is greater than that one.
## Ephraim and Manasseh
13:4 And in another prophecy Jacob says it more plainly to his son Joseph: "Look, the Lord has not kept me from seeing your face. Bring me your sons, so that I may bless them."
13:5 So Joseph brought Ephraim and Manasseh. He wanted Manasseh to be blessed, because he was the older; that is why Joseph brought him to the right hand of his father Jacob. But Jacob saw in the Spirit a figure of the people to come. And what does it say? "And Jacob crossed his hands and put his right hand on the head of Ephraim, the second and younger son, and blessed him. And Joseph said to Jacob, 'Move your right hand onto Manasseh's head, because he is my firstborn son.' And Jacob said to Joseph, 'I know, my child, I know. But the greater will serve the lesser. And this one will be blessed too.'"
13:6 See of whom he has decided that this people is first, and the heir of the covenant.
## Abraham
13:7 If it was also called to mind through Abraham, then our knowledge is complete. So what does he say to Abraham, when he alone believed and was set down as righteous? "Look, Abraham, I have made you the father of the nations who believe in God while uncircumcised."
## The covenant broken and given
14:1 Yes. But let us see whether he has given the covenant that he swore to the fathers he would give to the people. He has given it. But they were not worthy to receive it, because of their sins.
14:2 For the prophet says: "And Moses was fasting on Mount Sinai for forty days and forty nights, to receive the Lord's covenant for the people." And Moses received from the Lord the two tablets, written in the Spirit by the finger of the Lord's hand. And when Moses had received them, he was carrying them down to give them to the people.
14:3 And the Lord said to Moses: "Moses, Moses, go down quickly, because your people, whom you brought out of the land of Egypt, have acted lawlessly." And Moses understood that they had again made themselves cast idols. And he threw the tablets out of his hands, and the tablets of the Lord's covenant were smashed.
14:4 Moses received it, but they were not worthy. Now learn how we received it. Moses received it as an attendant, but the Lord himself gave it to us, to be the people of his inheritance, by enduring suffering for our sake.
14:5 He was revealed so that they would reach the full measure of their sins, and so that we would receive the covenant through the Lord Jesus, who inherits it. He was prepared for this: to appear and ransom from the darkness our hearts, which were already wasted away by death and handed over to the lawlessness of error, and so by his word to set up the covenant in us.
## Ransomed from darkness
14:6 For it is written how the Father commands him to ransom us from the darkness and prepare a holy people for himself.
14:7 So the prophet says: "I, the Lord your God, have called you in righteousness. I will hold your hand and give you strength. I have made you a covenant for the human race and a light for the nations, to open the eyes of the blind, to bring prisoners out of their chains, and out of the prison house those who sit in darkness." So we know where we were ransomed from.
14:8 Again the prophet says: "Look, I have set you as a light for the nations, so that you may be salvation to the farthest end of the earth. This is what the Lord says, the God who ransomed you."
14:9 And again the prophet says: "The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he anointed me to tell the good news of grace to the lowly. He has sent me to heal those whose hearts are crushed, to proclaim release to the captives and sight to the blind, to announce the year of the Lord's favor and the day of repayment, and to comfort all who mourn."
## The Sabbath
15:1 And further, it is also written about the Sabbath in the Ten Words, which he spoke to Moses face to face on Mount Sinai: "And keep the Lord's Sabbath holy, with clean hands and a clean heart."
15:2 And in another place he says: "If my sons keep the Sabbath, then I will put my mercy on them."
15:3 He speaks of the Sabbath at the beginning of creation: "And God made the works of his hands in six days, and he finished on the seventh day, and rested on it, and made it holy."
## Six thousand years
15:4 Pay attention, children, to what "he finished in six days" means. It means that in six thousand years the Lord will bring everything to its end. For in his reckoning a day stands for a thousand years. He himself is my witness when he says, "Look, the Lord's day will be like a thousand years." So, children, in six days, that is, in six thousand years, everything will be brought to its end.
15:5 "And he rested on the seventh day." This means: when his Son comes, he will put an end to the time of the lawless one. He will judge the ungodly and change the sun, the moon and the stars. Then he will truly rest on the seventh day.
## The true rest
15:6 What is more, he says: "You are to keep it holy with clean hands and a clean heart." So if anyone could now keep holy the day that God made holy, by being clean in heart, then we have been deceived in everything.
15:7 See, then: we will truly rest and keep it holy at that time, when we ourselves are able to, once we have been made right with God and have received the promise, when lawlessness is no more and the Lord has made all things new. Then we will be able to keep it holy, because we ourselves will have been made holy first.
## The eighth day
15:8 What is more, he says to them: "Your new moons and your Sabbaths I cannot stand." Do you see what he means? "It is not the Sabbaths of the present time that I accept, but the one I have made. On it I will bring everything to rest, and make the beginning of an eighth day, which is the beginning of another world."
15:9 That is why we also keep the eighth day with gladness. It is the day on which Jesus rose from the dead, and after he had been revealed, went up into heaven.
## The temple
16:1 I will also tell you about the temple. Those miserable people wandered off and hoped in the building itself, as if it were God's house, and not in their God who made them.
16:2 For they dedicated him in the temple almost the way the nations do. But learn how the Lord speaks when he does away with it: "Who measured heaven with a span, or the earth with a handful? Was it not I?" says the Lord. "Heaven is a throne for me, and the earth is a stool for my feet. What sort of house will you build me, or what place could be my place of rest?" You know that their hope is empty.
## Those who tore it down
16:3 What is more, he says again: "Look, those who tore this temple down will build it themselves."
16:4 It is happening. For because they went to war, it was torn down by their enemies. Now the very servants of their enemies will build it up again.
16:5 Again, it was revealed beforehand that the city, the temple and the people of Israel were going to be handed over. For the Scripture says: "And it will happen in the last days that the Lord will hand over the sheep of the pasture, and their fold and their tower, to destruction." And it happened just as the Lord had said.
## The temple in the heart
16:6 But let us look into whether there is a temple of God. There is: where he himself says he makes it and completes it. For it is written: "And it will happen that when the week is completed, a temple of God will be built in glory in the name of the Lord."
16:7 So I find that there is a temple. Now learn how it will be built in the name of the Lord. Before we believed in God, the dwelling place of our heart was decaying and weak, truly like a temple built by hand. For it was full of idolatry and was a house of demons, because we did whatever was opposed to God.
16:8 "But it will be built in the name of the Lord." So pay attention, so that the Lord's temple may be built in glory. Learn how. When we received the forgiveness of sins and put our hope in the name, we became new, created all over again from the beginning. So God truly lives in our dwelling place, in us.
16:9 How? His word of faith, his call of promise, the wisdom of his decrees, the commandments of the teaching: he himself prophesies in us, he himself lives in us. We had been slaves to death, but he opens for us the door of the temple, which is the mouth, and gives us repentance. In this way he leads us into the temple that does not decay.
16:10 For whoever longs to be saved looks not at the man, but at the one who lives and speaks in him. He is amazed at him, because he has never heard such words from a speaker's mouth, and never even wished to hear them. This is a spiritual temple being built for the Lord.
## The end of the first part
17:1 As far as it was possible to explain things to you simply, my soul hopes that, in my eagerness, I have not left out anything that has to do with salvation.
17:2 For if I write to you about things present or to come, you will not understand, because they lie hidden in parables. So much for these things.
## The two ways
18:1 Now let us move on to another knowledge and teaching. There are two ways of teaching and of power, one of light and one of darkness. And there is a great difference between the two ways. For over the one are stationed light-bringing angels of God, and over the other, angels of Satan.
18:2 And the one is Lord from eternity and to eternity, while the other is the ruler of the present time of lawlessness.
## The way of light
19:1 So this is the way of light. If anyone wants to travel the road to the appointed place, let him hurry there by his works. So the knowledge given to us for walking in this way is as follows.
19:2 You are to love the one who made you. You are to fear the one who formed you. You are to give glory to the one who ransomed you from death. You are to be simple in heart and rich in spirit. You must not stick close to those who walk in the way of death. You are to hate everything that does not please God. You are to hate every kind of hypocrisy. You must never abandon the Lord's commandments.
19:3 You must not lift yourself up, but you are to be humble in everything. You must not take glory for yourself. You must not plot evil against your neighbor. You must not let your soul grow brash.
19:4 You must not commit sexual sin. You must not commit adultery. You must not sexually abuse boys. The word of God must never go out from you among people who are impure. When you correct someone for a wrong, you must not play favorites. You are to be gentle. You are to be quiet. You are to tremble at the words you have heard. You must not keep remembering wrongs your brother has done you.
19:5 You must not be double-minded about whether it will happen or not. You must not take up the name of the Lord for something empty. You are to love your neighbor more than yourself. You must not murder a child by abortion, and you must not kill one that has been born either. You must not take your hand off your son or your daughter. Instead, from their youth you are to teach them to fear God.
19:6 You must not become someone who craves what belongs to your neighbor. You must not become greedy. You must not stick close with all your soul to the high and mighty. Instead, you are to live among the lowly and the righteous. You are to accept whatever happens to you as good, knowing that without God nothing happens.
## Slaves and masters, sharing and speech
19:7 You must not be two-minded or a big talker. You are to submit to masters as to a figure of God, with modesty and fear. You must not give orders in bitterness to your slaves, men or women, who hope in the same God, or they may stop fearing the God who is over both of you. For he did not come to call people by their standing, but those whom the Spirit has made ready.
19:8 You are to share everything with your neighbor, and you must not say that anything is your own. For if you are partners in what never decays, how much more in the things that decay? You must not be quick to speak, for the mouth is a death trap. As far as you can, you are to keep yourself pure for your soul's sake.
19:9 Do not be someone who holds out his hands to take, but pulls them back when it is time to give. You are to love, like the pupil of your eye, everyone who speaks the Lord's word to you.
## Remember the day of judgment
19:10 Night and day you are to remember the day of judgment. Every day you are to seek out the faces of the saints. Either you are to work hard with words, going out to encourage people and taking care to save a soul by the word, or you are to work with your hands as a ransom for your sins.
19:11 You must not hesitate to give, and you must not grumble when you give. But you will find out who is the good one who pays back your wages. You are to keep the things you received, without adding or taking away anything. You are to hate evil to the very end. You are to judge fairly.
19:12 You must not cause a split. Instead, you are to bring together people who are fighting and make peace between them. You are to confess your sins. You must not come to prayer with a bad conscience. This is the way of light.
## The way of the Black One
20:1 But the way of the Black One is crooked and full of cursing. For it is the way of eternal death with punishment. In it are the things that destroy people's souls: idol worship, brashness, the haughtiness of power, hypocrisy, a double heart, adultery, murder, robbery, pride, transgression, deceit, malice, stubbornness, sorcery, magic, greed, no fear of God.
20:2 They persecute good people. They hate truth and love lies. They do not know the wages of righteousness. They do not hold tight to what is good, or to just judgment. They pay no attention to the widow and the orphan. They lie awake, not in the fear of God, but for evil. Gentleness and endurance are far, far away from them. They love worthless things and chase after rewards. They show no mercy to the poor, and they do not wear themselves out for the one who is worn down. They are quick to slander. They do not know the one who made them. They are murderers of children and destroyers of what God has formed. They turn away the person in need and wear down the one who is in trouble. They are advocates for the rich and lawless judges of the poor, sinners through and through.
## Walk in the decrees
21:1 So it is good to learn the Lord's decrees, all that are written, and to walk in them. For whoever does these things will be given glory in the kingdom of God. Whoever chooses the other things will perish together with his works. That is why there is a resurrection. That is why there is repayment.
21:2 I ask those of you who stand above others, if you will take any advice from my good will: you have people among you to whom you can do good. Do not fail them.
21:3 The day is near when everything will perish together with the evil one. The Lord is near, and his reward with him.
21:4 Again and again I ask you: be good lawgivers for each other. Stay faithful advisers to each other. Get rid of all hypocrisy among you.
21:5 And may God, who rules the whole world, give you wisdom, understanding, insight, knowledge of his decrees, and endurance.
21:6 Be people taught by God. Search out what the Lord asks of you, and do it, so that you may be found on the day of judgment.
## Farewell
21:7 And if there is any memory of what is good, remember me as you think these things over, so that my longing and my sleepless nights may come to some good. I ask you this as a favor.
21:8 While the good vessel is still with you, do not fall short in anything among yourselves. Keep searching these things out, and carry out every commandment, for they are worth it.
21:9 That is why I have been all the more eager to write to you what I could, to make you glad. Farewell and be saved, children of love and peace. The Lord of glory and of all grace be with your spirit. The Letter of Barnabas.
