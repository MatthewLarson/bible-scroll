# Hostile review: Galatians 1-3 (part 01-03) and book.json

Reviewer: hostile-reviewer. Sources: texts/orig/Gal.json (sbl, byz, word flags), build check (0 errors, 0 warnings for Gal/01-03 and Gal), web checks (listed per finding).

## Verdict

A careful, well-sourced part with a good Plain: the translation is faithful, its choices (attendant, "faith in Jesus Christ" as at Romans 3:22, "works of the law", "accursed") are mostly defended in notes, the audience and settings cards are specific, and the 1:19 positions (Helvidian younger brother, Epiphanian stepbrother, Hieronymian cousin) are now stated correctly. I found no invented source, no invented fragment and no NIV/ESV copying. The weak spot is the variant apparatus, which is the thing Matt asked for most: several entries name no witnesses, two (2:12, 3:14) misdescribe the witnesses in a way that changes the evidence, one place where the Plain departs from the SBL base (1:3) has no entry, and the promised 3:22 *pistis Christou* entry does not exist. A few cross-checks fail (Menachot 43b, Dunn on Galatians 2, a Small Catechism citation, an invented paraphrase of 2:14). Several tradition cards are unsourced and the 3:28 "Jew and Greek" note/card sets up "replacement" as the foil. No critical findings; ten major; the rest minor.

Counts: critical 0, major 10, minor 20.

## Major

### 1. Variant 2:12: wrong witnesses, wrong rendering, understated weight (variants.json, 2:12)
The entry says "Tregelles' edition... following some early witnesses" and the `p` line says "One early editor read 'someone came'". The singular ἦλθεν ("he came") is a major early reading: ℵ B (and, per the NA28 apparatus as reported, P46 D* F G 33, Origen) against A C D² H Ψ 1739 and the Byzantine text for ἦλθον (Wikipedia's variants list gives ℵ B D² G³ for ἦλθεν; the sigla for D differ between sources, so check the NA28 apparatus). The word-list flag shows only Tregelles because texts/orig tracks editions, not manuscripts; the writer read an edition flag as the whole story. Also the reading's `plain` "someone" cannot come from τινας ... ἦλθεν: the Greek is "some people ... he came", and the point scholars make is who the singular "he" is (Cephas, James, or a leader of the delegation), with a bearing on the chronology of the delegation. Fix: name ℵ, B, D*/F/G, P46 (verify), Origen; give `plain` as "some people... when he came"; explain the singular, and rewrite the `why` ("fits 'those from the circumcision'" is weak against ℵ B).

### 2. Variant 3:14: P46 omitted, "mostly Western" is misleading (variants.json, 3:14)
εὐλογίαν ("blessing of the Spirit") is read by P46 (c. AD 200) with D* F G (88*, some Old Latin) (Wikipedia's list; confirm in NA28). The entry says "A few early witnesses, mostly Western" and names none, then dismisses the reading as "a copyist's echo". The oldest manuscript of Galatians is the lead witness for it; that must be stated, and the `why` should concede the echo argument is not obvious (the reading is also the more repetitive and so arguably the one scribes would not choose). Reading 0 should name ℵ A B C D² Ψ 33 and the Byzantine text. Same fix pattern as 1.

### 3. 1:3: Plain follows NA28 against the SBL base with no variant entry (plain.txt 1:3; variants.json)
texts/orig 1:3 flags ἡμῶν[NnW] after πατρός: NA28/WH read "God our Father and Lord Jesus Christ"; SBL/Byzantine/TR read "God the Father and our Lord Jesus Christ". The Plain ("God our Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ") follows NA28, changing the English. brief-nt.md requires a variants.json entry for every SBL/NA28/THGNT split that changes the English. Add one, with witnesses (name them from the apparatus: this one I have not verified), and say that the Plain departs from SBL. (The book `text` section says "The Plain follows NA28", but 1:15 and 2:16 are where the Plain follows SBL, so say which per verse.)

### 4. The 3:22 *pistis Iesou Christou* entry is promised and missing (variants.json; notes.json 3:22; chapters.json ch. 2 deep dive; topics `pistis-christou`)
brief-pauline.md: "Gal 2:16 and 3:22 ... get a full variant/note with both readings". The deep dive says the Plain "records the other reading in the variants at 2:16 and 3:22", and the topic contribution says the same and adds that at 3:22 "the objective reading is hardest, since the verse already speaks of 'those who believe'". There is no variant at 3:22 and the 3:22 note ("Shut up under sin") says nothing of it. The Plain at 3:22 ("the promise that comes from faith in Jesus Christ would be given to those who believe") is exactly where the objective rendering yields a redundancy the writer's own topic page admits. Add a 3:22 variant (readings and champions, as at 2:16) plus a note; correct the cross-references. Also 2:20 and 3:26 are named in the deep dive but only 3:26 has a hint in a note; 2:20's article τῇ τοῦ υἱοῦ deserves one sentence in the 2:20 note.

### 5. Variant entries without named witnesses (variants.json 1:6, 1:15, 2:9, 3:14, 3:17, 2:4)
brief-nt.md: "Name real witnesses with dates ... Never invent a manuscript reading" and "names the editions and the manuscripts behind each reading". Entries 1:6 ("A few early witnesses... no witnesses are named here because the editions only mark the doubt") and 1:15 (editions only) are admissions that the work was not done. Specifics:
- 1:6: NA28 brackets Χριστοῦ; the omission is reported for P51vid (c. 400) and some Western/Latin and Marcion-related evidence (check Metzger/NA28 apparatus). The `why` ("a scribe is more likely to have dropped a name than to have added one") is backwards for nomina sacra in Paul, where scribes habitually expand (compare 1:6 `why` with Metzger's reason for brackets: uncertainty). Rewrite after checking.
- 1:15: ὁ θεός is bracketed in NA28 (reported absent from P46, B, D*, F, G; verify). The `why` ("All the major editions but the SBL print 'God'") is edition-counting, not evidence, and omits that the earliest witnesses are on the other side. Say so, and state clearly that the Plain departs from its own SBL base here and why.
- 2:9: name P46 ℵ A B C (James, Cephas, John) against D* F G and Old Latin (Peter, James, John). The `why` argument ("later Christians would not move Peter from first place") is Metzger's; attribute it.
- 3:17, 2:4 are fine in content but name no manuscripts either; at least name ℵ A B C D² for the shorter 3:17 reading against D*/Byzantine εἰς Χριστόν.

### 6. Menachot 43b and the "daily prayer" claim (notes.json #114 at 3:28; chapters.json ch. 2 deep dive "Neither Jew nor Greek")
Both say "a man thanked God each day for not making him a Gentile, a slave or a woman ... Babylonian Talmud, Menachot 43b". What Menachot 43b has: a baraita (printed text: Rabbi Meir; many manuscripts: Rabbi Judah) with three blessings: not a non-Jew (goy), not a woman, not an ignoramus (bor). The "slave" form appears in the Gemara's discussion that follows (Rav Aha bar Yaakov's son proposes "not a slave"), and the Gentile-slave-woman triad is the Tosefta/Yerushalmi form (check the Tosefta Berakhot reference in Lieberman: I could not confirm "6:18"). All of it is rabbinic, recorded about 150 years or more after Paul, though the note presents it as the background of his hearers. Fix: cite Menachot 43b as "Gentile, woman, ignoramus (Meir/Judah), with 'slave' in the discussion and in the Tosefta and Yerushalmi forms"; add "these sources are later than Paul; whether an earlier custom lies behind them is uncertain". Also cite the Greek parallel (Thales, in Diogenes Laertius 1.33: not a barbarian, not a woman, not a beast) if the writer wants a pre-Pauline parallel.

### 7. Who holds which Acts 15 / Galatians 2 position (book.json "When was it written?"; chapters.json ch. 2 deep dive and settings 2:1-10; notes 2:46)
The text lists "Lightfoot, Betz, Dunn" together for "2:1-10 = Acts 15", and ties that to the North Galatian view. I could not confirm this for Dunn: his later work (*Beginning from Jerusalem*, 2009) is generally reported as treating Galatians 2:1-10 as the Acts 11:30 visit, and his Black's commentary is cautious. Verify and move or qualify. Further: the two positions are presented as bundles (South Galatia + famine visit + early date; North Galatia + Council + mid-50s) but several scholars combine South Galatia with the Council (a common modern evangelical option, for example Witherington; check Moo/Schreiner) and a third option (Acts 15 is a later, conflated account of an earlier meeting; or Gal 2 and Acts 15 are two stages of one visit) exists. The deep dive's third view is garbled: "Galatians 2 is one meeting and Acts 15 another that reworks it ... a historian has to choose Paul's own letter as the earlier witness". State it plainly (Haenchen, Lüdemann: Acts 11:30 and 15 are Luke's doubling of one visit). And the book.json sentence "the letter's place near Romans is the common reason for the later dates" is unclear; rewrite. The deep dive also says Paul and Barnabas "came from Antioch to Jerusalem" in Galatians; Paul does not say from where (Acts does).

### 8. A paraphrase put in Paul's mouth (chapters.json ch. 2 deep dive "What happened at Antioch?")
"Paul says he told Cephas ... that his conduct was a lie against the truth of the good news." Galatians 2:14 says they "were not walking straight toward the truth of the good news" (ὀρθοποδοῦσιν) and calls the behavior "hypocrisy" (2:13); "lie" is not in the text (it is Jerome/Augustine's vocabulary in the later debate). Replace with the text's words.

### 9. 1:9 Plain loses a plural the note depends on (plain.txt 1:9; notes.json #13)
προειρήκαμεν is plural ("as we have said before"); λέγω is singular. The Plain "I said it before, and I am saying it again now" erases the shift that note #13 explains ("Paul moves from 'we' to 'I'"). Fix: "As we have said before, so I now say again". The same verse: ἄρτι πάλιν "right now, again" is flattened to "now".

### 10. Fairness: the 3:28 "Jew and Greek" note and card, the justification card, and unsourced cards
a. Note #112 and the card "Does 'no Jew and no Greek' end Jewish identity in the church?" frame the mainstream Christian reading as "the church has replaced Israel ... a replacement reading that many now reject". The majority reading in Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran and Reformed teaching is not "both categories are finished" but "no difference in standing before God / one people of God"; and a historic supersessionist position exists that is not a straw man (the older Reformed and patristic view that the church is the new Israel). Present: (1) equal standing, distinctions continue socially (Dunn, Wright, many Protestants and Catholics); (2) distinctions continue and Jewish Torah-observance continues (Nanos, Gager, Messianic Jewish); (3) distinction dissolved (Boyarin's reading; some historic readings); (4) the older church-as-new-Israel view, named and not preemptively dismissed. Same wording issue in note #85 ("the older replacement view, which many now reject").
b. The justification card's `q` field contains an editorial aside: "(2:16; the New Perspective is a scholarly reading and not a church, see 'Works of the law' in the deep dives)". This is shown to the reader as the question. Move it to a card `note`/the deep dive. The New Perspective is rightly kept out of the TRADITIONS list; a one-line pointer in the chapter text suffices. The six-view brief is otherwise met (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist, Baptist, Anglican) and fair; Lutheran "the chief article by which the church stands or falls" is Luther's chief article (Smalcald II.1) plus the later formula; add the source key `smalcald` or soften.
c. Unsourced tradition entries (no source array, no named text or teacher): Anglican (3:28 women), Orthodox and Reformed and Dispensationalist (3:28 church-israel), Methodist (3:19-25, though Wesley is named), Adventist, Dispensationalist, Messianic Jewish (3:19-25), Anglican (2:11-14 uses the Articles but no source for the "college" claim). brief-nt.md requires "sourced to the churches' own teachers, confessions". Add keys (Fundamental Beliefs 19 for Adventists, a dispensationalist source such as Ryrie, a Messianic Jewish statement of faith, Wesley sermon "The Original, Nature, Property and Use of the Law").
d. The 3:28 women card has no Reformed or Methodist voice though both are divided on the point (PCA/OPC against PC(USA)/CRC; UMC ordains since 1956). Add them. Also the Church of England "bishops since 2014": legislation passed in November 2014, first woman bishop consecrated January 2015.
e. Sanders "showed" (book.json "Galatians and the Jewish people") and "few now think first-century Judaism taught salvation by earning" (deep dive 2:16): the covenantal nomism picture is widely accepted but contested (Carson, O'Brien, Seifrid, *Justification and Variegated Nomism*; Gathercole). Say "argued, and many accepted" and cite Westerholm (already a source) as the main critic.
f. Gloss 2:16 "might be justified: might be declared to be in the right" (and 3:8 "justify") adopts one of the three views the note presents. Make the gloss neutral ("put in right standing with God") or give two.

## Minor

11. **audience.to** ends "towns and villages of central Asia Minor,." (stray ", ."), and asserts "towns and villages" and "small groups meeting in houses" as settled although the North/South question determines whether these were cities (Pisidian Antioch, Iconium) or villages. Qualify.
12. **book.json authorship**: "No serious ancient or modern case has been made against it". The Dutch Radical school (Loman, van Manen) and Bruno Bauer disputed all the letters; say "no case that has persuaded scholars".
13. **Luther anecdotes**: the "Katie von Bora" remark is attested (Table Talk, WA TR 1, no. 146), but it is reported by others, not written by Luther, and says "betrothed". The Wesley anecdote is accurate (Charles read Luther's Galatians on 17 May 1738; peace 21 May; John's heart "strangely warmed" 24 May at Aldersgate), but John's experience came while hearing Luther's *Preface to Romans* read, not Galatians; the Methodist card and "Galatians through history" imply a Galatians link. Add the correction.
14. **Jerome-Augustine** (deep dive): Augustine's Letter 28 (c. 394/395) did not reach Jerome; the correspondence that matters is Augustine Ep. 40 (c. 397), Jerome Ep. 56/67 (c. 402) and following. "Augustine wrote to Jerome in about 394" is true of Ep. 28 but gives the wrong impression of a reply chain; say "from 394/395 (Ep. 28, which went astray) and Ep. 40 (397)". Jerome's later defense (Ep. 112) says he was following Origen and others, and Origen as the originator should also be in the 2:14 note #59.
15. **Orthodox card (2:11-14)** places the "staged quarrel" view under Orthodox and calls Jerome "in the West"; Jerome is also a Doctor of the Catholic church, so the Catholic card should say Catholic teachers were divided (Jerome and Augustine both Doctors). The sentence "Orthodox ... often cite the episode to illustrate that the apostles were equal" has no source.
16. **Baptism card, Lutheran**: Luther "puts on Christ (Small Catechism, part 4)". The Small Catechism's baptism part does not use "put on Christ"; the phrase belongs to his Galatians 3:27 lectures (Galatians lectures 1535) or his *Taufbüchlein*. Re-source.
17. **Orthodox tradition card in chapter 3 'T' view**: "3:28 in the liturgy of baptism". What the Byzantine rite does is sing Galatians 3:27 ("As many as have been baptized into Christ") in place of the Trisagion at baptismal feasts, and the baptismal epistle is Romans 6:3-11. Correct.
18. **Calvin on James the Lord's brother (1:19 card, Reformed)**: "refused to build on 'brothers' either way" understates his view. Calvin (Commentary on Galatians 1:19 and on Matthew 13:55) took "brothers" as kinsmen (cousins) and rejected Helvidius's argument from Matthew 1:25 as unproved. Re-read his Gal 1:19 text (calvin-galatians is already a source) and correct; also add Origen and the *Protevangelium of James* as the early source of the stepbrother view in note #30, and say the Catechism (500) speaks of "close relations", not "cousins" (the card turns this into "cousins").
19. **Note #8 (1:6)**: "The verb is in the middle voice" is stated as fact; μετατίθεσθε can be middle or passive, and the choice (deserting vs. being moved) is the dispute. Say "probably middle".
20. **Note #24 (1:16)** speaks of "'Consult' translates prosanethemen", but the Plain says "talk it over". Align. The note also says "a gloss gives the sense 'shows no favorites'" at #45 (2:6): there is no 2:6 gloss in glosses.json. Add the gloss or remove the sentence.
21. **1:12 "take it over"** vs 1:9 "received": same verb (παραλαμβάνω). Use "receive" in both; the link to 1 Corinthians 15:1-3 depends on it.
22. **1:19 Plain** "of the other apostles I saw no one, except James" leans to counting James an apostle, which note #30 correctly calls open. Use "I saw no other apostle, except James" (closer to ἕτερον τῶν ἀποστόλων οὐκ εἶδον εἰ μή).
23. **Note #99 (3:19)**: `p` says "at least four ways", the body lists five.
24. **Note #93 (3:13)**: "Paul ... adds 'everyone'": the Greek Deuteronomy already has πᾶς; he adds it only against the Hebrew. Say so (the OT table in book.json repeats this). The same table row for 3:10 ("adds 'all'") is true only against the Hebrew (LXX has ἐν πᾶσιν).
25. **Note #89 (3:11)**: 1QpHab is columns VII 17 - VIII 3, and the Hebrew word is 'amal ("toil", often rendered "suffering" or "deeds") and ʾemunah ("faith/faithfulness") "in the Teacher of Righteousness". The note's "faithfulness to the Teacher" blurs the preposition; use "because of their toil and their faith in the Teacher of Righteousness" and cite col. 7:17-8:3. Same for the 4QMMT summary in note #63: accurate (reckoned as righteousness, C 30-31), but add its date (c. 150 BC) and that it is a sectarian letter.
26. **Note #57 (2:14)**: the Vulgate reads *non recte ambularent*, not *recte ingrediuntur* (verify against the Clementine text; *ingrediuntur* may be the Vetus Latina).
27. **Note #107 (3:24)**: confirm Longenecker's rendering ("disciplinarian"? He uses "pedagogue/custodian" in some places) before attributing it; the deep dive on 3:19-25 mixes "custodian", "warden" and "attendant" for the same word. Use "attendant" throughout and mention the others once. The choice "attendant" itself is defensible (not copied from KJV schoolmaster, NIV/ESV guardian), the gloss is good, and the note correctly discards "schoolmaster".
28. **Variant 2:16 (b field)** lists Philippians 3:9 only as an argument for the objective reading; both sides use it (the second ἐπὶ τῇ πίστει is the subjectivists' point). Also it is a translation ambiguity rather than a manuscript variant, and the real manuscript variants at 2:16 (Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ order, δέ, διότι/ὅτι, Western ἀλλά) are not recorded; add at least one entry.
29. **Missing KJV-visible variants**: 3:12 (Byzantine/TR "the man that doeth them", ἄνθρωπος), 3:10 (ἐν πᾶσιν), 1:11 (δέ: "But I certify you"), 3:29 (καί), 3:7 (word order). The first is visible to every KJV reader; brief-nt.md requires an entry.
30. **Typos/odd phrasing**: ch. 1 timeline "beside Acts and outside history" (meaning "outside sources"?); ch. 2 `readings` "the oldest account of the meeting, written within a decade or two"; note #60 etc. fine. Fix the first.
