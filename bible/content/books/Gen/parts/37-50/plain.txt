## Joseph the dreamer
37:1 Jacob settled in the land where his father had lived as a foreigner, the land of Canaan.
37:2 This is the family history of Jacob. When Joseph was seventeen years old, he shepherded the flock together with his brothers. He was a lad working with the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives, and Joseph brought their father a bad report about them.
37:3 Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his other sons, because he was the son of his old age, and he made him a robe that reached to his hands and feet.
37:4 His brothers saw that their father loved him more than all of them, so they hated him and could not speak to him in peace.
37:5 Then Joseph dreamed a dream and told it to his brothers, and they hated him all the more.
37:6 He said to them, "Please hear this dream that I dreamed.
37:7 Look, we were tying up bundles of grain out in the field, and look, my bundle got up and stood upright, and look, your bundles gathered around it and bowed down to my bundle."
37:8 His brothers said to him, "So you will be king over us, will you? You will rule us, will you?" And they hated him even more because of his dreams and because of what he said.
37:9 Then he dreamed yet another dream and told it to his brothers. "Look," he said, "I have dreamed another dream: the sun, the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me!"
37:10 He told it to his father as well as his brothers, and his father scolded him. He said to him, "What is this dream you have had? Are we really going to come, I and your mother and your brothers, and bow down to the ground in front of you?"
37:11 His brothers envied him, while his father held on to what he had said.
## Sold by his brothers
37:12 His brothers went off to graze their father's flock near Shechem.
37:13 Israel said to Joseph, "Your brothers are grazing the flock at Shechem, aren't they? Come, I am going to send you to them." "Here I am," he answered.
37:14 He told him, "Go, please, and find out how your brothers are doing and how the flock is doing, then come back and tell me." He sent him out from the valley of Hebron, and Joseph arrived at Shechem.
37:15 A man came upon him wandering about in the open country. The man asked him, "What are you looking for?"
37:16 He said, "I am looking for my brothers. Please tell me where they are grazing the flock."
37:17 "They have pulled up and left," the man said. "I heard them say, 'Let's head for Dothan.'" So Joseph followed his brothers and found them at Dothan.
37:18 They spotted him from far away. Before he could come near them, they schemed together to put him to death.
37:19 They said to one another, "Here comes that master dreamer.
37:20 Come on now, let's kill him and throw him into one of the pits. We will say a wild animal ate him. Then we will see what becomes of his dreams."
37:21 But Reuben heard, and he saved him from them. "We must not strike him dead," he said.
37:22 "Shed no blood," Reuben told them. "Throw him into this pit out here in the wilderness, but do not raise a hand against him." He meant to rescue him from them and return him to his father.
37:23 So when Joseph reached his brothers, they stripped Joseph of his robe, the long robe that was on him.
37:24 They took hold of him and threw him down into the pit. It was a dry pit, with no water in it.
37:25 Then they sat down to eat their meal. Looking up, they saw a line of Ishmaelite traders coming from Gilead, their camels carrying gum, balm and myrrh down to Egypt.
37:26 Judah said to his brothers, "What is the profit if we kill our brother and hide his blood?
37:27 Come, let's sell him to the Ishmaelites. Our hand must not be against him, for he is our brother and our own flesh." And his brothers listened.
37:28 Then Midianite traders passed by. They pulled Joseph up out of the pit and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver, and they took Joseph to Egypt.
37:29 When Reuben came back to the pit, Joseph was not in the pit! He tore his clothes.
37:30 He went back to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! And I, where can I go?"
37:31 They took Joseph's robe, killed a male goat, and soaked the robe in the blood.
37:32 They sent the long robe and had it brought to their father, saying, "We found this. Please look closely: is it your son's robe or not?"
37:33 He knew it at once. "My son's robe!" he said. "A wild beast has eaten him. Joseph is torn, torn to pieces!"
37:34 Jacob tore his clothing and wrapped sackcloth around his hips, and he mourned for his son for many days.
37:35 All his sons and all his daughters came to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted. He said, "No, I will go down to my son in Sheol, still mourning." And his father wept for him.
37:36 In Egypt, the Midianites sold him to Potiphar, a court official of Pharaoh who commanded the bodyguard.
## Judah and Tamar
38:1 About that time Judah went down, away from his brothers, and turned aside to a certain Adullamite whose name was Hirah.
38:2 There Judah caught sight of the daughter of a Canaanite man whose name was Shua. Judah took her and lay with her.
38:3 She became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and he named him Er.
38:4 She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son, and she named him Onan.
38:5 Once more she bore a son, and she named him Shelah. He was at Kezib when she bore him.
38:6 For his firstborn, Er, Judah took a wife whose name was Tamar.
38:7 But Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the LORD's eyes, and the LORD put him to death.
38:8 Then Judah said to Onan, "Go in to your brother's wife. Do a brother-in-law's duty to her, and raise up offspring for your brother."
38:9 But Onan knew that the children would not count as his. So whenever he slept with his brother's wife, he spilled his seed on the ground, so that he would not give children to his brother.
38:10 What he did was evil in the LORD's eyes, and he put him to death too.
38:11 Then Judah said to Tamar his daughter-in-law, "Stay a widow in your father's house until my son Shelah is grown." For he thought, "Or he too will die, like his brothers." So Tamar went and stayed in her father's house.
38:12 A long time went by, and Shua's daughter, Judah's wife, died. When Judah's time of mourning was over, he went up to Timnah to the men shearing his sheep, he and his friend Hirah the Adullamite.
38:13 Word came to Tamar: "Your father-in-law is on his way up to Timnah to shear his sheep."
38:14 She put aside the clothes she wore as a widow, veiled herself, wrapped herself up, and sat at the gateway of Enaim, on the way to Timnah. She could see that Shelah was grown, and still she had not been given to him as his wife.
38:15 When Judah saw her, he took her for a prostitute, because she had covered her face.
38:16 He turned aside to her by the road and said, "Come, let me sleep with you." He did not know she was his daughter-in-law. She said, "What will you give me to sleep with me?"
38:17 He said, "I will send you a young goat from the flock." She said, "Only if you give me something as a pledge until you send it."
38:18 He said, "What pledge shall I give you?" She said, "Your seal and your cord, and your staff there in your hand." He gave them to her and went in to her, and she conceived by him.
38:19 Then she got up and left, took off her veil, and put her widow's clothes back on.
38:20 Judah had his friend the Adullamite take the young goat to her and bring back the pledge from the woman's hand. But the friend could not find her.
38:21 He asked the men of the place, "Where is the holy woman who was at Enaim by the road?" They said, "There has been no holy woman here."
38:22 He came back to Judah and said, "I could not find her. What's more, the local men told me, 'No holy woman has been in this place.'"
38:23 "Let her keep the things," Judah said, "so that we don't become a joke. I sent this goat, as you know, and you could not find her."
38:24 Some three months passed, and someone told Judah, "Tamar, your daughter-in-law, has sold herself as a prostitute, and look, she is pregnant from it." "Bring her out," said Judah, "and let her be burned!"
38:25 While they were bringing her out, she sent a message to her father-in-law: "The man these belong to is the father of my child." She added, "Please look closely. Whose are these: the seal, the cord and the staff?"
38:26 Judah knew them. "She is more in the right than I am," he said, "because I never gave her to my son Shelah." He did not lie with her again after that.
38:27 When her time to give birth came, there were twins in her womb!
38:28 During the birth one baby reached out a hand. The midwife took it, tied a scarlet thread around it, and said, "This is the one who came out first."
38:29 But as he pulled his hand back, out came his brother! She said, "What a breach you have made for yourself!" So he was named Perez.
38:30 After that his brother came out, the one with the scarlet thread on his hand, and he was named Zerah.
## Joseph in Potiphar's house
39:1 Joseph, meanwhile, had been brought down to Egypt. An Egyptian named Potiphar, a court official of Pharaoh who commanded the bodyguard, bought him from the Ishmaelites who had brought him there.
39:2 The LORD was with Joseph, and everything he did went well. He stayed in his Egyptian master's house.
39:3 His master saw that the LORD was with him and that the LORD made everything he did succeed in his hands.
39:4 Potiphar was pleased with Joseph, and Joseph became his personal servant. He made Joseph manager of his house and put all he had into his hands.
39:5 As soon as he made Joseph manager of his house and of everything he had, the LORD began to bless the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake. The LORD's blessing rested on all he owned, indoors and out in the fields.
39:6 He left all he owned with Joseph and, with Joseph there, gave no thought to anything but the food on his plate. Joseph had a fine build and good looks.
39:7 Some time later, his master's wife cast her eyes on Joseph. "Lie with me," she said.
39:8 But he refused. He said to his master's wife, "Look, with me here my master gives no thought to anything in the house. He has placed everything he owns in my hands.
39:9 He is not greater in this house than I am, and he has kept nothing back from me except you, because you are his wife. So how could I do this great evil and sin against God?"
39:10 She spoke to Joseph day after day, but he would not listen to her, to lie beside her or even to be with her.
39:11 Then came a day when he went indoors to do his work, and not one of the men of the house was there inside.
39:12 She grabbed him by his garment and said, "Lie with me!" But he left his garment in her hand and ran outside.
39:13 When she saw that he had left his garment in her hand and run outside,
39:14 she called out to the men of her house and told them, "See this! He brought us a Hebrew to toy with us. He came in to lie with me, and I cried out at the top of my voice.
39:15 When he heard me raise my voice and scream, he left his garment beside me and ran outside."
39:16 She laid his garment beside her and kept it there until Joseph's master came back to his house.
39:17 She told him the same tale: "That Hebrew servant you brought into our house came in to me to toy with me.
39:18 But when I raised my voice and screamed, he left his garment beside me and ran outside."
39:19 His master listened to his wife's words, "Your servant did these things to me," and his anger flared.
39:20 So Joseph's master took him and locked him in the prison house, where the king's prisoners were kept, and there he stayed, in the prison house.
## The LORD with Joseph in prison
39:21 Yet the LORD was with Joseph. He showed him loyal love and made the man in charge of the prison house look on him kindly.
39:22 The warden put all the prisoners in the prison in Joseph's hands. Whatever was done there, he was the one who did it.
39:23 The warden did not look into anything in Joseph's care, because the LORD was with him. Whatever he did, the LORD made it succeed.
## Two dreams in prison
40:1 After these things the cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the baker did wrong against their lord, the king of Egypt.
40:2 Pharaoh was furious with his two officials, the chief of the cupbearers and the chief of the bakers,
40:3 He had them held under guard in the house of the commander of the bodyguard, in the very prison house where Joseph was locked up.
40:4 The captain of the guard assigned Joseph to them, and he served them. They were in custody for some time.
40:5 Both of them dreamed on the same night, each his own dream, and each dream with its own meaning: the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were locked in the prison house.
40:6 Joseph came to them in the morning and looked at them, and they were downcast.
40:7 He asked Pharaoh's officials, who were held with him in his master's house, "What has made your faces fall today?"
40:8 They said to him, "We each had a dream, and there is no one to interpret it." Joseph said to them, "Don't interpretations belong to God? Please tell me your dreams."
40:9 The chief cupbearer described his dream to Joseph. "I dreamed," he said, "and look, a vine stood before me.
40:10 The vine had three branches. It seemed to bud, and at once its blossoms burst out and its clusters ripened into grapes.
40:11 I was holding Pharaoh's cup. I picked the grapes, pressed them into Pharaoh's cup, and set the cup in Pharaoh's palm."
40:12 Joseph told him, "This is its meaning: the three branches stand for three days.
40:13 Three days from now Pharaoh will lift up your head and put you back in your post. You will hand Pharaoh his cup as you did before, when you served as his cupbearer.
40:14 Only, keep me in mind when things are going well for you. Please do me a kindness: speak of me to Pharaoh, and bring me out of this place.
40:15 I was stolen away, stolen from the land of the Hebrews, and here too I have done nothing to be thrown into this pit."
40:16 The chief baker saw that Joseph had given a good meaning, so he said to Joseph, "I too had a dream. Look, there were three baskets of white bread on my head.
40:17 In the top basket were all kinds of food for Pharaoh, the work of a baker, and the birds were eating it out of the basket on my head."
40:18 Joseph answered, "This is what it means: the three baskets are three days.
40:19 Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head, off of you! He will hang you on a tree, and the birds will eat your flesh off of you."
40:20 On the third day, which was Pharaoh's birthday, he gave a feast for all his servants. He lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and the head of the chief baker in front of his servants.
40:21 He put the chief cupbearer back in his post, and he put the cup into Pharaoh's hand.
40:22 But he hanged the chief baker, just as Joseph had told them it would happen.
40:23 But the chief cupbearer gave no thought to Joseph. He forgot all about him.
## Pharaoh's dreams
41:1 At the end of two full years, Pharaoh dreamed that he was standing by the Nile,
41:2 and up out of the Nile came seven cows, fine-looking and fat, and they grazed among the reeds.
41:3 After them seven other cows came up out of the Nile, ugly and thin, and stood beside the first cows on the bank of the Nile.
41:4 Then the ugly, thin cows ate up the seven fine-looking, fat cows. And Pharaoh woke up.
41:5 He slept, and he dreamed again. This time seven ears of grain, fat and fine, came up on one stalk.
41:6 And look, after them seven heads of grain sprang up, thin and burned by the east wind.
41:7 The thin ears gulped down the seven fat, full ears. Pharaoh woke, and there it was: a dream.
41:8 When morning came, his mind was so restless that he sent for all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men. Pharaoh told them his dream, but not one of them could interpret it for Pharaoh.
41:9 Then the chief cupbearer spoke up and said to Pharaoh, "Today I must bring my failings to mind.
41:10 Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and he put me in custody in the house of the captain of the guard, me and the chief baker.
41:11 One night we each had a dream, he and I, and each dream had its own meaning.
41:12 A young Hebrew was there with us, a slave of the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted them for us. He told each of us the meaning of his dream.
41:13 And it turned out just as he interpreted it for us: I was put back in my post, and the other man was hanged."
## Joseph before Pharaoh
41:14 So Pharaoh sent for Joseph, and they rushed him out of the pit. He shaved, changed his clothes, and came in to Pharaoh.
41:15 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I dreamed a dream, and there is no one who can interpret it. I have heard people say of you that you can hear a dream and interpret it."
41:16 Joseph answered Pharaoh, "It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer for his good."
41:17 Then Pharaoh told Joseph, "In my dream, there I was, standing on the bank of the Nile.
41:18 Up out of the Nile came seven cows, fat and fine-looking, and they grazed among the reeds.
41:19 Then up came seven more cows behind them, weak, badly formed and skinny. I have never seen any so bad in the whole land of Egypt.
41:20 The thin, ugly cows ate up the first seven fat cows.
41:21 They went down into their bellies, yet nobody could have known it, because they looked as bad as they had at first. And I woke up.
41:22 Then, in my dream, I looked and saw seven ears of grain coming up on one stalk, full and fine.
41:23 And look, after them seven heads of grain sprang up, dried up, thin, burned by the east wind.
41:24 The thin ears gulped down the seven fine ears. I told it to the magicians, and there was no one to make it plain to me."
41:25 Joseph said to Pharaoh, "Pharaoh's dreams are one and the same. God has told Pharaoh what he is about to do.
41:26 The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads of grain are seven years. It is one dream.
41:27 The seven lean and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and the seven empty heads burned by the east wind will be seven years of famine.
41:28 That is what I said to Pharaoh: God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do.
41:29 Look, seven years are coming with great plenty in all the land of Egypt.
41:30 But after them seven years of famine will come, and all the plenty in the land of Egypt will be forgotten. The famine will wear out the land.
41:31 The plenty in the land will leave no trace, because of the famine that follows it, for it will be very severe.
41:32 As for the dream coming to Pharaoh twice: it means God has settled the matter, and God is hurrying to do it.
41:33 Now, then, Pharaoh should pick out a man with understanding and wisdom, and set him over the land of Egypt.
41:34 Let Pharaoh act: let him appoint overseers over the land and take a fifth of the harvest of the land of Egypt during the seven years of plenty.
41:35 Have them gather all the food in the good years ahead and heap up grain under Pharaoh's hand, food in the towns, kept under guard.
41:36 This food will be a reserve for the land for the seven years of famine that will come on the land of Egypt, so that the land will not be wiped out by the famine."
## Joseph rules Egypt
41:37 Pharaoh liked the proposal, and so did all his servants.
41:38 Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find anyone like this, a man who has the spirit of God in him?"
41:39 Then Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has let you know all this, no one is as discerning and wise as you are.
41:40 You will be in charge of my household, and all my people will obey your every word. Only on the throne will I be greater than you."
41:41 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "See, I am putting you over all the land of Egypt."
41:42 Pharaoh slipped the signet ring off his own hand and set it on Joseph's hand. He had him clothed in garments of fine linen and hung the gold chain around his neck.
41:43 He had him ride in his second chariot, and they shouted ahead of him, "Abrek!" So he put him over all the land of Egypt.
41:44 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh. Apart from you, nobody in all the land of Egypt will move a hand or a foot."
41:45 Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-paneah, and he gave him Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, as his wife. And Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.
41:46 Joseph was thirty when he stood before Pharaoh, king of Egypt. He left Pharaoh's presence and went through the whole land of Egypt.
41:47 During the seven years of plenty the land produced by the handful.
41:48 Joseph gathered all the food of the seven years in the land of Egypt and stored the food in the cities. In each city he stored the food from the fields around it.
41:49 Joseph stored up grain like the sand of the sea, so much that he stopped measuring it, because it was beyond measure.
41:50 Two sons were born to Joseph before the year of famine came. Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, bore them to him.
41:51 Joseph called the firstborn Manasseh, "for God has made me forget all my hardship and all my father's house."
41:52 He named the second Ephraim, "because God has made me fruitful in the land of my suffering."
41:53 Then the seven years of plenty that had been in the land of Egypt ended,
41:54 and the seven years of hunger set in, as Joseph had said they would. Every land had famine, but throughout Egypt there was food.
41:55 When all the land of Egypt grew hungry, the people cried out to Pharaoh for bread. Pharaoh said to all the Egyptians, "Go to Joseph. Do whatever he tells you."
41:56 The famine covered the whole face of the earth. Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to Egypt, for the famine gripped the land of Egypt hard.
41:57 And the whole world came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was severe over the whole world.
## The brothers go down to Egypt
42:1 Jacob learned that there was grain in Egypt, and he said to his sons, "Why do you keep staring at one another?"
42:2 And he said, "Look, I have heard there is grain in Egypt. Go down there and buy grain for us from there, so that we will live and not die."
42:3 So Joseph's ten brothers went down to buy grain from Egypt.
42:4 But Benjamin, Joseph's brother, Jacob kept at home and did not send with the others, for he thought, "Some harm might come to him."
42:5 So the sons of Israel came to buy grain along with others who came, because the famine was in the land of Canaan.
42:6 Joseph was the man in power over the land; he was the one selling grain to all its people. Joseph's brothers came and bowed low before him, faces to the earth.
42:7 When Joseph saw his brothers, he recognized them, but he acted like a stranger to them and spoke harshly to them. "Where have you come from?" he asked. They said, "From the land of Canaan, to buy food."
42:8 Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him.
42:9 Joseph remembered the dreams he had once dreamed about them. "You are spies," he told them. "You came to see where the land lies exposed."
42:10 They said to him, "No, my lord. Your servants have come to buy food.
42:11 We are all sons of one man. We are honest men. Your servants are not spies."
42:12 "Not so," he said. "It's the land's exposed places you have come to see."
42:13 They said, "We your servants are twelve brothers, sons of one man in the land of Canaan. The youngest is with our father right now, and one is gone."
42:14 But Joseph said to them, "That is what I told you. You are spies.
42:15 By this you will be tested: as Pharaoh lives, you will not go out from here unless your youngest brother comes here.
42:16 Send one of your number to bring your brother, and the rest of you will stay locked up. That way your words will be put to the test, whether there is truth in you. If there isn't, by Pharaoh's life, you are spies!"
42:17 He put them all together under guard for three days.
## Simeon held as hostage
42:18 On the third day Joseph told them, "Here is what you must do to stay alive, for I am a man who fears God.
42:19 If you are honest, one brother of yours will stay bound in the house where you are held. The others may go and carry grain home to feed your hungry families.
42:20 Then bring your youngest brother to me. That way your words will be proved true, and you will not die." And they did so.
42:21 They said one to another, "Truly we are guilty over our brother. We watched his anguish as he begged us for mercy, and we would not listen. That is why this anguish has come upon us."
42:22 Reuben answered them, "Didn't I tell you, 'Don't sin against the boy'? But you would not listen. Now we are being called to account for his blood."
42:23 They did not know that Joseph understood them, because he spoke to them through an interpreter.
42:24 He turned away from them and wept. Then he came back and spoke to them, and he took Simeon from among them and tied him up before their eyes.
42:25 Then Joseph ordered their containers filled with grain, each man's silver returned to his own sack, and food given them for the road. And so it was done.
42:26 They lifted their grain onto their donkeys and went on their way.
42:27 At the night's lodging one brother untied his sack to give his donkey fodder, and there, at the top of the sack, was his silver.
42:28 "My silver has come back!" he told his brothers. "Look, it is in my sack!" Their hearts failed them. Shaking, they turned to each other: "What is this God has done to us?"
42:29 Back in the land of Canaan with Jacob their father, they told him everything that had happened to them:
42:30 "The man who rules the land spoke roughly to us and took us for men spying on the country.
42:31 We told him, 'We are honest. Spies we are not.'
42:32 We are twelve brothers, our father's sons. One is gone, and the youngest is with our father right now in the land of Canaan.'
42:33 Then the man who rules the land said to us, 'This will show me whether you are honest: one of your brothers stays with me, and you take food for your hungry families and go.
42:34 Bring your youngest brother to me, and I will know that you are honest men and not spies. Then I will give your brother back to you, and you may move about in the land.'"
42:35 Then they began to empty their sacks, and every man found his bag of silver inside. When they saw the bags of silver, they and their father were afraid.
42:36 Jacob their father said to them, "You are making me childless! Joseph is gone, Simeon is gone, and you would take Benjamin too. All of this falls on me!"
42:37 Reuben said to his father, "Put my two sons to death if I do not bring him to you. Put him in my hands, and I will bring him back to you."
42:38 "My son is not going down with you," he said. "His brother is dead, and he is the only one left. Let harm come to him on the road you take, and you will send me down to Sheol, gray-haired and grieving."
## The second journey
43:1 The famine was still severe in the land.
43:2 When they had finished eating the grain they had brought from Egypt, their father said to them, "Go back. Buy us a little food."
43:3 But Judah told him, "The man gave us a stern warning. He said, 'My face you will not see unless your brother is with you.'
43:4 If you send our brother with us, we will go down and buy you food.
43:5 If you do not send him, we will not go down, for the man told us, 'You will not see my face unless your brother is with you.'"
43:6 Israel said, "Why did you do me this wrong, telling the man you had another brother?"
43:7 "The man kept asking about us and our relatives," they said. "He asked, 'Is your father still alive? Have you another brother?' and we answered what he asked. Could we ever have known that he would say, 'Bring your brother down'?"
43:8 Then Judah said to Israel his father, "Send the boy with me, and let us get up and go, so that we may live and not die, we and you and our little ones.
43:9 I will be his guarantor; you can demand him from my hand. If I do not bring him to you and stand him in front of you, then I will carry the guilt before you all my days.
43:10 If we had not delayed, we could have gone there and back twice by now."
43:11 Their father Israel said to them, "If that is how it is, then do this: take some of the land's finest produce in your bags, and carry it down to the man as a gift: a little balm, a little honey, gum and myrrh, pistachios and almonds.
43:12 Take double the silver with you, and take back the silver that was returned in the mouths of your sacks. Perhaps it was a mistake.
43:13 Take your brother too, and get up and go back to the man.
43:14 May God Almighty give you mercy before the man, so that he will release your other brother and Benjamin to you. As for me, if I am bereaved, I am bereaved."
## A meal in Joseph's house
43:15 So the men took the gift, and they took double the silver with them, and Benjamin. They got up, went down to Egypt, and stood before Joseph.
43:16 When Joseph saw Benjamin with them, he said to the man in charge of his house, "Bring these men into the house. Slaughter an animal and prepare it, because the men will eat with me at noon."
43:17 The man did as Joseph said and brought the men into Joseph's house.
43:18 Being brought into Joseph's house frightened the men. "It's about the silver that turned up in our sacks last time," they said. "That's why we are being brought in. He means to roll over us, fall on us, and take us for slaves, with our donkeys too."
43:19 At the doorway of the house they stepped up to the man in charge of Joseph's household and spoke with him.
43:20 They said, "Excuse us, my lord. We came down here the first time only to buy food.
43:21 When we got to our night's lodging and opened our sacks, there was each man's silver at the top of his sack, our own silver, its full weight. We have brought it back in our hands.
43:22 And we have brought more silver down with us to buy food. We have no idea who put our silver in our sacks."
43:23 He said, "Peace to you, do not be afraid. Your God, the God of your father, put treasure in your sacks for you. I received your silver." Then he brought Simeon out to them.
43:24 The man brought the men into Joseph's house and gave them water, and they washed their feet, and he gave feed to their donkeys.
43:25 They set out the gift and waited for Joseph to come at noon, since they had been told they would have their meal there.
43:26 When Joseph came home, they brought him the gift they had with them into the house, and they bowed down to the ground before him.
43:27 He asked how they were. Then he said, "How is your old father you told me about? Is he still alive?"
43:28 They said, "Your servant our father is well. He is still alive." And they bowed low and bowed down.
43:29 He looked up and saw Benjamin his brother, his mother's son, and said, "Is this your youngest brother, the one you spoke of to me?" And he said, "God be gracious to you, my son."
43:30 Then Joseph rushed away, because his heart burned with love for his brother and he was close to tears. He went into an inner room and wept there.
43:31 Then he washed his face, came out, and controlled himself. He said, "Serve the meal."
43:32 Joseph's food was set out for him alone, the brothers' for them alone, and the Egyptians who ate with him had theirs apart, because Egyptians could not share a meal with Hebrews. To the Egyptians that was a thing they loathed.
43:33 They were seated before him, the firstborn according to his birthright and the youngest according to his youth, and the men looked at one another in amazement.
43:34 Joseph had portions sent to them from his own table, and Benjamin's was five times as large as anyone else's. They drank with him, and they drank deep.
## The silver cup
44:1 Joseph instructed the man in charge of his house, "Load the men's sacks with food, all they can carry, and put each one's silver at the top of his sack.
44:2 And put my cup, the silver cup, in the mouth of the youngest one's sack, along with the silver for his grain." He did as Joseph told him.
44:3 At first light the men were sent on their way, they and their donkeys.
44:4 They had left the city but were not yet far off when Joseph told the man in charge of his house, "Up, chase the men! When you overtake them, ask them, 'Why have you paid back evil for good?
44:5 This is what my master drinks from, is it not, and what he reads omens with? You did wrong when you did this.'"
44:6 He caught up with them and said these words to them.
44:7 They said to him, "Why does my lord say such things? Your servants would never do such a thing!
44:8 Look, the silver we found in the mouths of our sacks we brought back to you from the land of Canaan. So how could we steal silver or gold from your lord's house?
44:9 Whichever of your servants has it on him must die, and the rest of us will be our lord's slaves."
44:10 "All right," he said, "it will be as you say, with this change: the one who has it will become my slave, and the rest of you will be cleared."
44:11 Quickly every man lowered his sack to the ground, and every man opened his sack.
44:12 He searched, starting with the oldest and finishing with the youngest, and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack.
44:13 They tore their clothes. Then each man loaded his donkey, and they went back to the city.
44:14 Judah and his brothers came to Joseph's house while he was still there, and they threw themselves on the ground before him.
44:15 "What have you done?" Joseph asked them. "Did you not know that a man like me can read omens?"
44:16 Judah said, "What are we to say to my lord? What words do we have? How can we clear ourselves? God has found out your servants' guilt. Here we are, my lord's slaves, all of us, along with the one who had the cup in his hand."
44:17 But he said, "I would never do such a thing! The man in whose hand the cup was found will be my slave. The rest of you, go up in peace to your father."
## Judah offers himself
44:18 Then Judah came close to him and said, "Please, my lord, let your servant speak a word in my lord's ears, and do not let your anger burn against your servant, for you are like Pharaoh himself.
44:19 My lord asked his servants, 'Do you have a father, or a brother?'
44:20 We told my lord, 'We have a father who is old, and a young boy, the child of his old age. That boy's brother is dead; he alone is left of his mother's sons, and his father loves him.'
44:21 And you said to your servants, 'Bring him down to me, so that I can set my eyes on him.'
44:22 We said to my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father. If he leaves his father, his father will die.'
44:23 Then you said to your servants, 'If your youngest brother does not come down with you, you will not see my face again.'
44:24 So we went up to your servant, my father, and reported to him my lord's words.
44:25 Later our father said, 'Go back and buy us a little food.'
44:26 We said, 'We cannot go down there. If our youngest brother comes with us, then we will go. We are not allowed to see the man's face without our youngest brother along.'
44:27 Your servant my father said to us, 'You yourselves know that my wife bore me two sons.
44:28 One of them left me, and I said, "He is torn, surely torn to pieces," and I have not seen him to this day.
44:29 Now if you take this one from my sight as well and harm meets him, you will send me down to Sheol, gray-haired and in misery.'
44:30 So now, if I go to your servant my father and the boy is not with us, since his life is bound up with the boy's life,
44:31 when he sees that the boy is not there, he will die. Your servants will bring the gray hair of your servant our father down to Sheol in grief.
44:32 Your servant became the boy's guarantor to my father. I said, 'If I do not bring him back to you, I will carry the guilt before my father all my days.'
44:33 So now, please let your servant stay as my lord's slave in place of the boy, and let the boy go back up with his brothers.
44:34 How could I go up to my father without the boy beside me? I could not bear to watch the grief that would overtake my father."
## "I am Joseph"
45:1 Joseph could no longer hold himself back in front of all who stood around him, and he cried out, "Send everyone away from me!" So no one was with him when Joseph made himself known to his brothers.
45:2 He wept so loudly that the Egyptians heard it, and Pharaoh's household heard it.
45:3 "I am Joseph," he told his brothers. "Is my father really still alive?" His brothers could not get a word out; they were too shaken to face him.
45:4 Then Joseph said to his brothers, "Please come close to me." And they came close. He said, "I am Joseph your brother, the one you sold into Egypt.
45:5 Now do not be pained, and do not blame yourselves for selling me to this place. God sent me here before you to keep people alive.
45:6 The famine has gripped the land these two years, and five years are still to come with no plowing and no harvest.
45:7 God sent me ahead of you to keep a remnant alive for you on the earth and to save your lives by a great rescue.
45:8 You were not the ones who sent me here; God was. He has made me a father to Pharaoh, master of his whole house, and the one who governs all the land of Egypt.
45:9 Hurry up to my father and say to him, 'Your son Joseph sends this word: God has made me master of all Egypt. Come down to me; do not wait.
45:10 You will settle in the land of Goshen, close to me, you and your sons and your sons' sons, your sheep and your cattle, and everything that is yours.
45:11 There I will keep you fed, for the famine still has five years to run. Otherwise you will be reduced to poverty, you, your household and all you have.'
45:12 Look, your own eyes can see, and my brother Benjamin's eyes can see, that it is my own mouth speaking to you.
45:13 Tell my father about all my glory in Egypt, and all you have seen, and hurry and bring my father down here."
45:14 Then he threw his arms around his brother Benjamin's neck and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck.
45:15 He kissed all his brothers and wept over them. After that his brothers talked with him.
## Pharaoh's invitation
45:16 The news was heard in Pharaoh's house: "Joseph's brothers have come!" And it pleased Pharaoh and his servants.
45:17 Pharaoh told Joseph, "Say to your brothers, 'Here is what to do. Load up your animals and go straight back to the land of Canaan.
45:18 Get your father and your households and come to me. I will give you the best of the land of Egypt, and you will eat the fat of the land.'
45:19 And you are to give them this order as well: 'Take carts from the land of Egypt for your little ones and your wives, bring your father, and come.
45:20 Don't worry about your belongings, because the best of all the land of Egypt is yours.'"
45:21 The sons of Israel did as they were told. At Pharaoh's word Joseph gave them carts, and he gave them food for the road.
45:22 He gave each of them a change of clothes, but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothes.
45:23 To his father he sent the same: ten donkeys carrying fine things of Egypt, and ten she-donkeys carrying grain, bread and food for his father's journey.
45:24 Then he sent his brothers on their way. As they left, he said to them, "Don't get stirred up on the way."
45:25 So they went up from Egypt and came to their father Jacob in the land of Canaan.
45:26 They told him, "Joseph is still alive! He is ruler over all the land of Egypt!" Jacob's heart went numb, because he did not believe them.
45:27 Then they told him all that Joseph had said to them. He saw the carts Joseph had sent to carry him, and their father Jacob's spirit lived again.
45:28 Israel said, "Enough! My son Joseph is still alive. I will go and see him before I die."
## Jacob goes down to Egypt
46:1 Israel set out with everything he had. He came to Beersheba, and there he offered sacrifices to the God of Isaac his father.

46:3 He said, "I am God, the God of your father. Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, because I will make you a great nation there.
46:4 I will go down with you to Egypt, and I will also bring you up again, surely up. And Joseph will lay his hand on your eyes."

## Jacob blesses Pharaoh
47:1 So Joseph went and told Pharaoh, "My father and my brothers have come from the land of Canaan with their flocks and herds and all they own. They are now in the land of Goshen."
47:2 He took five of his brothers and presented them to Pharaoh.
47:3 Pharaoh asked his brothers, "What is your work?" They said to Pharaoh, "Your servants are shepherds, both we and our fathers."
47:4 They told Pharaoh, "We have come to stay in the land as foreigners. In Canaan the famine is heavy, and there is no grazing for your servants' flocks. So please let your servants live in the land of Goshen."
47:5 Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Your father and your brothers have come to you.
47:6 The land of Egypt lies before you. Settle your father and brothers in the best of the land; let them live in the land of Goshen. And if you know any able men among them, make them chief herdsmen over my own livestock."
47:7 Joseph brought in his father Jacob and had him stand before Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed Pharaoh.
47:8 Pharaoh asked Jacob, "How many years have you lived?"

47:10 Then Jacob blessed Pharaoh and went out from Pharaoh's presence.
47:11 Joseph gave his father and brothers a home and a holding of land in Egypt, in the best part of the country, the land of Rameses, as Pharaoh had ordered.
47:12 Joseph kept his father, his brothers and his father's whole household supplied with food, down to the youngest mouths.
## Joseph and the famine
47:13 Nowhere in the land was there bread, for the famine was very heavy, and Egypt and Canaan both grew faint from hunger.
47:14 Joseph gathered up all the silver that was found in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan, for the grain they were buying, and Joseph brought the silver into Pharaoh's house.
47:15 When the silver in the land of Egypt and the land of Canaan was used up, all the Egyptians came to Joseph and said, "Give us food! Why should we die in front of you? Our silver is gone."
47:16 Joseph answered, "Hand over your livestock, and I will give you food for them, if your silver has run out."
47:17 They brought their animals to Joseph, and he gave them bread for horses, for flocks of sheep, for herds of cattle and for donkeys. That year he kept them going on bread in trade for all their livestock.
47:18 That year came to an end, and the next year they came to him and said, "We won't hide it from my lord: the silver is spent, and the herds belong to my lord. Nothing is left for my lord but our bodies and our land.
47:19 Why should we die in front of you, both we and our fields? Buy us and our land for bread, and we and our land will be slaves to Pharaoh. Give us seed, so that we live and do not die, and the ground does not lie waste."
47:20 Joseph bought up all Egypt's land for Pharaoh. The Egyptians sold off their fields, every one of them, because the famine bore down hard on them, and the land came to belong to Pharaoh.
47:21 As for the people, he moved them into the cities, from one end of Egypt to the other.
47:22 Only the priests' land he did not buy. The priests had a fixed share from Pharaoh, and they lived on the share Pharaoh gave them, so they did not have to sell their land.
47:23 Joseph said to the people, "Look, today I have bought you and your land for Pharaoh. Here is seed for you. Sow the land.
47:24 At harvest time, give a fifth to Pharaoh. Four-fifths will be yours, as seed for the field and as food for yourselves, your households and your little ones."
47:25 They said, "You have kept us alive! If it please my lord, we will be Pharaoh's slaves."
47:26 Joseph made this the rule for the land of Egypt, and it stands to this day: one fifth goes to Pharaoh. Only the priests' land never became Pharaoh's.
## "Do not bury me in Egypt"
47:27 Israel lived in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen. They gained property there, and they were fruitful and multiplied greatly.
47:28 Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years, and the length of Jacob's life was a hundred and forty-seven years.
47:29 The days drew near for Israel to die. He called his son Joseph and said to him, "If I have found favor in your eyes, please put your hand under my thigh, and deal with me in faithful love and truth: please do not bury me in Egypt.
47:30 Let me lie with my fathers: carry me out of Egypt and lay me in the grave where they lie." Joseph promised, "I will do what you have asked."
47:31 He said, "Swear to me." So he swore to him. And Israel bowed down at the head of the bed.
## Jacob adopts Ephraim and Manasseh

48:3 Jacob said to Joseph, "God Almighty appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and he blessed me.

## The blessing on Joseph's sons
48:15 Then he blessed Joseph and said, "God, in whose presence my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, / God, my shepherd from my first day until this day,
48:16 the angel who has redeemed me from all harm, / may he bless these boys. / May my name live on in them, / and the names of my fathers Abraham and Isaac, / and may they grow into a great many on the earth."

48:18 Joseph said to his father, "Not that way, my father! This one is the firstborn. Put your right hand on his head."
48:19 But his father would not. "I know, my son, I know," he said. "He too will become a people, and he too will be great. Yet his younger brother will be greater than he, and his offspring will become a whole throng of nations."
48:20 That day he blessed them and said, "In your name Israel will give blessings, saying, / 'God make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh.'" So he set Ephraim ahead of Manasseh.
48:21 Israel said to Joseph, "Look, I am dying. But God will be with you, and he will bring you back to the land of your fathers.

## Jacob's last words
49:1 Then Jacob called his sons and said, "Gather together, and I will tell you what will meet you in the last days.
49:2 Assemble and hear, sons of Jacob; / hear Israel your father.
49:3 Reuben, you are my firstborn, / my strength and the first fruit of my manhood, / first in honor and first in power.
49:4 Boiling over like water, you will not be first, / because you went up onto your father's bed. / Then you defiled it: he went up onto my couch!
49:5 Simeon and Levi are brothers; / their blades are tools of violence.
49:6 May I never come into their council; / may my honor never be joined to their assembly. / For in their anger they killed men, / and at their whim they crippled oxen.
49:7 Their anger be cursed, for it was savage, / their rage, for it was relentless. / I will divide them among Jacob / and spread them thin through Israel.
49:8 Judah, your brothers will praise you. / Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies; / your father's sons will bow down to you.
49:9 Judah is a lion's cub; / you have come up from the prey, my son. / He crouches, he lies down like a lion, / like a lioness: who dares to rouse him?
49:10 The scepter will not leave Judah, / nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, / until Shiloh comes, / and to him the peoples will give obedience.
49:11 He ties his donkey to the vine, / his donkey's colt to the choice vine. / He washes his clothes in wine, / his robe in the blood of grapes.
49:12 His eyes are dark from wine, / his teeth white from milk.
49:13 Zebulun will live by the shore of the seas; / he will be a harbor for ships, / and his far side will reach Sidon.
49:14 Issachar is a strong-boned donkey, / stretched out between the two folds.
49:15 He saw that a resting place was good / and that the land was pleasant, / so he bent his shoulder to carry loads / and became a slave at forced labor.
49:16 Dan will judge his people / as one of the tribes of Israel.
49:17 May Dan be a snake on the road, / a horned viper on the path, / biting the horse's heels / so that its rider falls backward.
49:18 I wait for your rescue, O LORD.
49:19 Gad: raiders will raid him, / but he will raid them at their heels.
49:20 Asher's bread will be rich, / and he will furnish the dainty food of kings.
49:21 Naphtali is a doe let loose; / he gives beautiful words.
49:22 Joseph is the son of a fruitful tree, / the son of a fruitful tree beside a spring; / its daughters climb over the wall.
49:23 The archers made his life bitter; / they shot at him and hounded him with hatred.
49:24 But his bow stayed steady, / and his arms were made quick / by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob, / from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel,
49:25 by the God of your father, who will help you, / by the Almighty, who will bless you / with blessings of the heavens above, / blessings of the deep that lies below, / blessings of breasts and womb.
49:26 The blessings of your father are stronger / than the blessings of the eternal mountains, / the longed-for riches of the everlasting hills. / May they rest on the head of Joseph, / on the brow of the one set apart from his brothers.
49:27 Benjamin is a wolf that tears its prey: / in the morning he devours what he has caught, / and in the evening he divides the spoil."
49:28 These are all the tribes of Israel, twelve of them. This is what their father said to them as he blessed them; he gave each one the blessing that was his.
## The death of Jacob
49:29 He gave them his last orders: "My time has come to be gathered to my people. Lay me with my fathers in the cave that is in Ephron the Hittite's field,
49:30 the cave in the field of Machpelah, facing Mamre in the land of Canaan. Abraham bought that field from Ephron the Hittite to own as a burial ground.
49:31 There they buried Abraham and his wife Sarah. There they buried Isaac and his wife Rebekah. And there I buried Leah.
49:32 The field and its cave were purchased from the sons of Heth."

## Jacob's funeral
50:1 Joseph fell upon his father's face. He wept over him and kissed him.
50:2 Then Joseph ordered his servants, the physicians, to embalm his father. So the physicians embalmed Israel.
50:3 It took forty days, the full time needed for embalming. And the Egyptians wept for him seventy days.
50:4 The days of weeping for him went by, and Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household: "If you look on me with favor, please put a word in Pharaoh's ear for me:
50:5 'My father put me under oath. He said, "I am dying. Bury me in my grave, the one I dug for myself in the land of Canaan." So please let me go up and bury my father, and I will come back.'"
50:6 Pharaoh said, "Go up and bury your father, just as he made you swear."
50:7 So Joseph went up to bury his father. All Pharaoh's servants went up with him, the elders of his household and all the elders of the land of Egypt,
50:8 along with all Joseph's household, his brothers and his father's household. Only their little ones, their flocks and their herds were left in the land of Goshen.
50:9 Chariots and horsemen went up with him as well; it was a huge procession.
50:10 When they came to the threshing floor of Atad, beyond the Jordan, they mourned there with a great and very bitter lament. Joseph held seven days of mourning for his father.
50:11 When the Canaanites who lived in the land saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad, they said, "This is a bitter mourning for the Egyptians." That is why the place beyond the Jordan is called Abel-mizraim.
50:12 So his sons did for him just as he had commanded them.
50:13 His sons carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, east of Mamre, the field Abraham had bought from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place of his own.
50:14 After he had buried his father, Joseph went back to Egypt, he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father.
## "God meant it for good"
50:15 Now that their father was dead, Joseph's brothers began to think, "Suppose Joseph turns hostile toward us and pays us back for all the evil we did to him!"
50:16 So they sent word to Joseph: "Before he died your father gave this command:
50:17 'This is what you are to say to Joseph: Please, forgive the crime of your brothers and their sin, for they did you wrong.' So now, please forgive the crime of the servants of your father's God." Joseph wept when they spoke to him.
50:18 Then his brothers came themselves and fell down before him. "Here we are," they said, "your slaves."
50:19 Joseph said to them, "Don't be afraid. Am I in God's place?
50:20 You planned evil against me, but God planned it for good, to do what is happening today: to keep many people alive.
50:21 So now, do not be afraid. I will provide for you and your little ones." So he comforted them and spoke kindly to them.
## The death of Joseph
50:22 So Joseph lived on in Egypt, he and his father's household. Joseph reached one hundred and ten years.
50:23 Joseph saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children of Machir son of Manasseh were also born on Joseph's knees.
50:24 Joseph told his brothers, "I am dying. But God will surely come to help you, and he will bring you up from this land to the land he swore to give Abraham, Isaac and Jacob."
50:25 Joseph had the sons of Israel take an oath: "God will surely come to help you. When he does, carry my bones up from here."
50:26 Joseph died, one hundred and ten years old. They embalmed him, and he was laid in a coffin in Egypt.
