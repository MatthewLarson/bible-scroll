## Wisdom Praises Herself
24:1 The praise of Wisdom. / Wisdom will sing her own praise / and boast among her own people.
24:2 She will speak up in the assembly of the Most High / and boast before his armies:
24:3 "I came out of the Most High's mouth / and lay over the earth like a mist.
24:4 I pitched my tent in the heights, / and my throne stood in a pillar of cloud.
24:5 Alone I went all the way around the circle of the sky, / and I walked about down in the deep abysses.
24:6 Among the waves of the sea and over all the earth, / among every people and nation, I gained a holding.
24:7 Among all these I looked for a place to rest. / In whose inheritance should I make my camp?
24:8 Then the Maker of everything gave me his order, / and the one who created me found my tent a place to rest. / He said, 'Pitch your tent in Jacob, / and take up your inheritance in Israel.'
24:9 He created me before the ages, at the beginning, / and to the end of the ages I will never give out.
24:10 I served in worship before him in the holy tent, / and so I was made firm in Zion.
24:11 He gave me rest in the city he loves as well, / and my authority is in Jerusalem.
24:12 I put down roots in a people given glory, / in the Lord's portion, his inheritance.
## Like a Cedar, Like a Vine
24:13 Like a cedar in Lebanon I rose high, / like a cypress on the mountains of Hermon.
24:14 Like a palm tree on the seashore I rose high, / like rose shrubs at Jericho, / like a handsome olive tree in the plain; / I rose high like a plane tree.
24:15 Like cinnamon and aspalathus I gave off the scent of spices; / I spread a sweet smell like the finest myrrh, / like galbanum, onycha and stacte, / like the smoke of frankincense in the tent.
24:16 Like a terebinth I reached out my branches, / and my branches are branches of glory and grace.
24:17 Like a vine I budded with grace, / and my flowers are a crop of glory and wealth.
24:18 [I am the mother of beautiful love, / of fear, of knowledge and of holy hope. / So I am given to all my children, / I who am forever, to those he has named.]
24:19 Come to me, you who long for me, / and be filled with what I grow.
24:20 To remember me is sweeter than honey, / and to have me for an inheritance is better than honey from the comb.
24:21 Whoever eats me will still be hungry, / and whoever drinks me will still be thirsty.
24:22 Anyone who listens to me will never be ashamed, / and no one who works at my side will fall into sin."
## The Law Moses Commanded
24:23 All these things are the book of the covenant of God Most High, / the law Moses commanded, / to be inherited by the gatherings of Jacob.
24:24 [Do not let your strength give way in the Lord; / cling to him, so that he will make you strong. / The Lord Almighty alone is God, / and besides him there is no other savior.]
24:25 It is full to the brim with wisdom, like the Pishon, / like the Tigris in the days of new fruit.
24:26 It makes understanding rise high like the Euphrates, / like the Jordan in the days of harvest.
24:27 It makes instruction shine out like light, / like the Gihon in the days of the grape harvest.
24:28 The first person did not finish knowing her, / and neither will the last one trace her out.
24:29 Her thinking is wider than the sea, / and her counsel greater than the vast abyss.
## A Canal from the River
24:30 As for me, I flowed out like a ditch dug from a river, / like an aqueduct running into an orchard.
24:31 I said, "I will give my garden water / and soak my flower beds." / And look, my ditch became a river, / and my river became a sea.
24:32 Once more I will light up instruction like the dawn, / and make these things shine far into the distance.
24:33 Once more I will pour out teaching as if it were prophecy, / and leave it behind for every generation to come.
24:34 See: my hard work was not only for me, / but for everyone who searches for her.
## Three Lovely Things, Three Hateful
25:1 I am made beautiful by three things, / and with them I stand beautiful before the Lord and people: / brothers who agree, neighbors who are friends, / and a wife and a husband who go along with each other.
25:2 But there are three kinds of people my soul hates, / and the way they live disgusts me deeply: / a poor man who is proud, a rich man who lies, / and an old man who commits adultery, short on sense.
25:3 You stored nothing up when you were young: / how will you find anything when you are old?
25:4 How lovely good judgment is in gray hair, / and for elders to know how to give advice!
25:5 How lovely is wisdom in the old, / and thought and counsel in those held in honor!
25:6 Long experience is the crown of old men, / and the fear of the Lord is what they boast in.
## Ten Happy Men
25:7 There are nine things I have thought over and called happy in my heart, / and a tenth I will put into words: / a man who has joy in his children, / who lives to see his enemies fall;
25:8 happy is the man who shares his home with a wife who has good sense, / who has not slipped with his tongue, / and who has not served a master unworthy of him;
25:9 happy is the one who has found good sense, / and the one who talks to ears that are listening.
25:10 The one who finds wisdom, how great he is! / Yet he does not rank above the one who fears the Lord.
25:11 The fear of the Lord goes beyond everything. / The one who holds on to it, who can compare with him?
25:12 [The fear of the Lord is the beginning of loving him, / and faith is the beginning of clinging to him.]
## The Evil Wife
25:13 Let it be any wound, but not a wound to the heart; / any evil, but not the evil of a woman;
25:14 any attack, but not an attack from those who hate; / any revenge, but not revenge from enemies.
25:15 No head is worse than the head of a snake, / and no anger worse than an enemy's anger.
25:16 Living with a lion and a dragon would suit me better / than keeping house with an evil woman.
25:17 The evil of a woman changes how she looks; / it makes her face as dark as sackcloth.
25:18 Her husband takes his place at table among his neighbors, / and hearing it, he groans bitterly.
25:19 Every evil is small next to the evil of a woman. / May the lot of a sinner land on her!
25:20 Like a sandy climb for an old man's feet / is a wife with a busy tongue to a quiet husband.
25:21 Do not fall for a woman's beauty, / and do not set your longing on a woman.
25:22 If a wife pays her husband's way, / it brings anger, brazenness and deep shame.
25:23 A heart brought low, a gloomy face, / a wound in the heart: that is an evil woman. / Hands that hang limp and knees that give way: / that is a wife who does not make her husband happy.
25:24 The beginning of sin came from a woman, / and on account of her we all die.
25:25 Do not give water a way out, / or an evil woman power to do as she likes.
25:26 If she does not walk as your hand guides her, / cut her away from your flesh.
## The Good Wife
26:1 The man with a good wife is a happy man; / his days will add up to twice as many.
26:2 A wife of strength cheers her husband, / and he will fill out his years in peace.
26:3 A good wife is a good portion; / she is given as part of the portion of those who fear the Lord.
26:4 Rich man or poor man, he has a good heart / and a cheerful face in every season.
## Three Fears and a Fourth
26:5 My heart shrinks back from three things, / and in front of a fourth I plead for help: / slander spread through a town, a mob coming together, / and false accusation. All these are worse than death.
26:6 Heartache and grief: a wife who is jealous of a rival wife, / and her tongue is a whip she shares with everyone.
26:7 An evil wife is an ox yoke that shakes loose; / the man who tries to control her has a scorpion in his grip.
26:8 Great anger comes with a drunken woman, / and she will not cover her own disgrace.
26:9 You can tell a woman's sexual sin by the way her eyes rove upward; / her eyelids will give it away.
## Guarding a Daughter
26:10 Keep an unbending watch over a daughter, / so that she does not find some freedom and use it for herself.
26:11 Keep watch on a shameless eye, / and do not be surprised if she wrongs you.
26:12 Like a thirsty traveler, she opens her mouth / and drinks from whatever water is close by; / she sits down facing every tent peg / and opens her quiver for any arrow.
## Grace upon Grace
26:13 A wife's grace delights her husband, / and his bones grow strong from her good sense.
26:14 A quiet wife is the Lord's gift, / and nothing can be given in exchange for a well-taught soul.
26:15 A modest wife is grace on top of grace, / and no scale can weigh what a self-controlled soul is worth.
26:16 The sun as it climbs in the Lord's high places, / and the beauty of a good wife in the order of his house.
26:17 A lamp blazing on the holy lampstand, / and a lovely face above a firm, full-grown figure.
26:18 Golden pillars on a silver base, / and shapely feet set under a steady breast.
## The Longer Greek: Your Own Field
26:19 [My child, keep the prime of your life healthy, / and do not give your strength to strangers.]
26:20 [Search the whole plain for a fertile plot, / and sow your own seed there, trusting in your good family line.]
26:21 [So your children will live on after you, / and with the confidence of good birth they will grow great.]
26:22 [A woman for hire will count as spit, / but a married woman will count as a tower of death for those who make use of her.]
26:23 [An ungodly woman will be given as a portion to a lawless man, / but a godly woman is given to the one who fears the Lord.]
26:24 [A shameless woman will spend her days in disgrace, / but a modest daughter will treat even her husband with respect.]
26:25 [A shameless woman will be thought of as a dog, / but one who has a sense of shame will fear the Lord.]
26:26 [A woman who honors her own husband will seem wise to everyone, / but one who dishonors him in her pride will be known by all as ungodly. / The man with a good wife is a happy man; / his years will add up to twice as many.]
26:27 [A loud woman with a busy tongue / will be looked on like a war trumpet sounded to rout an enemy. / And every person with a soul like hers / will live out life in the chaos of war.]
## What Makes Me Angry
26:28 Two things grieve my heart, / and a third makes me angry: / a soldier who goes without because he is poor, / sensible men treated like trash, / and one who leaves what is right and goes back to sin. / The Lord will make him ready for the sword.
26:29 It is hard for a merchant to stay free of wrongdoing, / and a shopkeeper will not be cleared of sin.
## Sin Between Selling and Buying
27:1 Many have sinned for something of no real value, / and whoever is out to get more will turn a blind eye.
27:2 A peg will be driven in where stones are joined, / and sin will be ground in between selling and buying.
27:3 Unless a man holds on tight to the fear of the Lord, / his household will come crashing down fast.
## Tested by Talk
27:4 Shake a sieve and the rubbish stays in it; / so the trash in a man shows in his reasoning.
27:5 A furnace proves a potter's jars, / and people are tested by how they talk things through.
27:6 The fruit shows how a tree was tended; / so the words a man says show the thoughts of his heart.
27:7 Do not praise anyone until you have heard him reason, / for this is how people are tested.
27:8 If you go after what is right, you will catch it / and put it on like an ankle-length robe of glory.
27:9 Birds settle down with their own kind, / and truth returns to the people who work at it.
27:10 A lion sets an ambush for its prey; / so sins ambush those who do wrong.
27:11 The talk of a godly person is always wisdom, / but a fool keeps changing, like the moon.
27:12 Among people without sense, keep an eye on the time; / among thinkers, stay as long as you like.
27:13 What fools talk about is disgusting, / and their laughter comes from enjoying sin.
27:14 The talk of someone who swears oaths all the time makes your hair stand on end, / and their quarrels make you stop up your ears.
27:15 When proud people fight, blood is spilled, / and their insults are painful to hear.
## Giving Away a Secret
27:16 Whoever tells secrets has ruined trust, / and he will never find a friend who suits him.
27:17 Be fond of your friend and be someone he can trust. / But if you let out his secrets, do not run after him.
27:18 You have wiped out your neighbor's friendship / just as a man wipes out his enemy.
27:19 You have let your neighbor go, like a bird let out of your hand, / and you will not trap him again.
27:20 Do not chase him, for he has gone far away; / he has run off like a gazelle out of a trap.
27:21 For a wound can be bound up, / and an insult can be patched up, / but the one who has given away secrets has lost all hope.
## The Winker and His Trap
27:22 A man who winks is building up trouble, / and no one will talk him out of it.
27:23 In front of you he will sweet-talk you / and marvel at your words, / but later he will twist his mouth / and set a stumbling block in your words.
27:24 Many things I have hated, but none compare with him; / the Lord will hate him as well.
27:25 Toss a stone up high and it lands on your own head; / a sneaking blow hands out wounds all around.
27:26 The one who digs a pit falls into it, / and the one who sets a trap is caught in it.
27:27 Evil things come rolling back on the one who does them, / and he cannot tell where they came from.
27:28 Mockery and abuse are the way of the proud, / but vengeance waits to pounce on him like a lion.
27:29 A trap will catch those who are glad when godly people fall, / and agony will wear them away before they die.
## Anger and Forgiveness
27:30 Fury and anger are abhorrent things too, / and a sinner clings tight to both.
28:1 The one who takes revenge will find revenge from the Lord, / and the Lord will keep his sins firmly fixed.
28:2 Forgive the wrong your neighbor did to you, / and then, when you pray, your own sins will be released.
28:3 One person keeps up his anger against another, / and does he look to the Lord for healing?
28:4 He has no mercy on a man like himself, / and does he plead for his own sins?
28:5 He is only flesh, yet he holds on to his wrath; / who will make atonement for his sins?
28:6 Remember the last things and stop being an enemy; / remember decay and death, and keep living by the commandments.
28:7 Keep the commandments in mind and do not hold a grudge against your neighbor; / keep in mind the covenant of the Most High, and let mistakes go.
## Quarrels and the Spark
28:8 Stay away from fights, and you will sin less; / for a hot-tempered man sets fights ablaze,
28:9 and a sinful man stirs up trouble between friends / and throws slander in among people at peace.
28:10 A fire burns as much as it has fuel, / and a man's anger is as great as his strength; / his rage rises with his wealth, / and the more stubborn the fight, the hotter it burns.
28:11 A quarrel in a hurry lights a fire, / and a fight in a hurry spills blood.
28:12 Blow on a spark and it bursts into flame; / spit on it and it goes out. / Both come from your own mouth.
## The Tongue of a Third Party
28:13 Curse the whisperer and the double-talker, / for they have brought ruin on many who were at peace.
28:14 The tongue of a third party has shaken many / and driven them from one nation to another; / it has torn down fortified cities / and overturned the houses of great men.
28:15 The tongue of a third party has driven out wives of strength / and taken away all they had worked for.
28:16 Whoever pays attention to it will never find rest / or pitch his tent in quiet.
28:17 A whip's lash leaves welts, / but a tongue's lash breaks bones.
28:18 The sword's mouth has cut down many, / yet not so many as the tongue has brought down.
28:19 Happy the one who has found shelter from it, / who has not passed through its rage, / who has not pulled its yoke / or been bound in its chains.
28:20 For its yoke is an iron yoke, / and its chains are bronze chains.
28:21 The death it brings is an evil death, / and Hades would be more welcome than that.
28:22 It will have no power over godly people, / and its flame will not burn them.
28:23 Those who leave the Lord behind will fall into it; / it will blaze inside them and never go out. / It will be let loose on them like a lion / and maul them like a leopard.
28:24 Look: put a hedge of thorns around your land, / and tie up your silver and gold.
28:25 And for your words make a scale and a weight, / and for your mouth make a door and a bar.
28:26 Be careful not to slip up with it, / so you do not fall down in front of an ambush.
## Lending to a Neighbor
29:1 Whoever shows mercy will lend to his neighbor, / and whoever gives him strength with his hand keeps the commandments.
29:2 Lend to your neighbor when he is in need, / and in turn pay your neighbor back on time.
29:3 Stand by your word and keep faith with him, / and at any time your needs will be met.
29:4 Many have treated a loan like a lucky find / and have made trouble for those who helped them.
29:5 Until he gets it, he kisses the man's hand / and lowers his voice when he speaks of his neighbor's money. / But when it is time to pay it back, he keeps putting it off, / pays back in tired excuses, and blames the times.
29:6 If the lender presses him, he will barely get back half / and will count even that a lucky find. / If not, the borrower has robbed him of his money, / and he has gotten himself an enemy, at no small cost. / The borrower pays him back with curses and insults, / and instead of honor he pays him back with dishonor.
29:7 Many have refused to lend because of such wickedness; / they are afraid of being robbed for nothing.
## The Poor and the Treasure
29:8 Still, be patient with a person in a low position, / and do not keep him waiting for alms.
29:9 Because the commandment says so, take hold of the poor man and help him; / when he is in want, do not turn him away with nothing.
29:10 Be willing to lose money for your brother and your friend, / rather than let it rust away under a rock until it is gone.
29:11 Store up your treasure as the Most High commands, / and it will pay you better than gold.
29:12 Lock up alms in your storerooms, / and alms will rescue you from every harm.
29:13 It will do battle on your side when the enemy faces you, / better than a strong shield or a powerful spear.
## Standing Good for a Neighbor
29:14 A good man will stand good for his neighbor, / but one who has lost all shame will leave him in the lurch.
29:15 Never forget what your guarantor did for you; / he put his life up for you.
29:16 A sinner will overturn the good things his guarantor has,
29:17 and a man with an ungrateful mind will desert the one who saved him.
29:18 Standing good for others has ruined many who were doing well / and tossed them like a wave of the sea; / it has sent strong men into exile, / and they roamed about among foreign peoples.
29:19 The sinner who rushes into being a guarantor, / chasing profit from contracts, will fall into lawsuits.
29:20 Help your neighbor as far as you are able, / and watch yourself so that you do not fall in.
## Better Your Own Roof
29:21 What life needs first is water, bread and clothing, / and a house to keep you decently covered.
29:22 Better to live poor under a shelter of beams / than to eat splendid meals in strangers' homes.
29:23 Be satisfied with a little or a lot.
29:24 A wretched life it is, going from house to house; / wherever a man lodges as a stranger, he will not open his mouth.
29:25 You will serve as host and pour the wine, all for no thanks, / and on top of that you will hear bitter words:
29:26 "Come over here, lodger, and set the table; / if you have anything on hand, give it to me to eat."
29:27 "Out you go, lodger; an honored guest is here. / My brother has come to stay, and I need the room."
29:28 These things are hard for a person of good sense to bear: / being scolded over a place to stay, / and being insulted by a lender.
