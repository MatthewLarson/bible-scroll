# Hostile review: Sirach 13-18 (part 13-18, tier dc)

Reviewed 2026-10-31 against `texts/orig/Sir.json` (Swete; `fixed` list read), `texts/src/Sir.app.json`, `texts/src/Sir.heb.json` (Kahana), `texts/src/Sir.lat.json`, `texts/en/Sir.json` (KJV Apocrypha), and sources opened in this session: bensira.org transcriptions by Martin Abegg and manuscript images (MS A leaves V recto to VI verso, MS C V recto), Hart 1909 (archive.org `ecclesiasticusgr00hart`, OCR text, plus page images n38 and n39 viewed), Calvin's Institutes (CCEL, Beveridge), Luther's Bondage of the Will (archive.org scan `martinlutheronth00luthuoft`, Vaughan 1823), Augustine's On Grace and Free Will (newadvent), the Confession of Dositheus (catholicity.elcore.net and crivoice.org), the Catechism (scborromeo.org copy of the 2nd ed.), Sefaria (Bava Kamma 92b, Sanhedrin 100b, Avot 3:15, Berakhot 9:5, Kiddushin 30b, Genesis Rabbah 9:7), and Perseus (Iliad 6.146ff). `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Sir/13-18` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings (168 verses, 97 notes, 41 variants, 74 glosses, 6 chapters, 21 topic contributions).

## Verdict

The part is careful and well read, and the Greek base text is followed closely. I found no invented source, reading or fragment, and the three departures from Vaticanus (13:24, 14:20, 17:13) are real readings that Swete's notes confirm. But it is not ready to publish. The free-will card (ch. 15) asks a question that most of its rows do not answer, and it asserts "Every person now" for the Catholic and Orthodox rows with no source that says so. Several Hebrew readings credited to "manuscript A" are really Kahana's 1912 text and differ from the manuscript (13:12 reads "ruler" in the manuscript, not "stumbling block"; 16:14 has a second line the part says does not exist). The Hebrew given for 18:23 cannot be traced to any manuscript I could read. The gap-line for 13:14 follows the Vulgate's punctuation, not Hart's. "Atonement" with God as subject is loaded and unexplained in three of five places. The 16:16 theory note contains a forbidden hedge and states an inference from silence as fact. The chapter `settings` are one boilerplate block pasted eleven times, with an internal contradiction and no sources. Everything is fixable by editing; nothing needs a rewrite.

Count: 0 critical, 9 major, 22 minor.

## MAJOR

### 1. chapters.json ch. 15 `traditions` card, and the ch. 15 `readings` and `deep`: most rows do not answer the card's own question, and "Every person now" is unsupported
Question asked: does 15:14 "describe every person now, or humanity as first created?" Check of each row's source against that question:
- **Catholic (Catechism 1730, 1739).** I read both at scborromeo.org. 1730 says "God created man a rational being" and quotes Gaudium et Spes 17 (footnote cites Sir 15:14) for freedom as created. 1739 says "Man's freedom is limited and fallible. In fact, man failed." Neither paragraph says the verse describes "every person now". The card's lead-in "Every person now" is the writer's inference. 1730 is in fact the "as created" use. Fix: say what the Catechism does with the verse (cites it for the dignity of freedom as created, then 1739 on its wounding) and drop "Every person now".
- **Orthodox (Dositheus, Decree 14).** Quotes are exact (read at elcore and crivoice). But Decree 14 never cites Sirach 15:14. It also says the regenerate "must be guided and prevented by grace" for spiritual good; the card applies that to "a person" generally and omits "before he is regenerated, is able by nature to incline to what is good ... and work moral good". The card's "Every person now" and ch. 15's `views.O` sentence "Ben Sira's chapter fits that teaching" are the writer's, not Dositheus's. Decree 4 ("God is in no wise the author of evil") is cited in the 15:20 note but `dositheus-decrees-ezk` lists only Decrees 3, 6 and 14.
- **Lutheran (Luther, Vaughan).** Quotes verified in Part III (the "might justly reject this book" passage; the dominion-over-creatures reading; Sect. XIV "makes against Freewill, not for it"). But Luther's reading is about two kingdoms (earthly rule vs. God's commands), not "as first created". In Sect. XVIII he even says the passage speaks "not of the first man only, but of any man, and every man". So the row does not fit the card's either/or.
- **Reformed (Calvin 2.5.18).** This is the only row that answers the question as posed. Verified in CCEL: "well known to be a writer of doubtful authority", "Grant that man received at his creation a power ... what, then, if we ... reply that he has lost it?", "what he now wants is a physician, and not a defender". The card splices "fallen man 'now wants is a physician'", which is ungrammatical (see minor 17). The translation (Beveridge) is not named in `calvin-institutes`.
- **Anglican (Articles VI, X).** Neither article mentions 15:14 or the question; Article VI is about the book's status and Article X is about the fallen will. The row does not answer the question.
- **ch. 15 `readings`, second paragraph:** "Catholic and Orthodox teaching have read it of every person; Luther and Calvin read it of creation, or of the lower world." The first half is unsourced; "Luther ... of creation" is wrong (see Lutheran above).
- **Fix.** Reframe the question to what the sources actually answer, for example: "What does 'left in the hand of his own counsel' (15:14) show about the human will?" Rows: Catholic (CC 1730/1739 as above), Lutheran (Luther: rule over creatures, makes against free will), Reformed (Calvin: true of creation, lost in the fall), and either drop Orthodox and Anglican or keep them with an honest label ("Does not cite the verse; on the will itself:"). Remove the unsupported "Every person now" lines from the card, the `views` and the `readings`.

### 2. notes.json 13:12 ("keep your words to himself"): the Hebrew credited to manuscript A is Kahana's reading, not the manuscript's
Note says: "The Hebrew of Genizah manuscript A reads: 'a cruel man sets a stumbling block and does not pity; against the lives of many he ties a plot.'" Kahana prints אַכְזָרִי יִתֵּן כֹּשֶׂל. The Abegg transcription of MS A (leaf A V verso, line 5v:6) reads אכזרי יתן **מושל**, and I looked at the manuscript image: the word is plainly מושל ("ruler/dominion"). Hart (1909, commentary on 13:12) also reports the Hebrew as "he that is cruel will establish dominion". So "stumbling block" is Kahana's emendation or misprint, and the note attributes it to the manuscript. The Plain does not depend on it, but the note's Hebrew claim is wrong. Fix: give MS A's מושל ("a cruel man will give (set up) a ruler/dominion") with Hart's rendering, and say Kahana prints כשל.

### 3. variants.json 16:14, notes.json 16:14, topics.json `almsgiving`: the Hebrew has two lines, not one
The variant (R2, `why`) and the note say the Hebrew has "a single short line" "Everyone who does tsedaqah has a reward". Kahana prints only that first colon. MS A (leaf A VI verso, line 6v:17) continues: וכל אדם כמעשיו יצא לפניו, "and every person, according to his deeds, will come out before him". That second line matches the Greek's second line ("each one will find according to his works"). So the Hebrew does not replace the Greek line; it has a different first line and a parallel second line. The note title "Almsgiving, or righteousness?" and the topic sentence ("where the Hebrew has 'everyone who does tsedaqah has a reward'") remain true for line 1 but the "different line / single line" claim must go. Fix: give both Hebrew lines from MS A.

### 4. variants.json 18:23 and 18:33, chapters.json ch. 18 `views.J` and `views.H`, notes.json 18:23 and 18:33: Hebrew of chapter 18 is not traced to a manuscript
Brief rule: cite Hebrew only from manuscripts read in the session. Kahana has only 18:16, 18:32 and 18:33 for this chapter.
- **18:32-33 (Greek 18:32-33):** these are in MS C leaf C V recto (bensira.org: "Ben Sira 18:31-33; 19:1-3; 20:5-6", image from Gaster's 1900 JQR article, location unknown). I read Abegg's transcription (5r:1-4): אל תשמח אל שמץ תענוג אשר פי שנים רישו / אל תהי זולל וסובא ומאומ[ה] אין **בכיס**. Kahana prints **בַּכִּים** (a misprint; "purse" is כיס), and the final ה of ומאומה is bracketed (lost) in the transcription. The variant's Hebrew text `אֵין בַּכִּים` is therefore not a word and not the manuscript's. The label "a Cairo Genizah manuscript" should name MS C, and the final ה should not be presented as read.
- **18:16 (Kahana) = Greek 18:23:** I scanned every bensira.org transcription for MSS A (3:6-16:26), B, C, D, E, F (and the listing for Masada and Qumran covers other chapters). No leaf contains בטרם תדור הכן נדרך. I cannot show that this line is from any manuscript. The variant (R2) and note call it "a Cairo Genizah manuscript"; ch. 18 `views.J` states "the Hebrew of 18:23 says 'before you vow, prepare your vow.'" That is an unverified Hebrew claim of the kind the lead's points forbid.
- **Fix:** name MS C for 18:32-33 and correct the word; for 18:23 either drop the Hebrew reading and the J sentence, or cite it only as "Kahana's edition (1912), source not named there".

### 5. ἐξιλασμός / ἐξιλάσκομαι as "atonement" with God as subject: 16:7, 16:11, 17:29, 18:12, 18:20
The Plain says "He made no atonement for the giants" (16:7), "mighty in atonement" (16:11), "the atonement he gives" (17:29), "he made his atonement abundant" (18:12), "you will find atonement" (18:20). This follows the term entry in the works proposal, but:
- English "atonement" carries the Christian and Yom Kippur sense of a sin-covering that God provides or that a priest performs. In 16:7 the Hebrew is "did not forgive/lift" (נשא, which I read in MS A at 6v:10); in 16:11 the Hebrew is "bears and pardons" (ונושא וסולח). The 16:11 note says the Greek is "the Greek word for making atonement (Hebrew kipper)". The Hebrew behind 16:11 is not kipper.
- 17:29 and 18:12 have no note at all; 16:7 has one that says "did not wipe their sin away". The gloss at 18:12 ("wiped away their sins generously") and at 16:11 impose one meaning.
- 16:7 as a bare sentence ("made no atonement for the giants") reads as nonsense to an 8th-grade reader.
- **Fix:** keep "atonement" only where it can be explained, or use "forgiveness/pardon" with a note: "Greek *exilasmos*, usually 'atoning sacrifice' or 'appeasement'; here the Hebrew has 'bear/forgive'". Put one note at 16:7 (first use) and short pointers at 16:11, 17:29, 18:12, 18:20. Remove "(Hebrew kipper)" from the 16:11 note.

### 6. notes.json 16:16 `spec` note, "why 16:15-16 dropped out"

Also states as flat fact "but not in Vaticanus, Sinaiticus or Alexandrinus. The Latin Vulgate lacks them too." The Vulgate point I verified in `Sir.lat.json` (16:15 there is the "merit of works" line, no Pharaoh line). ### 7. variants.json 13:14 (and 13:13 R2): the gap line does not follow Hart's text
Brief: gap lines must come from a witness actually read. The variant says the Plain is "translated from Hart's text of minuscule 248 and the Vulgate, which agree." I viewed Hart's page (scan n38). Hart's 248 prints, at the end of 13:13: ἀκούων ταῦτα ἐν ὕπνῳ σου γρηγόρησον **πάσῃ ζωῇ.** ἀγάπα τὸν κύριον καὶ ἐπικαλοῦ αὐτὸν εἰς σωτηρίαν σου. With the full stop after ζωῇ, "all your life" goes with "be wakeful", not with "love the Lord". The Vulgate (13:18 in Latin numbering, `Sir.lat.json`) reads "Omni vita tua dilige Deum", with "all your life" on the loving. So the two do not agree, and the variant's typed Greek πάσῃ ζωῇ ἀγάπα ... silently moves the stop. Hart's commentary (13:13, 106. 248. 253 and "p") also notes "γρηγόρησον πάσῃ ζωῇ ... ἀγάπα" as one sentence in the Greek with "wake" first. Also R2's Plain ("When you hear this, wake up from your sleep") turns ἐν ὕπνῳ ("in your sleep") into "from your sleep", which the variant's own `en` ("in your sleep, wake up") does not say. The accents and letters of the typed Greek match the scan. Fix: follow Hart's pointing (or say plainly that the Plain follows the Vulgate's division here), and make R2's Plain say "in your sleep".

### 8. variants.json 14:20, 14:16 (and 13:24 `why`): witness lists understated at the departures
- **14:20.** The variant lists Vaticanus against "a later corrector of Sinaiticus" and the Hebrew. Swete's note reads τελευτησει] μελετησει ℵc.a, so ℵ (first hand) and A agree with Vaticanus and the Vulgate has "morietur" (Hart notes it as "preserving the true reading in an edifying gloss"). On the other side, Hart's commentary credits μελετήσει to ℵc.a, 70, 106, 248, 253 and "p" (the main body of cursives), with καλά added in 70, 106, 248, 253. The Plain's choice (departing from Vaticanus) is defensible and the Hebrew יהגה (read in MS A, 6r:9) is decisive. But a reader is told the choice is Vaticanus vs. one corrector. List the real witnesses on both sides.
- **14:16.** Reading "sanctify" (ἁγίασον) is credited only to ℵc.a; Hart credits 106, 248, 253, 296 and "p", and I saw 248 print ἁγίασον on the scan (n39). Add them.
- **13:24 `why`:** "A single letter (eusebous / asebous) separates the two" is wrong: εὐ- vs α- differs by two letters. Swete: ευσεβους] ασεβους ℵA (so ℵ and A, not just "Sinaiticus and Alexandrinus" as first-hand witnesses; the part's wording is fine) and the Hebrew "insolent" (MS A 5v:18-19: על פי זדון, verified).
- **17:13** is correct (Swete: ουτων 2⁰] αυτου ℵC; Hart adds 70, 155, 253, 254, 296, 307). Fine, but the `why` ("Israel did not hear its own voice at Sinai") argues from sense against a reading the base text has; say that the choice is a reasoned departure and give the extra witnesses.

### 9. chapters.json: every `settings` card is the same boilerplate; internal contradiction; no sources; debated points stated flat
- The same `to` ("his students ... sons of Jerusalem's upper class who would deal with rich patrons, rulers, banquets and loans") and the same `when` ("about 190-180 BC ... before the crisis of the 160s") are used for the disputation on free will (15:11-20), the creation and covenant hymn (16:24-17:14), the call to repentance (17:25-32) and the hymn on God's greatness (18:1-14). For those passages the "rich patrons, banquets and loans" audience makes no sense and `why` does not say what changes once you know who heard it.
- Ch. 13: `to` says "sons of Jerusalem's upper class" but `why` says "a young man of modest means". Contradictory. The class of Ben Sira's students is an inference (51:23 says only "house of instruction"); it should be in `views` as debated, with evidence, not stated flat.
- No `s` on any setting; the dates (c. 190-180 BC; grandson in Egypt after 132 BC) are given with no source opened (see the lead's note that Wikipedia is not a source for dates). Rule 2 applies.
- `readers`: "Cairo Genizah manuscript A holds 13:7-16:26" (copied into 11 cards) is wrong: A V recto (T-S 12.864) holds 11:34-13:6 and the manuscript runs from 3:6 to 16:26 (bensira.org listing: V verso 13:7-14:6; VI recto 14:11-15:19; VI verso 15:19-16:26). It also contradicts the part's own note that 13:1 is on A V recto.
- Fix: write a distinct `to`/`why`/`readers` for each unit, put the class question into `views`, cite a printed source for dates, and correct the manuscript range.
