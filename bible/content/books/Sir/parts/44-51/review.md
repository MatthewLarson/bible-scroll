# Hostile review: Sirach 44-51 (Praise of the Fathers, Simon, closing prayer, Hebrew psalm, acrostic)

Reviewer note on method. I read the Plain against `texts/orig/Sir.json` verse by verse, and Swete's apparatus (`texts/src/Sir.app.json`) and the Vulgate (`texts/src/Sir.lat.json`). I downloaded Abegg's MS B transcriptions (folios XIII v to XXI v), Abegg-Parker's translations, Reymond's Masada column VII and the 11QPsa transcription from bensira.org in this session and checked every cited line. I checked Josephus (Whiston, lexundria.com) and the Mishnah (Sefaria) and ran a script over all quotations of the 66 books in the notes against the current Plain. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Sir/44-51` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is careful, mostly faithful work. The Hebrew line references hold up: I found no invented MS B, Masada or 11QPsa reading, and the quotations of the 66 books match the current Plain (four trivial quote-mark or paraphrase mismatches, listed under minor). The seven departures from Swete are real, correctly identified and justified in the variants. But the part has one verse-break error the lead already knows about (45:12/13), one translation that tilts a contested verse (48:11 "a true life"), one misreported Josephus passage that supports the whole Simon dating, one MS B "reading" that is the writer's own gloss (50:3), at least two false or unsupported church/Jewish-practice statements, and a cluster of superlatives ("oldest", "earliest", "only") that STYLE.md rule 2 does not allow without a source. Several departures from Swete (50:8, 45:17, 47:5) have no variant entry although the project rule demands one. The 49:10 Twelve Prophets wording is verified below so the lead can restore the Zechariah note, but with a weaker claim than the current topic page makes.

Counts: critical 0, major 10, minor 22.

## Major findings

1. **Major. plain.txt 45:12 and 45:13 (verse break).** The digital break was corrected after writing. `ὡραῖα` ("beautiful things") now opens 45:13: `ὡραῖα πρὸ αὐτοῦ οὐ γέγονεν τοιαῦτα`. The Plain ends 45:12 with "adorned and lovely" (that is `ὡραῖα`, borrowed from 45:13) and 45:13 begins "Before him there had never been anything like them", dropping the word. Fix: 45:12 end "...delights for the eyes, adorned." and 45:13 begin "Beautiful things like these had never been before him;". Also re-read the notes on 45:12 and 45:13 (nothing there depends on "lovely").

2. **Major. plain.txt 48:11, "For we too will live a true life."** Greek `ζωῇ ζησόμεθα` is a cognate-dative intensifier ("we shall surely live"), not "a true life". "True" is not in the Greek and pushes the verse toward an eternal-life reading, which the note on this verse itself calls the less cautious one. The Vaticanus line is also set against a Latin line that denies it, so the Plain should not lean. Fix: "For we too shall surely live." (NRSV-type wording is "surely live"; do not copy it, but the sense is the same.) The gloss "made beautiful by love" / "whose love made them shine" is fine.

3. **Major. notes.json 50:1 ("Which Simon?"), topics.json `onias`, chapters.json ch. 50 views H.** The note says Josephus "says that when an Onias died, 'left the high priesthood to his son Simeon' (Antiquities 12.224) ... in the time of Antiochus III, around 200 BC." I opened 12.224 (and the paragraph around it, Whiston 12.4.10). The sentence is there, but Josephus places it when "Seleucus, who was called Soter, reigned over Asia, being the son of Antiochus the Great", that is Seleucus IV (187-175 BC), and the Onias who dies is Joseph's uncle (Onias II). Josephus gives no "around 200 BC" and no Antiochus III here. So the note, topic page and the H view attribute to Josephus a date he does not give. The 12.138 and 12.141 quotations (garrison; "the work about the temple finished, and the cloisters", timber tax-free) are exact. Fix: cite 12.224 only for "Onias died and left the high priesthood to his son Simeon" and say that Josephus sets this in the days of Seleucus IV; put the 200 BC date on a scholarly source actually opened (none is in sources.json now), or drop "most historians" and "about 200 BC" to what can be sourced. Remove "Ben Sira, who saw him" from topics.json `onias` (an assumption; the chapter's own setting says "probably after Simon's death").

4. **Major. notes.json 50:3 (and variants.json 50:3).** The note says MS B has "'a pool like the sea in its abundance' (אשיח בם בהמונו)". The Hebrew in Abegg's transcription (19r:9) is `אשר בדורו נכרה מקוה אשיח בם בהמונו`. There is no word for "sea" in it, and Abegg-Parker translate the line "the reservoir was dug, the pool in them in its vastness". "like the sea" is the writer's own gloss, borrowed from the Greek, shown as the manuscript's meaning. The same note says Alexandrinus `λακκος` for `χαλκος` is "a one-letter difference". It is two letters (`χαλ-` against `λακ-`). Fix: give B as "the reservoir was dug; the pool in them in its vastness" (the second line is obscure) and drop "like the sea" from B; say two letters. The Plain's "cut a pool" is fine and the variant is right to mark it as a departure, but add that ℵA read plural `ἀποδοχεῖα`/`-χια` (Swete's note).

5. **Major. chapters.json ch. 49 views C.** "Catholic and Orthodox Bibles follow the Greek here, which lacks Job; the Latin agrees with the Greek ('inimicorum')." The Latin point is right (Vulgate 49:11 `commemoratus est inimicorum in imbre`). But the claim about Catholic Bibles is not safe and I could not confirm it: search results show the NRSV (the Bible most Catholic study editions share) renders 49:9 with Job, with a footnote on the Greek, and the NAB text I found also lacks "enemies". Nothing opened supports "Catholic Bibles follow the Greek". Remove or replace with what is sourced (the Vulgate and Douay read "enemies"). The O line for ch. 49 ("Orthodox readers note Jeshua's Greek name... and the Christian reading of Zechariah's 'two sons of oil'") and the O/C lines for ch. 45, 46, 50 and 51 say nothing church-specific and name no source; `agent-writer-works.md` says a row with nothing distinct is better dropped. Drop them or source them.

6. **Major. Superlatives without sources: notes.json 49:10, 48:10, 48:24, topics `book-of-the-twelve`, `elijah`, `isaiah`, chapters.json ch. 48 H, ch. 49 readings.** Claims: "the oldest known reference to the Minor Prophets as one group", "the earliest writer outside the prophets to take up Malachi 4:5-6", "48:10 is the earliest use of Malachi outside the prophets, which shows the Prophets were a fixed collection", "about 180 BC the whole book, chapters 1-66, was read as Isaiah's", "So about 180 BC Hosea to Malachi were already one collection". None is sourced in sources.json and none can be read off the text. The text gives less: 48:24 alludes to Isaiah 40:1 and 61:2-3 only; 49:10 says "the twelve prophets", which can mean twelve individual men.
   **Verified wording for the lead (49:10).** Greek (Swete, Vaticanus): `καὶ τῶν ιβ᾽ προφητῶν τὰ ὀστᾶ ἀναθάλοι ἐκ τοῦ τόπου αὐτῶν. παρεκάλεσεν δὲ τὸν Ἰακώβ, καὶ ἐλυτρώσατο αὐτοὺς ἐν πίστει ἐλπίδος.` The number is the numeral ιβ᾽ with the article `τῶν`; Alexandrinus spells `δώδεκα` and reads plural `παρεκάλεσαν`, `ἐλυτρώσαντο` (and `γάρ` for `δέ`, with ℵ corrected). MS B 18v:15-16 (Abegg): `וגם שנים עשר הנביאים ... תהי עצמתם פרחות מתחתם ... אשר החלימו את יעקב ... וישעוהו ב[`, "and also the twelve prophets, may their bones sprout from beneath them, who restored Jacob and saved him". Hebrew has no article. The Vulgate (49:12) reads `duodecim prophetarum ossa pullulent de loco suo: nam corroboraverunt Jacob, et redemerunt se`, plural verbs. So three witnesses (A, B, Latin) make the Twelve the subject of "comforted/restored", and the singular is Vaticanus alone. Safe statement for a Zechariah note: "Ben Sira, about 180 BC, speaks of 'the twelve prophets' as a group whose bones he wishes to sprout; most scholars take this as evidence the Twelve were already read together." Do not say "oldest", "earliest" or "already one book" unless a source is opened.

7. **Major. chapters.json ch. 48 views J: "the cup of Elijah at the Passover table and the chair of Elijah at a circumcision come from that hope [Elijah as herald of the Messiah]".** The chair does not come from messianic hope. Search results (JTSA commentary; Maḥzor Vitry; Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer ch. 29, an 8th-9th century work) tie the chair to a midrash in which Elijah, who complained that Israel had abandoned the covenant (1 Kings 19), must witness every circumcision, and to Malachi 3:1 "messenger of the covenant". No source for the cup is in the folder. Fix: rewrite with a source actually opened, or cut to "Jewish tradition gave Elijah a central place as herald of the Messiah" (Malachi 3:23-24, which the note quotes) and say the chair of Elijah is tied to circumcision, not to this hope. The same "broad facts kept without sources" problem hits the Orthodox line ("honors Elijah as a living prophet who did not die") and the Catholic line on relics; source or soften.

8. **Major. Unflagged departures from Swete (variant entry required).** (a) 50:8 "like rose blossoms in the spring days": Greek `ἐν ἡμέραις νέων`, no "spring". "Spring" is the Vulgate (`in diebus vernis`, Vulgate 50:8); MS B has `בימי מועד` ("days of the appointed time", line 19r:15, garbled). Add a variant. (b) 45:17 "He gave him power through his commandments": Swete prints accusative `ἔδωκεν αὐτὸν ἐν ἐντολαῖς αὐτοῦ`; the Plain reads the dative of ℵA (Swete's note `αυτον] αυτω ℵA`) and B's `ויתן לו כל מצותיו`. Add a variant. (c) 47:5 "lift up the horn of his people": B has finite future `ἀνυψώσει`, the Plain follows the infinitive of ℵAC (apparatus). Add a variant (minor in sense). (d) 44:20 "and God took him into a covenant": subject of `ἐγένετο ἐν διαθήκῃ μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ` is Abraham ("he came to be in covenant with him"); MS B has `ובא בברית עמו`. The Plain changes the subject. It is a mistranslation, not a variant; fix to "and he entered into a covenant with him".

9. **Major. chapters.json ch. 50 readings: "This is the only description of the temple service by someone who saw it, written about 180 BC."** Unsourced superlative and an unproved premise: whether Ben Sira describes the service from sight is an inference, and "only" is a claim about every other text. (I could not verify it. The Letter of Aristeas, which describes the Jerusalem temple service and claims an eyewitness, is a lead the writer should check before keeping "only".) Fix: "Ben Sira describes the service so vividly that many take it as memory". Same for "Ben Sira, who saw him" in topics `onias` (see 3).

10. **Major. notes.json 50:22 and sources.json `rinkart-nun-danket`.** Source for Rinkart is the Wikipedia article, which the lead's points say is not an acceptable source for dates. The note adds "Rinkart followed the Greek text the KJV also used", which no source supports; Rinkart worked from Luther's German, which cannot be assumed to follow a Greek text the KJV used. Replace the source with a printed or hymnal reference actually opened, or reduce the note to what that source says ("Nun danket alle Gott", from Sirach 50 in Luther's Bible), and delete the KJV claim.

## Minor findings

11. **Minor. plain.txt 46:19.** "he swore before the Lord and his anointed" renders `ἐπεμαρτύρατο ἔναντι Κυρίου καὶ χριστοῦ` ("called as witness", "testified"; MS B `העיד ייי ומשיחו`, Abegg-Parker "testified to the LORD and his anointed"). Not an oath. Fix "he called the Lord and his anointed to witness".

12. **Minor. plain.txt 49:6-7.** "That nation burned the chosen city... because of Jeremiah. ... They had done him harm": "They" now reads as the foreign nation. Greek and Hebrew (`ביד ירמיהו כי ענוהו`) have a different subject in 49:7 (the people of Judah). Abegg-Parker take `ביד ירמיהו` as "in accordance with the message... by the hand of Jeremiah". The Plain picks the less usual sense ("because of") for the line; the note admits "as Jeremiah foretold" is the usual meaning. Prefer "by the hand of Jeremiah" or "as Jeremiah said", and name the subject of 49:7 ("the people had done him harm").

13. **Minor. plain.txt 44:11-12.** Verse break splits `ἐν ταῖς διαθήκαις ἔστη σπέρμα αὐτῶν`. The Plain supplies "live" in 44:11 and then repeats "stood firm" in 44:12. Acceptable under the one-Greek-verse rule, but "live" is added; mention in a note or smooth.

14. **Minor. plain.txt 44:17 "he became the exchange".** Definite article is awkward (`ἀντάλλαγμα`, "a substitute / a ransom"). "an exchange" or "a substitute".

15. **Minor. plain.txt 50:2.** "laid to its full height": "full" is not in `ἐθεμελιώθη ὕψος διπλῆς`. MS B (19r:10) differs (`נבנה קיר פנות מעון בהיכל מלך`). No note or variant records any of this; the gloss "retaining wall" is fine. Add a short note that the verse is hard.

16. **Minor. plain.txt 50:24.** "May his mercy stay faithfully with us": `ἐνπιστεῦσαι` is "to entrust", as the variant says. The Plain gives the Hebrew sense (`יאמן`). Either say so in the variant `why` or render "entrust his mercy to us". The variant `why` also calls the Hebrew "clearly the older form" and the note says "one likely reason... could no longer be prayed as written": both are unsourced claims about scholarly consensus. Soften to "probably" and name a source, or say "some think".

17. **Minor. plain.txt 47:6** "honored him over his tens of thousands": `ἐν μυριάσιν` is "among/with tens of thousands" (the women's song); "over" reads as ranking. **47:10** "until the cycle was complete" is an interpretation of `μέχρι συντελείας`. **51:28** "Pay a large sum" for `μετάσχετε` ("share in/partake of"). **51:20** "left alone" for `ἐγκαταλειφθῶ` (better "abandoned"). **48:12** "wrapped from sight": "from sight" is added. **50:9** "cup" for `σκεῦος` ("vessel").

18. **Minor. variants.json 44:16 and notes 44:16 (Masada).** True to the transcription: line 22 = 44:15, line 24 = 44:17. But Reymond's column VII line 23 is blank, which the variant does not say. A blank line may be a vacat or the place where 44:16 would have stood. Say "line 23 is empty", and avoid "lacks the verse" as settled; "Masada has no text of 44:16" is accurate.

19. **Minor. variants.json 49:10.** Omits the Vulgate (plural `corroboraverunt`) and ℵc.a (reads `γάρ`, with A). Add both witnesses. Also 51:19 and 51:20 cite only Swete and B; 11QPsa col. XXI lines 19-20 (`חריתי נפשי בה ופני לוא השיבותי; טרתי נפשי בה וברומיה לוא פתחתי ומערמיה אתבונן`) and B 21r:15-17 show the Hebrew differs a lot from the Greek (nothing like `ἐν ποιήσει λιμοῦ`; `וברומיה` fits `πρὸς ὕψος`). The writer read both and should record them.

20. **Minor. notes.json 51:12 and chapters ch. 51.** The note says the Hebrew psalm has "a sixteen-line litany" (field `p`). I count fifteen lines in B (20v:12-18 and 21r:1-8, labelled 51:12a-o). The brief asks for a translation of the psalm; the note gives a list with "each with the refrain", not a translation of each line. Either give the 15 lines or call it a summary. "call-and-response form" in the chapter setting is not shown by the manuscript. "the line on the sons of Zadok would suit a time before the Hasmoneans" and "is used to argue for an early date" are unsourced; source or soften.

21. **Minor. notes.json 48:11.** B reads `אשר ראך` ("who saw you") followed by a damaged `ומת`; the note says "happy is he who saw you". Abegg-Parker restore "Blessed is the one who sees you before he dies". Say "who saw you" and mark "happy" as restored. Variant 48:11 says KJV "slept in love" comes from "a Greek text with 'fell asleep'"; the writer saw no such Greek. Say "from a longer or different text the KJV followed".

22. **Minor. notes.json 49:9, variants 49:9.** In B 18v:14 the letters of `נביא` and of `דרכי` are marked uncertain in Abegg's transcription. The variant's `orig` prints them as plain text. Mark them as damaged or note "prophet" is partly lost.

23. **Minor. chapters.json settings (all chapters).** The repeated `readers` paragraph says "His grandson brought the book to Egypt in 132 BC". The prologue says the grandson came to Egypt in the 38th year of Euergetes; whether he brought the book is not stated. Say "came to Egypt in 132 BC and translated". It is repeated six times, so one fix reaches all. Chapter 49 has an empty `settings` list; the roll call is covered in ch. 44, but 49:8-13 (Ezekiel, the Twelve, builders) could use a short one, and the prayers/blessings at 45:26, 46:11-12 are covered only by ch. 45 and ch. 46's single entries. Consider adding.

24. **Minor. chapters.json ch. 44 deep: "The order shows that by about 180 BC the Law and the Prophets were already a known sequence of books, a point Ben Sira's grandson makes in his prologue."** Overclaim. The grandson (prologue) names "the law, the prophets and the other books", and that is after 132 BC. Soften the first sentence to "is consistent with".

25. **Minor. chapters.json ch. 49 views J.** "agrees with the Jewish view that Job was a real figure". Bava Batra 15a (opened on Sefaria) records a sage saying "Job never existed... his story was a parable" and a rebuttal; it is a debate, and the Gemara ends on a real person. Say that.

26. **Minor. chapters.json ch. 45 views J.** "Manuscript B's call... shows the hymn being used in worship": an inference, not shown. Say "suggests".

27. **Minor. notes.json 48:17.** The Siloam inscription ("an inscription carved by the workers telling how two crews tunneled from each end and met") and "still carries the spring's water to the Pool of Siloam" have no source in sources.json. Source them from something opened or cut the sentence; 2 Kings 20:20 and 2 Chronicles 32:30 are enough. Also B's `כנחשת` ("like bronze") against Greek "iron" is noted, but Plain "iron" has no variant; fine as a Greek reading.

28. **Minor. notes.json 49:13, 49:14.** In MS B 49:14 the Hebrew has "few... like Enoch" and `נלקח פנים` (A&P "taken up to God's presence"); the note says "perhaps 'into the presence'". Fine. But 49:16 Hebrew adds Enosh (`ושם ושת ואנוש`) which the note mentions; no variant entry for the missing Enosh. Add one.

29. **Minor. glosses.json.** 46:12 "sprout again": "come back to life like plants" prejudges the question the note leaves open (memory vs resurrection). Use "flourish again". 50:5 "the house of the curtain": "the holy room behind the curtain" settles the Yom Kippur-or-daily debate in favour of the inner room; use "the part of the temple behind the curtain" or drop. 50:16 gloss "hammered silver" adds "silver" the Greek does not have (Numbers 10 does). 44:17 gloss "the one kept in place of the rest" is unclear.

30. **Minor. Cross-part term.** 51:23 "untaught" matches part 01-06; part 19-23 uses "undisciplined" for `ἀπαίδευτος` (lead's point). Align before publishing.

31. **Minor. Quotation mismatches (script run).** notes 45:10: "the breastpiece for decisions" is Plain-site wording, fine. notes 45:23: "as Phinehas did" is not the current Plain of 1 Maccabees 2:26 ("as Phinehas had been against Zimri"); quote a few words that match or paraphrase without quote marks. notes 47:11 and 49:15 differ from the Plain only in nested quote marks (fix the marks).

32. **Minor. Attributions of church views.** chapters.json ch. 46 O ("have long read him as a type of Jesus"), ch. 48 C and P ("Catholic readers cite 48:13-14..."; "Protestant readers often cite 48:10..."), ch. 51 O, P: none cites a catechism, confession or named theologian. Per the brief, source or drop each.
