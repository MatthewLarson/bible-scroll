# Hostile review: Sirach 37-43 (part 37-43, tier dc)

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-31. Nothing in the content was edited. `cd bible && VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Sir/37-43` gives 210 verses, 130 notes, 30 variants, 60 glosses, 7 chapter pages, 17 topic contributions, 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The translation is faithful. I read all 210 Plain verses against Swete's Greek (`texts/orig/Sir.json`) and found no dropped, added or reversed clause worth a critical finding. The four departures (43:8, 43:9, 43:23, 43:26) are each backed by Swete's own apparatus and the Hebrew, and each has a variant entry. The Hebrew work is mostly honest. I tested every Hebrew string in the notes, variants, chapters and topic files (69 distinct strings) against Abegg's MS B and MS D transcriptions, Reymond's Masada transcription (decoded from the reversed Latin-letter code) and Kahana. None sits inside Kahana's square brackets, and I re-decoded three Masada lines myself from a fresh copy of the PDFs on bensira.org (41:3, 42:15, 41:2c-d) and got the writer's reading each time. The 103 quotations of other books that I could match (notes, chapters, topics, variants) all agree with the current Plain. Sefaria (Sanhedrin 100b, Genesis Rabbah 10:6) and the Catechism (2288, 36) say what the notes say. The problems are these: a 40:25 choice that is inconsistent with the other four departures; one Masada reading reported backwards (41:2) and two overread (39:28, 39:29); a Plain at 42:2 that softens the verb and a 42:14 note whose main reading has no source; the writer's own Asa idea leaking into unlabeled text as "many read"; an unnamed "minority" characterized in 38:13 after the writer chose to drop those views; the Satire of the Trades stated as mockery on a source that says scholars dispute it; dates with no opened source; and several meaning-changing variants in Swete's notes and the Hebrew that have no entry. No finding is critical (no invented source, reading or fragment that I could find). Ten are major.

Count: 0 critical, 10 major, 21 minor.

## Major

**1. 40:25: the Plain follows B* ("wife") against every other witness, which is inconsistent with the four departures.** plain.txt 40:25 "better than both is a wife who is valued"; variants.json 40:25.
Evidence: Swete's note (at 40:2 page) reads `25 γυνη] βουλη BᵃᵇΝAC`: the first hand of Vaticanus has γυνή; a Vaticanus corrector, Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus and Ephraemi all have βουλή ("counsel"). Kahana prints עצה טובה, but Abegg's transcription of MS B 40:25 shows only `זהב וכס]ף -- רג[ל … ומשנ]יהם[`, so the Hebrew "counsel" rests on Kahana's 1912 reading alone (the leaf is now lost at that word). The writer's own "why" concedes the evidence is strong and that B* probably repeated "wife" from 40:19 and 40:23. With "wife" the chain has three wife-sayings in a row (40:19, 40:23, 40:25) and the note at 40:19 counts 40:25 as a third praise of wives ("a wife who is valued (40:25, but see the variant)"). The writer departed from Swete for 43:8, 43:9, 43:23, 43:26 on the same kind of evidence, so the policy is unequal.
Fix: follow βουλή at 40:25 ("better than both is good advice, which is valued"), move "wife" to the second reading, and drop 40:25 from the 40:19 note. If the Plain stays with B*, say in the variant that the Hebrew is Kahana's alone and that B itself was corrected. Either way, label the Hebrew "Kahana's reading, leaf now lost" in the variant's `w`.

**2. 41:2: the variant's "why" says the Masada scroll has the "sight" reading, but the Masada letters it prints are different.** variants.json 41:2.
Evidence: Reymond's transcription, col. III line 2 (decoded and re-checked by me from the PDF: `hwqt dwb)w hrmh sp)`) is אפס המרה ואבוד תקוה, with no alef. Abegg's MS B 10v:9-10 has main text סרב ואבד תקוה ("contrary", the Greek ἀπειθοῦντι) and a margin אפס המראה ("sight"). The entry files Masada under the "sight" reading and prints the margin's spelling `אפס המראה ואבד תקוה` as its `orig`, then says "The older Masada scroll has the reading about sight." As transcribed, Masada's המרה is closer to B's main סרב and the Greek than to the margin. Either the entry misdescribes the oldest witness or it reads an alef that the transcription does not show. The note at 41:1 compounds this: it says Masada "has 'Ha, to death, how good…'" at 41:2, but Reymond's first word there is `(h` = הע (he, ayin), not האח, and the note does not say so; it also says the scroll is "damaged" at 41:1 when Reymond prints the start of הוי (`הו°]י[`).
Fix: print Masada's own spelling in its own reading line, say plainly that it matches B's main text and the Greek in sense (or that its המרה could be a defective spelling of "sight", if a published reading says so, naming it), and rewrite the "why". Quote Reymond's הע as read and do not render it "Ha" unless the transcription says האח.

**3. Masada readings overread at 39:28 and 39:29.** variants.json 39:28; chapters.json ch. 39 `deep` "The oldest Hebrew"; notes 39:28 and 39:31.
Evidence: Reymond col. I line 3 (39:28) is `[ים יע] [ת·יקו`: ים יע, then a lacuna, then תיקו with the ת marked only "possible" (`X`). Line 5 (39:29) is `[ראו`: three letters, ראו. The variant says Masada "keeps the end of the line, יעתיקו", and the chapter deep says "a few words are legible, such as 'they move' at 39:28 and 'were created' at 39:29" and that these "confirm that the Hebrew of manuscript B … goes back to an ancient text." "Were created" is not on the scroll (ראו alone could be "they saw"; נבראו is a restoration), and "they move" joins two fragments across a gap. This is the "restoration cited as reading" error the lead's rule forbids.
Fix: say "Masada col. I keeps only scraps of 39:27-32: at 39:28 the letters ים יע … יקו, with a gap and a doubtful ת, which fit the verb in B (Abegg, `ם יעתיק]ו[`)"; at 39:29 say only ראו and do not call it "were created". Keep the claim that B and Masada are compatible, but do not write that Masada "confirms" a whole word.

**4. 42:2: the Plain softens the verb and the note dismisses the exact verbal parallels with Proverbs 17:15 and Romans 4:5.** plain.txt 42:2; notes.json 42:2 ("To justify the ungodly").
Evidence: Greek περὶ κρίματος δικαιῶσαι τὸν ἀσεβῆ, "of a judgment, to justify the ungodly". Hebrew, Masada col. IV line 7 and MS B: להצדיק רשע. Proverbs 17:15 is מַצְדִּיק רָשָׁע, the same two words (checked in `texts/orig/Pro.json`); the Plain of Proverbs 17:15 is "Declaring the wicked innocent". Romans 4:5 is τὸν δικαιοῦντα τὸν ἀσεβῆ (checked in `texts/orig/Rom.json`): the same verb and object as Sirach's Greek. The Plain gives "a judgment that gives the ungodly his due", which is reading (2) of the note and is neither the Greek nor the Hebrew wording. The note never tells the reader that the Hebrew phrase is word-for-word Proverbs 17:15 or that the Greek phrase is Paul's; it says Romans 4:5 "is a different matter in a different setting." It names no one who holds readings (1), (2) or (3), so they cannot be weighed.
Fix: put the literal wording in the Plain ("and of a judgment that declares the ungodly in the right"), make the "gives his due" sense a gloss or a note reading, state the two verbal matches, and name a source for each reading or label the ranking as the writer's. Romans 4:5 should be stated as a verbal match with a different sense, not waved off.

**5. 42:14: the "in its setting" reading has no source, and the note omits what the Hebrew says about daughters.** notes.json 42:14 (canon); chapters.json ch. 42 `settings` 42:9-14 `views`; topics.json `marriage`.
Evidence: the note says that "in its setting" the line "may mean that a young man is safer with a harsh man than with a kind woman", and ch. 42 repeats it as the "Advice for a young man" view. Nothing in `sources-checked.md` or any source key supports it or names anyone who reads it so; I could not source it either. The Hebrew second line is more important than the note says. Masada col. IV line 25: טוב רע איש מטוב אשה | ובת מפחדת מבול חרפה ("and a daughter who frightens … reproach"); MS B 12r:4 (Abegg): מטוב רוע איש מטיב אשה | ובית מחרפת תביע אשה ("and a house/woman that shames brings reproach"). The note says only that the second lines "differ and are hard to read." The Masada "daughter" is the best evidence that 42:14 belongs with 42:9-13, and it is not given; the second-line difference has no variant entry. Also missing: any named reading of "a woman who does good" (what good, done to whom), and any Jewish or Christian reception.
Fix: give the three second lines (Greek "a woman who shames", B "house/woman", Masada "daughter") in a variant; keep the first line at full strength (it is firm in Greek, B and Masada); state the readings of "does good" with a named scholar or commentary for each, or drop the unsourced "safer for a young man" reading; keep the canon comparison.

**6. The Asa idea is a labeled spec in one place and an unlabeled scholarly view in three others.** chapters.json ch. 38 `readings` ("Read as a defense of medicine"), `settings` 38:1-15 `views` ("Against suspicion of doctors"), and the `deep` spec box.
Evidence: the spec box says "I have not found this connection argued in a source I could check, so it remains my own reading." Yet the reading and the setting view say "Many read the passage as a defense of medicine against Jews who, like the Chronicler's verdict on Asa, suspected doctors," and "In an age when some saw going to doctors as distrust of God (2 Chronicles 16:12)." Both claim a scholarly consensus and an Israelite attitude that no source in the part supports. The second setting view ("Greek physicians practiced without reference to Israel's God") is also unsourced.
Fix: either source the "many read" lines or rewrite them as the writer's reading and point to the spec box. The `spec` box should also drop "perhaps two centuries before Ben Sira" (a date nobody opened).

**7. 38:13 characterizes an unnamed "minority" after the writer chose to drop those views.** notes.json 38:13 ("God's healing through the doctor").
Evidence: "The minority who reject medicine for faith alone must argue against this passage, which Catholic and Orthodox Christians count as Scripture." No group, text or source is named, and the lead decided to omit Christian Science and faith-healing views because no source was opened (rightly). The sentence is exactly the unsourced view-of-others the rule forbids, and it is also wrong in logic: a church that does not count Sirach as Scripture has no need to argue against it. The `medicine` page already has the sourced Pentecostal view (Trask) and the Catholic Catechism 1510-1511 and 2288.
Fix: delete the sentence, or replace it with a pointer to the medicine page's "How Christians differ" section. The decision to leave out Christian Science and faith-healing in this part is correct.

**8. The Satire of the Trades is stated as mockery, and the only source says scholars dispute that.** notes.json 38:25 (myth); chapters.json ch. 38 `deep` and `views` H; topics.json `work`; sources.json `wiki-satire-of-trades`.
Evidence: the opened Wikipedia text (`dl/satire.json`) says "Egyptologists disagree on whether or not the text is satirical," with Rollston calling it "scribal propaganda," Helck agreeing it is not satire, and Lichtheim holding it is satire. The notes and the topic say flatly that "The Egyptian mocks the workers" and "makes the same comparison in order to mock the trades." The note's verdict that the likeness "may well be known to Ben Sira" is stronger than the source, which only says the work "is referenced by Ben Sira" (itself unsourced there). A literary-dependence claim cannot rest on Wikipedia alone.
Fix: say that the Egyptian text exalts the scribe and describes other trades as hard, that scholars dispute whether it is satire, and that comparison with Sirach 38:24-39:11 is common; add a printed or scholarly source (an edition of Kheti's Instruction, or a Sirach commentary that makes the comparison) or reduce the dependence verdict to "similar form".

**9. Dates and background facts with no opened source in this part.** chapters.json (ten `readers` fields), notes 38:21, 39:4, 41:8, 41:14, topics.json `death`, spec box in ch. 38.

The site is also inconsistent with itself: book.json facts say "about 190-175 BC" (from Gigot, Toy and Lévi), while the part says 190-180 and "about 180". The lead's review notes already say these dates need an opened scholarly source.
Fix: reuse the book-level sources already opened (Gigot, Toy-Lévi) and the same range as book.json; cut "perhaps two centuries" and the Daniel "within a generation" unless a source is opened; give one source for 1964 and AD 73/74 or use book.json's "1963-1965 excavations" and "before AD 73".
