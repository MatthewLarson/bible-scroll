# Hostile review: Sirach book-level files (book.json, sources.json)

Reviewed: `content/books/Sir/book.json` (all of it, including the `canon` block, the 11 `cited_in` entries, the 30-36 tables, `spec`, `audience`, `lenses`) and `content/books/Sir/sources.json`. Brief: `tools/brief-sir.md` ("Texts and numbering", "book.json"); models: `content/books/2Ma/review-book.md`, `content/books/Wis/review-book.md`; rules: `tools/agent-reviewer.md`, `agent-reviewer-works.md`.

How I checked:
- A script pulled every quoted string from book.json and looked for it in the eight parts' current `plain.txt` and in the Plain of the other books. I then checked the misses by hand (they are translations of Hebrew, Latin, German, KJV, creeds and Talmud, plus the findings below).
- Greek: `texts/orig/Sir.json` (Swete) for 1:0, 4:1, 5:11, 11:19, 24:8, 32:22, 44:16, 48:10, 51:23.
- The 30-36 tables: I rebuilt every row of the second table ("Every verse") from `tools/sir-numbers.tsv` and compared. All 53 rows agree with the tsv, including the split verses (30:36-37, 31:10-11 and the other pairs, 33:1-2, 33:13, 36:16). I read KJV text under Greek 30:24, 30:25, 30:38, 31:10-11, 32:21-22, 33:1-2, 33:13, 36:16 and 36:17 in `texts/en/Sir.json`; each matches its row. The summary table (first table) also agrees. No unwrapped "KJV n:n" appears outside a `nowork` span.
- Opened in this session: Sefaria (Tosefta Yadayim 2, Sanhedrin 100b, Niddah 16b, Bava Batra 98b), Catholic Encyclopedia (Gigot) at newadvent.org, the NETS introduction to Sirach (Göttingen PDF, read as text), web searches on Masada, Philaret, the EOTC list, the Catechism and Rahlfs numbering. Not reachable or not conclusive: the Catechism paragraph itself (a search result gave only the footnote list), the EOTC list entry, Rahlfs's own numbering.
- `cd bible && VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Sir`: **0 errors, 0 warnings** (1403 verses, 931 notes, 266 variants, 543 glosses, 51 chapter pages, 134 topic contributions). No em-dashes in book.json or sources.json.

## Verdict

This is careful, fair and mostly well sourced work. Sirach is never called Scripture in the site's own voice, every church is stated through its own documents, the reliability analysis is labeled as Claude's and ranks no church, the Proverbs card is pointed to rather than copied, and no "is it Scripture?" card is added. Nearly every quotation of Sirach matches the Plain word for word, the prologue date reckoning (132 BC, Ptolemy VIII Euergetes counting from 170) is what Gigot says, and the Talmud, Tosefta, Trent, Westminster, 1689 and Article VI statements check out. The weak points are these. (1) The claim that Rahlfs, the Göttingen edition and "Orthodox Bibles" use the Greek order and Swete's numbers came from the brief and was never opened; the NETS introduction says Ziegler's Göttingen edition restores the Hebrew/Latin order. (2) "Hidden at Masada" has no support. (3) One Sirach quotation (4:1) does not match the Plain, and the sentence about it says the verse "opens with" a verb it does not open with. (4) Two `cited_in` strength words are stronger than the entries' own text. (5) The Qumran and Masada claims and a string of superlatives ("oldest", "earliest", "only") rest on Wikipedia, a 1901/1909 encyclopedia or nothing opened. (6) The subtitle says the Hebrew was "lost for a thousand years", which the book's own text contradicts. There are no critical findings; nothing is invented.

## Findings

### Major

1. **Major. The Greek-order claim for Rahlfs, the Göttingen edition and Orthodox Bibles is unverified, and partly wrong.**
   Location: facts "Numbering on this site" ("as in Swete's edition, Rahlfs and Orthodox Bibles"); section "The order of chapters 30-36" ("the Greek Bibles, the Orthodox Bibles and editions such as Swete and Rahlfs follow the Greek"); lens O ("in the Greek order of chapters this site follows"); canon `accepted_by` Orthodox ("in its order of chapters"). The brief ("also used by Rahlfs, the Göttingen edition and Orthodox Bibles") is the only source, and the Sir progress notes list no page that shows it. Also the part 30-36 note at 36:1 says "Rahlfs, the Göttingen edition, Orthodox Bibles and this site keep the Greek numbers" (outside this review's files, but the same claim).
   Evidence: the NETS introduction to Sirach (septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de, `30-sirach-nets.pdf`): "All the Greek manuscripts extant for Sirach contain a textual displacement in which the order of 30.25-33.13a and 33.13b-36.16a is reversed. Ziegler has placed the Greek in the correct order. The NRSV along with ... Skehan's and Di Lella's Anchor Bible commentary follow Ziegler in giving the text in its proper order." So the Göttingen edition is the opposite of what the brief says, and the NRSV and Skehan-Di Lella follow it. For Rahlfs, a search result on versification charts says Rahlfs's numbers differ from the NRSV's in these chapters (Rahlfs 33.13a against NRSV 36.13), which suggests Rahlfs keeps the manuscript order but I could not confirm that Rahlfs's numbers equal Swete's; they may differ. Nothing opened shows how any Orthodox Bible orders these chapters.
   Fix: say only what is shown: "the order of the Greek manuscripts and of Swete's edition". Drop "Rahlfs", "Göttingen" and "Orthodox Bibles" unless someone opens Rahlfs and an Orthodox Bible (for example the Orthodox Study Bible's Sirach 33) and records what they show. Add that Ziegler's edition, NETS (which puts the Greek numbers in parentheses) and the NRSV restore the Hebrew/Latin order, and that numbers in Rahlfs and NETS may not equal the numbers here, so a reader should check before citing. Lens O and the Orthodox `accepted_by` line should drop "in its order of chapters" unless verified. Also tell the part 30-36 owner to correct the 36:1 note.

2. **Major. "Hidden at Masada" is unsupported.**
   Location: lens J ("a Hebrew copy was hidden at Masada before AD 73"); canon provenance "How the Hebrew was kept" ("A scroll of it was hidden at Masada before AD 73").
   Evidence: the sources the writer logged (BYU Studies via Wikipedia; Crawford's review) give a casemate room, not a hiding place; my search found no source calling the Ben Sira fragments hidden or buried on purpose, and says Yadin thought many Masada fragments were cut and torn, possibly by Roman soldiers. The "hidden" detail seems to have come from the Genizah idea of putting away a worn book.
   Fix: "a Hebrew scroll of 39:27-44:17 was left at Masada, where it was found in a casemate room in 1964". The "before AD 73" is fine (the fortress fell in AD 73). Also in reliability section 2, "the Hebrew read in the time of Jesus" is loose: Yadin dated the scroll to the early first century BC by its script, so say "a century or more before Jesus" only after a source is opened, or just "before AD 73".

3. **Major. Sir 4:1 is quoted wrongly, and the sentence about it is false.**
   Location: `cited_in` Mark 10:19 at 4:1: "Sirach 4:1 opens with the same verb: 'My child, do not cheat the poor of his living'".
   Evidence: current Plain 4:1 is "My child, do not rob the poor man of what he lives on, / and do not drag out the wait for eyes that look to you in need." The Greek is `Τέκνον, τὴν ζωὴν τοῦ πτωχοῦ μὴ ἀποστερήσῃς`: the verb comes after "the life of the poor", so the verse does not "open with" it (it opens with Τέκνον). The Plain says "rob", Mark's Plain says "cheat" (10:19), so an English reader cannot see the shared verb the entry is about.
   Fix: quote the Plain exactly ("do not rob the poor man of what he lives on") and say "the same verb (ἀποστερήσῃς) stands in 4:1 and in Mark, though the Plain renders it 'rob' in Sirach and 'cheat' in Mark". Consider asking the part 01-06 owner whether "cheat" would be the better rendering at 4:1, so the Plain does not hide the link.

4. **Major. Two strength words are stronger than the entries' own text, and one claim is overstated.**
   Location: `cited_in`.
   - James 1:19 / 5:11, kind `allusion`: the entry itself says "advice to listen before speaking is common (Proverbs 18:13), so this one parallel proves little by itself". Label: `shared tradition` (or `disputed`).
   - Matthew 6:12-15 / 28:2, kind `allusion`: the entry says "Whether Jesus knew Sirach or both express a teaching common among Jews is not something the texts can settle; the words are different." Label: `shared tradition`.
   - John 1:14 / 24:8, kind `allusion`: the shared verb root (σκηνόω) is real, but the entry itself describes this as a background read against Jewish Wisdom tradition, not a use of Sirach 24 specifically. `disputed` or `shared tradition` is fairer; if `allusion` stays, say who argues it (a named scholar, opened).
   - Luke 18:1-8: "is the same phrase as Sirach's" is not exact: Luke has μακροθυμεῖ ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῖς (present), Swete's Sirach 32:22 has μακροθυμήσει ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῖς (future). Say "the same verb and preposition (different tense)". The entry's label `allusion` is acceptable.
   Matthew 11:28-30, Luke 12:16-21, Luke 18:1-8 as `allusion`; Luke 1:17, Hebrews 11:5, James 2:21-23 as `shared tradition`; Luke 24:50-53 and Mark 10:19 as `disputed`: I agree with these. All 11 entries point to real verses and the Greek in them matches `texts/orig` (checked 4:1, 5:11, 11:19, 24:8, 32:22, 44:16, 48:10, 51:23). The Plain quotations of the NT (Mark 10:19, Luke 12:19-20, 18:7-8, 24:44, 24:50, James 1:19, Matthew 6:12, 14, 11:28-29, Luke 1:17, Hebrews 11:5, John 1:14) match the current Plain.

5. **Major. The Qumran, Masada and Genizah claims rest on Wikipedia and one site, and have no source key.**
   Location: facts "Original language" (2Q18 "from chapter 6", 11QPs-a "lines of 51:13-30", Masada "39:27-44:17", "six medieval manuscripts"); section "The Hebrew, lost and found"; provenance "How the Hebrew was kept"; reliability section 2.
   Evidence: the writer's `sources-checked.md` records the verse spans for Mas1h, 2Q18 and 11QPsa as taken from "Wikipedia 'Book of Sirach' (raw)", with the Qumran and Masada existence supported by one sentence of the bensira.org introduction ("Qumran and Masada documents c. 50 BCE-c. 70 CE"). `sources.json` has no key for the Qumran editions (Baillet in DJD III for 2Q18; Sanders in DJD IV for 11QPsa) and the Masada key (Yadin) was not opened for the verse span; only Crawford's review was. STYLE.md rule 2 wants these opened. (The specific claim 39:27-44:17 is probably right; the problem is the route.) Part 44-51 says its writer read the Masada scroll and 11QPsa on bensira.org; if so, cite that, with the manuscript and column, and take the verse spans from there.
   Fix: either open the DJD volumes or the bensira.org pages for 2Q18 and 11QPsa and the Masada column list, add source keys for them, and cite; or say only what the bensira.org introduction says ("pieces from Qumran and a scroll from Masada, about 50 BC to AD 70") and give verse spans only in the part notes that read them. Also add that the Genizah dates (900s-1000s) come from Gigot (1909); current datings of the individual manuscripts should come from bensira.org (opened) or be left out. Lewis and Gibson: Gigot names only Mrs. Lewis; the sister Gibson is from Toy and Lévi; both are fine, but "brought back to Cambridge a leaf of Hebrew from the East" should say they bought it in Cairo if a source says so (Wikipedia does; not opened here).

6. **Major. The subtitle says the Hebrew was "lost ... for a thousand years"; the book's own text says otherwise.**
   Location: `subtitle`; lead paragraph 3 ("Its Hebrew was lost for centuries until 1896").
   Evidence: section "The Hebrew, lost and found" says "For more than a thousand years Christians read Sirach only in translation", the rabbis quoted it, Jerome had a Hebrew copy, "Jews were still copying it in the Middle Ages" (lens J), and the Genizah copies date to the 900s-1000s at the earliest; Toy and Lévi (which the writer logged) mention Saadia quoting it. So the Hebrew was not lost; it was lost to Christian Europe. Rewrite: "lost to Christian readers in Hebrew for a thousand years" or "its Hebrew unknown in Europe until 1896".

7. **Major. Unsourced superlatives stated in the site's voice (STYLE.md rule 2).**
   - "This is the oldest witness to a Scripture in three parts" (section "The grandfather and the grandson"). The reader is then told scholars differ on how much it shows, which is good, but "oldest" has no source.
   - "Sirach is the earliest witness, after Malachi, that Jews expected Elijah's return" (Luke 1:17 entry).
   - "The Hymn of the Fathers is the earliest survey of Israel's story outside the Bible" (reliability 1).
   - "No earlier book joins wisdom and Torah so plainly" ("What Ben Sira teaches"); Deuteronomy 4:6 and Psalm 119 are the obvious counter-examples.
   - "He is the only writer of the Old Testament or the Apocrypha who signs his book" (facts "Author"). It is a 1901 sentence by Toy and Lévi; in the authorship section it is attributed to them, but the facts card states it flat. Prophets, Nehemiah's memoir and Ezra's first-person passages make "signs" a matter of definition.
   - "Sirach ... is its [forgiveness for forgiving's] oldest clear statement outside the 66 books" (Matthew 6:12-15 entry); "no New Testament writer quotes Sirach by name or as Scripture" (rejected_why, inside the Protestant argument). The second is probably true but is asserted, not shown.
   - "He knew the Greek Bible already existed" (grandson section): the prologue implies Greek versions of the law and prophets; say "implies".
   Fix: attribute each to a named source opened in this session (Skehan-Di Lella, Beentjes, a named article), or soften to "among the earliest" or cut. Facts "Author": "Toy and Lévi note that he alone of the Old Testament and apocryphal writers signs his work".

### Minor

9. **Minor. The date is given several ways, and the Simon identification is not shown as an assumption.** Facts "Date" says 190-175 BC; the grandson section gives 190-170 (Gigot); reliability says "about 180 BC"; audience says "early 100s BC". Choose one range, cite who gives it, and use it everywhere. The date rests on "Simon son of Onias" being Simon II. Gigot (opened) says some Catholic scholars favored Simon I, and Kahana's Hebrew of 50:1 reads "Simon son of Yohanan the priest" (logged in `sources-checked.md`). Neither is mentioned in book.json. A sentence ("the usual identification is Simon II, who died about 196 BC; the Greek names Onias, the Hebrew of 50:1 as Kahana prints it names Yohanan; part 44-51 gives the evidence") would make the dating honest. The 132 BC reckoning itself is confirmed by Gigot ("Ptolemy ... shared the throne from 170 B.C. and ruled alone from 145 B.C., but counted his reign from the earlier date") and by the logged Toy-Lévi reading.

12. **Minor. Codex 248 deserves one sentence.** The Greek section says only "Some later Greek copies, and the Latin, have extra lines" and "The KJV follows the longer text in places". Suggest: "The longer Greek text survives chiefly in a few late copies, the best known being minuscule 248; many lines the KJV prints (and the bracketed lines here) come from it." Do not state its date unless a source is opened. Also say how the KJV got there only if a source is opened; the parts only show the match.
