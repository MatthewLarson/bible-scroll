# Hostile review: Martyrdom of Polycarp (MPo), flat folder

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-12. Checked: plain.txt verse by verse against `texts/orig/MPo.json` (Lake vol. 2) and the Internet Archive OCR (`.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers02lakeuoft.txt`, including Lake's introduction, English and footnotes); `texts/src/MPo.app.json` and `MPo.extra.json`; variants, notes, chapters, glosses, topics, book.json, sources.json; Eusebius, Church History 4.14-15 in NPNF (newadvent) AND in Greek (First1KGreek tlg2018.tlg002, Lake-Oulton Loeb text, fetched this session); Catholic Encyclopedia "Martyr" and "St. Polycarp" (newadvent); OCA life of Polycarp (writer's saved copy); Tertullian, Apology 32-33; Wikipedia "Martyrdom of Polycarp" and "Polycarp" (raw wikitext); web search on Moss and Hartog. `node tools/build.js --check MPo` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings. A script checked every quotation of a site verse in notes, chapter pages and views against the other books' plain.txt: all match.

## Verdict

The Plain English Translation is sound. I found no mistranslation that changes the sense, only small softenings and additions (items 16-21). Greek slips beyond the writer's list: none that matter (the scan OCR is too noisy to add more). Quotations of Lake, the Catholic Encyclopedia, Tertullian and the OCA check out. The weak parts are the apparatus and the commentary. (1) The writer never opened Eusebius's Greek, though the brief required it, so at least six real Eusebian readings are missing as variants, and some notes say things that Eusebius's text contradicts or that rest on hedges. (2) The Wikipedia relay ("as Wikipedia reports", "Wikipedia's article reports") that the batch ruling bans runs through book.json, notes and chapter pages, and the "minority led by Moss" is not even what Wikipedia says. (3) The Roman-law claims rest on a Wikipedia summary of a book nobody opened. (4) The relics and saints material takes sides, mostly in one direction, in what it says "goes beyond the Bible", and drops the Greek verb question and the main critical doubt (Moss on relics). (5) Several "views" are credited to "some", "others" or "most scholars" with no names, or to Lake for things he does not say. No invented sources or fragments (no criticals).

## Major

**1. Wikipedia relays throughout (batch ruling and STYLE rule 2).**
Locations: book.json `facts` (Date: "as Wikipedia reports"), `authorship` (Doubts), `audience.views`, `sections` ("The account and the gospel"; "The beginning of the cult of the martyrs"), `spec` ("as Wikipedia reports"), `canon.accepted_by` (Catholic, Anglican and Lutheran rows, each "as Wikipedia reports"), `provenance` ("Wikipedia's article counts seven Greek manuscripts"), `reliability` (verdict and section 3); notes 1:1 (#7), 2:1, 5:1, 7:2, 8:1, 12:2, 18:2; chapters 1 H, 9 H, 9 deep, 12 H, 12 settings source, 18 deep, 21 deep; source keys `wp-martyrdom-polycarp-nov2026`, `wp-polycarp-nov2026`, `wp-saints-calendars-nov2026`.
Evidence: I opened the article. It says only "Moss has suggested a date around 200 AD or later" (Moss 2010, pp. 573-574). It does not say "a minority, led by Candida Moss" (chapter 1 H; the audience views). The Foster and Parvis words in quote marks in chapter 9 deep ("in the middle of a sport stadium, with no use of the tribunal...") are Wikipedia's wording, not a quotation from Foster and Parvis (2007, p. 128), which nobody opened. Moss's own date is reported differently by secondary sources I found (200 or later; early third century; middle of the third century), so "about 200 or later" is not safe even as a paraphrase of her.
Fix: open Moss 2010 (Early Christianity 1:539-574; DOI 10.1628/186870310793597051) and state her argument and date from it, or cut every Moss mention. Cut the Foster and Parvis quotations unless the book is opened. Replace the calendar rows with a source that says it (Roman Martyrology or the General Roman Calendar text, the Church of England's Common Worship calendar, the LCMS or ELCA calendar), or drop the rows. Remove "as Wikipedia reports" everywhere; if a view cannot be sourced elsewhere, cut it.

**2. Eusebius's Greek was not opened; real Eusebian readings are missing, and notes contradict or hedge around them.**
The brief required opening the Greek and an English of Eusebius and entering E's readings. `sources-checked.md` lists only the NPNF English. I compared the Greek of HE 4.15 (Lake-Oulton) with Lake's Martyrdom text. E differs from the Martyrdom text in these places, none entered as variants (Lake's digital apparatus does not carry them; the writer should add them from E itself, with the Greek read there):
- 13:2: E has `πρὸ τῆς πολιᾶς ἐκεκόσμητο` ("even before his gray hair", NPNF: "before his gray hairs came"); the manuscripts have `πρὸ τῆς μαρτυρίας` ("before his martyrdom", the Plain English Translation). A real change of sense.
- 14:3: E has `σὺν αὐτῷ ἐν πνεύματι ἁγίῳ` ("with him, in the Holy Spirit"), where Lake prints `σὺν αὐτῷ καὶ πνεύματι ἁγίῳ`; E also lacks `ἐπουρανίου` ("heavenly high priest"). Note 14:3 (#71) says the NPNF English "may reflect a different Greek; Lake notes no variant". That is a hedge about something one fetch settles, and the topic `trinity` and the chapter 14 summary ("with glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit") build a Trinitarian claim on one side of this variant without saying so.
- 15:2: E lacks `ὡς ἄρτος ὀπτώμενος ἢ` ("like a loaf in the oven"); it has only the gold and silver. Note 15:2 (#74) says "The bread image is the letter's own" (unsourced and unsupported by E), the chapter 15 title is "Like bread in the oven", and the chapter 15 `spec` box ("a martyrdom shaped like the Lord's Supper") builds on the bread without telling the reader that Eusebius's copy has none. The theory is labeled, but the missing fact is the key fact.
- 16:1: E has `παραβῦσαι ξίφος` ("sword"), the manuscripts `ξιφίδιον` ("dagger"). Not entered. The 16:1 variant entry covers the dove only.
- 17:2: E has `ὑπέβαλον γοῦν τινες Νικήτην` ("certain ones prompted Nicetes"), against `ὑπέβαλεν γοῦν Νικήτην` (the evil one prompted Nicetes); E also lacks `ἄμωμον ὑπὲρ ἁμαρτωλῶν` and reads `ἡγεμόνι` for `ἄρχοντι`. Note 17:2 (#83) builds a 1 Peter 3:18 echo on exactly the words E lacks, and the agent of the prompting bears on the anti-Judaism questions.
- 18:1: E has `ἑκατοντάρχης`; the manuscripts have the Latin loanword `κεντυρίων`; note 18:1 says the Latin word and Mark 15:39, but not that E replaced it.
- 1:0: E reads `τῆς ἁγίας καθολικῆς ἐκκλησίας` (no `καὶ`); the Plain English Translation has "holy and catholic".
Also `reliability` says E "agrees closely with the manuscripts ... with a few notable readings (the dove, 'catholic')"; E has the omissions above. And note 14:2 (#67) says "Some manuscripts, and Eusebius, lack 'your'": Lake's note is `om. E p`, one manuscript.
Fix: add the variant entries (13:2, 14:3, 15:2, 16:1, 17:2, 18:1, 1:0) from E's Greek, rewrite notes #67, #71, #74, #83, the chapter 15 `spec`, the trinity contribution and the `reliability` sentence, and drop the hedges.

**3. `παῖς` as "servant" produces nonsense at 14:1 and 20:2 (lead decision, but the site should not print it unglossed).**
14:1 "Father of your beloved and blessed servant Jesus Christ": the Greek makes God the Father (`πατήρ`) of the `παῖς`. 20:2 "one and only servant" renders `μονογενοῦς παιδός` (Lake: "only begotten Child"). Lake's footnote: "Here it is clearly 'Child'." Eusebius's English translator also wrote "Son" (4.15.33, 35). The site's Acts 3:13 and 4:27 rationale (David pairing, Isaiah 52:13) does not apply where "Father of" and "only-begotten" sit beside the word. Note #65 admits "child is the warmer sense and Lake's choice", then keeps "servant" for consistency.
Fix: report to the lead that consistency here costs sense. Minimum: a gloss in glosses.json on "servant" at 14:1, 14:3 and 20:2 ("Greek pais: 'child' or 'servant'; Lake: 'Child'") so the reader sees the word at the point of reading, and a note at 20:2 that "only" goes with `μονογενής` ("only-begotten"). Better: "child" or "child (Greek pais, also 'servant')" at 14:1 and 20:2, as Lake does.

**4. 8:2 "Caesar is Lord".**
The Greek is `Κύριος καῖσαρ`, two nominatives with no verb. Eusebius (Greek `κύριος Καῖσαρ`, NPNF "Lord Cæsar") and Lake read it as a title. The Plain English Translation adds a verb ("saying 'Caesar is Lord'") and, through the chapter title, chapter summary, the `imperial-cult` contribution, book.json lead and `outline`, builds a reading on that. The note's two reasons ("say" implies a formula; the contrast with "my King") do not decide it: one also "says" an acclamation. The note's claim that "*Kyrios* was a common title for emperors, and Christians reserved it in this sense for Christ" is unsourced (memory). Note: the Greek of 12:2 needs no such addition either.
Fix: use the literal "Lord Caesar" in 8:2 (the lead decision says the writer renders from the Greek, and the Greek has no verb), keep the confession reading in the note as one option with its holders named, and source the "title of emperors" claim from an opened text (Tertullian, Apology 34 is the obvious one to open; I did not open it). The Romans note's quotation then needs the lead's sync.

**5. The relics and saints section (17-18) is not fair or sourced as the brief requires.**
(a) Verbs. 17:2 `ἄρξωνται σέβεσθαι` and 17:3 `προσκυνοῦμεν` are both "worship", and 12:2 `προσκυνεῖν` is "worship" too; the contrast `προσκυνοῦμεν` / `ἀγαπῶμεν` is presented, but no note or deep section says that `proskynein` can mean "bow before" and that the churches' argument turns on this word (Catholic and Orthodox distinguish veneration from worship by words other than the one the letter uses). The deep section says "Every church can accept the sentence", with no source; `notes` #84 and chapter 17 M say "the churches agree". Needs a source (catechisms, confessions) or removal.
(b) "Goes beyond the Bible". Note 18:2 (#86), chapter 18 N and `reliability` say the Bible has "no gathering of bones for honor" and that this "goes beyond" it. The note's own refs list Genesis 50:25 and Exodus 13:19 (Joseph's bones carried out of Egypt); and note 13:2 itself cites Acts 19:12 and 5:15. A statement the site's own cross-references undercut is not "agree/goes beyond" analysis in the strongest form of each church's view. Reword: what the Bible records (burial, 2 Kings 13:21, Joseph's bones) and that the churches read it differently, pointing to the 2 Kings 13 card.
(c) Missing critical view. The one scholar the writer cites, Moss, argues (per her *The Myth of Persecution*, as quoted in a source I found; not opened in full) that collecting and venerating martyrs' remains is unparalleled in the second century apart from this account. That bears directly on the relics passage and is absent.
(d) "First/oldest" claims. Note #86 is titled "The first account of relics" but its only support (Wikipedia) says "one of the earliest, if not the earliest". Chapter 18 M says "The earliest account of a church keeping a martyr's remains and the day of his death" with no credit. Also unsourced: "The 'birthday' is the root of the saints' days" (note #87, chapter 18 M), "This letter is often quoted in those debates because it is so early" (chapter 17), "The honor of the martyrs rests on their likeness to Christ, not on any power of their own" (chapter 17, stated as fact where churches differ), and note #82's "The officials ... misunderstood what Christians did with their martyrs" (takes the letter's side). book.json `reading` calls Eusebius "the oldest witness to the letter" and the 16:2 variant "Eusebius, the oldest witness": Lake does not say so and the Latin version's date was not opened.
Fix as above: credit to an opened source or cut.

**6. Roman legal procedure claims have no opened source.**
Chapter 9 deep ("The scene breaks the usual rules ... no tribunal, no formal accusation, no official sentence"), chapter 9 H, the `spec` ("the irregular trial that some scholars find hard to believe") all rest on Wikipedia's summary. The deep section then sets Lake's "obviously genuine" next to it, as if Lake had answered the legal point; he had not. Also unsourced Roman-law statements: chapter 2 R ("the proconsul ... could stop the torture at any time if the accused would deny Christ"), note 13:3 ("Burning victims were fastened to a stake"), note 18:1 ("apparently the Romans' way with condemned bodies": hedge as well), chapter 17 R ("the refusal is pressed by local leaders"), chapter 20 R and chapter 21 R ("Official dates named the office-holders of the year"), note 7:1 ("sent against an old man of eighty-six": assumes age 86 = lifespan, see 9). The CE passage on permitting burial of martyrs is real (verified in "Martyr"), but its use in chapter 17 R, "here the refusal is pressed by local leaders", is the writer's gloss.
Fix: open a primary or scholarly source (Pliny, Letters 10.96-97; Tertullian; Foster and Parvis) and cite exactly, or cut.

**7. Invented or unsupported attributions.**
- book.json `authorship` "Lake and most scholars: ... The details of the trial, the officials' names and the date in the Roman, Macedonian and Jewish calendars fit a writer who was there." Lake says "obviously genuine and contemporary"; he gives no such reasons, and "most scholars" has no holder. Note #46 likewise says small details "are among the reasons Lake calls the account 'obviously genuine'": Lake gives no reasons.
- Note #50 (9:3): "Lake's introduction puts his death at the age of eighty-six." The vol. 2 introduction does not; vol. 1's Polycarp introduction does ("at the age of eighty-six"), and the section "Polycarp's life" quotes vol. 1 but lists only vol. 2 as source. Cite the right volume (`lake-apostolic-fathers`).
- Note #78 and chapter 16 M: "the manuscripts' headings call Polycarp 'overseer (bishop) of Smyrna'". Lake: the printed title "has no special support. Each MS. gives its own title and ... no two are the same." Only Lake's composite title is known to say so.
- Note #91: the circular letter "is how the letter reached Eusebius, and why so many churches came to keep Polycarp's day": unsourced causal claim.
- Note #92: Evarestus "put the account on papyrus": the Greek says only `ὁ γράψας`; "papyrus" is invented.
- John 19:31 in `cited_in`: "The account borrows the Gospel's phrase." The Greek phrases differ (`σάββατον μέγα` against `μεγάλη ἡ ἡμέρα ἐκείνου τοῦ σαββάτου`), and Lake gives two other explanations (Purim; Passover-week Sabbath). "Borrows" asserts literary dependence nobody shows. The kind is "shared tradition"; the text says more.
Fix each: source or cut.

**8. "Some / others / most" views with no holders.**
Note 12:2 (#57), chapter 12 H, topic `anti-judaism` ("Some read this as a report ... others see it shaped by the Gospels' passion pattern"), `authorship` ("most scholars"). Per the batch ruling, name the holders from an opened work or cut. Note #49 (9:2) "The real atheists, he means ... the first of his refusals" is one unsourced reading presented as the meaning, and conflicts with chapter 8's setting, which calls 8:2 "the first of the letter's three confessions (8:2; 9:3; 10:1)". The alternative reading (he simply obeys the proconsul's words, turned back on the crowd) is missing.

**9. Date apparatus.**
- Note #94 summary `p` says "Saturday, 23 February" with the year "155 or 156 most often": on the 156 theory (Turner, Schwartz) the date is 22 February, which the note's body does say. "Most often" has no source.
- Notes #94, #97 and book.json say Quadratus became proconsul "in 153-154" (Lake) and that 23 February fell on a Saturday in 155, with no explanation how a proconsulship begun in 153-4 covers February 155. The CE "St. Polycarp" (opened) gives Waddington's result as 154-55 and says the proconsular year began in May; the writer cites the CE for Schmid and Ramsay but not for this, and omits its alternative ("the possibility remains that the subscription was tampered with by a later hand"). Present the discrepancy.
- The OCA life (a church source the book cites) places the persecution under Marcus Aurelius (161-180), the Eusebian line; the Orthodox canon row cites the OCA and does not note this.
- Chapter 1 settings `when`: "Soon after Polycarp's death": no source. Chapter 22 H: "Irenaeus (about 180)": no source opened.
- 9:2 chapter settings `when`: "before the games ended (12:2)"; 12:2 says the hunts were already completed (`πεπληρώκει τὰ κυνηγέσια`). Wrong.

**16. Translation: small departures (Plain English Translation against the Greek).**
- 13:2 "he had been held in every honor because of his good way of life": `ἐκεκόσμητο` is "had been adorned" (with every good thing for his good conduct); "held in every honor" changes the image. (See also item 2: E's reading here.)
- 2:1 "We must take care, in reverence, to give God ...": `εὐλαβεστέρους ἡμᾶς ὑπάρχοντας` is "being more reverent"; "take care" is added.
- 3:1 `ἐπισήμως ἐθηριομάχησεν` "fought the beasts with honor": `ἐπισήμως` is "conspicuously, notably".
- 19:1 omits `μόνος` ("alone ... above all", present in E too): "it is he above all whom everyone remembers".
- 22:2 "papers of Irenaeus": the main text has only `ἐκ τῶν Εἰρηναίου` ("from Irenaeus's [things]"); "papers" is supplied (the Moscow text has `συγγραμμάτων`). The 22:2 variant entry and book.json then quote "papers" as if the Greek said it. Put "papers" in a gloss or note it as supplied.
- 18:1 "the quarrel the Jews had stirred up": `τὴν τῶν Ἰουδαίων γενομένην φιλονεικίαν` is "the contentiousness that had come about on the part of the Jews"; "stirred up" adds agency. Given item 8's sensitivity, use "the Jews' quarrelsomeness" or "the quarrel on the Jews' side".
- 8:3 "pushed him out in a hurry": `καθῄρουν` "were taking him down"; "pushed" is Eusebius's translator's and the CE's wording (checked), a defensible reading but not literal.
- 13:1 "faster than they can be told": Greek past: "faster than it was told".
- 2:4 "sharp shells" and the gloss "spiked seashells": `κήρυκας` has no adjective; "sharp" comes from Lake's English (and Eusebius's added "pointed spits"). Fine as Lake's reading, but note #19's "a sea snail with a sharp, spiked shell" is the writer's, unsourced (LSJ was opened but the note does not quote it).
- 5:2 vs 12:3: item 12.
