## Greeting
1:0 The church of God that lives as a resident alien in Smyrna, to the church of God that lives as a resident alien in Philomelium, and to all the communities of the holy and catholic church in every place. Mercy, peace and love from God the Father and our Lord Jesus Christ: may they be multiplied.
## A martyrdom in line with the gospel
1:1 We are writing to you, brothers, about those who were martyred and about blessed Polycarp, who by his martyrdom set a seal, as it were, on the persecution and brought it to an end. For nearly everything that led up to it happened so that the Lord might show us from above a martyrdom in line with the gospel.
1:2 For he waited to be handed over, as the Lord also did, so that we too might become his imitators, keeping an eye not only on what concerns ourselves but also on what concerns our neighbors. For true and steady love wants not only oneself to be saved, but every one of the brothers too.
## The courage of the martyrs
2:1 So all the martyrdoms that happened by the will of God are blessed and noble. For we must be all the more reverent and give God the power over everything.
2:2 Who would not marvel at their nobility, their endurance and their love for their Master? Some were so torn by whips that the inner workings of their flesh lay open, down to the veins and arteries deep inside, yet they endured, so that even the people who stood by felt sorry for them and cried. Others reached such heights of nobility that not one of them muttered or groaned. They showed all of us that in that hour of their torture the most noble martyrs of Christ were away from the flesh, or rather, that the Lord was standing beside them and talking with them.
2:3 Their minds were fixed on the grace of Christ, and they looked down on the tortures of this world, buying eternal life at the price of a single hour. The fire of their savage torturers felt cold to them, for they kept before their eyes the escape from the fire that is eternal and never put out. With the eyes of their heart they looked up at the good things kept for those who have endured, which no ear has heard and no eye has seen, and which have not come up into a human heart. But the Lord was showing these things to them, for they were no longer human beings but already angels.
2:4 In the same way, those condemned to the beasts endured dreadful punishments. They were laid on trumpet shells and punished with other kinds of torture of every sort, so that the tyrant, if he could, might turn them to denial by punishment that never let up. For the devil was scheming many things against them.
## Germanicus
3:1 But thanks be to God: he did not win against them all. For the most noble Germanicus made their timid hearts strong by the endurance that was in him, and his fight with the beasts stood out. When the proconsul wanted to persuade him and told him to take pity on his youth, he forced the beast to come at him and dragged it onto himself, because he wanted to be set free sooner from their unjust and lawless life.
3:2 At this the whole crowd, amazed at the nobility of the God-loving and God-fearing race of the Christians, shouted, "Away with the atheists! Let Polycarp be searched for!"
## Quintus, who gave himself up
4:1 But one man, named Quintus, a Phrygian who had come from Phrygia not long before, lost his nerve when he saw the beasts. He was the one who had pushed himself and some others to come forward of their own accord. The proconsul pleaded with him at length and talked him into swearing the oath and offering sacrifice. That is why, brothers, we give no praise to people who turn themselves in, because that is not what the gospel teaches.
## Polycarp withdraws
5:1 But the most marvelous Polycarp, when he first heard of it, was not upset, and wanted to stay in the city. Most of them, though, urged him to slip away, and he slipped away to a small farm not far from the city. There he stayed with a few others, night and day doing nothing but pray for everyone and for the churches all over the world, as he always did.
5:2 While he was praying, three days before he was arrested, he had a vision and saw his pillow being burned up by fire. He turned and said to those with him, "I must be burned alive."
## Betrayed by his own household
6:1 When those searching for him kept at it, he moved to another farm, and at once the searchers arrived. When they did not find him, they seized two slave boys, and one of them confessed under torture.
6:2 For it was impossible for him to stay hidden, since the ones who betrayed him belonged to his own household. And the police captain, who had been given the very same name, for he was called Herod, was eager to bring him into the stadium, so that Polycarp might fulfill his own lot by becoming a sharer with Christ, and those who betrayed him might suffer the punishment of Judas himself.
## The arrest
7:1 So, taking the slave boy with them, on the Day of Preparation, about suppertime, police and horsemen went out with their usual weapons, as if they were rushing out against a bandit. Late in the evening they closed in together and found him lying down in an upstairs room. Even from there he could have gone away to another place, but he would not. He said, "May God's will be done."
7:2 When he heard that they had arrived, he came down and talked with them. Those who were there were amazed at his age and his calm, and that there was such a rush to arrest so old a man. At once he gave orders for food and drink to be set before them at that hour, as much as they wanted, and he asked them to give him an hour to pray in peace.
7:3 When they allowed it, he stood and prayed, so full of the grace of God that for two hours he could not stop. Those who heard him were astonished, and many were sorry that they had come against so godly an old man.
## Brought into the city
8:1 When at last he finished his prayer, after remembering everyone who had ever come his way, small and great, well known and unknown, and the whole catholic church throughout the world, the hour came to leave. They sat him on a donkey and brought him into the city. It was a great Sabbath.
8:2 The police captain Herod and his father Nicetes met him. They moved him into their carriage, sat beside him and tried to win him over, saying, "What harm is there in saying 'Lord Caesar,' and offering sacrifice and the rest that goes with it, and so saving yourself?" At first he did not answer them. But when they kept on, he said, "I am not going to do what you are urging me to do."
8:3 When they failed to persuade him, they said terrible things to him and took him down in such a hurry that he scraped his shin as he got down from the carriage. He did not turn around. As if nothing had happened to him, he walked on eagerly and quickly and was taken to the stadium. The uproar in the stadium was so loud that no one could even be heard.
## Before the proconsul
9:1 As Polycarp was entering the stadium, a voice came from heaven: "Be strong, Polycarp, and show courage." No one saw who spoke, but those of our people who were there heard the voice. Then, as he was brought forward, there was a great uproar when they heard that Polycarp had been arrested.
9:2 So he was brought forward, and the proconsul asked him whether he was Polycarp. When he said he was, the proconsul tried to persuade him to deny it, saying, "Respect your age," and other things like that, as they usually say: "Swear by the fortune of Caesar. Repent. Say, 'Away with the atheists!'" But Polycarp looked with a stern face at the whole crowd of lawless Gentiles in the stadium, waved his hand at them, groaned, looked up to heaven and said, "Away with the atheists!"
9:3 When the proconsul pressed him and said, "Swear, and I will set you free. Insult Christ," Polycarp said, "For eighty-six years I have been his slave, and he has never wronged me. How can I speak evil of my King, the one who saved me?"
10:1 When he kept on again and said, "Swear by the fortune of Caesar," Polycarp answered, "If you vainly imagine that I will swear by the fortune of Caesar, as you put it, and you pretend not to know who I am, listen plainly: I am a Christian. And if you want to learn what Christianity teaches, set a day and hear me."
10:2 The proconsul said, "Persuade the people." Polycarp said, "With you I would have thought it right to reason, for we have been taught to give rulers and authorities appointed by God the honor that is due them, as long as it does us no harm. But as for those people, I do not think them worth making a defense to."
## Threats of beasts and fire
11:1 The proconsul said, "I have wild beasts. I will throw you to them unless you repent." He said, "Call them. For us, a repentance from better to worse is out of the question. But it is good to turn from what is cruel to what is right."
11:2 He said to him again, "I will have you burned up by fire, since you look down on the beasts, unless you repent." Polycarp said, "You threaten a fire that burns for an hour and is soon put out. For you do not know the fire of the coming judgment and eternal punishment, which is kept for the ungodly. But why are you waiting? Bring on whatever you want."
## The crowd cries for fire
12:1 As he said these things and many more, he was filled with courage and joy, and his face was full of grace. He was not crushed or shaken by what was said to him. Instead it was the proconsul who was amazed, and he sent his own herald into the middle of the stadium to announce three times, "Polycarp has confessed that he is a Christian."
12:2 When the herald said this, the whole crowd of Gentiles and Jews who lived in Smyrna shouted with rage they could not hold back and with a loud voice, "This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians, the destroyer of our gods, the one who teaches many not to sacrifice or to worship!" Saying this, they shouted and asked Philip the Asiarch to let a lion loose on Polycarp. But he said that he was not allowed to, since he had brought the animal hunts to a close.
12:3 Then they decided to shout with one voice that Polycarp should be burned alive. For the vision of his pillow had to be fulfilled, when he saw it burning while he prayed, and turned and said to the believers with him, as a prophet, "I must be burned alive."
## The pyre
13:1 These things then happened very fast, faster than it could be told. The crowds at once gathered wood and brushwood from the workshops and the baths, with the Jews especially eager to help with this, as is their habit.
13:2 When the pyre was ready, he took off all his outer clothes, loosened his belt, and tried to take off his own sandals too. He had not done this before, because each of the believers was always eager to be the first to touch his skin. For even before his martyrdom he had been adorned with every good thing because of his good way of life.
13:3 At once the things prepared for the pyre were put around him. When they were about to nail him as well, he said, "Leave me as I am. For the one who lets me endure the fire will let me stay on the pyre without flinching, even without the security you get from the nails."
## The prayer at the stake
14:1 So they did not nail him, but they tied him. With his hands put behind him and tied, like a choice ram taken from a great flock for an offering, a whole burnt offering made ready and acceptable to God, he looked up to heaven and said: "Lord God, the Almighty, Father of your beloved and blessed servant Jesus Christ, through whom we have come to know you, God of angels and powers and of all creation, and of the whole race of the righteous who live in your presence:
14:2 I bless you for judging me worthy of this day and this hour: to have my share, counted among the martyrs, in the cup of your Christ, for the resurrection to eternal life, of soul and body, in the incorruption of the Holy Spirit. Today may I be taken in among them before you, as a sacrifice rich and acceptable, just as you prepared it beforehand and showed it beforehand and have now fulfilled it, you the God who does not lie, the true God.
14:3 For this, and for everything, I praise you, I bless you, I glorify you, through the eternal and heavenly high priest Jesus Christ, your beloved servant. Through him be glory to you, with him and the Holy Spirit, both now and for the ages to come. Amen."
## The fire that would not burn him
15:1 When he had sent up the Amen and finished the prayer, the men in charge of the fire lit it. A great flame blazed up, and we saw a wonder, we to whom it was given to see. And we were kept safe so that we could tell the others what happened.
15:2 For the fire took the shape of a vaulted room, like a sail swelling with wind on a ship, and walled in the martyr's body all around. He was in the middle of it, not like flesh being burned, but like a loaf in the oven, or like gold and silver being refined in a furnace. And we caught a scent as sweet as rising incense or some other precious spice.
## The dagger
16:1 At last the lawless men saw that his body could not be consumed by the fire, and they ordered an executioner to step up and drive a dagger into him. When he did this, a dove came out, and so much blood that it put out the fire. The whole crowd marveled that there was such a difference between the unbelievers and the chosen.
16:2 One of the chosen was this man, the most marvelous martyr Polycarp, who in our own times was an apostolic and prophetic teacher, overseer of the catholic church in Smyrna. For every word that came from his mouth has been fulfilled and will be fulfilled.
## His body and his bones
17:1 But the jealous and envious evil one, who opposes the race of the righteous, saw the greatness of his martyrdom and his way of life, blameless from the beginning. He saw him crowned with the crown of incorruption, carrying off a prize no one could dispute. So he made sure that not even his poor body would be taken away by us, though many wanted to do this and to have a share in his holy flesh.
17:2 So he prompted Nicetes, Herod's father and the brother of Alce, to appeal to the governor not to give up his body, "or else," he said, "they will abandon the crucified one and start to worship this man." They said this at the prompting and urging of the Jews, who also kept watch when we were about to take him from the fire. They did not know that we will never be able to abandon Christ, who suffered for the salvation of the whole world of those who are saved, the blameless one for sinners, or to worship anyone else.
17:3 For Christ we worship, because he is the Son of God. The martyrs we love as disciples and imitators of the Lord, and they deserve it, for a loyalty no one could surpass to their own King and Teacher. May we too become their partners and fellow disciples.
18:1 So when the centurion saw the contention on the Jews' part, he set the body in the middle, as was their custom, and burned it.
18:2 And so we later took up his bones, worth more than costly gems and more prized than gold, and laid them where it was fitting.
18:3 There, as we are able, the Lord will let us gather in gladness and joy and celebrate the birthday of his martyrdom, to remember those who have already fought the contest and to train and ready those still to come.
## The twelfth martyr
19:1 Such is the story of blessed Polycarp. He was martyred in Smyrna, the twelfth together with those from Philadelphia, but he alone is remembered by everyone more than the others, so that he is talked about everywhere, even by the Gentiles. He was not only a famous teacher but also an outstanding martyr, whose martyrdom everyone longs to imitate, because it followed the gospel of Christ.
19:2 By his endurance he defeated the unjust ruler and so received the crown of incorruption. Now, rejoicing with the apostles and all the righteous, he glorifies God the Father Almighty and blesses our Lord Jesus Christ, the Savior of our souls, the Pilot of our bodies, and the Shepherd of the catholic church throughout the world.
## Send the letter on
20:1 You asked for a fuller account of what happened, but for now we have reported it in summary through our brother Marcion. So when you have learned these things, send the letter on to the brothers farther away, so that they too may glorify the Lord who chooses from among his own slaves.
20:2 To him who is able to bring all of us by his grace and gift into his heavenly kingdom, through his one and only servant, Jesus Christ, be glory, honor, power and greatness forever. Greet all the saints. Those with us greet you, and so does Evarestus, who wrote this, with his whole household.
## The date
21:1 Blessed Polycarp was martyred on the second day of the month Xanthicus, counting from its start, seven days before the Kalends of March, on a great Sabbath, at the eighth hour. He was arrested by Herod when Philip of Tralles was high priest, when Statius Quadratus was proconsul, and while Jesus Christ was reigning as King forever. To him be the glory, honor, greatness and an eternal throne, from generation to generation. Amen.
## Farewell, and the copyists
22:1 We pray that you fare well, brothers, as you walk by the word of Jesus Christ that follows the gospel. With him be glory to God the Father and the Holy Spirit, for the salvation of the holy chosen ones, just as blessed Polycarp was martyred. May we be found following in his footsteps in the kingdom of Jesus Christ.
22:2 Gaius copied these things from the papers of Irenaeus, a disciple of Polycarp, and Gaius had also lived with Irenaeus. And I, Socrates, wrote them out in Corinth from the copies of Gaius. Grace be with all.
22:3 And I, Pionius, wrote them out again from the copy just named, after searching for them, because blessed Polycarp showed them to me in a revelation, as I will explain in what follows. I gathered them together when they were nearly worn out with age, so that the Lord Jesus Christ may also gather me with his chosen ones into his heavenly kingdom. To him be glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen.
