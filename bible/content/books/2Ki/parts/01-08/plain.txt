## Ahaziah Sends to Baal-zebub
1:1 Moab revolted against Israel after Ahab died.
1:2 In Samaria, Ahaziah fell through the window screen of his roof room and was taken ill. He sent messengers with this order: "Go and consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, to learn if I will survive this sickness."
1:3 But the angel of the LORD told Elijah the Tishbite, "Get up. Go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say to them, 'Is it for lack of a God in Israel that you go to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron?
1:4 So now the LORD says this: You will never come down from the bed you went up into. You are sure to die.'" And Elijah went.
1:5 The messengers came back to the king. "What is this?" he asked them. "Why are you back?"
1:6 They told him, "A man came up to meet us. He said to us, 'Go back to the king who sent you, and tell him, "The LORD says this: Is it for lack of a God in Israel that you send to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? So now you will never come down from the bed you went up into. You are sure to die."'"
1:7 He asked them, "This man who came up to meet you and spoke these words to you: what was he like?"
1:8 "He was a hairy man," they said, "with a leather loincloth tied around his waist." "That was Elijah the Tishbite," he said.
## Fire on the Fifties
1:9 The king sent a commander of fifty to him, with his fifty men. The commander went up to him, and there Elijah was, sitting on the top of the hill. "Man of God," he said to him, "the king orders you: 'Come down!'"
1:10 Elijah answered the commander of fifty, "If I am a man of God, then let fire come down out of the sky and devour you and your fifty." And fire came down out of the sky and devoured him and his fifty.
1:11 The king sent him another commander of fifty, with his fifty. This one spoke up and said to him, "Man of God, the king says this: 'Come down, and be quick!'"
1:12 Elijah answered them, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down out of the sky and devour you and your fifty." And the fire of God came down out of the sky and devoured him and his fifty.
1:13 The king sent a third commander of fifty, with his fifty. This third commander went up and came and dropped to his knees in front of Elijah. He pleaded with him. "Man of God," he said, "please treat my life, and the lives of these fifty servants of yours, as something precious to you.
1:14 Look, fire came down out of the sky and devoured the first two commanders of fifty and their fifties. But now let my life be precious to you."
1:15 The angel of the LORD said to Elijah, "Go down along with him, and have no fear of him." So Elijah got up and went down with him to the king.
1:16 He told the king, "The LORD says this: 'You sent messengers to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron. Was it for lack of a God in Israel whose word you could consult? So now you will never come down from the bed you went up into. You are sure to die.'"
1:17 So Ahaziah died, true to the LORD's word that Elijah had spoken. Jehoram became king in his place, in the second year of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, because Ahaziah had no son.
1:18 The rest of Ahaziah's story, what he did, is written down, isn't it, in the scroll of the day-by-day records of the kings of Israel?
## Elijah Taken Up
2:1 The time came for the LORD to lift Elijah up to the heavens in a windstorm. Elijah and Elisha set out from Gilgal.
2:2 Elijah said to Elisha, "Wait here, please. The LORD is sending me on to Bethel." But Elisha said, "By the life of the LORD, and by your own life, I will never leave you." So they went down to Bethel.
2:3 The sons of the prophets at Bethel came out to Elisha. "Do you know that the LORD is taking your master from over your head today?" they asked him. "I know it too," he said. "Say nothing."
2:4 Elijah said to him, "Elisha, wait here, please. The LORD is sending me on to Jericho." But he said, "By the life of the LORD, and by your own life, I will never leave you." So they came to Jericho.
2:5 The sons of the prophets in Jericho came up to Elisha. "Do you know that the LORD is taking your master from over your head today?" they asked him. "I know it too," he said. "Say nothing."
2:6 Elijah said to him, "Wait here, please. The LORD is sending me on to the Jordan." But he said, "By the life of the LORD, and by your own life, I will never leave you." So the two of them walked on.
2:7 Fifty men from the sons of the prophets went too. They stood some way off, facing them, while the two of them stood at the Jordan.
2:8 Elijah took his cloak and wrapped it into a roll. He struck the water, and the water split apart, one way and the other, so that the two of them crossed over on dry ground.
2:9 When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, "Ask! What should I do for you before I am taken from your side?" Elisha said, "Please, let me have a double share of your spirit."
2:10 "What you ask is hard," Elijah said. "If you see me being taken from you, then you will have it. If you don't, you won't."
2:11 They kept walking and talking. Then, look, a chariot of fire and horses of fire came between the two of them, and Elijah went up to the heavens in the windstorm.
2:12 Elisha was looking on and shouting, "My father! My father! Israel's chariots and riders!" He never saw him again. Then he gripped his clothes and ripped them into two pieces.
2:13 He lifted up the cloak that had fallen off Elijah. Then he turned back and took his stand at the edge of the Jordan.
2:14 He took Elijah's cloak, the one that had fallen off him, and struck the water. "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" he said. "Yes, where is he?" He struck the water, and it split apart, one way and the other, and Elisha crossed over.
2:15 The sons of the prophets from Jericho, who stood watching from across the way, saw him. "Elijah's spirit has settled on Elisha," they said. They came to meet him and bowed low to him, down to the ground.
2:16 "Look," they said to him, "your servants have fifty capable men here. Let them go and search, please, for your master. The LORD's Spirit may have carried him off and thrown him down on one of the mountains or into one of the valleys." "You must not send them," he said.
2:17 But they pushed him until he was embarrassed. "Send them, then," he said. They sent the fifty men, who hunted for three days and did not find him.
2:18 They came back to him while he was staying on in Jericho. "Didn't I say to you, 'Don't go'?" he asked them.
## Elisha Heals the Water
2:19 The men of the town said to Elisha, "Look, please, this town is a good place to live, as my lord can see. But the water is bad, and the land causes miscarriages."
2:20 "Fetch me a new bowl and put salt in there," he said. So they fetched it for him.
2:21 He went out to the place where the water came out of the ground and threw the salt in there. He said, "The LORD says this: 'I have healed this water. From there no more death or miscarriage will come.'"
2:22 The water has stayed healed down to this day, just as Elisha said it would.
## The Boys at Bethel
2:23 He went on from there, up to Bethel. While he was climbing the road, some young lads came out of the town and mocked him. "Go on up, baldhead!" they said to him. "Go on up, baldhead!"
2:24 Elisha turned back and saw them, and he put a curse on them in the LORD's name. Then out of the forest came two female bears, and they ripped into forty-two of the youngsters.
2:25 He left there for Mount Carmel, and after that he returned to Samaria.
## Jehoram of Israel
3:1 In the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, Jehoram son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria. He ruled for twelve years.
3:2 He did what the LORD sees as evil, but not like his father and his mother. He took away the standing stone of Baal that his father had made.
3:3 Still, he stuck to the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who led Israel into sin, and did not depart from them.
## War with Moab
3:4 Mesha, the king of Moab, was a sheep breeder. He would hand over to the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and wool from a hundred thousand rams.
3:5 Once Ahab was dead, though, the king of Moab revolted against the king of Israel.
3:6 King Jehoram marched out of Samaria at that time and enrolled all Israel for war.
3:7 He also sent word to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: "The king of Moab has revolted against me. Will you go with me to fight Moab?" "I will go up," he said. "I am one with you, my people one with your people, my horses one with your horses."
3:8 "Which road shall we take up there?" he asked. "The road through the wilderness of Edom," Jehoram answered.
3:9 The king of Israel went, and the king of Judah, and the king of Edom. For seven days they circled around on the march. Then the troops had no water, and neither did the animals that went with them.
3:10 "Oh no!" said the king of Israel. "The LORD has summoned this trio of kings only to give them into Moab's power!"
3:11 But Jehoshaphat asked, "Isn't there a prophet of the LORD here, so we can consult the LORD through him?" One of the servants of the king of Israel answered, "Elisha son of Shaphat is here, the one who poured water over Elijah's hands."
3:12 "He has the LORD's word," Jehoshaphat said. So down to him went the king of Israel, Jehoshaphat, and the king of Edom.
3:13 Elisha said to the king of Israel, "What is there between you and me? Go to your father's prophets and your mother's prophets." "No," the king of Israel said to him, "because the LORD has summoned this trio of kings to give them into Moab's power."
3:14 Elisha said, "By the life of the LORD of Armies, whom I stand before: if it were not out of respect for Jehoshaphat king of Judah, I would not so much as look at you or notice you.
3:15 But now, get me someone who plays the lyre." As the player played, the LORD's hand came over Elisha.
3:16 He said, "The LORD says this: 'Make this wadi into trenches, trench after trench.'
3:17 For the LORD says this: 'You will not see wind, and you will not see rain, but that wadi will fill up with water. You will drink, you and your herds and your pack animals.'
3:18 This is a small thing in the LORD's eyes. He will hand Moab over to you as well.
3:19 You will strike down every walled town and every first-rate town. You will bring down every good tree. Every spring of water you will block, and every good plot of farmland you will spoil with stones."
3:20 In the morning, about when the grain offering goes up, water suddenly came flowing in by way of Edom, and the land was filled with the water.
3:21 Meanwhile all Moab heard that the kings had marched up to make war on them. Every man who could buckle on a belt, and anyone older, was called out, and they stood ready at the border.
3:22 They were up early in the morning, and as the sun rose over the water, Moab saw the water from the other side, red like blood.
3:23 "It's blood!" they said. "The kings have been fighting each other, surely, and every man has struck down his fellow. So to the loot, Moab!"
3:24 They reached Israel's camp, but Israel rose up and attacked Moab, and the Moabites ran from them. Israel pushed on into the land, cutting Moab down.
3:25 The towns they pulled down. Each man threw his stone onto every good field until they had filled it. Every spring they blocked, and every good tree they felled. In the end only Kir-hareseth was left with its stones, and the men with slings surrounded it and struck at it.
3:26 When the king of Moab saw that the battle was too strong for him, he took seven hundred men who carried swords and tried to cut his way through toward the king of Edom. They could not do it.
3:27 So he took his firstborn, the son who would rule in his place, and sacrificed him as a burnt offering on the wall. And there was great wrath upon Israel. They pulled away from him and went back to their own land.
## The Widow's Oil
4:1 A woman, the wife of one of the sons of the prophets, cried out to Elisha. "My husband, your servant, has died," she said. "You know how he, your servant, feared the LORD. Now the lender is coming to take my two boys for himself as slaves."
4:2 "What am I to do for you?" Elisha asked her. "Tell me what you have in the house." "There is nothing in the house that belongs to your servant," she said, "nothing but a small pot of oil."
4:3 He said, "Go outside and borrow containers from every neighbor you have. Get empty ones, and get plenty.
4:4 Then go in and close the door on yourself and your sons, and pour into all these containers. Move the full ones to the side."
4:5 So she went from him and closed the door on herself and her sons. They kept handing her containers, and she kept pouring.
4:6 Once every container was full, she told her son, "Hand me one more." He said, "There are no containers left." And the oil stood still.
4:7 She went and reported to the man of God. "Go and sell the oil," he said, "and pay what you owe. You and your sons will live on the remainder."
## The Woman of Shunem
4:8 One day Elisha crossed over to Shunem. A woman of standing lived there, and she pressed him to have a meal. From then on, every time he came through, he stopped in there for a meal.
4:9 "Listen," she said to her husband. "I am sure the man who is always passing by our house is a holy man of God.
4:10 Please, let's build him a little roof room with walls. We can put a bed in it for him, and a table, a chair and a lampstand. Then whenever he comes to us, he can stop there."
4:11 The day came when he arrived there, went into the roof room, and lay down to rest.
4:12 "Call the woman from Shunem here," he told Gehazi, his servant. Gehazi called her, and she came and stood in front of him.
4:13 Elisha told Gehazi, "Tell her, please: 'Look, you have taken all this anxious care for us. What could we do for you in return? Shall we put in a word for you with the king or with the army commander?'" She said, "I live among my own family."
4:14 "So what is there to do for her?" Elisha asked. Gehazi said, "The truth is, she has no son, and her husband is old."
4:15 "Call her," he said. Gehazi called her, and she came and stood at the door.
4:16 Elisha said, "At this set time, when the season comes round again, you will be hugging a son of your own." "Don't, my lord!" she said. "Man of God, don't deceive your servant."
4:17 But the woman became pregnant, and she gave birth to a son at that set time, when the season came round again, just as Elisha had told her.
## The Boy Raised to Life
4:18 The boy grew older. There came a day when he went out to join his father with the harvest crew.
4:19 He cried to his father, "My head! My head!" "Take him to his mother," the father told one of the young men.
4:20 The young man carried him and brought him to his mother. He sat on her knees till midday, and he died.
4:21 She carried him up and laid him down on the man of God's bed. Then she closed the door behind her and left.
4:22 She called out to her husband, "Please send me one of the young men and one of the donkeys. I am going to run to the man of God, and I'll come straight back."
4:23 "Why are you going to him today?" he asked. "It isn't a new moon or a Sabbath." "It is well," she said.
4:24 She saddled the donkey. "Drive on, go!" she told her servant. "Don't hold back the ride for my sake unless I say so."
4:25 Off she went, and she reached the man of God at Mount Carmel. The man of God spotted her a long way off. "Look," he said to Gehazi, his servant, "it's the woman from Shunem.
4:26 Now run, please, to meet her, and ask her, 'Is it well with you? Is it well with your husband? Is it well with the boy?'" "It is well," she said.
4:27 She came to the man of God on the mountain and clutched his feet. Gehazi stepped up to shove her off. "Let her be," said the man of God. "She is bitter to the depths of her soul. The LORD has kept it hidden from me and has not told me."
4:28 "Was it I who asked you for a son, my lord?" she said. "Didn't I tell you, 'Don't lead me on'?"
4:29 Elisha said to Gehazi, "Belt up your clothes, take my staff in your hand, and go. Whoever you meet, don't say a blessing to him, and whoever blesses you, don't answer him. Put my staff down on the boy's face."
4:30 But the boy's mother said, "By the life of the LORD, and by your own life, I will never leave you." So he rose and went after her.
4:31 Gehazi had gone on before them and set the staff on the boy's face, but there was not a sound, and nobody was listening. He turned back to meet Elisha and reported, "The boy did not wake."
4:32 Elisha entered the house, and there lay the boy, dead, stretched out on his bed.
4:33 He went in and closed the door, with only the two of them inside, and he prayed to the LORD.
4:34 He climbed up and lay down on the child, putting his own mouth on the boy's mouth, his own eyes on his eyes, his own palms on his palms. He crouched over him, and the child's flesh grew warm.
4:35 He got down and paced the house, once this way and once that way. Then he went up and crouched over him. The boy sneezed, seven times, and the boy opened his eyes.
4:36 Elisha called for Gehazi. "Call the woman from Shunem," he said. Gehazi called her, and she came in to him. "Lift up your son," he told her.
4:37 She dropped down at his feet and bowed low, to the ground. Then she lifted her son up and left the room.
## Death in the Pot
4:38 When Elisha returned to Gilgal, famine had hit the land. The sons of the prophets were sitting in front of him, and he told his servant, "Put the big pot on and boil up a stew for the sons of the prophets."
4:39 One man had gone out to the open country to pick greens. He came on a wild vine, and he picked wild gourds from it until his robe was full. He came back and chopped them into the stewpot. They did not know the plant.
4:40 They served it out for the men to eat. But when they tasted the stew, they shouted, "Death is in the pot, man of God!" They were not able to eat it.
4:41 "Then bring flour," he said, and he tossed it into the pot. "Serve it to the people and let them eat," he said. Now nothing bad was in the pot.
## Bread for a Hundred
4:42 Someone from Baal-shalishah arrived with a gift for the man of God: firstfruits bread, twenty barley loaves, and fresh ears of grain in his bag. "Let the people have it to eat," Elisha said.
4:43 "What? Am I to put this in front of a hundred men?" his attendant said. "Let the people have it to eat," he said. "For the LORD says this: 'They will eat, and there will be extra.'"
4:44 He put it in front of them, and they ate, and there was extra, just as the LORD's word had said.
## Naaman Healed
5:1 Naaman, the army commander of the king of Aram, was a great man in his master's sight and held in high honor, because it was through him that the LORD had rescued Aram. The man was a strong warrior, but he had a skin disease.
5:2 Aram had sent out raiding parties, and they had carried off a young girl from the land of Israel. She waited on Naaman's wife.
5:3 One day she told her mistress, "I wish my master could stand before the prophet in Samaria. Then the prophet would take his skin disease away from him."
5:4 Naaman went to his master and told him, "The girl from Israel's land said such-and-such."
5:5 "Go on, go," said the king of Aram, "and let me send a letter along to Israel's king." He set out, and in his hands he took ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of clothes.
5:6 He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It said, "Now, with the arrival of this letter, look: I have sent you Naaman, my servant. Take his skin disease away from him."
5:7 The king of Israel read the letter and ripped his clothes. "Am I God, able to kill and make alive," he said, "that this fellow sends a man to me to have his skin disease taken away? Think it over, please, and you will see that he is picking a fight with me."
5:8 Word reached Elisha, the man of God, that Israel's king had ripped his clothes. He sent a message to the king: "What did you rip your clothes for? Let the man come to me, please. Then he will know there is a prophet in Israel."
5:9 Naaman arrived with his horses and chariot. He halted at the entrance of Elisha's house.
5:10 Elisha sent a messenger out with this word: "Go now, and wash seven times in the Jordan. Then your flesh will return to you, and you will be clean."
5:11 Naaman went off angry. "Look," he said, "I thought for sure he would come out to me. I thought he would stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and move his hand back and forth over the place, and take the skin disease away.
5:12 The rivers of Damascus, the Abana and the Pharpar, are better than any water in Israel, aren't they? Couldn't I bathe in them and be clean?" He turned on his heel and stormed off.
5:13 But his servants came near to talk with him. "Father," they said, "suppose the prophet had given you some big thing to do. You would have done it, wouldn't you? So surely you will, when he only tells you, 'Wash and be clean.'"
5:14 Down he went into the Jordan and plunged under seven times, following the word of the man of God. His flesh came back like the flesh of a young lad, and he was clean.
5:15 He returned to the man of God with his whole troop. He came and stood before him and said, "Look, I know it now: there is no God in all the earth, only in Israel. So please take a blessing from your servant."
5:16 But Elisha said, "By the life of the LORD, whom I stand before, I will not take it." Naaman pressed him to take it, but he refused.
5:17 "Then if you won't," Naaman said, "please let your servant have a load of soil, as much as two mules can carry. From now on your servant will not offer up a burnt offering or a sacrifice to other gods, but to the LORD alone.
5:18 On this matter, may the LORD forgive your servant: when my master goes into Rimmon's house to bow down there, he leans on my hand, and I bow down in Rimmon's house. When I bow down in Rimmon's house, may the LORD forgive your servant in this matter."
5:19 "Go in peace," Elisha told him. Naaman went off from him and was some way down the road.
## Gehazi's Greed
5:20 Gehazi, servant of Elisha the man of God, said, "Look at that! My master has let Naaman here, the Aramean, off, and has not taken from his hand what he brought. By the life of the LORD, I'm going to run after him and take a little something from him."
5:21 Gehazi went chasing after Naaman. Naaman saw a man running behind him, and he let himself down from the chariot to meet him. "Is it well?" he asked.
5:22 "It is well," he said. "My master sent me with a message: 'Look, two young men of the sons of the prophets have this very moment come to me from the hills of Ephraim. Give them a talent of silver, please, and two changes of clothes.'"
5:23 "Please, take two talents," Naaman said, and he pressed him. He wrapped up the silver, two talents in two bags, along with two changes of clothes, and gave them to two of his young men, who carried them in front of Gehazi.
5:24 At the hill, Gehazi took the bags out of their hands and hid them in the house. He sent the men off, and they went.
5:25 Then he went and took his place before his master. "Gehazi, where are you coming from?" Elisha asked him. "Your servant hasn't been here or there," he said.
5:26 Elisha said to him, "Wasn't my heart there with you when a man turned down off his chariot to meet you? Is this a time to take silver, to take clothing, or olive groves and vineyards, flocks and herds, servants, men and women?
5:27 So Naaman's skin disease will stick to you and your descendants for all time." And Gehazi left Elisha's presence with skin disease, like snow.
## The Ax Head That Floated
6:1 The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "Look, please, the place where we sit before you has gotten too cramped for us.
6:2 Please let us go down to the Jordan, and each one take a beam from there. We'll make ourselves a place to stay there." "Go ahead," he said.
6:3 One of them said, "Do us the kindness, please, of going with your servants." "I'll go," he said.
6:4 He went along with them. When they reached the Jordan, they set to chopping down trees.
6:5 One man was bringing down a beam when the iron dropped off into the water. "Ah, my lord!" he shouted. "And it was a borrowed one!"
6:6 "Where did it drop?" the man of God said. The man pointed out the spot. Elisha trimmed off a piece of wood, tossed it in at that spot, and set the iron floating.
6:7 "Lift it out for yourself," he said. The man put out his hand and grabbed it.
## Raiders Struck Blind
6:8 Once, while the king of Aram was fighting Israel, he talked it over with his officers. "I'll make camp at such-and-such a spot," he said.
6:9 The man of God would then send a message to Israel's king: "Watch out, don't go by this place. Aram is heading down there."
6:10 Israel's king would send to the spot the man of God had named. Elisha warned him, and the king kept watch there, and it happened not just once or twice.
6:11 The king of Aram's heart was in a storm over this. He called his officers. "Won't you tell me," he said, "who among us is working for Israel's king?"
6:12 One of his officers said, "No, my lord king. It's Elisha. The prophet in Israel reports to Israel's king even what you say in the room where you sleep."
6:13 "Go, look where he is," he said, "and I'll send for him and take him." Someone told him, "There, in Dothan."
6:14 He sent horses and chariots there, a powerful army. They arrived in the night and ringed the town.
6:15 Early in the morning the man of God's attendant rose and stepped outside. All around the town stood an army, horses and chariots. The young man said to Elisha, "Ah, my lord! What do we do now?"
6:16 "Don't be afraid," he said. "There are more with us than with them."
6:17 Then Elisha prayed. "LORD," he said, "open his eyes, please, and let him see." Then the LORD opened the young man's eyes, and he saw: the hill was filled with horses and fiery chariots, surrounding Elisha.
6:18 When they came down at him, Elisha prayed to the LORD, "Strike this nation blind, please." So he struck them blind, as Elisha had said.
6:19 "You're on the wrong road," Elisha told them, "and this is the wrong town. Come along behind me, and I'll take you to the man you're hunting for." Then he walked them to Samaria.
6:20 As they came into Samaria, Elisha said, "LORD, open these men's eyes, and let them see." The LORD opened their eyes, and they saw that they stood right in the middle of Samaria.
6:21 Israel's king saw them and said to Elisha, "Do I cut them down, my father? Do I cut them down?"
6:22 "No, don't cut them down," he said. "Do you cut down the people your own sword and bow have captured? Put food and water before them. Let them eat and drink, and then go home to their master."
6:23 The king threw a big feast for them, and when they had eaten and drunk, he let them go, and they went back to their master. After that, Aram's raiding parties stopped coming into the land of Israel.
## Samaria Starving
6:24 Later on, Ben-hadad king of Aram called together all his forces. He went up and put Samaria under siege.
6:25 Samaria went hungry, a great famine. The siege went on until the price of a donkey's head was eighty pieces of silver, and a quarter kab of dove's dung cost five pieces of silver.
6:26 Once, when Israel's king was going along on top of the wall, a woman called out to him, "Save me, my lord king!"
6:27 "If the LORD won't rescue you," he said, "what could I rescue you with? Something from the threshing floor? From the winepress?"
6:28 "What is it?" the king asked her. She said, "This woman here told me, 'Hand over your son. We'll eat him today, and my son we'll eat tomorrow.'
6:29 We boiled my son, and we ate him. On the day after, I told her, 'Hand over your son, and we'll eat him.' But she has hidden her son away."
6:30 The king heard the woman's words and ripped his clothes. Since he was going along the wall, the people could see it: sackcloth was underneath, against his skin.
6:31 He said, "God do this to me, and more besides, if Elisha son of Shaphat keeps his head on him today!"
6:32 Elisha sat at home, and the elders sat with him. The king had sent a man on ahead. Before the messenger got to him, Elisha said to the elders, "Do you see? This son of a murderer has sent someone to remove my head! Watch: when the messenger comes, close the door and push him back with it. Aren't his master's footsteps sounding right behind him?"
6:33 He was still talking with them when the messenger came down to him. "Look," he said, "this disaster is the LORD's doing. Why keep hoping in the LORD any longer?"
## Flour for a Shekel
7:1 "Listen to the LORD's word," Elisha said. "The LORD says this: 'By this time tomorrow, in Samaria's gate, one seah of fine flour will cost a shekel, and two seahs of barley a shekel.'"
7:2 The officer whose hand the king leaned on spoke up to the man of God. "Look, suppose the LORD were to make windows in the sky. Could a thing like this happen?" "You are going to see it with your own eyes," Elisha said, "but you won't eat from it."
## Four Men at the Gate
7:3 At the entrance of the gate there were four men with skin disease. "What are we doing, sitting here till we die?" they asked each other.
7:4 "Say we decide to go into the town: the famine is there, and we'll die in there. Say we stay put: we die here. So come on, let's desert to Aram's camp. If they keep us alive, we'll live. If they put us to death, well, we'll die."
7:5 In the evening twilight they got up to go to the Aramean camp. They reached the outer part of Aram's camp, and look, not a soul was there.
7:6 The Lord had let Aram's camp hear a noise: the noise of chariots, the noise of horses, the noise of a huge army. "Listen!" they had said to one another. "Israel's king has paid the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt to come at us!"
7:7 They had sprung up in the twilight and run off, abandoning their tents, their horses and their donkeys. They left the camp just the way it was and ran to save their lives.
7:8 So when these men with skin disease reached the edge of the camp, they walked into a tent and ate and drank. They hauled silver, gold and clothing out of it, and they went off and hid it. Back they came, into a second tent. They hauled things out of that one too and went and hid them.
7:9 "This isn't right, what we're doing," they said to each other. "Today is a day of good news, and we're sitting on it. If we hold off till daybreak, guilt will find us out. Come on, then. Let's go and report it at the king's house."
7:10 They came and shouted up to the town's gatekeeper. "We went into Aram's camp," they told them, "and look, no one is there, not a sound of any person. Just the horses tied up, the donkeys tied up, and the tents the way they were."
7:11 The gatekeepers raised a shout, and the report went in to the king's house.
7:12 The king rose while it was still night. "I'll tell you what Aram is up to," he said to his officers. "They know that we're hungry. So they've slipped out of camp to hide in the fields. Their plan is, 'When they come out of the town, we'll catch them alive and walk into the town.'"
7:13 One of his officers spoke up. "Please, let some men take five of the horses that are left, the ones still remaining here. Look, they are like the whole crowd of Israel still left in the town. Look, they are like the whole crowd of Israel already gone. Let's send them and find out."
7:14 They picked out two chariot teams, and the king sent them off in the tracks of Aram's army. "Go and look," he told them.
7:15 They went after them all the way to the Jordan. The whole road was full of clothes and gear the Arameans had tossed aside as they rushed away. The messengers returned with their report for the king.
7:16 Out went the people and stripped Aram's camp. One seah of fine flour now cost a shekel, and two seahs of barley a shekel, just as the LORD's word had said.
7:17 The king had stationed the officer whose hand he leaned on to take charge of the gate. The crowd ran over him in the gateway and he died, as the man of God had spoken when the king went down to see him.
7:18 This came true just as the man of God had told the king: "By this time tomorrow, in Samaria's gate, two seahs of barley will cost a shekel, and one seah of fine flour a shekel."
7:19 The officer had answered the man of God, "Look, suppose the LORD were to make windows in the sky. Could a thing like this happen?" And Elisha had said, "You are going to see it with your own eyes, but you won't eat from it."
7:20 That is exactly how it went for him: the crowd ran over him in the gateway, and he died.
## The Shunammite Gets Her Land Back
8:1 Elisha had talked with the woman whose son he brought back to life. "Up, go away, you and your household," he said. "Go and live as a foreigner wherever you can. The LORD has summoned a famine. It is coming on the land, and it will last seven years."
8:2 So the woman rose and followed the word of the man of God. She and her household left and lived as foreigners in Philistine country for seven years.
8:3 When seven years were over, the woman returned from Philistine country. She went to plead with the king for her house and her field.
8:4 Just then the king was speaking with Gehazi, the man of God's servant. "Tell me, please," he said, "about all the great things Elisha has done."
8:5 Gehazi was in the middle of telling the king how Elisha had brought the dead to life. Just then in came the woman whose son he had brought to life, crying out to the king for her house and her field. "My lord king," Gehazi said, "this is the woman, and this is her son, the one Elisha brought to life!"
8:6 The king questioned the woman, and she told him all of it. So the king put one of his officials on her case. "Return everything she owns," he ordered, "and all that her field has produced, from the day she left the country up to now."
## Elisha Weeps before Hazael
8:7 Elisha went to Damascus at a time when Ben-hadad king of Aram lay sick. Someone told the king, "The man of God has come this far."
8:8 The king told Hazael, "Carry a gift in your hand and go out to meet the man of God. Through him, consult the LORD. Ask, 'Will I survive this sickness?'"
8:9 So Hazael went out to meet him, with a gift in hand: the best of everything Damascus had, enough to load forty camels. He came in and stood in front of him. "Your son, Ben-hadad king of Aram, sent me to you," he said. "He asks, 'Will I survive this sickness?'"
8:10 Elisha said to him, "Go and tell him, 'You are sure to live.' But the LORD has shown me he is sure to die."
8:11 Elisha held his face steady and kept it fixed on him until he was ashamed. Then the man of God burst into tears.
8:12 "What makes my lord cry?" Hazael asked. "I know the evil you are going to do to the Israelites," he answered. "Their strongholds you will send up in flames. Their young men you will kill by the sword. Their little ones you will smash, and their pregnant women you will slash open."
8:13 "Your servant is just a dog," Hazael said. "How could he do such a great thing?" "The LORD has let me see you as king over Aram," said Elisha.
8:14 He left Elisha and came to his master. "What did Elisha say to you?" the king asked him. He said, "He told me, 'You are sure to live.'"
8:15 The next day he took the bed covering, dipped it in water, and laid it across his face, and Ben-hadad died. Hazael became king in his place.
## Jehoram of Judah
8:16 In the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Jehoshaphat being king of Judah, Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat became king over Judah.
8:17 He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he ruled for eight years in Jerusalem.
8:18 He walked in the road of Israel's kings, just as Ahab's house did, because his wife was a daughter of Ahab. He did what the LORD sees as evil.
8:19 But because of his servant David, the LORD did not want to wipe out Judah. He had told David he would give him a lamp, for his sons, for all time.
8:20 During his reign Edom revolted out from under Judah's hand and made itself a king.
8:21 Joram went across to Zair, and all the chariots went with him. In the night he rose and struck Edom, which had surrounded him, and the chariot commanders. And the people fled to their tents.
8:22 Edom has stayed in revolt, out from under Judah's hand, down to this day. Then Libnah also revolted, in that same period.
8:23 The rest of Joram's story, all he did, is written down, isn't it, in the scroll of the day-by-day records of the kings of Judah?
8:24 Joram lay down with his ancestors and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. His son Ahaziah became king in his place.
## Ahaziah of Judah
8:25 In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah became king.
8:26 Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he ruled for one year in Jerusalem. His mother was named Athaliah daughter of Omri king of Israel.
8:27 He walked in the road of Ahab's house and did what the LORD sees as evil, as Ahab's house did, because he was tied to Ahab's house by marriage.
8:28 Together with Joram son of Ahab, he went to fight Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead, and the Arameans wounded Joram.
8:29 King Joram returned to Jezreel to get well from the wounds Aram had dealt him at Ramah in his fight with Hazael king of Aram. Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to Jezreel to visit Joram son of Ahab, since he was laid up.
