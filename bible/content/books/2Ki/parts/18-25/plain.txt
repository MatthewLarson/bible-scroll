## Hezekiah king of Judah
18:1 In the third year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel, Hezekiah son of Ahaz became king of Judah.
18:2 He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Abi, daughter of Zechariah.
18:3 He did what was right in the LORD's eyes, in everything just as his forefather David had done.
18:4 He was the one who took away the high places. He smashed the standing stones and cut down the Asherah pole. He crushed to pieces the bronze snake Moses had made, because up to those days the Israelites had been burning incense to it. People called it Nehushtan.
18:5 He trusted in the LORD, the God of Israel. Among all the kings of Judah, none after him was like him, and none who came before him.
18:6 He held on to the LORD and did not turn away from following him. He kept the LORD's commands, the ones he gave Moses.
18:7 So the LORD was with him. In all his ventures he did well. He rebelled against the king of Assyria and would not serve him.
18:8 He struck down the Philistines all the way to Gaza and its lands, from lookout tower to walled town.
## Samaria taken
18:9 It was King Hezekiah's fourth year, the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel, when Shalmaneser king of Assyria marched against Samaria and put it under siege.
18:10 They captured it after three years. Samaria fell in Hezekiah's sixth year, which was Hoshea's ninth as king of Israel.
18:11 The king of Assyria carried Israel off into exile in Assyria. He brought them to Halah, to the Habor River of Gozan, and to the towns of Media.
18:12 It happened because they had not listened to the voice of the LORD their God. They had broken his covenant, all that Moses the LORD's servant had commanded. They would not listen, and they would not do it.
## Sennacherib invades
18:13 In King Hezekiah's fourteenth year, Sennacherib king of Assyria marched on all the walled towns of Judah and seized them.
18:14 Hezekiah king of Judah sent word to the king of Assyria at Lachish: "I have done wrong. Pull back from me. Whatever you lay on me, I will carry." The king of Assyria laid on Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.
18:15 Hezekiah handed over all the silver that could be found in the LORD's house and in the treasuries of the king's house.
18:16 At that time Hezekiah cut off the doors of the LORD's temple and the doorposts that he, Hezekiah king of Judah, had overlaid, and he gave them to the king of Assyria.
## The Rabshakeh at the wall
18:17 The king of Assyria sent the Tartan, the Rab-saris and the Rabshakeh from Lachish to King Hezekiah with a huge army, to Jerusalem. They went up and came to Jerusalem. They went up, came, and took their stand by the water channel of the Upper Pool, on the main road to the Launderers' Field.
18:18 They called out for the king. Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the palace, went out to them, along with Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder.
18:19 The Rabshakeh said to them, "Tell Hezekiah, please, 'The great king, the king of Assyria, says this: What is this trust of yours that you trust in?
18:20 Do you think mere words from your lips make a plan and strength for war? Now, who are you leaning on, that you have rebelled against me?
18:21 Look, now you are relying on Egypt, this crushed reed you use as a walking stick. A man leans on it, and it goes into his palm and pierces it. That is Pharaoh king of Egypt to everyone who relies on him.
18:22 And if you tell me, "We trust in the LORD our God," aren't his high places and altars the very ones Hezekiah has done away with? He has told Judah and Jerusalem, "You must bow down in front of this altar in Jerusalem."
18:23 So now, please, place a bet with my master the king of Assyria. Let me give you two thousand horses, if you can find riders to put on them.
18:24 How could you beat back one governor, even one of my master's lowliest servants? And you count on Egypt for chariots and horsemen!
18:25 Now, is it without the LORD that I have marched up against this place to ruin it? It was the LORD who said to me, "March up against this land and ruin it."'"
18:26 Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, Shebna and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, "Please, talk to us, your servants, in Aramaic. We can understand it. Don't talk with us in Judean while the people on the wall are listening."
18:27 But the Rabshakeh said to them, "Is it to your master and to you that my master sent me with this message? No, it is to the men sitting on the wall. They are the ones who, along with you, will eat their own dung and drink their own urine."
18:28 The Rabshakeh stood up and shouted at the top of his voice in Judean. "Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria!
18:29 The king says this: 'Don't let Hezekiah fool you, for he cannot rescue you from his hand.
18:30 Don't let Hezekiah get you to rely on the LORD with his talk: "The LORD will surely rescue us. This city will not be handed over to the king of Assyria."
18:31 Don't listen to Hezekiah! For the king of Assyria says this: Make a blessing with me and come out to me. Then you can each eat from your own vine and your own fig tree, and drink the water of your own cistern,
18:32 until I come and take you away to a land like your own land. It is a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of olive oil and honey. Live, and don't die! Don't listen to Hezekiah, for he is leading you astray when he says, "The LORD will rescue us."
18:33 Of all the nations' gods, did a single one ever rescue his land from the king of Assyria's hand?
18:34 Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena and Ivvah? Did they rescue Samaria from my hand?
18:35 Which one of all the gods of these lands has rescued his land from my hand? And the LORD is going to rescue Jerusalem from my hand?'"
18:36 The people stayed silent. Not one of them answered him a word. That was the king's order: "Don't answer him."
18:37 Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the palace, Shebna the secretary, and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder, came to Hezekiah with their clothes ripped. They reported to him what the Rabshakeh had said.
## Hezekiah sends to Isaiah
19:1 When King Hezekiah heard it, he tore his clothes, wrapped himself in sackcloth, and went into the LORD's house.
19:2 He sent Eliakim, who was over the palace, Shebna the secretary, and the elders of the priests, all wrapped in sackcloth, to the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz.
19:3 They said to him, "Hezekiah says this: 'Today is a day of trouble, of rebuke and of scorn. The children have come right to the mouth of the womb, but there is no strength to push them out.
19:4 Maybe the LORD your God will hear everything the Rabshakeh said. His master, the king of Assyria, sent him to insult the living God. Maybe the LORD your God will call him to account for the words the LORD your God has heard. So raise a prayer for the remnant still here.'"
19:5 So King Hezekiah's servants came to Isaiah,
19:6 and Isaiah told them, "Tell your master this: 'The LORD says: Don't be afraid because of what you heard, the words the errand boys of Assyria's king used to revile me.
19:7 I am about to put a spirit into him. A report will reach him, and he will go back to his own land. There, in his land, I will bring him down by the sword.'"
## Sennacherib's letter
19:8 So the Rabshakeh turned back. He had heard that the king had pulled out of Lachish, and he found the king of Assyria at war with Libnah.
19:9 Word reached the king about Tirhakah king of Cush: "He has marched out to fight you." So he once more sent messengers to Hezekiah with this message:
19:10 "Say this to Hezekiah king of Judah: 'Don't let your God, the one you trust, fool you when he says, "Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria."
19:11 Look, you yourself have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, how they put them under the ban to wipe them out. And you think you will be rescued?
19:12 My fathers wiped out Gozan, Haran, Rezeph and the people of Eden who were in Telassar. Did the gods of those nations rescue them?
19:13 Where is Hamath's king now? Or Arpad's? Or the king of the town of Sepharvaim, of Hena or of Ivvah?'"
## Hezekiah's prayer
19:14 Hezekiah took the letter from the messengers' hands and read it through. Then he went up to the LORD's house, and there Hezekiah spread it out before the LORD.
19:15 Hezekiah prayed in the LORD's presence. He said, "LORD, God of Israel, seated on the cherubim! You are God, and you alone, over every kingdom on earth. It was you who made the heavens and the earth.
19:16 Bend your ear, LORD, and listen. LORD, open your eyes and look. Listen to the message Sennacherib sent to insult the living God.
19:17 LORD, it is true. Assyria's kings have turned the nations and their lands into ruins.
19:18 They threw the gods of those nations into the fire. Those were no gods at all, only things that human hands had made out of wood and stone. So they destroyed them.
19:19 Now, LORD our God, rescue us, please, out of his hand. Then every kingdom on earth will know that you, LORD, are God, and only you."
## Isaiah's answer
19:20 Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent this message to Hezekiah: "The LORD, the God of Israel, says this: You prayed to me about Sennacherib king of Assyria. I have heard.
19:21 This is the word the LORD has spoken against him: / 'She looks down on you, she laughs at you, / the virgin, Daughter Zion. / Behind your back she shakes her head, / Daughter Jerusalem.
19:22 Who is it you have insulted and reviled? / Whom have you raised your voice against, / and lifted your eyes at so high? / The Holy One of Israel!
19:23 Through your messengers you have insulted the Lord. / You said, "With my many chariots / I have climbed the mountain heights, / the far slopes of Lebanon. / I chopped down its tall cedars, / its choice cypresses. / I reached its farthest lodge, / its garden-like forest.
19:24 I dug, and I drank the water of strange lands. / Under my marching feet / I dried up every channel of the Nile."
19:25 Haven't you heard? / I did this long ago. / In ancient days I shaped it, / and now I have made it happen: / you were there to crush walled cities / into piles of rubble.
19:26 Those who lived there had no power left. / They were terrified and ashamed. / They were like the plants of the field, / like fresh green grass, / grass on the rooftops, / dried up before it can stand tall.
19:27 I know when you sit down, / when you go out, when you come in, / and how you storm against me.
19:28 Since you storm against me, / and your arrogance has come up to my ears, / I will put my hook in your nose / and my bridle on your lips. / I will lead you back / by the same road you came.'
19:29 And this will be your sign: This year you will eat the grain that comes up on its own, / and the year after, the grain that sprouts from that. / Then in the third year sow and harvest, / plant vineyards and eat what they bear.
19:30 Those who escape, the house of Judah that remains, / will again send roots down / and put out fruit on top.
19:31 Out of Jerusalem a remnant will go out, / survivors from Mount Zion. / The zeal of the LORD of Armies will make it happen.
19:32 So the LORD says this about the king of Assyria: / He will not get into this city. / He will not shoot a single arrow at it. / No shield of his will face it. / He will not heap up a ramp against it.
19:33 He will go home by the road he came. / Into this city he will not come, / the LORD declares.
19:34 I will cover this city like a shield, to rescue it, / for my own sake, and for David my servant's sake."
## The night of the angel
19:35 It happened that night. The angel of the LORD went out and struck the camp of Assyria: one hundred eighty-five thousand. People rose early in the morning, and there they all were, dead bodies.
19:36 So Sennacherib king of Assyria pulled up stakes and left. He went back home and stayed in Nineveh.
19:37 He was bowing down in the house of Nisroch his god when Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, cut him down with the sword. They escaped to the land of Ararat, and his son Esarhaddon became king in his place.
## Hezekiah's sickness
20:1 In those days Hezekiah was sick and dying. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and said, "The LORD says this: 'Give orders to your household, for you are about to die. You will not live.'"
20:2 He turned his face to the wall and prayed this to the LORD:
20:3 "Oh, LORD, please remember: I have lived my life before you in truth, with an undivided heart. I have done what is good in your eyes." And Hezekiah cried and cried.
20:4 Isaiah was not yet out of the middle courtyard when the LORD's word came to him:
20:5 "Turn around and say to Hezekiah, ruler of my people: 'The LORD, the God of your forefather David, says this: I have heard your prayer. I have seen your tears. Look, I am going to heal you. On the third day you will go up to the LORD's house.
20:6 I will add fifteen years to your days. I will rescue you and this city out of the king of Assyria's hand. I will cover this city like a shield, for my own sake and for David my servant's sake.'"
20:7 Then Isaiah said, "Take a lump of pressed figs." They took it and put it on the sore, and he lived.
20:8 Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What sign is there that the LORD is going to heal me, and that on the third day I will go up to the LORD's house?"
20:9 Isaiah said, "Here is the sign the LORD gives you, to show that the LORD will do what he has said. The shadow has moved forward ten steps. Shall it go back ten steps?"
20:10 "It is a small thing for the shadow to grow longer by ten steps," said Hezekiah. "No, let the shadow go back, backward ten steps."
20:11 Isaiah the prophet cried out to the LORD. Then the LORD turned the shadow backward ten steps, on the steps of Ahaz where it had gone down.
## Envoys from Babylon
20:12 At that time Berodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for news had reached him that Hezekiah had been sick.
20:13 Hezekiah listened to them and gave them a tour of his whole treasure house: the silver and gold, the spices and fine oil, his arsenal, and everything to be found in his storerooms. In his house and in his whole kingdom, Hezekiah left nothing unshown.
20:14 Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah. "What did these men say?" he asked. "And where have they come to you from?" "From a land far away," Hezekiah answered. "They came from Babylon."
20:15 He asked, "In your house, what did they see?" Hezekiah said, "Everything in my house, they saw. In my storerooms there was not a thing I didn't show them."
20:16 Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the LORD's word:
20:17 'Look, the days are coming. Everything in your house, all that your ancestors have piled up until today, will be hauled away to Babylon. Not a thing will remain, the LORD says.
20:18 Some of your own sons, born from your body, sons you will father, they will take. They will serve as eunuchs in the Babylonian king's palace.'"
20:19 Hezekiah answered Isaiah, "This word of the LORD that you have spoken is good." He went on, "Is it not good, if there will be peace and safety in my days?"
20:20 The rest of Hezekiah's story, all his might, and how he made the pool and the channel and brought the water into the city, is written in the scroll of the records of the kings of Judah, is it not?
20:21 Hezekiah lay down with his ancestors, and his son Manasseh became king in his place.
## Manasseh king of Judah
21:1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hephzibah.
21:2 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes. He copied the abhorrent ways of the nations that the LORD drove out ahead of the Israelites.
21:3 The high places that his father Hezekiah had torn down, he built again. He put up altars for Baal and made an Asherah pole, just like Ahab king of Israel. He bowed down to all the army of the heavens and served them.
21:4 He even built altars inside the LORD's house, the very place the LORD had spoken of when he said, "I will put my name in Jerusalem."
21:5 In both courtyards of the LORD's house he built altars to all the army of the heavens.
21:6 He made his son pass through the fire. He read signs and looked for omens. He appointed people to deal with ghosts of the dead and spirits that know things. He did a great deal of what was evil in the LORD's eyes, to provoke him.
21:7 The idol of Asherah that he had carved, he placed in the house about which the LORD had told David and his son Solomon: "This house, and Jerusalem, the city I chose out of all Israel's tribes, is where I will put my name for all time.
21:8 Never again will I send Israel's feet wandering away from the soil I gave their ancestors, as long as they take care to do everything I commanded them, the whole law that my servant Moses laid on them."
21:9 They did not listen, though. Manasseh lured them off course, and they did worse evil than the nations the LORD wiped out ahead of the Israelites.
## The LORD's sentence on Judah
21:10 So the LORD spoke by means of his servants the prophets. He said,
21:11 "Manasseh king of Judah has done these abhorrent things, worse than anything the Amorites before him did. With his idols he has made Judah sin as well.
21:12 So the LORD, the God of Israel, says this: Look, I am bringing on Jerusalem and Judah a disaster that will make both ears ring for anyone who hears about it.
21:13 Over Jerusalem I will stretch Samaria's measuring cord and the leveling weight of Ahab's house. I will wipe Jerusalem out the way someone wipes out a bowl: he wipes it, then turns it face down.
21:14 The leftover part of my inheritance I will abandon. I will give them into their enemies' hands, and every enemy they have will carry them off as loot and spoils,
21:15 because they did what was evil in my eyes. They have been making me angry since the day their ancestors left Egypt, right up to today."
21:16 On top of that, Manasseh shed innocent blood, a great deal of it, until he had filled Jerusalem with it, brim to brim. That was apart from the sin he led Judah into, doing what was evil in the LORD's eyes.
21:17 The rest of Manasseh's story, all he did and the sin he sinned, is written in the scroll of the records of the kings of Judah, is it not?
21:18 Manasseh lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the garden of his house, the garden of Uzza. His son Amon became king in his place.
## Amon
21:19 Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Meshullemeth, daughter of Haruz, from Jotbah.
21:20 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes, as his father Manasseh had done.
21:21 He walked the whole road his father had walked. The idols his father served, he served too, and he bowed down to them.
21:22 He left the LORD, the God of his ancestors, and did not walk in the LORD's way.
21:23 Amon's servants plotted against him and killed the king in his own house.
21:24 Then the people of the land killed everyone in the plot against King Amon. And the people of the land put his son Josiah on the throne after him.
21:25 The rest of what Amon did is written in the scroll of the records of the kings of Judah, is it not?
21:26 They buried him in his grave in the garden of Uzza, and his son Josiah became king in his place.
## Josiah king of Judah
22:1 Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jedidah, daughter of Adaiah, from Bozkath.
22:2 He did what was right in the LORD's eyes. He walked the whole road of his forefather David, swerving neither right nor left.
## Repairs in the LORD's house
22:3 In King Josiah's eighteenth year, the king sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, son of Meshullam, the secretary, to the LORD's house with these orders:
22:4 "Go up to Hilkiah the high priest. Have him total up the silver brought into the LORD's house, which the guards of the doorway have gathered from the people.
22:5 Let them put it into the hands of the work supervisors who oversee the LORD's house, and let them pay it out to the workmen in the LORD's house who are fixing what is broken in the house:
22:6 to the woodworkers, the builders and the stonemasons, and for buying lumber and cut stone to repair the house.
22:7 No one is to keep accounts with them for the silver put in their hands, because they act in good faith."
## The scroll found in the temple
22:8 Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the secretary, "The scroll of the law! I have found it in the LORD's house." Hilkiah gave the scroll to Shaphan, and Shaphan read it.
22:9 Shaphan the secretary came to the king and reported back to him. "Your servants have emptied out the silver that was in the house," he said, "and put it in the hands of the work supervisors who oversee the LORD's house."
22:10 Shaphan the secretary went on to tell the king, "There's a scroll Hilkiah the priest gave me." Then Shaphan read it out in front of the king.
22:11 The king heard the words in the scroll of the law, and he tore his clothes.
22:12 The king gave orders to Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Achbor son of Micaiah, Shaphan the secretary and Asaiah the king's servant:
22:13 "Go and seek the LORD for me, for the people and for all Judah, about the words of this scroll that was found. The LORD's anger is great. It has caught fire against us, because our ancestors did not listen to the words of this scroll and do everything written in it for us."
## Huldah the prophet
22:14 So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan and Asaiah went to Huldah, a woman prophet. She was the wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, son of Harhas, the one in charge of the robes, and she lived in Jerusalem in the second district. They talked with her.
22:15 She told them, "The LORD, the God of Israel, says this: 'Tell the man who sent you to me:
22:16 The LORD says this: Look, I am bringing disaster on this place and on those who live here, all the words of the scroll the king of Judah read.
22:17 They left me. They sent up smoke to other gods, to anger me with everything their hands made. So my anger has caught fire against this place, and no one will put it out.'
22:18 And to the king of Judah, who sent you to seek the LORD, say this: 'The LORD, the God of Israel, says this about the words you heard:
22:19 Your heart grew soft. You humbled yourself before the LORD when you heard what I said about this place and its people, that they would become a ruin and a curse. You tore your clothes and wept in front of me. So I, for my part, have heard, the LORD declares.
22:20 That is why I am going to gather you in with your ancestors. You will be gathered into your grave in peace, and your eyes will not have to see all the disaster I am bringing on this place.'" The men reported this to the king.
## The covenant renewed
23:1 The king sent out a call, and all the elders of Judah and of Jerusalem came together to him.
23:2 The king went up to the LORD's house, and with him went all the men of Judah and all who lived in Jerusalem, the priests and the prophets, all the people, small and great alike. Into their ears he read every word of the covenant scroll that had been found in the LORD's house.
23:3 The king stood beside the pillar and made the covenant before the LORD: to walk after the LORD, to keep his commands, his decrees and his rules with all heart and all being, and so to make the words of this covenant written in this scroll stand. All the people took their stand in the covenant.
## Josiah cleans out Judah
23:4 The king ordered Hilkiah the high priest, the priests of the second rank and the guards of the doorway to carry out of the LORD's temple every item made for Baal, for Asherah and for all the army of the heavens. Outside Jerusalem, in the Kidron fields, he burned them, and he hauled their dust to Bethel.
23:5 He got rid of the idol priests. The kings of Judah had put them in place to burn incense at the high places in Judah's towns and all around Jerusalem. He also got rid of those who sent up smoke to Baal, to the sun, to the moon, to the star signs and to all the army of the heavens.
23:6 The Asherah pole he carried out of the LORD's house, out of Jerusalem, down to the Kidron Wadi, and in the Kidron Wadi he burned it. He beat it into dust and flung its dust onto the burial ground of the ordinary people.
23:7 He pulled down the quarters of the shrine men in the LORD's house, where women wove coverings for Asherah.
23:8 He brought in all the priests from Judah's towns. From Geba all the way to Beersheba, he made the high places where those priests had burned incense unclean. He knocked down the gate shrines too, the ones at the entrance to the Gate of Joshua, the city's governor, on your left as you go through the city gate.
23:9 Still, those priests of the high places never went up to the LORD's altar in Jerusalem. But they ate bread made without yeast with their brother priests.
23:10 Topheth, in the Valley of Ben-hinnom, he made unclean, so that no one would ever again make his son or his daughter pass through the fire for Molech.
23:11 He did away with the horses the kings of Judah had set up for the sun at the way into the LORD's house, beside the room of Nathan-melech the court official, out in the Parbar. The sun's chariots he burned in the fire.
23:12 The king also pulled down the altars on the roof of Ahaz's upper room, which the kings of Judah had built, and the altars Manasseh had built in both courtyards of the LORD's house. He rushed them away from there and threw their dust into the Kidron Wadi.
23:13 The king made unclean the high places facing Jerusalem, south of the Mount of Destruction, which Solomon king of Israel had built for Ashtoreth, the vile idol of the Sidonians, for Chemosh, the vile idol of Moab, and for Milcom, the abhorrent thing of the Ammonites.
23:14 He broke the standing stones, chopped down the Asherah poles, and filled the places where they had stood with human bones.
## Bethel and Samaria
23:15 The altar at Bethel as well, the high place made by Jeroboam son of Nebat, the one who led Israel into sin: that altar too, and the high place, he tore down. He set fire to the high place and beat it to dust, and he set fire to the Asherah pole.
23:16 Josiah turned and saw the graves there on the mountain. He sent men to take the bones out of the graves, and he burned them on the altar. So he made it unclean, just as the LORD's word had said, the word that the man of God called out, the man who called out these things.
23:17 He asked, "That stone marker I'm looking at, what is it?" The townspeople answered him, "That's the grave of the man of God who came from Judah. He called out these things you have just done to the altar at Bethel."
23:18 "Leave him be," he said. "Nobody touch his bones." So they spared his bones, and with them the bones of the prophet who had come from Samaria.
23:19 Throughout the towns of Samaria, the kings of Israel had made high-place shrines to stir up anger. Josiah took away every one of them, and he did to them all that he had done at Bethel.
23:20 On the altars he sacrificed every priest of the high places who was there, and he burned human bones on them. After that he returned to Jerusalem.
## Josiah's Passover
23:21 Then the king gave an order to all the people: "Hold a Passover for the LORD your God, by what is written in this covenant scroll."
23:22 No Passover like this one had been held since the time of the judges who led Israel, through all the years of Israel's kings and Judah's kings.
23:23 Only in King Josiah's eighteenth year was this Passover held for the LORD in Jerusalem.
23:24 Josiah also swept away the ghosts of the dead and the spirits that know things, the household gods, the idols and all the disgusting things to be seen in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem. He did it to make the words of the law stand, the words written in the scroll Hilkiah the priest had found in the LORD's house.
23:25 No king before him was like him, turning back to the LORD with all his heart, with all his being and with all his strength, in keeping with the whole law of Moses. And none like him rose up after him.
## The anger that did not turn
23:26 Still, the LORD did not turn back from his great burning anger. His anger burned against Judah over all the things Manasseh had done to provoke him.
23:27 The LORD said, "Judah as well I will put away out of my sight, the way I put Israel away. I will throw off this city I chose, Jerusalem, and the house I spoke of when I said, 'There my name will be.'"
23:28 The rest of Josiah's story, and all he did, is written in the scroll of the records of the kings of Judah, is it not?
## Josiah dies at Megiddo
23:29 It was during his reign that Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt marched up toward the king of Assyria, to the Euphrates River. King Josiah went out to meet him, and the moment Neco saw him, he put him to death at Megiddo.
23:30 His servants put his dead body in a chariot and drove it from Megiddo to Jerusalem, where they buried him in his own grave. The people of the land took Josiah's son Jehoahaz. They anointed him and made him king to succeed his father.
## Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim
23:31 Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal, daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah.
23:32 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes, just as his ancestors had done.
23:33 Pharaoh Neco locked him up at Riblah in the land of Hamath to stop his rule in Jerusalem, and he fined the land one hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.
23:34 Then Pharaoh Neco put Eliakim son of Josiah on the throne in place of his father Josiah, and turned his name into Jehoiakim. Jehoahaz he took away. Jehoahaz came to Egypt and died there.
23:35 The silver and the gold Jehoiakim did pay to Pharaoh. But to give the silver at Pharaoh's word, he had to put a value on the land. He squeezed the silver and gold out of the people of the land, each man by the value set on him, to pay Pharaoh Neco.
23:36 Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zebudah, daughter of Pedaiah, from Rumah.
23:37 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes, just as his ancestors had done.
## Babylon's raiders
24:1 While he reigned, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon marched up. Jehoiakim served under him three years, and then he turned around and rebelled against him.
24:2 The LORD sent raiding parties against him: Chaldean raiders, Aramean raiders, Moabite raiders and Ammonite raiders. He let them loose on Judah to ruin it, keeping the word the LORD had spoken by means of his servants the prophets.
24:3 It was by the LORD's own word that this happened to Judah, to put them out of his sight. It was for Manasseh's sins, everything he did,
24:4 and for the innocent blood he poured out, too. He flooded Jerusalem with innocent blood, and that the LORD would not consent to forgive.
24:5 The rest of Jehoiakim's story, and all he did, is written in the scroll of the records of the kings of Judah, is it not?
24:6 Jehoiakim lay down with his ancestors, and his son Jehoiachin became king in his place.
24:7 Egypt's king never again marched out of his land. The king of Babylon had seized everything from the Wadi of Egypt to the Euphrates River, all that used to belong to Egypt's king.
## Jehoiachin and the first exile
24:8 Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Nehushta, daughter of Elnathan, from Jerusalem.
24:9 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes, just as his father had done.
24:10 At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon marched up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege.
24:11 Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon himself came to the city while his servants were laying siege to it.
24:12 Jehoiachin king of Judah came out to the king of Babylon, together with his mother, his servants, his commanders and his court officials. In the eighth year of his reign the king of Babylon took him.
24:13 From there he hauled out all the treasure of the LORD's house and the treasure of the king's house. All the gold objects that Solomon king of Israel had made in the LORD's temple, he cut to pieces, just as the LORD had said.
24:14 He marched all Jerusalem off into exile: every commander, every strong fighting man, ten thousand captives, and every craftsman and smith. Nobody stayed behind but the poorest of the country's people.
24:15 Jehoiachin he took away to Babylon, and the king's mother, the king's wives, his court officials and the powerful men of the land. He led them all from Jerusalem into exile in Babylon.
24:16 All the men of strength, seven thousand of them, and the craftsmen and smiths, a thousand, every one a strong man fit to fight: the king of Babylon brought them as captives to Babylon.
24:17 In Jehoiachin's place the king of Babylon set up Mattaniah, his uncle, as king, and turned his name into Zedekiah.
## Zedekiah
24:18 Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal, daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah.
24:19 He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes, just as Jehoiakim had done.
24:20 All this happened in Jerusalem and Judah because the LORD was angry, until at last he flung them away from his face. Then Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.
## The siege of Jerusalem
25:1 Then, in the ninth year of his reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon arrived at Jerusalem, he and his whole army. He camped against the city, and they built a siege wall all the way around it.
25:2 The city stayed under siege until King Zedekiah's eleventh year.
25:3 On the ninth day of the fourth month, hunger gripped the city hard. The people of the land had no bread.
25:4 Then the city was breached. All the fighting men got away that night through the gate between the double walls, by the king's garden, even with the Chaldeans surrounding the city. The king went off toward the Arabah.
25:5 The Chaldean army went after the king and caught him on the Jericho plains. All his troops had scattered and left him.
25:6 They took the king prisoner and hauled him up to Riblah, to the king of Babylon, and there his case was judged.
25:7 Zedekiah's sons they slaughtered while he watched. Then the king of Babylon put out Zedekiah's eyes, bound him in bronze fetters and brought him to Babylon.
## The temple burned
25:8 On the seventh day of the fifth month, which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan came into Jerusalem. He was chief of the guard and a servant of Babylon's king.
25:9 He set the LORD's house on fire, and the king's house, and all the houses of Jerusalem. Every house of any importance, he burned with fire.
25:10 Jerusalem's walls, all the way around, were torn down by the whole Chaldean army under the chief of the guard.
25:11 The people still left in the city, those who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and whatever was left of the crowd: Nebuzaradan the chief of the guard marched them all off into exile.
25:12 Some of the poor of the land the chief of the guard left behind, to tend vines and to plow.
25:13 The bronze pillars of the LORD's house, the stands, and the bronze Sea in the LORD's house, the Chaldeans smashed, and they hauled the bronze off to Babylon.
25:14 They took the pots, the shovels, the wick trimmers, the ladles, and every bronze object used for service there.
25:15 The fire pans and the bowls too, the gold ones for their gold and the silver ones for their silver: the chief of the guard took them.
25:16 The two pillars, the one Sea, and the stands that Solomon made for the LORD's house: no one could weigh the bronze in all those objects.
25:17 One pillar stood eighteen cubits tall, with a bronze capital on it. The capital rose three cubits, with network and pomegranates on it all around, all of bronze. The second pillar was like it, network and all.
## The leaders put to death
25:18 The chief of the guard took Seraiah the head priest, Zephaniah the deputy priest, and the three guards of the doorway.
25:19 Out of the city he took one court official who had been in charge over the fighting men, and five men of those who saw the king's face, who were still in the city. He took the secretary of the army commander, the one who drafted the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.
25:20 Nebuzaradan, chief of the guard, took them and marched them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.
25:21 At Riblah in the land of Hamath the king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death. And so Judah went into exile, away from its own soil.
## Gedaliah at Mizpah
25:22 As for the people still left in the land of Judah, the ones Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon let remain, he put Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, in charge of them.
25:23 The commanders of the forces, they and their men, got word that the king of Babylon had put Gedaliah in charge. So they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth from Netophah, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, with their men.
25:24 Gedaliah gave them and their men his oath. "Don't fear the Chaldeans' servants," he told them. "Stay in the land. Serve the king of Babylon, and things will be good for you."
25:25 Then, in the seventh month, Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, one of the royal line, came with ten men. They struck Gedaliah down and he died, and so did the Judeans and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.
25:26 So all the people, small and great, and the commanders of the forces got up and left for Egypt. They were afraid of the Chaldeans.
## Jehoiachin set free
25:27 It was the thirty-seventh year since Jehoiachin king of Judah went into exile, the twelfth month, the twenty-seventh day of the month. In the year Evil-merodach became king of Babylon, he lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah and brought him out of the prison house.
25:28 He spoke good words to him and set his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon.
25:29 He changed out of his prison clothes, and he ate bread in the king's presence always, every day he lived.
25:30 His food allowance was a steady one, given him by the king, each day's share on its day, for as long as he lived.
