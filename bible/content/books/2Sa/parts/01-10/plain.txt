## David hears of Saul's death
1:1 After Saul died, David came back from defeating the Amalekites and spent two days in Ziklag.
1:2 Then, on the third day, a man showed up, coming from the camp where Saul had been. His clothes were torn and he had earth on his head. He came to David, fell to the ground and bowed down.
1:3 David asked him, "Where are you coming from?" "I have escaped out of Israel's camp," he answered.
1:4 "What news is there?" David said. "Please tell me." "The army fled from the fighting," he said. "Large numbers of the people fell and are dead. Saul is dead as well, and his son Jonathan."
1:5 Then David asked the young man who was telling him this, "How do you know Saul is dead, and his son Jonathan?"
1:6 "I just happened to be up on Mount Gilboa," said the young man who was telling him. "I saw Saul. He was leaning on his spear, and the chariots and their riders had caught up with him.
1:7 He turned back, caught sight of me, and called out to me. I answered, 'Here I am.'
1:8 'Who are you?' he said. I told him, 'I am an Amalekite.'
1:9 'Please stand over me and kill me,' he said. 'A spinning dizziness has taken hold of me, though I still have all my life in me.'
1:10 So I stood over him and put him to death. I knew that once he had fallen, he would not live. Then I took the crown from his head and the band from his arm. Here they are. I have brought them to my lord."
1:11 David seized his garments and tore them apart. Every one of his men did it too.
1:12 They grieved and cried. They fasted until evening over Saul and over his son Jonathan, over the LORD's people and over the house of Israel, because the sword had cut them down.
1:13 David asked the young man who had brought the news, "Where do you come from?" "I am the son of a resident foreigner, an Amalekite," he said.
1:14 "How could you not be afraid to stretch out your hand and bring ruin on the LORD's anointed?" David asked him.
1:15 David called to one of his young men. "Come close," he said. "Fall on him." The man cut him down, and he died.
1:16 David said to him, "Your blood is on your head. You spoke against yourself with your own mouth. You said, 'I put the LORD's anointed to death.'"
## The song of the bow
1:17 David raised this lament over Saul and over Jonathan his son.
1:18 He told them to teach it to Judah's people, the song called "The Bow." Look, it is written in the Book of Jashar:
1:19 "Your splendor, Israel, lies struck down on your heights. / How the warriors have fallen!
1:20 Do not tell it in Gath. / Do not carry the news through the streets of Ashkelon, / or the Philistines' daughters will be glad, / the daughters of the uncircumcised will celebrate.
1:21 You mountains in Gilboa, / may no dew and no rain come down on you, / and may your fields bear nothing to offer. / For there the warriors' shield was soiled, / Saul's shield, with no oil to anoint it.
1:22 Jonathan's bow never pulled back / from the blood of the fallen, from the fat of warriors, / and Saul's sword never came home empty.
1:23 Saul and Jonathan, so dearly loved, so pleasant, / in their lives and in their deaths were never torn apart. / Faster than eagles they were, / mightier than lions.
1:24 Daughters of Israel, / cry over Saul. / He dressed you in scarlet and luxury. / He hung gold ornaments on your clothing.
1:25 How the warriors have fallen / in the thick of the battle! / Jonathan lies struck down on your heights.
1:26 I am in anguish for you, / my brother Jonathan. / You were a great delight to me. / Your love was a wonder to me, / more than the love of women.
1:27 How the warriors have fallen! / The weapons of war are lost."
## Judah anoints David at Hebron
2:1 After this, David asked the LORD for guidance. "Should I go up into one of Judah's towns?" "Go up," the LORD told him. "Where should I go up?" David asked. "Hebron," he said.
2:2 David went up there, and his two wives went too: Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail, who had been the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.
2:3 He brought up his men as well, every man with his family, and they made their homes in the towns of Hebron.
2:4 Judah's men came, and there they anointed David to be king over Judah's house. Someone told David, "The men of Jabesh-gilead are the ones who buried Saul."
2:5 David sent messengers to the people of Jabesh-gilead with this word: "The LORD bless you! You have done this act of loyal love for your lord, for Saul. You buried him.
2:6 So now, may the LORD deal with you in loyal love and faithfulness. I also will treat you well for this, because of what you have done.
2:7 So now, take courage and be men of valor. Your lord Saul is dead, and as for me, Judah's house has anointed me to be their king."
## Ish-bosheth, king of Israel
2:8 But Abner son of Ner, who commanded Saul's army, took Saul's son Ish-bosheth across to Mahanaim.
2:9 There Abner made him king of Gilead, of the Ashurites and of Jezreel; also of Ephraim and of Benjamin; of all Israel, in fact.
2:10 Saul's son Ish-bosheth was forty years old when he took the throne of Israel, and he ruled as king for two years. Only the house of Judah followed David.
2:11 David's time as king in Hebron, over the house of Judah, came to seven years and six months.
## The contest at the pool of Gibeon
2:12 Abner son of Ner went out from Mahanaim toward Gibeon with the servants of Saul's son Ish-bosheth.
2:13 Joab son of Zeruiah and David's servants marched out too, and the two sides met at the pool of Gibeon. They sat down facing each other, these men on this side of the pool and those men on that side.
2:14 Abner called to Joab, "Let's have the young men stand up and put on a contest for us." "Let them stand up," Joab said.
2:15 They stood up and crossed over, counted one by one. Benjamin and Ish-bosheth son of Saul had twelve, and David's servants had twelve.
2:16 Every man seized the man across from him by the head and put his sword into the other's side, and they all fell at once. So that place in Gibeon was named Helkath-hazzurim.
2:17 That day the fighting was terribly hard, and David's servants beat Abner and Israel's men.
## Abner kills Asahel
2:18 Zeruiah's three sons were there, Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel could run like one of the gazelles out in the open country.
2:19 He went after Abner and would not turn aside to the right or the left. He stayed right behind Abner.
2:20 Abner turned to look back. "Asahel? Is this you?" he asked. "Yes," he said.
2:21 Abner told him, "Turn off to your right or to your left. Seize one of the young soldiers and take his armor for yourself." But Asahel refused to turn away from following him.
2:22 Abner spoke to Asahel once more. "Turn away from chasing me. Why should I knock you to the ground? Then how could I face Joab, your brother?"
2:23 Still he would not turn away. So Abner drove the back end of his spear into his belly, and the spear came out behind him. Asahel fell and died right where he was. Anyone who reached the spot where Asahel had fallen and died stopped and stood still.
2:24 Joab and Abishai went after Abner. The sun had gone down by the time they reached the hill of Ammah, which faces Giah along the road to the Gibeon wilderness.
2:25 The men of Benjamin came together behind Abner, all in one body, and stood on the top of a certain hill.
2:26 Abner shouted to Joab, "Will the sword keep eating forever? Surely you know this will turn bitter at the end. When are you going to order the troops back from chasing their own brothers?"
2:27 "As God lives," Joab said, "if you had not said a word, the troops would have gone home this morning and stopped chasing their brothers."
2:28 Joab sounded the horn, and the whole army stood still. They did not chase Israel any further, and they did not go on fighting.
2:29 Abner and his men walked all that night through the Arabah. They crossed the Jordan, went through the whole Bithron, and reached Mahanaim.
2:30 When Joab turned back from following Abner, he gathered all the people together. Of David's servants, nineteen men were missing, and Asahel.
2:31 David's servants, though, had cut down men of Benjamin, Abner's men: three hundred and sixty of them were dead.
2:32 They carried Asahel away and laid him in the tomb of his father at Bethlehem. Joab and his men walked the whole night, and day was breaking for them when they reached Hebron.
## David's house grows strong
3:1 It was a long war, the war between Saul's house and David's house. David went on getting stronger, and Saul's house went on getting weaker.
3:2 While David was in Hebron, sons were born to him. The first was Amnon, whose mother was Ahinoam the Jezreelite.
3:3 The second was Chileab, whose mother was Abigail, Nabal the Carmelite's wife. The third was Absalom, the son of Maacah, the daughter of King Talmai of Geshur.
3:4 The fourth was Adonijah, Haggith's son. The fifth was Shephatiah, Abital's son.
3:5 The sixth was Ithream, whose mother was David's wife Eglah. These sons were born to David in Hebron.
## Abner changes sides
3:6 As the war went on between Saul's house and David's house, Abner kept building up his own power within Saul's house.
3:7 Saul had a concubine whose name was Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah. Ish-bosheth said to Abner, "Why have you gone to bed with my father's concubine?"
3:8 Ish-bosheth's words made Abner burn with anger. "So I am a dog's head, am I, one that belongs to Judah?" he said. "Up to this day I have kept loyal love with the house of your father Saul, with his brothers and his friends. I have not let you fall into David's hands. And today you hold this woman against me as a crime!
3:9 God do this to Abner, and more besides, if I do not carry out for David exactly what the LORD swore to him.
3:10 I will take the kingdom away from Saul's house and make David's throne firm over Israel and over Judah, all the way from Dan to Beersheba."
3:11 Ish-bosheth was too afraid of Abner to say one more word back to him.
3:12 Abner sent messengers to David to speak for him. "Who does the land belong to?" he said. "Make your covenant with me, and you will have my help. I will bring all Israel over to your side."
3:13 "Good," David answered. "I will make a covenant with you. There is just one thing I ask of you. You will not see my face unless you bring Saul's daughter Michal with you when you come to see me."
3:14 David also sent messengers to Saul's son Ish-bosheth with this demand: "Hand over Michal, my wife. I paid a hundred Philistine foreskins to make her mine."
3:15 Ish-bosheth sent men who took her away from a husband, from Paltiel son of Laish.
3:16 Her husband came along with her. He walked behind her crying, all the way to Bahurim. "Go on. Turn back," Abner told him, and he turned back.
3:17 Abner had talks with the elders of Israel. "For a long while now, you have been trying to get David made king over you," he said.
3:18 "So now, act. The LORD has spoken to David and said, 'Through David my servant I will rescue my people Israel. I will free them from the Philistines' power and from the power of all their enemies.'"
3:19 Abner also talked with the men of Benjamin face to face. Then he went to Hebron to tell David himself all that Israel and the whole house of Benjamin had agreed was good.
3:20 Abner arrived at Hebron with twenty men, and David held a feast for Abner and the men who came with him.
3:21 Abner told David, "Let me get up and go. I will gather all Israel to my lord the king. They will make a covenant with you, and you will be king over everything you long for." David let Abner go, and he left in peace.
## Joab kills Abner
3:22 Right then David's servants and Joab came in from a raid, with a great load of plunder. Abner was not with David in Hebron anymore. David had sent him off, and he had gone in peace.
3:23 Joab and the whole army with him came in. People told Joab, "Abner son of Ner came to the king. The king let him go, and he has left in peace."
3:24 Joab went in to the king. "What is this you have done?" he said. "Abner came here to you! Why did you send him off so that he simply walked away?
3:25 You know what Abner son of Ner is like. He came to fool you. He came to learn when you go out and when you come in, and to find out everything you are doing."
3:26 Joab left David's presence and sent messengers to go after Abner. They brought him back from the Cistern of Sirah. David knew nothing about it.
3:27 Abner came back to Hebron. Joab drew him aside into the middle of the gate to have a quiet word with him. There he stabbed him in the belly, and Abner died. It was for the blood of Asahel, Joab's brother.
3:28 Afterward David heard of it. "Before the LORD, my kingdom and I are clean of the blood of Abner son of Ner for all time," he said.
3:29 "Let it swirl down on Joab's head and on his whole father's house. May Joab's house never run out of men with a discharge, or with skin disease, or who grip a spindle, or who fall to the sword, or who go short of bread."
3:30 This is how Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner: in return for the death of their brother Asahel, whom he had killed at Gibeon during the battle.
## David mourns Abner
3:31 David ordered Joab and all the people who were with him, "Rip your clothes. Wrap yourselves in sackcloth. Go in front of Abner, mourning." King David himself walked after the bier.
3:32 They buried Abner in Hebron. The king raised his voice and cried at Abner's grave, and all the people cried.
3:33 The king chanted a lament over Abner. He said, / "Did Abner have to die the way a fool dies?
3:34 Your hands were never tied, / your feet were never brought to bronze chains. / As a man falls in front of crooked men, / so you fell." / All the people wept over him once more.
3:35 All the people came to get David to eat some food while it was still daytime. But David swore, "God do this to me, and more besides, if I eat any bread, or anything else, before sundown."
3:36 The people all noticed, and it looked good to them. In fact, whatever the king did looked good in the eyes of all the people.
3:37 That day all the people and all Israel understood that killing Abner son of Ner had not come from the king.
3:38 The king said to his servants, "Surely you know that a leader, a great man, has fallen in Israel today.
3:39 I am soft today, even though I have been anointed king, and these men, Zeruiah's sons, are too harsh for me. Let the LORD pay back the one who did this evil as his evil deserves."
## The murder of Ish-bosheth
4:1 Saul's son heard that Abner was dead in Hebron. His hands went limp, and all Israel was terrified.
4:2 Two men served the son of Saul as captains of raiding parties. The name of one was Baanah, and the name of the other was Rechab. They were sons of Rimmon of Beeroth, men of Benjamin. Beeroth, too, is counted as belonging to Benjamin.
4:3 The Beerothites had run away to Gittaim, and they have lived there as resident foreigners until today.
4:4 Saul's son Jonathan had a son with injured feet. He was a boy of five when the report came from Jezreel about Saul and Jonathan. His nurse lifted him up and ran. In her hurry to get away, he fell, and he was left lame. His name was Mephibosheth.
4:5 Rechab and Baanah, the sons of Rimmon of Beeroth, set out. They reached Ish-bosheth's house in the hottest part of the day, while he lay resting at noon.
4:6 The woman who kept the door of the house had been cleaning wheat, but she grew drowsy and fell asleep. So Rechab and his brother Baanah slipped in.
4:7 They went into the house. He was lying on his bed in the room where he slept. They struck him dead, cut his head off and carried the head away. All night long they walked along the road through the Arabah.
4:8 They brought Ish-bosheth's head to David at Hebron. "Look," they said to the king. "The head of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, your enemy, who was out to take your life! The LORD has let my lord the king take revenge today on Saul and on his descendants."
4:9 David gave his answer to Rechab and to Baanah his brother, the sons of Rimmon of Beeroth. "As the LORD lives, who has set my life free from every kind of trouble," he said,
4:10 "there was a man who brought me word, 'Saul has died.' In his own eyes he was bringing good news. I took hold of him and killed him at Ziklag. That was what I paid him for his good news.
4:11 How much worse is this! Evil men have murdered a man who did nothing wrong, inside his own house, on his own bed. So now, will I not demand his blood from your hands, and wipe you off the earth?"
4:12 David gave the order, and his young men killed them. They cut off their hands and their feet and hung them up beside the pool at Hebron. Ish-bosheth's head they took and buried in Abner's tomb in Hebron.
## King over all Israel
5:1 Every tribe of Israel came to David at Hebron. "Look," they said, "we are your own bone and flesh.
5:2 Even before, while Saul was our king, you were the one leading Israel out and bringing them home. And the LORD told you, 'You are the one who will shepherd my people Israel. You will be ruler over Israel.'"
5:3 So the elders of Israel, all of them, came to the king at Hebron. There in Hebron, in the LORD's presence, King David made a covenant with them, and they anointed him as king over Israel.
5:4 David was thirty when he took the throne, and his reign lasted forty years.
5:5 In Hebron he ruled Judah for seven years and six months. In Jerusalem he ruled all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.
## David takes Jerusalem
5:6 Then the king marched with his men on Jerusalem, against the Jebusites who lived in that land. They told David, "You will not come in here. The blind and the lame will turn you back." By this they meant, "David will never come in here."
5:7 Yet David took Zion's fortress. It is the City of David.
5:8 That day David said, "Whoever would strike the Jebusites must go up through the water shaft to reach 'the lame and the blind,' the ones David's soul hates." That is why people say, "No one blind or lame may come into the house."
5:9 David made his home in the fortress and named it the City of David. He built all around it, from the Millo inward.
5:10 David kept on growing greater, and the LORD, the God of Armies, was with him.
5:11 Hiram, king of Tyre, sent messengers to David. He also sent cedar wood, woodworkers and men who cut stone for walls, and they built David a house.
5:12 Then David understood. The LORD had set him firmly in place as Israel's king, and he had raised his kingdom high for the good of his people Israel.
## David's sons born in Jerusalem
5:13 When David had moved from Hebron, he took still more wives and concubines out of Jerusalem, and still more sons and daughters were born to him.
5:14 The children born to him in Jerusalem were named Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,
5:15 Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia,
5:16 Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.
## Victory over the Philistines
5:17 News reached the Philistines that Israel had anointed David as its king. Their whole force came up hunting for him. David learned of it and went down to the stronghold.
5:18 The Philistines arrived and fanned out across the Valley of Rephaim.
5:19 David asked the LORD about it. "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you put them in my power?" "Go up," the LORD answered David. "I will certainly put the Philistines in your power."
5:20 David went to Baal-perazim and beat them there. "The LORD has burst through my enemies ahead of me," he said, "the way water bursts through." So the place was named Baal-perazim.
5:21 There the Philistines left their idols behind. David and his men hauled them off.
5:22 Once more the Philistines came up and fanned out across the Valley of Rephaim.
5:23 When David asked the LORD again, he answered, "Don't go up at them. Swing around to their rear, and come at them from in front of the baka trees.
5:24 The moment you hear something like marching in the treetops of the baka trees, act fast. That will mean the LORD has gone out ahead of you to strike down the Philistine camp."
5:25 David did it, exactly as the LORD had told him. He cut down the Philistines from Gibeon all the way to where you enter Gezer.
## The ark and Uzzah
6:1 David again brought together Israel's picked men, thirty thousand in all.
6:2 David got up and went, with all the people who were with him, to Baalah, that is, Kiriath-jearim, which belongs to Judah. They went to bring up from there the ark of God, which is called by the Name, the name of the LORD of Armies, who sits on the cherubim as his throne.
6:3 They loaded God's ark onto a new cart and took it away from the house of Abinadab up on the hill. Abinadab's sons Uzzah and Ahio were leading the new cart.
6:4 They went with God's ark, and Ahio walked ahead of the ark.
6:5 David and the whole house of Israel made merry before the LORD with all their strength and with songs, with lyres, harps, hand drums, rattles and cymbals.
6:6 When they came to Nacon's threshing floor, the oxen stumbled, and Uzzah reached his hand out to God's ark and grabbed hold of it.
6:7 The LORD's anger burned against Uzzah, and God struck him down there for his offense. And there he died, beside God's ark.
6:8 It made David angry that the LORD had burst out in a breakout against Uzzah. To this day the place is called Perez-uzzah.
6:9 That day David feared the LORD. "How can I ever have the LORD's ark come to me?" he asked.
6:10 David would not take the LORD's ark home with him to the City of David. He had it turned aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.
6:11 For three months the LORD's ark remained at the house of Obed-edom the Gittite. The LORD brought blessing on Obed-edom and everyone in his house.
## The ark comes to Jerusalem
6:12 Someone told King David, "Because of God's ark, the LORD has blessed Obed-edom's family and everything he owns." So David went and, with joy, brought God's ark up from Obed-edom's house to the City of David.
6:13 The bearers of the LORD's ark took six steps, and then David sacrificed an ox and a fat calf.
6:14 David whirled before the LORD with all his strength. He had a linen ephod tied around him.
6:15 David and all Israel's house were bringing the LORD's ark up, shouting and blowing the ram's horn.
6:16 The LORD's ark was coming into the City of David when Saul's daughter Michal looked down from the window. She saw King David leaping and whirling in front of the LORD, and in her heart she felt contempt for him.
6:17 They carried the LORD's ark in and put it in its place, within the tent that David had set up for it. Then David offered up burnt offerings in the LORD's presence, and peace offerings.
6:18 When David was done offering up the burnt offering and the peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD of Armies.
6:19 To every person in the whole crowd of Israel, man and woman alike, he gave one round loaf of bread, one date cake and one raisin cake. Then all the people went off, each one home.
## Michal and David
6:20 When David came back to bless his own household, Michal, Saul's daughter, came out to meet him. "What honor the king of Israel won today!" she said. "He stripped himself today where his servants' slave girls could see, the way some empty-headed fellow would strip himself without shame."
6:21 David answered Michal, "I did it in front of the LORD. He chose me instead of your father and anyone in his family, and he made me ruler over the LORD's people, over Israel. In front of the LORD I will make merry.
6:22 I will lower myself even more than this. I will be small in my own eyes. But those slave girls you talked about, they are the ones who will honor me."
6:23 And Michal, Saul's daughter, had no child until the day she died.
## God's promise to David
7:1 The king had made his home in his house, and the LORD had let him rest from every enemy around him.
7:2 "Please, see how it is," the king said to Nathan the prophet. "I live in a house made of cedar, and God's ark lives inside curtains."
7:3 Nathan answered the king, "Whatever is in your heart, go and do it. The LORD is with you."
7:4 That night the LORD's word came to Nathan. It said,
7:5 "Go and say to David, my servant, 'The LORD says this: Is it you who will build a house for me to live in?
7:6 From the day I brought the Israelites up from Egypt until today, I have never lived in a house. I have walked about in a tent, in a tabernacle.
7:7 All the time I walked about among the Israelites, did I ever say a single word to any of Israel's tribes, the ones I ordered to shepherd my people Israel? Did I ever say, "Why have you not built me a cedar house?"'
7:8 Now then, tell my servant David, 'The LORD of Armies says: It was I who took you off the grazing land, from walking behind the flock, to make you ruler over my people, over Israel.
7:9 Everywhere you went, I was with you. I wiped out all your enemies ahead of you. I will give you a name as great as the names of the great men on the earth.
7:10 I will pick out a place for my people, for Israel, and plant them there. They will live in it and not be shaken anymore. Evil men will not go on crushing them as they did at first,
7:11 ever since the days when I put judges in charge of my people Israel. I will let you rest, free from all your enemies. The LORD also tells you this: the LORD will make a house for you.
7:12 When your life has run its full course and you lie down with your ancestors, I will raise up your offspring to come after you, one who comes from your own body. I will make his kingdom stand firm.
7:13 He will build a house for my name. And the throne of his kingdom I will make secure for all time.
7:14 I will be a father to him, and he will be a son to me. If he does wrong, I will correct him with a rod such as men use, with blows such as people give.
7:15 But my loyal love will not turn away from him the way I took it from Saul. I pushed Saul aside to make way for you.
7:16 Your house and your kingdom will be made secure forever in front of you. Your throne will stand firm for all time.'"
7:17 Nathan passed on to David all these words, the whole of this vision.
## David's prayer
7:18 King David went in and sat down before the LORD. "Lord GOD, who am I?" he said. "And what is my house, that you should bring me to where I am now?
7:19 Yet even this seemed too little to you, Lord GOD. You have also spoken about your servant's house far into the future. And this is the law for humankind, Lord GOD!
7:20 What else could David add, speaking to you? You know your servant, Lord GOD.
7:21 You have done this whole great thing because of your word and because your heart wanted it, and you have let your servant know it.
7:22 This is why you are great, Lord GOD. No one is like you. No God exists except you. Everything we have ever heard with our ears says so.
7:23 Is there any other nation on earth like your people Israel? God went to redeem them as a people for himself, to make a name for himself. He did great and awesome things for them, for your land, in front of your people, whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt, from nations and their gods.
7:24 You made your people Israel firmly your own people for all time, and you, LORD, have become their God.
7:25 So now, LORD God, make the word you spoke about your servant and his house stand firm forever. Do just what you said.
7:26 May your name be great for all time, so that people say, 'The LORD of Armies is God over Israel!' And may your servant David's house stand secure in your presence.
7:27 You, LORD of Armies, God of Israel, opened your servant's ear and said, 'I will build a house for you.' That is why your servant has found it in his heart to pray to you with this prayer.
7:28 Now, Lord GOD, you and no other are God. Your words can be trusted, and you have spoken this good word to your servant.
7:29 Now then, may you be willing to bless your servant's house so that it lasts forever in your presence. You, Lord GOD, have said it. Your blessing will rest on your servant's house, and it will be blessed for all time."
## David's wars
8:1 Some time later David beat the Philistines and humbled them. He took Metheg-ammah out of Philistine hands.
8:2 He beat Moab too. He had them lie down on the ground and measured them with a cord. Two cord-lengths of them he marked for death, and one full cord-length he let live. Moab became David's servants, bringing him tribute.
8:3 David also beat Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he was on his way to take back his control at the Euphrates River.
8:4 David captured from him a thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers. He crippled all the chariot horses by cutting their leg tendons, except that he spared enough of them for a hundred chariots.
8:5 Aram of Damascus came to help Hadadezer, king of Zobah. David cut down twenty-two thousand men of Aram.
8:6 David stationed troops in Aram of Damascus, and Aram became David's servants, bringing him tribute. Everywhere David went, the LORD made him win.
8:7 The gold shields carried by Hadadezer's officers David took away and brought to Jerusalem.
8:8 King David also carried off huge amounts of bronze from Betah and Berothai, towns that belonged to Hadadezer.
8:9 Toi, the king of Hamath, heard that David had beaten Hadadezer's entire army.
8:10 He sent his son Joram to King David to wish him well and to bless him for fighting Hadadezer and beating him. Hadadezer had been Toi's enemy in many wars. In his hands Joram had things made of silver, gold and bronze.
8:11 These King David set apart as holy for the LORD as well, together with the silver and gold he had set apart from every nation he conquered:
8:12 from Edom and Moab, from the Ammonites, the Philistines and Amalek, and from what he had plundered from Hadadezer son of Rehob, the king of Zobah.
8:13 David won fame as well. On his way back, he struck down eighteen thousand men of Edom in the Valley of Salt.
8:14 He stationed troops in Edom, all through Edom, and every Edomite became David's servant. Everywhere David went, the LORD made him win.
## David's officials
8:15 David ruled as king over all Israel. He kept practicing justice and righteousness toward all his people.
8:16 Joab son of Zeruiah commanded the army. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud kept the records.
8:17 The priests were Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar. The royal scribe was Seraiah.
8:18 Benaiah son of Jehoiada was in charge of the Kerethites and Pelethites. And David's sons served as priests.
## Kindness to Mephibosheth
9:1 David said, "Has anyone from Saul's house been left alive? I would like to show him loyal love because of Jonathan."
9:2 Saul's household had had a servant named Ziba, and he was summoned to David. "Are you Ziba?" the king asked. "At your service," he answered.
9:3 The king asked, "Isn't there someone still left from Saul's house? I want to treat him with the loyal love of God." "Jonathan has a son who is still living," Ziba answered the king. "Both his feet are injured."
9:4 "Where is he?" the king asked. "Look, he is staying in Lo-debar, in the house of Machir son of Ammiel," Ziba told him.
9:5 King David sent for him and had him fetched from the house of Ammiel's son Machir in Lo-debar.
9:6 When Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan son of Saul, came in to David, he dropped face down and bowed low. "Mephibosheth," said David. "Your servant is here," he replied.
9:7 "Don't be afraid," David told him. "I mean to treat you with loyal love because of your father Jonathan. I will give back to you all the land of Saul your grandfather. And you will eat your meals at my table from now on."
9:8 He bowed low again. "What am I, your servant," he said, "that you would turn toward a dead dog like me?"
9:9 The king summoned Ziba, Saul's servant. "Everything that was Saul's, and his whole family's, I now give to your master's grandson," he said.
9:10 "You are to work the land for him, you and your sons and your servants. Bring in the harvest, so that there will be food for your master's grandson to eat. But Mephibosheth himself, your master's grandson, will eat at my table every day." Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants.
9:11 "Your servant will do just what my lord the king orders," Ziba said to the king. So Mephibosheth ate at David's table as if he were one of the king's sons.
9:12 Mephibosheth had a little son called Mica. All the people living in Ziba's household were servants of Mephibosheth.
9:13 Mephibosheth made his home in Jerusalem, since he ate at the king's table every day. And both of his feet were lame.
## War with Ammon and Aram
10:1 After this, the Ammonite king died, and his son Hanun took his place as king.
10:2 "I will keep loyal love with Hanun son of Nahash," David said, "just as his father kept loyal love with me." David sent some of his servants to comfort him over his father's death, and they arrived in Ammonite country.
10:3 The Ammonite leaders said to their lord Hanun, "Is David really honoring your father in your eyes by sending you men to comfort you? No, David has sent his servants to you to look the city over, to spy on it and to tear it down."
10:4 Hanun grabbed David's servants. He shaved half the beard off each man and cut their robes off halfway up, as high as their buttocks. Then he sent them off.
10:5 David heard about it and sent men to meet them, since they were badly shamed. "Stay in Jericho," the king said. "When your beards grow back, come home."
10:6 The Ammonites could see that they had made themselves stink to David. So they sent and paid for help: twenty thousand foot soldiers from Aram of Beth-rehob and Aram of Zobah, the king of Maacah with a thousand men, and twelve thousand men of Tob.
10:7 David heard of it and sent out Joab with the whole army, the fighting men.
10:8 The Ammonites marched out and formed their battle lines at the city gate. Aram of Zobah and of Rehob, with the men of Tob and Maacah, stood apart from them out in the open fields.
10:9 Joab saw that he would have to fight on two fronts, ahead of him and behind him. So out of all Israel's picked men he took some, and he set them in battle lines facing Aram.
10:10 He put the rest of the troops under his brother Abishai, and they formed lines facing the Ammonites.
10:11 "If Aram gets the upper hand on me," Joab said, "then you be my rescue. And if the Ammonites get the upper hand on you, I will come and save you.
10:12 Be strong. Let's stand firm and be strong for the sake of our people and our God's cities. The LORD will do whatever he sees is good."
10:13 Joab and his troops closed in to fight Aram, and the Arameans ran from him.
10:14 The Ammonites saw Aram running, and they too ran, away from Abishai, and went inside the city. Joab came back from fighting the Ammonites and went to Jerusalem.
10:15 Aram saw that Israel had beaten them, so they massed their forces together.
10:16 Hadadezer sent word and called out the Arameans from the far side of the River. They came to Helam, and Shobach, commander of Hadadezer's army, led them.
10:17 The news reached David. He called up all Israel, went across the Jordan and came to Helam. Aram drew up its lines facing David and fought him.
10:18 But Aram ran before Israel. David killed seven hundred of Aram's chariot drivers and forty thousand of its horsemen. He also struck Shobach, the commander of their army, and he died on the spot.
10:19 All the kings who were Hadadezer's vassals saw that Israel had beaten them. They made peace with Israel and served them. After that Aram was afraid to come to the Ammonites' rescue again.
