## Joab shakes the grieving king
19:1 Word reached Joab: "Look, the king is weeping. He is mourning over Absalom."
19:2 The rescue of that day turned to mourning among all the troops, because that day they heard it said, "The king is hurting over his son."
19:3 That day the troops slipped quietly into the city, like troops who sneak in ashamed after running away in battle.
19:4 The king had covered his face, and he kept crying out at the top of his voice, "My son Absalom! Absalom, my son! My son!"
19:5 Joab went in to the king at the house. "Today you have made the faces of all your servants burn with shame," he said. "Today they rescued your life, and the lives of your sons and daughters, your wives and your concubines.
19:6 You love the people who hate you, and you hate the people who love you! Today you have announced that officers and servants count for nothing with you. Now I know it: if Absalom were living and every one of us lay dead today, that would be right in your eyes.
19:7 So get up now. Go out and speak to your servants' hearts. By the LORD I swear, unless you go out, no one will stay the night with you. And that will be worse for you than every disaster that has hit you from your youth until today."
19:8 The king rose and sat down in the gate. Word went out to all the troops: "The king is sitting in the gate!" And all the troops came before the king. Israel, though, had run away, each man to his tent.
## The tribes argue over the king
19:9 In every tribe of Israel the people were quarreling. "The king rescued us from our enemies," they said. "He was the one who got us free of the Philistines. And now he has fled the land because of Absalom.
19:10 And Absalom, the man we anointed to be over us, has died in the battle. Why keep quiet now? Why not bring the king back?"
19:11 King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar, the priests: "Speak to the elders of Judah. Ask them, 'Why lag behind everyone in bringing the king back to his house?'" What all Israel was saying had already reached the king where he stayed.
19:12 "'My brothers, that is what you are, my own bone and flesh! So why lag behind in bringing the king back?'
19:13 And tell Amasa, 'Aren't you my own bone and flesh? God do this to me, and more besides, if you do not serve as my army commander from now on, for good, in Joab's place.'"
19:14 He turned the hearts of all the men of Judah as if they were one man. They sent to the king: "Come back, you and all your servants."
19:15 The king started back and reached the Jordan. Judah had come to Gilgal on their way to meet the king and bring him over the Jordan.
## Shimei, Mephibosheth and Barzillai
19:16 Shimei son of Gera hurried too. He was the Benjaminite from Bahurim, and he came down with the men of Judah to meet King David.
19:17 A thousand men of Benjamin were with him. Ziba, the servant of Saul's house, came too, with his fifteen sons and his twenty servants. They pushed through the Jordan ahead of the king.
19:18 They went back and forth across the ford, carrying the king's household over and doing whatever he wanted. Shimei son of Gera fell down in front of the king as the king was crossing the Jordan.
19:19 "My lord, please do not charge me with guilt," he said to the king. "Forget the crooked thing your servant did on the day my lord the king went out of Jerusalem. Do not let the king brood on it.
19:20 I, your servant, know that I have sinned. That is why I am here today. Of the whole house of Joseph, I am the first to come down to meet my lord the king."
19:21 Abishai son of Zeruiah answered, "Shimei cursed the LORD's anointed. Shouldn't he die for that?"
19:22 But David said, "Sons of Zeruiah, what is there between you and me? Today you have turned into my adversary. Is anyone in Israel going to be put to death today? I know it, don't I: today I am king over Israel."
19:23 The king told Shimei, "You will not die," and he gave him his oath on it.
19:24 Mephibosheth son of Saul came down to meet the king too. From the day the king went away until the day he came home in peace, Mephibosheth had not tended his feet or his mustache, and he had not washed his clothes.
19:25 When he reached Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said, "Mephibosheth, why didn't you come along with me?"
19:26 "My lord the king," he answered, "my servant tricked me. Your servant said, 'I'll saddle up the donkey, ride on it, and go with the king,' because your servant is lame.
19:27 Then he told lies about your servant to my lord the king. But my lord the king is like God's angel. Do whatever you think best.
19:28 My father's whole family were nothing but dead men to my lord the king. Even so, you seated your servant with those who eat at your table. What claim do I have left? Why should I keep crying to the king?"
19:29 "Why go on talking?" the king said. "I say this: you and Ziba will split the land between you."
19:30 "He can even take the whole thing," Mephibosheth told the king, "since my lord the king has come back to his house in peace."
19:31 Barzillai of Gilead had come down from Rogelim too. He went over the Jordan with the king to see him off at the river.
19:32 Barzillai was very old, a man of eighty. He was a very great man, and he had fed the king the whole time he stayed at Mahanaim.
19:33 "Come across with me," the king told Barzillai, "and I will take care of you at my side in Jerusalem."
19:34 Barzillai answered the king, "How many days are left in my life? Should I really go up to Jerusalem with the king?
19:35 I am eighty now. Do I know good from bad anymore? Does your servant taste his food or his drink? Do I still hear the singers, the men and the women? Your servant would only weigh my lord the king down. Why do that?
19:36 Your servant will go only a short way over the Jordan with the king. Why should the king pay me back with a gift like this?
19:37 Please let your servant go home. Let me die in my hometown, where my father and mother are buried. But look, here is your servant Kimham. He can go across with my lord the king. Treat him as you think best."
19:38 "Kimham will go across with me," said the king, "and I will treat him as you think best. Whatever you pick out for me to do, I will do it for you."
19:39 All the troops went over the Jordan, and the king went over. The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and Barzillai went back to his own place.
## Judah and Israel quarrel
19:40 From there the king crossed on to Gilgal, with Kimham beside him. All the people of Judah had escorted the king over, and half the people of Israel too.
19:41 And now every man of Israel was coming to the king. "Why did our kinsmen, the men of Judah, steal you away?" they asked the king. "Why did they take the king and his household over the Jordan, and all David's men with him?"
19:42 Every man of Judah answered the men of Israel, "The king is our near kin. Why does this make you burn? Did we get one meal out of the king? Did we carry anything off for ourselves?"
19:43 The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, "Ten shares in the king belong to us. Even in David we have more than you do. Why did you look down on us? Wasn't it our word first, to bring our king back?" But the men of Judah spoke more harshly than the men of Israel.
## Sheba's revolt
20:1 A worthless man was there by chance, named Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite. He blew the ram's horn and said, "We have no share in David, no inheritance in the son of Jesse! Everyone to his tents, Israel!"
20:2 So all the men of Israel went up from following David and followed Sheba son of Bichri. But the men of Judah kept close to their king from the Jordan as far as Jerusalem.
20:3 David came to his house in Jerusalem. The king took the ten concubines he had left to keep the house, and he placed them in a house of confinement and supplied their needs, but he did not go in to them. They stayed shut in until the day they died, widows for life.
20:4 The king said to Amasa, "Call out the men of Judah to me. You have three days. Then present yourself here."
20:5 Amasa went to call out Judah, but he took longer than the time David had set for him.
20:6 David said to Abishai, "Now Sheba son of Bichri will be worse for us than Absalom was. Take the servants of your lord and go after him, or he will find fortified towns for himself and slip out of our sight."
20:7 Joab's men went out after him, with the Kerethites and the Pelethites and all the warriors. They marched out of Jerusalem to chase Sheba son of Bichri.
## Joab kills Amasa
20:8 They were at the great stone in Gibeon when Amasa came to meet them. Joab was dressed in his military tunic, and over it he had a belt with a sword in its sheath, strapped to his waist. As he stepped forward, it fell out.
20:9 Joab said to Amasa, "Are you well, my brother?" And Joab's right hand took hold of Amasa's beard to kiss him.
20:10 Amasa did not watch out for the sword in Joab's hand. Joab stabbed him in the belly with it, and his insides spilled onto the ground. He did not need to strike him again, and Amasa died. Then Joab and his brother Abishai chased after Sheba son of Bichri.
20:11 One of Joab's young men stood over Amasa and called out, "Anyone who likes Joab, anyone who stands with David: after Joab!"
20:12 Amasa lay in the middle of the road, rolling in his blood. The man saw that all the troops stopped there. So he dragged Amasa off the road into the field and threw a cloak over him, since he saw that everyone who came up to him stopped.
20:13 Once he had moved him off the road, every man went on past, following Joab to chase Sheba son of Bichri.
## The wise woman of Abel
20:14 Sheba went through all the tribes of Israel to Abel of Beth-maacah. All the Berites gathered too and followed him in.
20:15 Joab's men came and besieged him in Abel of Beth-maacah. They heaped up a ramp against the town, and it stood level with the outer wall. All the troops with Joab were battering the wall to bring it down.
20:16 Then a wise woman called out from the town, "Listen! Listen! Please tell Joab, 'Come close. I want to talk with you.'"
20:17 He drew near to her. The woman said, "Are you Joab?" "I am," he said. She said to him, "Hear the words of your servant." He said, "I hear."
20:18 She said, "People used to say long ago, 'Be sure to ask at Abel,' and that was how things got finished.
20:19 Here I stand, among the peaceful and loyal of Israel. You are out to kill a town, a mother in Israel. Why devour the LORD's inheritance?"
20:20 Joab answered, "Not me! I would never swallow up or destroy!
20:21 That is not how it is. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, named Sheba son of Bichri, has raised his hand against the king, against David. Hand him over, just him, and I will leave the town." The woman said to Joab, "Watch: his head is going to be thrown out to you over the wall."
20:22 Then the woman went to all the people with her wise plan. They cut off the head of Sheba son of Bichri and threw it out to Joab. He blew the ram's horn, and they scattered from the town, each to his tent. And Joab went back to the king in Jerusalem.
## David's officials
20:23 Joab was over the whole army of Israel. Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Kerethites and the Pelethites.
20:24 Adoram was over the forced labor. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder.
20:25 Sheva was the scribe. Zadok and Abiathar were priests.
20:26 And Ira the Jairite was David's priest as well.
## The Gibeonites and Saul's sons
21:1 In David's days there was a famine for three years running. David sought the LORD's face, and the LORD said, "Saul is the reason, Saul and his house of bloodshed, because he killed the Gibeonites."
21:2 The king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. The Gibeonites were not part of Israel. They were what was left of the Amorites, and the Israelites had sworn an oath to them. But Saul, burning with zeal for Israel and Judah, had tried to strike them down.
21:3 David said to the Gibeonites, "What must I do for you, and with what shall I make atonement? Then you can bless the LORD's inheritance."
21:4 The Gibeonites said to him, "Silver and gold are not what we want from Saul and his family. Nor are we free to kill anyone in Israel." "Name what I should do for you," he said.
21:5 They said to the king, "That man made an end of us. He schemed against us so that we were wiped out, with no foothold left in all Israel's territory.
21:6 Let seven men from among his sons be handed over to us. We will impale them before the LORD at Gibeah of Saul, the LORD's chosen one." The king said, "I will hand them over."
21:7 But the king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, son of Saul, because of the oath of the LORD between them, between David and Jonathan son of Saul.
21:8 The king took the two sons that Rizpah daughter of Aiah had borne to Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth. He also took the five sons of Merab daughter of Saul, whom she had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.
21:9 He handed them over to the Gibeonites, and they impaled them on the hill before the LORD. The seven of them fell together. They were put to death in the first days of harvest, when the barley harvest began.
## Rizpah's watch
21:10 Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and stretched it out on the rock as a place for herself. From the time harvest began until water poured down on them out of the sky, she let no bird of the sky rest on them by day and no wild animal by night.
21:11 David was told what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, Saul's concubine, had done.
21:12 So David went and took the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan from the men of Jabesh-gilead. They had stolen them from the town square of Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them up on the day the Philistines struck Saul down on Gilboa.
21:13 He brought the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan up from there, and they gathered the bones of the men who had been impaled.
21:14 They buried the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan in the land of Benjamin, at Zela, in the tomb of his father Kish. They did everything the king ordered. After that, God accepted the prayers made for the land.
## Giants of Gath
21:15 The Philistines went to war with Israel again. David went down with his servants, and they fought the Philistines, and David was worn out.
21:16 Ishbi-benob was one of those born to the Rapha. His spear weighed three hundred shekels of bronze, and he was armed with new gear. He said he would strike David down.
21:17 But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to David's aid. He struck the Philistine and killed him. Then David's men took an oath before him: "No more going out to battle with us! Do not snuff out Israel's lamp."
21:18 Later the fighting with the Philistines broke out again, at Gob. Then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, another of those born to the Rapha.
21:19 Once more there was fighting with the Philistines at Gob. Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite struck down Goliath the Gittite, whose spear shaft was like a weaver's beam.
21:20 There was war again at Gath. A man of huge size was there. The fingers of his hands and the toes of his feet came six to each, twenty-four of them. He too had been born to the Rapha.
21:21 He jeered at Israel, but Jonathan son of Shimeah, David's brother, struck him down.
21:22 These four had been born to the Rapha in Gath. David and his servants brought them down.
## David's song of rescue
22:1 These are the words of the song David spoke to the LORD on the day the LORD rescued him from the grip of all his enemies and from the grip of Saul.
22:2 He said: / The LORD is my high cliff, my fortress, / the one who gets me free.
22:3 The God of my rock, I take shelter in him, / my shield and the horn that saves me, / my high tower and my safe place, / my rescuer; from violence you rescue me.
22:4 To the LORD, the one worthy of praise, I call, / and I come out safe from my enemies.
22:5 Death's breakers swept around me; / the floods of ruin filled me with terror.
22:6 The ropes of Sheol were wound around me; / death's snares met me head-on.
22:7 When I was hemmed in, I called the LORD; / to my God I called. / He heard my voice in his temple; / my cry for help was in his ears.
22:8 The earth heaved and quaked; / the base of the heavens shuddered. / They heaved, for his anger was burning.
22:9 Up went smoke from his nose, / and a fire that eats from his mouth; / coals flared up from him.
22:10 He bent the sky low and came down, / storm-darkness beneath his feet.
22:11 Riding a cherub, he flew; / he came into view on the wind's wings.
22:12 He wrapped himself in darkness; / around him were shelters / of massed water and heavy cloud.
22:13 From the glare in front of him / fiery coals burst into flame.
22:14 From the sky the LORD thundered; / the Most High gave out his voice.
22:15 He let arrows fly and scattered them; / lightning, and he threw them into panic.
22:16 The sea's channels showed; / the world's footings lay uncovered / when the LORD rebuked them, / at the gust of breath from his nose.
22:17 From the heights he stretched out his hand and seized me; / he pulled me from great waters.
22:18 He snatched me away from my strong enemy, / from the people who hated me, / since they were stronger than I was.
22:19 They met me head-on in my day of disaster, / but the LORD became my staff to lean on.
22:20 He led me out where there was room; / he pulled me free, since he took pleasure in me.
22:21 The LORD treated me as my righteousness deserved; / he paid me back for my clean hands.
22:22 I have stayed on the LORD's roads; / I did not do wrong and leave my God.
22:23 All his rulings stand before me; / from his rules I have not swerved.
22:24 With him I have been wholehearted, / and I have guarded myself against my own guilt.
22:25 So the LORD repaid me as my righteousness deserved, / as I was clean before his eyes.
22:26 With the loyal you show yourself loyal; / with the wholehearted hero you show yourself wholehearted.
22:27 With one who keeps himself pure you are pure, / but with the twisted you twist and turn.
22:28 A people pushed down you rescue, / but you keep watch on the arrogant and lower them.
22:29 My lamp is you, LORD; / the LORD makes my darkness bright.
22:30 By your help I rush at a war party; / by my God's help I vault a wall.
22:31 God's way is flawless. / The LORD's word is refined like metal. / He is a shield for everyone who takes shelter in him.
22:32 Who is God, if not the LORD? / Who is a rock, if not our God?
22:33 God is my fortress of strength, / and he makes my way free and whole.
22:34 He makes my feet like a doe's / and stands me firm on my high places.
22:35 He teaches my hands to fight; / my arms can draw a bronze bow.
22:36 You handed me your rescue as a shield; / when you answer me, you make me great.
22:37 You widen the ground under my stride; / my ankles do not wobble.
22:38 I ran after my enemies and destroyed them; / there was no turning back till I made an end of them.
22:39 I made an end of them and smashed them; / they did not get up again; / under my feet they fell.
22:40 You wrapped me in strength for battle; / you bent my attackers low beneath me.
22:41 You gave me my enemies' necks; / the ones who hated me I wiped out.
22:42 They looked around, and there was no rescuer; / they looked to the LORD, and he gave them no answer.
22:43 I pounded them to powder like the ground's dust; / like the mud of the lanes I crushed them and flattened them.
22:44 You rescued me from my own people's feuds; / you guarded me to be chief over the nations. / Peoples I never knew are my servants.
22:45 Sons of foreign lands fawn on me; / the moment their ears hear, they do what I say.
22:46 Sons of foreign lands wilt; / they come limping out of their fortresses.
22:47 The LORD is alive! Blessed is my rock! / Let God be raised high, the rock that saves me!
22:48 This is the God who grants me revenge, / who lays nations low beneath me,
22:49 who leads me out from among my enemies. / Over my attackers you raise me; / from the man of violence you snatch me.
22:50 That is why I will give you thanks among the nations, LORD; / to your name I will make music.
22:51 He makes great the rescues of his king; / he shows loyal love to his anointed, / to David and his line for all time.
## David's last words
23:1 These are David's last words: / The declaration of David son of Jesse, / the declaration of the man set up on high, / the God of Jacob's anointed one, / the darling of Israel's songs:
23:2 The LORD's Spirit has spoken by me; / his message is on my tongue.
23:3 Israel's God has said it; / to me Israel's Rock has spoken: / Someone who rules humankind in righteousness, / who rules in reverence for God,
23:4 he is like morning light as the sun rises, / a morning with no clouds; / from the shine, from the rain, / green grass comes up from the ground.
23:5 Is my house not like this with God? / For he has set up a covenant with me that lasts forever, / laid out in every detail and guarded. / All my rescue and all my desire, / will he not make them grow?
23:6 But the worthless are thorns, all of them, chased off; / no one takes hold of them with a hand.
23:7 A man who would touch them / must be armed with iron and a spear's wooden shaft. / Then they are burned, burned with fire on the spot.
## David's warriors
23:8 These are the names of David's warriors. Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite was chief of the Three. He was Adino the Eznite; eight hundred fell at his hand at one time.
23:9 Next in rank was Eleazar son of Dodo, son of Ahohi, one of the Three warriors. He was with David when they jeered at the Philistines who had massed there for battle. Israel's men pulled back,
23:10 but he got up and cut down Philistines until his hand was exhausted and clung to the sword. That day the LORD won a great rescue. The troops followed him back only to strip the bodies.
23:11 After him came Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. The Philistines had massed at Lehi. A field there was planted thick with lentils, and the troops fled before the Philistines.
23:12 But he planted himself in the middle of that field and held it, and he cut the Philistines down. The LORD won a great rescue.
23:13 At harvest, three of the Thirty headmen went down to David at the cave of Adullam. A company of Philistines had made camp in the Valley of Rephaim.
23:14 At that point David was in the fortress, while a Philistine post held Bethlehem.
23:15 David felt a longing. "Who will bring me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem, the one at the gate?" he said.
23:16 The three warriors forced their way through the Philistine camp. They drew water from Bethlehem's well at the gate, lifted it, and brought it to David. He was not willing to drink it. Instead he poured it out to the LORD.
23:17 "LORD, I could never do this!" he said. "The blood of the men who went with their lives at stake?" So he would not drink it. Those are the deeds the three warriors did.
23:18 Abishai, Joab's brother, the son of Zeruiah, was head of the Thirty. He brandished his spear over three hundred slain, and he was famous alongside the Three.
23:19 Of the Thirty he was the most honored, was he not? He became their captain. But he never came up to the Three.
23:20 Benaiah son of Jehoiada was a fighter's son, a man of great deeds from Kabzeel. He struck down the two lion-men of Moab. He also went down and killed the lion inside the pit on the day of the snow.
23:21 He struck down an Egyptian too, a striking-looking man. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand, but Benaiah went down to him with a stick. He wrenched the spear out of the Egyptian's grip and killed him with it, his own spear.
23:22 Those are the things Benaiah son of Jehoiada did. He had a name among the Three warriors.
23:23 Among the Thirty he had more honor than the rest, but he never came up to the Three. David made him head of his guard.
23:24 Among the Thirty were Asahel, Joab's brother; Elhanan son of Dodo, from Bethlehem;
23:25 Shammah the Harodite; Elika the Harodite;
23:26 Helez the Paltite; Ira son of Ikkesh, from Tekoa;
23:27 Abiezer from Anathoth; Mebunnai the Hushathite;
23:28 Zalmon the Ahohite; Maharai from Netophah;
23:29 Heleb son of Baanah, from Netophah; Ittai son of Ribai, from Gibeah of the Benjaminites;
23:30 Benaiah from Pirathon; Hiddai from the ravines of Gaash;
23:31 Abi-albon the Arbathite; Azmaveth the Barhumite;
23:32 Eliahba the Shaalbonite; the sons of Jashen; Jonathan
23:33 son of Shammah the Hararite; Ahiam son of Sharar the Hararite;
23:34 Eliphelet son of Ahasbai, son of the Maacathite; Eliam son of Ahithophel the Gilonite;
23:35 Hezro the Carmelite; Paarai the Arbite;

## David counts the people
24:1 The LORD's anger burned against Israel once more. He stirred David up against them: "Go and count Israel and Judah."
24:2 The king told Joab, commander of the army, who was with him, "Go up and down through every tribe of Israel, from Dan as far as Beersheba. Enroll the people. I want to know the number of the people."
24:3 Joab answered the king, "The LORD your God make the people a hundred times more than they are, and may my lord the king see it with his own eyes! But this thing, why does my lord the king take pleasure in it?"
24:4 But what the king said won out over Joab and the army's commanders. Joab and the commanders left the king and went out to enroll the people, Israel.
24:5 They crossed the Jordan and set up camp in Aroer, on the south side of the town that sits in the middle of the ravine of Gad, and went on to Jazer.
24:6 They went to Gilead and to the land of Tahtim-hodshi. They came to Dan-jaan and around toward Sidon.
24:7 They came to the fortress of Tyre and all the towns of the Hivites and the Canaanites. Then they went out to the Negev of Judah, to Beersheba.
24:8 They went all over the land and arrived back in Jerusalem nine months and twenty days later.
24:9 Joab handed the king the count of the people enrolled. Israel had eight hundred thousand fighting men who could draw a sword. Judah had five hundred thousand men.
## The plague and the threshing floor
24:10 After he had counted the people, David's heart struck him. "What I have done is a great sin," David said to the LORD. "Now, LORD, please take away your servant's guilt. I have been a great fool."
24:11 David got up in the morning. Now the LORD's word had come to Gad the prophet, David's visionary:
24:12 "Go and speak to David. 'The LORD says this: I am laying three things before you. Pick one, and I will do it to you.'"
24:13 Gad came to David and told him. "Should seven years of hunger come on your land?" he asked. "Or should you run for three months from your foes, with them chasing you? Or should there be three days of pestilence in your land? Now consider it and see what word I should carry back to the one who sent me."
24:14 "I am in a terrible bind," David told Gad. "Please, let us fall into the LORD's hand, for his compassion runs deep. Into a human hand, let me not fall."
24:15 The LORD put pestilence on Israel from morning until the set time. From Dan to Beersheba, seventy thousand of the people died.
24:16 The angel reached out his hand over Jerusalem to ruin it. But the LORD regretted the harm, and he said to the angel who was destroying among the people, "Enough! Now let your hand drop." The LORD's angel was close to the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.
24:17 When David saw the angel striking the people down, he spoke to the LORD. "Look, it is I who sinned, I who did wrong," he said. "And these sheep, what did they do? Please turn your hand against me and against my father's family."
24:18 Gad came to David that same day. "Go up," he told him. "Put up an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite."
24:19 David went up, following Gad's word, as the LORD had commanded.
24:20 Araunah looked down and saw the king and his servants crossing over to him. He came out and bowed low before the king, his face to the earth.
24:21 "What brings my lord the king to his servant?" Araunah asked. "I have come to buy your threshing floor," David said, "and build the LORD an altar on it, so the plague on the people will be held back."
24:22 "My lord the king may take it," Araunah told David, "and sacrifice whatever looks good to him. See, there are the oxen for a burnt offering. The threshing sledges and the oxen's gear can be the firewood.
24:23 Everything, Your Majesty, Araunah hands over to the king." And Araunah said to the king, "May the LORD your God be pleased with you."
24:24 But the king said to Araunah, "No. I will surely buy it from you for a price. I will not offer the LORD my God burnt offerings I got for free." David paid fifty silver shekels for the threshing floor and the oxen.
