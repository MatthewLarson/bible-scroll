## David and Bathsheba
11:1 When the year came around again, at the season when kings march out, David sent Joab out, and his servants with him, and all Israel. They laid waste the Ammonites and put Rabbah under siege. But David was staying in Jerusalem.
11:2 As evening came on, David rose from his bed and was walking about on the roof of the king's house. From the roof he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very lovely to look at.
11:3 David sent to find out about the woman, and the answer came back: "Isn't that Bathsheba, Eliam's daughter, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"
11:4 Then David sent messengers and took her. When she came to him, he lay with her. She had been purifying herself from her uncleanness. Then she returned home.
11:5 The woman conceived. She sent a message telling David, "I'm pregnant."
## Uriah will not go home
11:6 David sent word to Joab: "Send me Uriah the Hittite." So Joab sent Uriah to David.
11:7 When Uriah came to him, David asked about Joab's well-being, the army's well-being and the war's well-being.
11:8 Then David said to Uriah, "Go on down home and wash your feet." As Uriah went out of the king's house, a gift from the king's table was carried out after him.
11:9 But Uriah slept at the entrance of the king's house among all his master's servants, and never went down to his own house.
11:10 David was told, "Uriah did not go down to his house." So David asked Uriah, "You have come off a journey, haven't you? Why not go down home?"
11:11 Uriah answered David, "The ark, Israel and Judah are living in shelters. My lord Joab and my lord's servants are camped on the bare field. And I should go home to eat and drink and sleep with my wife? I swear by your life, by your very self, I will not do it!"
11:12 "Then stay here today as well," David told Uriah, "and tomorrow I will let you go." So Uriah stayed on in Jerusalem, that day and the day after.
11:13 David called him in, and Uriah ate and drank in his presence, and David made him drunk. But at evening Uriah went out to sleep on his mat among his master's servants. To his house he did not go down.
## A letter carried by the man it kills
11:14 Next morning David wrote Joab a letter and sent it in Uriah's own hand.
11:15 This is what he wrote in it: "Station Uriah facing the hardest part of the battle. Then fall back and leave him, so he will be hit and die."
11:16 So while Joab kept watch on the city, he put Uriah at the place where he knew the strongest fighters stood.
11:17 The men of the city came out and fought with Joab. Some of the army fell, some of David's servants, and Uriah the Hittite died as well.
11:18 Joab sent a report to David of everything that had happened in the fighting.
11:19 He gave the messenger these orders: "When you finish reporting to the king everything about the battle,
11:20 the king may get angry and ask you, 'Why did you go so close to the city to fight? Didn't you know they would shoot down from the wall?
11:21 Who killed Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? Wasn't it a woman who dropped an upper millstone on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why go near the wall?' If he asks that, tell him, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too.'"
11:22 So the messenger set out. He came and told David everything Joab had sent him to say.
11:23 The messenger told David, "The men overpowered us and came out at us in the field. But we pushed them back as far as the entrance of the gate.
11:24 Then the bowmen on the wall shot at your servants, and some of the king's servants died. Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too."
11:25 David said to the messenger, "Say this to Joab: 'Do not let this thing look evil in your eyes. The sword eats one man now and another man then. Make your fight against the city stronger and tear it down.' Give him courage."
11:26 Uriah's wife heard that Uriah, her husband, had died, and she wailed for her husband.
11:27 When the time of mourning passed, David sent for her and brought her into his house. She became his wife and gave birth to a son for him. But what David had done looked evil in the LORD's eyes.
## Nathan's story
12:1 So the LORD sent Nathan to David. Nathan came in and told him: "Two men lived in one town. One was rich, the other poor.
12:2 The rich man owned a great many sheep and cattle.
12:3 The poor man had nothing at all but a single little ewe lamb he had bought. He kept it alive, and it grew up at his side together with his children. It ate from his own scraps of bread, drank from his own cup and lay in his lap. To him it was like a daughter.
12:4 One day a visitor dropped in on the rich man. He could not bear to take any of his own sheep or cattle to cook for the guest who had come to him. So he took the poor man's ewe lamb and cooked it for the man who had come to him."
12:5 David flared up in fury at the man. "By the life of the LORD," he told Nathan, "the man who did this deserves death!
12:6 For the lamb he must repay four lambs, since he did such a deed and showed no pity."
## "You are the man"
12:7 Nathan said to David, "You are the man! The LORD, the God of Israel, says this: 'I anointed you king over Israel. I rescued you from Saul's hand.
12:8 I gave you your master's house and put your master's wives into your lap. I gave you the house of Israel and Judah. And if that had been too little, I would have added this much and that much more.
12:9 Why have you despised the word of the LORD by doing what is evil in my eyes? Uriah the Hittite you struck down with the sword. His wife you took to be your wife. Him you killed with the sword of the Ammonites.
12:10 So the sword will not go away from your house, ever. That is the price for despising me and taking Uriah the Hittite's wife to be your own wife.'
12:11 The LORD says this: 'Look, I am raising up disaster against you out of your own house. I will take your wives while you watch and give them to someone close to you. He will sleep with your wives with this very sun watching.
12:12 You did it in secret. I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.'"
12:13 David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." Nathan said to David, "The LORD, for his part, has put away your sin. You will not die.
12:14 But because in this matter you have utterly scorned the word of the LORD, the son born to you will surely die."
## The child dies
12:15 Then Nathan went back to his house. And the LORD struck the boy whom Uriah's wife had borne to David, and he grew desperately ill.
12:16 David sought God on the boy's behalf. He went without food, and he went in and lay all night on the ground in sackcloth.
12:17 The senior men of his household stood by him, trying to lift him up off the ground. He refused, and would not share a meal with them.
12:18 The boy died on the seventh day. David's servants were scared to tell him. They said, "Think of it: when the boy was alive we talked to him, and he paid no attention to us. How can we tell him, 'The boy is dead'? He might do something terrible."
12:19 But David noticed his servants whispering among themselves and understood that the boy had died. "Has the boy died?" he asked them. "He has died," they answered.
12:20 David rose from the ground, bathed, rubbed himself with oil and changed into fresh clothes. He went to the LORD's house and bowed in worship. After that he went home and asked for a meal. They put food in front of him, and he ate.
12:21 His servants said, "What does this mean? You fasted and wept for the boy while he was alive. Now that he is dead, you get up and eat!"
12:22 He answered, "When the boy was still living, I fasted and cried, because I thought, 'Who knows? The LORD might show me favor, and the boy would live.'
12:23 Now he has died. What is the point of my fasting? Am I able to bring him back? I am the one who will go to him. He will not come back to me."
## Solomon is born
12:24 David comforted Bathsheba, his wife. He went in to her and lay with her, and she gave birth to a son. She named him Solomon. The LORD loved the boy,
12:25 and he sent word by Nathan the prophet, and he named him Jedidiah, because of the LORD.
## Rabbah falls
12:26 Joab fought against Rabbah of the Ammonites and took the royal city.
12:27 He sent messengers to David with this word: "I have fought Rabbah. I have even taken the water city.
12:28 So gather the rest of the army, set up camp against the city and take it. If I take the city myself, it will be called by my name."
12:29 David gathered all the army and marched to Rabbah. He fought it and took it.
12:30 He took their king's crown off his head. Its weight was a talent of gold, and it held a precious stone. It went onto David's head. He also carried away a very large amount of loot from the city.
12:31 He brought out the people who lived there and set them at the saws, the iron picks and the iron axes, and put them to work at the brick molds. That is how he treated all the Ammonite towns. Then David and the whole army went back to Jerusalem.
## Amnon and Tamar
13:1 Some time after this, David's son Absalom had a sister, a beautiful girl named Tamar. David's son Amnon loved her.
13:2 Amnon was so tormented over Tamar his sister that he was making himself sick. Because she was a virgin, doing anything to her looked impossible in Amnon's eyes.
13:3 Amnon had a friend called Jonadab, a son of David's brother Shimeah. Jonadab was an extremely wise man.
13:4 "Prince," he asked him, "why do you look so thin, morning after morning? Won't you tell me?" Amnon answered, "Tamar, the sister of my brother Absalom: I love her."
13:5 Jonadab told him, "Take to your bed and act sick. Your father will come to see you. Then tell him, 'Please, let Tamar my sister come and feed me. Have her make the meal where I can see it, so I can watch her and eat from her own hand.'"
13:6 So Amnon lay down and acted sick, and the king came to see him. Amnon said to the king, "Please let Tamar my sister come and shape two heart-cakes while I watch, so I can eat from her hand."
13:7 David sent a message to Tamar at home: "Please go to the house of Amnon your brother and fix him the meal."
13:8 Tamar went to the house of Amnon her brother. He was lying in bed. She took the dough and kneaded it and shaped the heart-cakes as he watched, then cooked them.
13:9 She picked up the pan and tipped them out in front of him. But he would not eat. "Get everyone out of here, away from me!" Amnon ordered. Every one of them left him.
13:10 Then Amnon told Tamar, "Bring the meal to the inner room and let me eat from your hand." So Tamar picked up the heart-cakes she had made and carried them in to Amnon her brother, into the inner room.
13:11 As she held them out for him to eat, he seized her. "Come, lie with me, my sister," he said.
13:12 "No, my brother!" she said to him. "Do not dishonor me. Such a thing is not done in Israel. Do not do this disgraceful thing.
13:13 And me, where would I carry my shame? And you, you would be one of the disgraceful men in Israel. Now, please, just speak to the king. He will not hold me back from you."
13:14 He refused to listen to her voice. He overpowered her, dishonored her and lay with her.
13:15 And now Amnon hated her, a huge hatred. The hate he felt for her was bigger than the love he had felt for her. "Up! Out!" Amnon told her.
13:16 "No!" she told him. "This evil, throwing me out, is greater than the other one you have done to me." But he would not listen to her.
13:17 He called the young man who waited on him and said, "Throw this one out of here, away from me, and lock the door behind her."
13:18 Now she had on a robe that reached to her hands and feet, because the king's daughters who were virgins dressed in such robes. His attendant put her out and locked the door behind her.
13:19 Tamar took ashes and put them on her head. The robe reaching to her hands and feet, the one she had on, she tore. She laid her hand on her head and walked off, screaming as she went.
13:20 Absalom her brother asked her, "Was it Amnon your brother who was with you? For now, my sister, say nothing. He is your brother. Do not let your heart dwell on this matter." And Tamar stayed on in the house of her brother Absalom, a woman left desolate.
13:21 King David heard about all these things, and it made him burn with rage. But he would not bring pain to the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him: Amnon was his firstborn.
13:22 Absalom had nothing to say to Amnon, nothing bad and nothing good. He hated Amnon over the matter of his dishonoring Tamar, his sister.
## Absalom's revenge
13:23 After two whole years, Absalom's sheep were being sheared at Baal-hazor, which is near Ephraim, and he invited all the king's sons.
13:24 Absalom went to the king. "Look," he said, "your servant's sheep are being sheared. Would the king please come, and his servants, along with your servant?"
13:25 But the king told Absalom, "No, my son, let's not all of us go. We would weigh you down." Absalom pushed him, but he was not willing to go. He blessed him instead.
13:26 "Then if not you," Absalom said, "please let Amnon my brother go with us." The king asked him, "Why should he go with you?"
13:27 Absalom kept pushing him until he sent Amnon and all the king's sons with him. And Absalom prepared a banquet like a king's banquet.
13:28 Absalom gave his servants orders: "Watch, please. When Amnon's heart is feeling good with the wine and I tell you, 'Strike Amnon down,' put him to death. Have no fear. Am I not the one giving you the order? Be strong and act like brave men."
13:29 Absalom's servants did to Amnon exactly what Absalom had commanded. At that, every one of the king's sons jumped up. Each got on his mule, and they ran.
13:30 They were still on the road when the news reached David: "Absalom has killed all the king's sons. Not a single one is left."
13:31 The king rose, ripped his clothes and lay down flat on the ground. All his servants stood around him, their clothes torn.
13:32 Then Jonadab son of David's brother Shimeah spoke up. "My lord must not think they have killed all the young men, the king's sons. Amnon alone is dead. Absalom has had this planned, by his own word, since the day Amnon dishonored Tamar his sister.
13:33 So my lord the king should not let this word sink into his heart, that all the king's sons have died. Only Amnon has died."
13:34 Absalom ran away. The young man on lookout raised his eyes and looked: a big crowd was coming along the road behind him, down the hillside. The lookout went and told the king, "I saw men coming down from the direction of Horonaim, along the mountainside."
13:35 Jonadab told the king, "There, the king's sons are here! It has turned out just as your servant said."
13:36 He had just finished speaking when the king's sons came in. They cried out loud and wept. The king and all his servants wept too, long and hard.
13:37 Absalom had fled to Talmai son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. And David kept mourning for his son, day in and day out.
13:38 So Absalom, having fled to Geshur, was there for three years.
13:39 The king's spirit pined to go out to Absalom. About Amnon he had been comforted, now that Amnon was dead.
## The wise woman of Tekoa
14:1 Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king's heart was turned toward Absalom.
14:2 So Joab sent to Tekoa and had a wise woman brought from there. "Please play a mourner," he told her. "Dress in mourning clothes and don't put oil on your skin. Act like a woman who has spent many days grieving over the dead.
14:3 Then go in to the king and say this to him." And Joab put the words in her mouth.
14:4 So the woman from Tekoa came to the king. She dropped face down on the ground and bowed low. "Save me, O king!" she said.
14:5 "What is troubling you?" the king asked her. "Oh, I am a widow," she said. "My husband died.
14:6 Your servant had two sons. They got into a fight out in the field with no one there to pull them apart, and one hit the other and killed him.
14:7 And now the whole family has turned against your servant. 'Give up the man who struck down his brother,' they say. 'We will put him to death to pay for the life of the brother he killed, and wipe out the heir as well.' So they would put out the one ember I have left, and leave my husband no name and no one remaining on the face of the land."
14:8 "Go home," the king told the woman. "I will give an order on your behalf."
14:9 The woman from Tekoa answered the king, "My lord the king, let any guilt fall on me and on my father's family. The king and his throne are free of blame."
14:10 "If anyone speaks against you," the king said, "bring him to me. He will never lay a hand on you again."
14:11 Then she said, "Please, let the king call on the LORD your God, so the blood avenger does no more destroying, and they do not wipe out my son." He said, "By the life of the LORD, not a single hair from your son's head will drop to the ground."
14:12 "Please let your servant say one more word to my lord the king," the woman said. "Go on," he said.
14:13 "Then why," the woman said, "have you planned something like this against God's people? By saying what he just said, the king is like a guilty man, because the king does not bring home the one he banished.
14:14 We will surely die. We are like water poured out on the ground, which no one can gather up. God does not take a life away; he works out plans so the banished one is not cast away from him.
14:15 I have come now to say this to my lord the king because the people frightened me. So your servant said to herself, 'I will speak to the king. Maybe the king will act on his servant's word.
14:16 Surely the king will listen and save his servant from the grip of the man who wants to cut off me and my son together from God's inheritance.'
14:17 Your servant said, 'Let the word of my lord the king bring me rest,' because my lord the king is like God's angel at hearing out good and evil. May the LORD your God be with you!"
14:18 The king answered the woman, "Please do not keep back anything I ask you." "Let my lord the king speak," said the woman.
14:19 The king asked, "Is Joab's hand in all this with you?" The woman answered, "By your life, my lord the king, nobody can veer right or left from anything my lord the king says. Yes, your servant Joab gave me orders. He put all these words in your servant's mouth.
14:20 He did this, your servant Joab, to put a different face on the matter. My lord is wise, like God's angel in wisdom, knowing everything that happens in the land."
## Absalom comes home
14:21 The king said to Joab, "Look, I am doing this thing. Go and bring the young man Absalom back."
14:22 Joab dropped face down on the ground, bowed low and blessed the king. "Today your servant knows that I stand in your favor, my lord the king," Joab said, "since the king has carried out his servant's word."
14:23 So Joab set off for Geshur and brought Absalom back to Jerusalem.
14:24 But the king said, "He is to turn aside to his own house. My face he will not see." So Absalom turned aside to his house, and the king's face he did not see.
14:25 Now there was not a man in all Israel as handsome as Absalom, or as greatly praised. From his heels to his scalp not a flaw could be found in him.
14:26 Whenever he shaved his head (he shaved it at the end of each year, because it grew heavy on him), he would weigh his hair: two hundred shekels by the royal standard.
14:27 Absalom had three sons born to him, and one daughter. Her name was Tamar, and she was a woman lovely to look at.
14:28 Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two whole years, and the king's face he did not see.
14:29 Absalom sent for Joab, meaning to send him to the king, but Joab would not come to him. He sent again, a second time, and still Joab would not come.
14:30 So he told his servants, "See, Joab's piece of land is right beside mine, and he has barley growing there. Go set fire to it." And Absalom's servants put the field to the torch. Then Joab's servants came to him with their clothes torn and said, "Absalom's servants have set the field on fire!"
14:31 Joab got up, came to Absalom's house and demanded, "Why did your servants burn my field?"
14:32 Absalom answered Joab, "Look, I sent you word: 'Come here. I want to send you to the king to ask, "What did I come back from Geshur for? I would be better off still there."' So now let me see the king's face. If there is guilt in me, let him put me to death."
14:33 Joab went in to the king and told him. The king called for Absalom. He came to the king and bowed low before him, face to the ground. And the king gave Absalom a kiss.
## Absalom steals hearts
15:1 Some time later Absalom acquired a chariot and horses for himself, and fifty men who ran in front of him.
15:2 He made a habit of rising early and taking his stand by the road leading to the gate. Anyone with a lawsuit to bring before the king for judgment would hear Absalom call out to him, "Which town are you from?" The man would say, "Your servant comes from one of Israel's tribes."
15:3 Then Absalom would tell him, "See here: your case is good and straight. But the king has no one to listen to you."
15:4 And Absalom would go on, "Oh, if someone would make me judge in the land! Then anyone with a lawsuit or a claim could come to me, and I would give him justice."
15:5 When a man came up to bow down to him, he would stretch out his hand, take hold of him and kiss him.
15:6 Absalom dealt this way with every Israelite who came to the king for a ruling. And so Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.
## The revolt at Hebron
15:7 When four years had passed, Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go and pay my vow, the one I vowed to the LORD, in Hebron.
15:8 When your servant was living at Geshur in Aram, I made a vow: 'If the LORD does bring me back to Jerusalem, I will serve the LORD in Hebron.'"
15:9 "Go in peace," the king told him. So Absalom got up and went off to Hebron.
15:10 But Absalom sent spies through all the tribes of Israel with this message: "When you hear the ram's horn blow, shout, 'Absalom is king in Hebron!'"
15:11 Two hundred men from Jerusalem had gone with Absalom. They were invited guests, and they went along innocently, knowing nothing of the plan.
15:12 While he was offering the sacrifices, Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's adviser, to come from his town, Giloh. The plot gained strength, and more and more people joined Absalom.
## David leaves Jerusalem
15:13 A messenger came to David: "The heart of the men of Israel has gone over to Absalom."
15:14 David told all his servants with him in Jerusalem, "Up! Let's run, or there will be no escape for us from Absalom. Leave fast, or he will move fast, catch us, bring ruin down on us and strike the city with the edge of the sword."
15:15 The king's servants answered the king, "Whatever my lord the king decides, here we are, your servants."
15:16 The king set out, his whole household following on foot behind him. He left ten women, concubines, to look after the house.
15:17 The king went out with all the people on foot behind him, and they halted at the Far House.
15:18 All his servants were marching past beside him. All the Kerethites and all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites, six hundred men who had followed him from Gath, were marching on ahead of the king.
15:19 The king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why are you coming with us too? Turn back and stay with the king. You are a foreigner, and an exile from your own place besides.
15:20 You came just yesterday. Should I make you a wanderer today, going with us, when I myself go wherever I go? Turn back, and take your brothers with you. Loyal love and faithfulness be with you."
15:21 Ittai answered the king, "By the life of the LORD and by the life of my lord the king: in whatever place my lord the king is, for death or for life, your servant will be right there."
15:22 "Come on, then, cross over," David told Ittai. And Ittai the Gittite went across with all his men and all the children with him.
15:23 The whole countryside was weeping out loud as all the people crossed over. The king was crossing the Kidron Valley, and all the people crossed toward the road to the wilderness.
## The ark goes back
15:24 Zadok was there as well, and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of God's covenant. They set the ark of God down, and Abiathar went up and stayed until the last of the people had come out of the city.
15:25 But the king told Zadok, "Take God's ark back into the city. If the LORD looks on me with favor, he will bring me back and show me the ark and the place where it lives.
15:26 But if this is what he says: 'I do not want you,' then here I am. Let him do with me whatever looks good to him."
15:27 The king said to Zadok the priest, "Look, go back to the city in peace, and your two sons with you: Ahimaaz your son and Jonathan son of Abiathar.
15:28 I am going to linger at the river crossings in the wilderness until a message comes from you to tell me what is happening."
15:29 Zadok and Abiathar returned God's ark to Jerusalem, and there they remained.
## Up the Mount of Olives
15:30 David was climbing the slope of the Olives, crying as he climbed. His head was covered, and he walked barefoot. All the people with him covered their heads too, and kept climbing and crying as they went.
15:31 Someone told David, "Ahithophel is one of the plotters with Absalom." And David said, "LORD, please make Ahithophel's advice foolish."
15:32 David was reaching the top, where people bowed down to God, and look: there to meet him was Hushai the Arkite, his tunic ripped and dirt on his head.
15:33 "If you cross over with me," David told him, "you will be a load on me.
15:34 But you can go back to the city and tell Absalom, 'I will be your servant, O king. I served your father before; now I am yours.' Then you can wreck Ahithophel's advice for me.
15:35 Zadok and Abiathar the priests will be there with you, won't they? Pass on to Zadok and Abiathar the priests every word you hear from the king's house.
15:36 They have their two sons there with them, Ahimaaz son of Zadok and Jonathan son of Abiathar. Use them to send me every word you hear."
15:37 Hushai, David's friend, came into the city just as Absalom was arriving in Jerusalem.
## Ziba's gift
16:1 David had gone on a little past the summit when Ziba, Mephibosheth's servant, came to meet him. He had a pair of donkeys, saddled, and on them were two hundred loaves, a hundred raisin clusters, a hundred bunches of summer fruit and a skin full of wine.
16:2 "What do you mean by these?" the king asked Ziba. "The donkeys are for the king's family to ride," Ziba said. "The bread and the summer fruit are food for the young men, and the wine is drink for anyone worn out in the wilderness."
16:3 The king asked, "And where is your master's son?" "Why, he is staying on in Jerusalem," Ziba told the king. "He said, 'Today the house of Israel will hand my father's kingdom back to me.'"
16:4 The king told Ziba, "All right: everything that was Mephibosheth's is yours." Ziba said, "I bow low before you. Let me always have your favor, my lord the king."
## Shimei curses David
16:5 As King David reached Bahurim, out came a man from the same clan as Saul's family, named Shimei son of Gera. He came out cursing, and kept on cursing.
16:6 He pelted David and all of King David's servants with stones, even with all the troops and all the mighty men on David's right and left.
16:7 This is what Shimei shouted as he cursed: "Out with you, out, you man stained with blood, you worthless man!
16:8 The LORD has brought back on you all the blood of Saul's family, whose throne you took as king. Now the LORD has handed the kingship to your son Absalom. Look at you, caught in your own ruin, because you are a man stained with blood!"
16:9 Abishai son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why let this dead dog curse my lord the king? Please, let me cross over and cut his head off."
16:10 But the king said, "What do you and I have in common, you sons of Zeruiah? He curses this way because the LORD told him, 'Curse David.' Who then can ask, 'Why do you do this?'"
16:11 David said to Abishai and all his servants, "See, my son, who came from my own body, is out to take my life. How much more, then, this Benjaminite! Let him be. Let him curse, for the LORD told him to.
16:12 Maybe the LORD will look at my trouble and pay me back good in place of his curse today."
16:13 David and his men kept on along the road. Shimei moved along the slope of the hill beside him, cursing as he walked, pelting him with stones and throwing dirt.
16:14 The king and all the people with him came in worn out, and there he caught his breath.
## Hushai and Ahithophel
16:15 Absalom and all the people, every man of Israel, came into Jerusalem, and Ahithophel with him.
16:16 Then Hushai the Arkite, David's friend, came to Absalom. "Long live the king!" Hushai said to Absalom. "Long live the king!"
16:17 Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your loyal love for your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"
16:18 "No," Hushai answered Absalom. "The one the LORD has chosen, and this people, and all the men of Israel: his I will be, and with him I will stay.
16:19 And second, whom should I serve? Should it not be his son? The way I served before your father, so I will serve before you."
16:20 Absalom said to Ahithophel, "All of you, give your advice. What shall we do?"
16:21 Ahithophel told Absalom, "Go in to your father's concubines, the ones he left to look after the house. All Israel will hear that you have made yourself stink to your father, and everyone on your side will grow bolder."
16:22 So they set up the tent for Absalom on the roof, and Absalom went in to his father's concubines with all Israel watching.
16:23 In those days, the advice Ahithophel gave was as if someone had asked for a word from God. That is how all Ahithophel's advice was, both to David and to Absalom.
## Two kinds of advice
17:1 Ahithophel also told Absalom, "Let me choose twelve thousand men. I will set out and chase after David this very night.
17:2 I will catch him tired and limp-handed and terrify him, and all the people with him will run. The king is the only one I will strike down.
17:3 I will bring all the people back to you, the way a bride comes back to her husband. You are after the life of only one man, and all the people will be at peace."
17:4 The proposal looked right in the eyes of Absalom and of all Israel's elders.
17:5 But Absalom said, "Please call Hushai the Arkite as well. Let's hear what he too has in his mouth."
17:6 Hushai came to Absalom, and Absalom told him, "This is what Ahithophel said. Shall we act on his word? If not, you tell us."
17:7 Hushai replied to Absalom, "This time the advice Ahithophel has given is not good."
17:8 "You know your father and his men," Hushai went on. "They are fighters, and they are angry to the bone, like a bear in the open country whose cubs have been taken. Your father is a man of war. He will not spend the night among the troops.
17:9 Right now he is hiding in one of the caves or some other place. When some of our men fall at the start, anyone who hears will say, 'Absalom's followers have been cut down.'
17:10 Then even a brave man with a heart like a lion's heart will melt away inside. All Israel knows your father is a hero, and the men with him are fighters.
17:11 My advice is this: let all Israel be called together to you, from Dan to Beersheba, as many as the sand beside the sea, and go into battle yourself, at their head.
17:12 We will come at him in whatever place he turns up, and settle on him the way dew settles on the ground. Not one will be left, not him and not any of his men.
17:13 If he pulls back into a town, all Israel will bring ropes to that town, and we will haul it down into the valley until not a pebble of it can be found."
17:14 Absalom and all the men of Israel said, "Hushai the Arkite's advice is better than Ahithophel's." The LORD had given orders to undo Ahithophel's good advice, so that the LORD could bring ruin on Absalom.
## The messengers in the well
17:15 Hushai told Zadok and Abiathar the priests, "Ahithophel gave Absalom and Israel's elders this and that advice, and I gave this and that.
17:16 Now send word fast and tell David, 'Don't stay the night at the river crossings in the wilderness. Cross over, without fail, or the king and everyone with him will be swallowed up.'"
17:17 Jonathan and Ahimaaz were waiting at En-rogel. A slave girl would go and pass the news to them, and they would go and pass it to King David, since they could not be seen going into the city.
17:18 But a boy spotted them and told Absalom. The two of them hurried off and came to a man's house in Bahurim. He had a well in his yard, and they went down into it.
17:19 His wife took the cover, stretched it over the opening of the well and spread crushed grain on top. Nobody could tell anything.
17:20 Absalom's servants came to the woman at the house and demanded, "Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?" "They went on across the water channel," the woman told them. They hunted for them, found nothing and went back to Jerusalem.
17:21 When the men had gone, the two climbed up out of the well and went on to report to King David. "Get moving!" they told David. "Cross the water fast, because that is the advice Ahithophel has given against you."
17:22 David set out with all the people with him, and they crossed the Jordan. By the light of morning everyone had crossed the Jordan. Not one was left behind.
## Ahithophel's end
17:23 Ahithophel saw that his advice had not been carried out. He saddled his donkey and left for home, to his own town. He gave orders for his household, then hanged himself. So he died, and he was buried in his father's tomb.
## Friends at Mahanaim
17:24 By then David had reached Mahanaim, and Absalom crossed the Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.
17:25 Absalom had put Amasa over the army in Joab's place. Amasa was the son of a man named Ithra the Israelite, who had gone in to Abigail, Nahash's daughter, the sister of Zeruiah, Joab's mother.
17:26 Israel camped with Absalom in the land of Gilead.
17:27 When David came to Mahanaim, Shobi son of Nahash from Rabbah of the Ammonites, Machir son of Ammiel from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim
17:28 brought bedding, bowls and clay pots, wheat, barley, flour, roasted grain, beans, lentils, roasted grain,
17:29 honey, curds, sheep and cheese from the herd, for David and the people with him to eat. For they said, "The people are hungry, tired and thirsty in the wilderness."
## "Go easy on the young man"
18:1 David mustered the people with him and put officers over them, commanders over thousands and over hundreds.
18:2 David sent the army out, one third under Joab's command, one third under Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and one third under Ittai the Gittite. The king told the army, "I am going out with you too, myself."
18:3 "You must not go out," the army said. "If we have to run, nobody will give us a thought. If half of us die, nobody will give us a thought. But you are like ten thousand of us. So it is better for you to be ready in the city to send us help."
18:4 "Whatever looks good to you, I will do," the king told them. The king stood at the side of the gate while all the army went out in hundreds and in thousands.
18:5 The king gave orders to Joab, Abishai and Ittai: "Go easy, for my sake, on the young man, on Absalom." The whole army heard when the king gave all the commanders his orders about Absalom.
## The battle in the forest
18:6 The army marched out into the open country to face Israel, and the battle was fought in the forest of Ephraim.
18:7 There Israel's army was beaten by David's servants. The slaughter there was huge that day: twenty thousand.
18:8 The fighting spread out over the face of the whole region, and the forest ate up more of the army that day than the sword ate.
## Absalom in the oak
18:9 Absalom came face to face with David's servants. He was riding on the mule, and the mule went in under the tangled branches of the great oak. His head caught fast in the oak, and he was left between the sky and the ground while the mule beneath him went on.
18:10 One man saw it and reported to Joab, "Look, I saw Absalom dangling in the oak."
18:11 Joab said to the man who had brought the news, "You saw him! Then why didn't you knock him to the ground right there? I would have had to give you ten pieces of silver and a belt."
18:12 The man answered Joab, "Even if I could feel a thousand pieces of silver weighed into my palms, I would not reach out my hand against the king's son. We heard the king order you, Abishai and Ittai with our own ears: 'Guard the young man Absalom, for my sake.'
18:13 And if I had acted falsely and risked my own life, nothing stays hidden from the king, and you would have kept your distance."
18:14 "I am not going to wait around like this with you," Joab said. He took three sticks in his hand and drove them into Absalom's heart while he was still alive in the heart of the oak.
18:15 Then ten young men, Joab's armor bearers, closed in around Absalom, struck him and finished him off.
18:16 Joab blew the ram's horn, and the army came back from chasing Israel, because Joab held the army back.
18:17 They took Absalom and flung him into the forest, into the big pit, and raised a huge pile of stones over him. And all Israel fled, every man to his own tent.
18:18 While he was alive, Absalom had taken a standing stone and set it up for himself in the King's Valley. "I have no son to keep my name remembered," he had said. He named the stone after himself, and to this day it is called Absalom's Monument.
## Two runners
18:19 Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Please let me run and take the king the good news that the LORD has given him justice and freed him from his enemies' hand."
18:20 But Joab told him, "Today you are not the man for news. Carry news some other day. Today you will not carry news, because the king's son is dead."
18:21 Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go and tell the king what you saw." The Cushite bowed to Joab and took off running.
18:22 But Ahimaaz son of Zadok spoke up again to Joab: "Whatever happens, please let me run after the Cushite too." "Why would you run, my son?" Joab asked. "You have no news that will pay you."
18:23 "Whatever happens, I will run." "Run, then," Joab told him. And Ahimaaz ran by the road through the Jordan plain and got past the Cushite.
18:24 David was sitting between the two gates. The lookout went up on the gate's roof, on the wall. He raised his eyes and looked: there was a man running by himself.
18:25 The lookout shouted down to tell the king. "If he is alone," said the king, "he has news in his mouth." The man kept coming, closer and closer.
18:26 Then the lookout saw a second man running. He called to the gatekeeper, "There is a man running alone!" The king said, "This one is bringing news too."
18:27 "The way the first one runs looks to me like the way Ahimaaz son of Zadok runs," the lookout said. "He is a good man," said the king. "He is coming with good news."
18:28 Ahimaaz called out to the king, "Peace!" He bowed to the king, face to the ground, and said, "Blessed is the LORD your God, who has handed over the men who lifted their hand against my lord the king!"
18:29 The king asked, "Is it peace for the young man, for Absalom?" Ahimaaz answered, "I saw a big crowd in an uproar when Joab, the king's servant, was sending your servant off, but I do not know what it was."
18:30 "Turn aside and take your place here," the king said. He turned aside and stood waiting.
18:31 Just then the Cushite came in. "Good news for my lord the king!" the Cushite said. "The LORD has given you justice today and freed you from the power of all who rose against you."
18:32 The king asked the Cushite, "Is it peace for the young man, for Absalom?" The Cushite answered, "Let my lord the king's enemies, and all who have risen against you to do you harm, end up like that young man."
## "My son, my son"
18:33 The king shook. He climbed to the room above the gate and wept. And this is what he said as he went: "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! Oh, if only I had died in your place, I myself! Absalom, my son, my son!"
