# Hostile review: Wisdom of Solomon 11–15 (part 11-15)

Reviewer: separate agent. Checked: plain.txt against `texts/orig/Wis.json` (Swete) verse by verse; Swete's apparatus (`texts/src/Wis.app.json`); notes, variants, glosses, chapters (views, deep, settings, the chapter 13 card), topics, sources; 66-book quotations against the current Plain of each book; web checks listed under each finding. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Wis/11-15`: 0 errors, 0 warnings (122 verses, 75 notes, 10 variants, 42 glosses, 5 chapter pages, 14 topic contributions). The build passes, so every problem below is one the build cannot see.

## Verdict

A careful, readable part with a sound Greek base, good use of the Septuagint echoes, and mostly honest labeling. It is not ready to publish. Two verses are mistranslated or mis-built (12:11 reverses its sense; 11:6 loses its subject and silently departs from Swete's reading with no variant entry). The chapter 13 card largely repeats the Romans 1 card: three of its four rows rest on the same sources that answer the Romans question, not the narrower question the card asks. Several scholarly and church claims have no locator ("most scholars think Paul knew this chapter", the Orthodox and Catholic `views` lines, the Fathers on 14:7, which rests on one seventh-century bishop quoted by John of Damascus). The book's own rule that Wisdom's last act as a person is 11:1 is contradicted by 14:2. Nothing invented was found, and the Leo of Neapolis quotation, Dei Filius, CCC 41 and 43, Calvin, Barth's sentence, Philo's Decalogue sections and the lectionary readings I opened all check out. I could not check the Plain against the NRSV, NABRE, RSV, NJB or NETS from an open text, so I do not claim the audit; the one place where the writer's own drift log shows a deliberate shape change (12:11) is the place where the sense broke.

Findings: 0 critical, 7 major, 24 minor.

## Major

**1. The chapter 13 card repeats the Romans 1 card and most rows do not address its question.** chapters.json, ch. 13 `traditions`; compare `content/books/Rom/parts/01-04/chapters.json`, ch. 1 card "Can people know God from creation alone?".
The Wisdom card asks a narrower question: is there a real likeness between creatures and God (analogy), so the mind can argue from world to Maker? Row by row:
- Catholic: the first half (Dei Filius ch. 2 and canon 2.1: God "can be known with certainty") is the Romans card's Catholic row almost word for word and answers the wider question. The second half (CCC 41, CCC 43 on Lateran IV, Aquinas ST I q.13 a.5 "according to proportion") does address analogy. I verified CCC 41 and its footnote 15 (Wis 13:5) at catholicculture.org, and the Lateran/DS 806 note at CCC 43 through a search result; the Dei Filius wording in a search result. Keep only the analogy half.
- Orthodox: John of Damascus 1.1 ("implanted ... in all by nature") and 1.4 ("absolutely incomprehensible and unknowable"), verified at newadvent.org/fathers/33041. These answer "does the mind reach God's existence / his essence", which the Romans card's Orthodox row already says. Neither states whether creatures truly resemble God. The row never uses the word analogy or likeness.
- Lutheran: Heidelberg Disputation theses 19–20 (verified through a search result for the wording). It is about whether looking at creation yields the God who saves, again the Romans question, and the Romans card cites the same theses. Nothing here is about analogy. Luther's own thesis is not a Lutheran confession either; no Lutheran Confession is cited.
- Reformed: Calvin 1.5.1, 1.5.14, 1.5.15 (verified at ccel.org). The witness that leaves no excuse is the Romans question, and Calvin is on the Romans card already. Only the Barth sentence addresses analogy.
So one row (Barth) plus half of one (Catholic) answers the card's question, and the other rows duplicate the Romans card's content and sources. The card's own parenthesis ("whether people can know God from creation at all is asked on the card at Romans 1") admits the overlap, then the rows answer it anyway.
Fix: either (a) rewrite each row to answer the analogy question with a source that addresses it (Orthodox: a source on likeness or the essence/energies distinction that was opened; Lutheran: a source that addresses analogy; Reformed: Calvin only if he speaks to resemblance, otherwise say Reformed teachers answer with Barth and others), or (b) drop the card and point to the Romans 1 card from the 13:5 note. Do not keep rows that restate the Romans rows.

**2. 12:11: the Plain reverses the sense.** plain.txt 12:11, "And you did not let their sins go unpunished because you were afraid of anyone."
Greek: `οὐδὲ εὐλαβούμενός τινα ἐφ᾽ οἷς ἡμάρτανον ἄδειαν ἐδίδους`. `ἄδειαν ἐδίδους` is "you were granting immunity / impunity". The sentence says that it was not from fear of anyone that God let their sins go (for a while) unpunished; the whole context (12:9–10, "a little at a time", "room for repentance") is that God delayed judgment and the delay was not weakness. The Plain says God did not let sins go unpunished, which asserts punishment and gives fear as a reason, the opposite. The writer's drift log marks this verse "changed ('unpunished for their sins')" and the change broke the meaning.
Fix: "You did not give them leave to sin because you were afraid of anyone" or "It was not out of fear of anyone that you let their sins go unpunished." Keep the Greek order of ideas.

**3. 11:6: the subject is lost, and the Plain follows a reading Swete's text does not print, with no variant.** plain.txt 11:6–7; variants.json has no 11:6 entry.
Swete prints `ταραχθέντες` (masculine plural, "the Egyptians, troubled with gory blood"). The Plain, "Instead of a river's never-failing spring, / churned and fouled with clotted blood", makes "churned and fouled" describe the river, which is the construction of the other reading. Swete's apparatus (page note at 11:18) reads `ταραχθεντος ℵA`: Sinaiticus and Alexandrinus have the genitive singular agreeing with "river". So the Plain follows ℵA's construction against its own base text and never says so, and in 11:7 "you gave your people plenty of water" now has no contrast with a group that was troubled. "Clotted" for `λυθρώδει` (gory, defiling) is also an addition; the gloss says "thick, gory blood".
Fix: render `ταραχθέντες` so the Egyptians are the ones troubled ("the Egyptians, stirred up by foul blood, instead of the ever-flowing spring of the river"), and add a variant entry for ℵA's `ταραχθέντος`.

**4. "Most scholars think Paul knew this chapter" has no locator, and its strength changes from place to place.** notes.json entry "Romans 1 and Wisdom 13" (13:5 echo): "Many scholars think Paul knew this book or the synagogue teaching it records"; chapters.json ch. 13 `H`: "Most scholars think Paul in Romans 1:18–32 draws on this chapter or on the synagogue teaching it records"; note 15:7: "Many scholars think Paul knew this passage"; ch. 15 `H`: "Most scholars think the potter of 15:7 and Paul's potter of Romans 9:21 are related"; ch. 15 `readings`: "Paul seems to know this chapter twice"; topic natural-revelation `common`: "widely held to stand behind Paul's argument". The Romans part's note at 1:20 says only that "the resemblance suggests Paul is using a way of arguing familiar in Jewish synagogues". No scholar is named in the Wisdom part and the strength words (many, most, widely) are not tied to anything opened. `sources-checked.md` lists no commentary. STYLE.md rule 2 requires a locator for what named scholars say, and consensus must be labeled with who dissents (rule 3).
Fix: name the commentators actually opened (the Romans part already has `dunn-romans`, `fitzmyer-romans`, `moo-romans`, `cranfield-romans` in its sources), state what each says about dependence versus shared tradition, and make the Wisdom and Romans parts say the same thing. If none was opened, say only what the texts show (the shared steps and words) and drop "most".

**5. Wisdom as a person: the part contradicts itself, and the term rule is not applied at 14:2.** notes.json 11:1 ("This is her last act as a person in the book"), 13:1 "the Craftsman", 14:2; plain.txt 14:2, 14:5; topics.json `wisdom`.
Greek 14:2: `τεχνῖτις δὲ σοφία κατεσκεύασεν`, "craftswoman Wisdom built it", the same epithet as 7:22 `ἡ γὰρ πάντων τεχνῖτις ... σοφία` and 8:6 `τεχνῖτις`. The Plain gives "a craftsman's wisdom" in lower case and no note says why Wisdom is not a person here, while the 11:1 note says she is not one after 11:1 ("for the rest of the book it is God, not Wisdom, who acts"). The brief (brief-wis.md, Terms) says to decide "by the Greek, not by a translation's habit" and to put a note at first use in each part. The topic `wisdom` even calls it "the craftsman's wisdom ... behind ships", as if the epithet were ordinary.
Also note 13:1 ("the Craftsman") has errors: its preview says `technitēs` is "the word for Wisdom in 7:21 and 8:6", but the word in both places is the feminine `τεχνῖτις`, and the verse is 7:22 (its own body says 7:22; 7:21 is `ἔγνων`). Hebrews 11:10 is quoted as "craftsman and builder"; the current Plain of Hebrews 11:10 reads "architect and maker", so the quotation does not match and the same Greek `τεχνίτης` is rendered two ways across the site (see 20 below).
Fix: decide 14:2 (capital W with a note, or a note that explains the lower case and that the epithet echoes 7:22), change the "last act" sentence in the 11:1 note and in topics.json, correct the 13:1 preview to 7:22 and `technitis`, and quote Hebrews 11:10 as the Plain has it.

**6. 14:7 and "the Fathers": one witness, and the claims around it go further.** notes.json 14:7 "The blessed wood"; chapters.json ch. 14 `readings`, `views` C/O, the 14:3–6 setting; sources.json `john-damascus-images-fordham`.
The brief asks which Fathers read 14:7 of the cross. The only evidence is one passage in John of Damascus's defense of images, quoting "Leo, Bishop of Neapolis in Cyprus ... against the Jews, on the Adoration of the Cross". I opened the Fordham page and it says exactly that ("And Solomon said, 'Blessed is the wood by which justice cometh.' (Wis. 14.7)", after Moses' staff and Elisha's iron), so the quotation is real. But:
- The note says "Christians soon heard the verse of the cross". Leo of Neapolis and John of Damascus are seventh and eighth century, so "soon" is not shown. I searched for earlier uses and could not confirm any (searches found medieval distinctiones, not Fathers), and the writer's sources-checked file lists none.
- The ch. 14 `C` row says "Catholic readers have applied 14:7 to the cross" with no source and no Catholic writer; the `O` row's "the Orthodox distinguish the honor given to icons from the worship condemned here: an idol is called a god (14:8), an icon is not" is an argument built by the writer, attached to no Orthodox text; the setting view says Christian readers found "a figure of the church and the cross" (the church typology comes from 1 Peter 3:20, not from any source about 14:7).
- Chapter 14 `readings` and the setting say "Christian readers" in the plural.
Fix: say plainly that one early medieval Greek text, preserved by John of Damascus, applies the verse to the cross, and give the Leo passage as the locator; remove "soon" and the plural unless more Fathers are opened (CCEL, Source Library); give the Catholic row a real source (a rite, a catechism, a named writer) or delete it; mark the icon argument as the writer's own or source it to John's Apology; drop "the church" unless a source reads 14:7 so.

## Minor

**8. 11:14 "they had thrown him out".** Greek `τὸν ... ἐν ἐκθέσει πάλαι ῥιφέντα` is a passive participle: "the one cast out long ago". The Plain supplies the Egyptians as the agents, and note 7 states it as fact ("counts this as Egypt's act") although the same note says Moses' mother laid the child in the reeds. Make the Plain passive, or say in the note that the agent is a reading (Pharaoh's decree).

**9. 12:5 "a frenzied cult".** `θιάσου` is a band of worshipers; "frenzied" is not in the Greek. The variant entry's own "en" line says "cult band", so the Plain differs from the entry's gloss. The note's claim of "the wild rites of Dionysus" and "charges Greeks made against peoples they despised" (also ch. 12 `H`) is unlocated. Drop "frenzied", and either source the Dionysus link or remove it.

**10. 13:9 "why were they so slow to find the Master".** `πῶς τάχιον οὐχ εὗρον`: "how did they not find [him] sooner / more readily?" The Plain implies they found him later; the Greek says they did not. Change to "why did they not find the Master of these things more quickly?" or "sooner".
