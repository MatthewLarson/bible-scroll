## Wisdom Leads Israel Through the Desert
11:1 Wisdom made their works succeed / through the hand of a holy prophet.
11:2 They crossed a desert where no one lived, / and set up their tents in places no one had walked.
11:3 They stood up to those who made war on them, / and they fought off their enemies.
11:4 They were thirsty, and they called on you, / and out of a sheer rock water was given to them, / a cure for their thirst out of hard stone.
## The Same Things Punish and Save
11:5 The very things that punished their enemies / were the things that did them good when they were in need.
11:6 Instead of a river's never-failing spring, / their enemies were thrown into turmoil by foul, gory blood
11:7 as a rebuke for the order to kill the babies, / but to your people you gave plenty of water when they had no hope of it.
11:8 By the thirst they felt at that time, you showed them / how you had punished their opponents.
11:9 For when they were put to the test, / even though they were disciplined with mercy, / they came to see what torments the ungodly suffered / when they were judged in anger.
11:10 You tested your own people like a father who warns, / but you put the others on trial like a harsh king who condemns.
11:11 Whether they were away or close by, / they were worn down just the same.
11:12 A double grief took hold of them, / and so did groaning as they remembered what had passed.
11:13 When they heard that their own punishments / had done the others good, / they became aware of the Lord.
11:14 Long ago he had been cast out as a baby to die, / and later they mocked him and turned him away. / But when it all came to an end, they were amazed at him, / for their thirst was not like the thirst of the righteous.
## Punished Through What They Worshiped
11:15 In return for the senseless reasonings of their wrongdoing, / by which they wandered off into worshiping mindless reptiles and worthless vermin, / you sent against them swarms of mindless animals to pay them back,
11:16 so that they would know this: / what a person sins with, he is punished with.
11:17 Your hand can do all things. It created the world out of formless matter, / and it had ways enough to send them crowds of bears or fierce lions,
11:18 or new kinds of beasts no one had seen, full of fury, / snorting out a blast of fire, / or blowing out foul-smelling smoke, / or shooting fearful sparks out of their eyes.
11:19 The harm they did could have wiped them out, / and even the sight of them could have killed them with fright.
11:20 Even without these, they could have fallen by a single breath, / hunted down by Justice / and scattered by the breath of your power. / But you set everything in its order: by measure, by number and by weight.
## Mercy on All
11:21 You can always act with great strength. / Who will hold out against the power of your arm?
11:22 Before you, the whole world is like the slight weight that tips a pair of scales, / like one drop of morning dew falling on the ground.
11:23 Yet because you can do all things, you show mercy to all. / You look past people's sins / to bring them to repentance.
11:24 You love all that is, / and nothing you made disgusts you, / for if you had hated a thing you would never have fashioned it.
11:25 How could anything have lasted unless you wanted it to? / Or how could anything be kept safe that you had not called?
11:26 You spare everything, because it is yours, / Master, lover of souls.
12:1 Your imperishable spirit is in everything.
12:2 That is why you correct those who stumble a little at a time. / You warn them by reminding them of their sins, / so that they will be freed from evil and believe in you, Lord.
## Even the Canaanites Were Given Time
12:3 The ancient inhabitants of your holy land
12:4 you hated, because they did the most hateful things: / acts of sorcery and unholy rites,
12:5 merciless murders of children, / feasts on human flesh and blood, eating the innards, / initiates from the midst of a cult band,
12:6 parents who killed helpless souls with their own hands. / You chose to destroy them by the hands of our fathers,
12:7 so that the land you prize above all others / would receive a worthy colony of God's children.
12:8 Yet you spared even them, since they were human. / You sent wasps to run ahead of your army / and wipe them out a little at a time.
12:9 It was not that you lacked the power to hand the ungodly over to the righteous in battle, / or to crush them all at once with terrible beasts / or with one harsh word.
12:10 But you judged them a little at a time, / and gave them room for repentance. / You knew well that they came from an evil origin, / that their wickedness was born in them, / and that their reasoning would never change.
12:11 For they were a seed cursed from the beginning. / And when you held back punishment for the sins they committed, / it was not because you were afraid of anyone.
## Who Can Call You to Account?
12:12 Who will say, "What have you done?" / Or who will stand against your judgment? / Who will accuse you over nations that perished, nations you made? / Or who will come into court against you / to take up the cause of unrighteous people?
12:13 There is no god but you, who cares for all, / and there is no one to whom you must prove / that you did not judge unjustly.
12:14 No king or absolute ruler can look you in the face / to argue for those you have punished.
12:15 Since you are righteous, you manage everything with righteousness. / You count it foreign to your power / to condemn a person who has not earned punishment.
12:16 Your strength is where righteousness begins, / and because you are Master of all, you spare all.
12:17 When people do not believe your power is complete, you put your strength on display, / and you expose the arrogance of those who know it.
12:18 But you, the master of strength, judge with kindness / and rule us with much restraint, / for the power to act is yours whenever you wish.
## What God Taught His People
12:19 By works like these you taught your people / that whoever is righteous must love people, / and you gave your children good hope, / because you grant repentance when people sin.
12:20 If you punished the enemies of your children, who deserved to die, / with so much attention and entreaty, / giving them time and room / to be set free from their wickedness,
12:21 then with how much more exactness you have judged your own children, / whose fathers received from you oaths and covenants of good promises!
12:22 So while you discipline us, / you whip our enemies ten thousand times as hard, / so that when we judge, we will think about your goodness, / and when we are judged, we will look for mercy.
## Mocked by Their Own Gods
12:23 That is why you tormented the unrighteous, / who lived their lives in folly, / with their own disgusting things.
12:24 They wandered farther down the roads of error than anyone, / taking as gods the most worthless even of hateful animals. / They were fooled like babies who have no sense.
12:25 So, as if to children who cannot reason, / you sent them a judgment that made fun of them.
12:26 But those who would not be warned by rebukes that were only a game / will feel a judgment worthy of God.
12:27 They suffered and grew angry at the creatures they thought were gods, / for they were being punished through them. / They saw him, and the one they had long refused to know / they now recognized as the true God. / That is why the final sentence of condemnation came upon them.
## Those Who Worship Nature
13:1 All people were empty by nature / who lived without knowing God. / From the good things they could see / they could not come to know the One Who Is, / and though they looked closely at his works, / they did not recognize the Craftsman.
13:2 But they took fire, or wind, or quick-moving air, / or the ring of the stars, or violent water, / or the lights of heaven, / to be gods who govern the world.
13:3 If the beauty of these things charmed them into thinking they were gods, / they should know how much better their Master is than they are, / for the very Source of beauty created them.
13:4 If they were struck with wonder at their power and their workings, / they should realize from these / how much greater in might is the one who built them.
13:5 From how great and how beautiful created things are, / their Maker is seen, by analogy.
13:6 But even so, the blame on these people is small, / for perhaps they only go astray / while they search for God and long to find him.
13:7 They spend their lives among his works and look into them closely, / and they are won over by what they see, / because what they see is beautiful.
13:8 But then again, even they are not to be excused.
13:9 If their knowledge was strong enough / to let them explore the world, / why did they not find the Master of these things sooner?
## Gods Made by Hand
13:10 But miserable are those who set their hopes on dead things, / who have called the works of human hands gods: / gold and silver shaped with craft, / likenesses of animals, / or a useless stone carved by some hand long ago.
13:11 Suppose a woodcutter, a carpenter, saws down a tree that is easy to handle. / With practiced hands he peels away all the bark, / and with fine work he makes a useful thing / to serve the needs of life.
13:12 He burns the scraps from his work to cook his food, / and he eats his fill.
13:13 But one scrap left over from these is good for nothing, / a crooked piece of wood grown full of knots. / He takes it and carves it with care when he has nothing else to do. / He shapes it with the skill of his free hours / and makes it look like a human being,
13:14 or into the likeness of some cheap animal. / He coats it with red ochre, / reddens its skin with rouge, / and paints over every flaw in it.
13:15 Then he makes a shrine worthy of it. / He fixes it into a wall / and makes it secure with iron.
13:16 He sees to it that it does not fall over, / knowing it is helpless to help itself, / since it is an image and has to be helped.
13:17 Yet when he prays about his property, his marriage and his children, / he feels no shame in talking to a thing without life. / For health he calls on what is weak,
13:18 for life he begs from what is dead, / for help he pleads with what knows nothing at all, / for a good journey he asks what cannot even take a step,
13:19 and for earnings and work and a good outcome for his hands, / he begs for power from the thing with the least power in its hands.
## The Wood That Saves
14:1 Again, someone getting ready to sail, about to cross wild waves, / cries for help to a piece of wood / that is more rotten than the ship carrying him.
14:2 The ship was thought up by the desire for profit, / and Wisdom, the craftswoman, built it.
14:3 But your providence, Father, is at the helm, / for you have given a road even in the sea / and a safe track among the waves.
14:4 This shows that you are able to rescue from anything, / so that even someone who knows nothing of sailing can climb aboard.
14:5 You do not want the works of your wisdom to lie idle. / So people hand over their souls to the tiniest scrap of wood, / and crossing the surf on a raft, they come through safe.
14:6 For in the beginning, too, when the proud giants were dying, / the world's hope fled for safety onto a raft. / Your hand steered it, / and it left to future ages the seed of new births.
14:7 For blessed is wood through which righteousness comes about.
## Cursed Is the Idol
14:8 But the handmade thing is cursed, and its maker with it: / he because he worked on it, / and the perishable thing because it was given the name of god.
14:9 God hates both alike, / the ungodly person and his ungodliness.
14:10 The deed and the doer / will be punished together.
14:11 So there will be a visitation even on the idols of the nations, / because, though they belong to God's creation, they became a disgusting thing, / stumbling blocks for people's souls / and a trap for the feet of fools.
## How Idols Began
14:12 Thinking up idols was how sexual sin began, / and inventing them brought corruption into life.
14:13 They were not there at the start, / and they will not be there forever.
14:14 They came into the world through human empty pride, / and that is why a quick end has been planned for them.
14:15 A father, worn down by grief over a child taken too soon, / made an image of the child so quickly snatched away. / The child had been a dead human being, / but now the father gave him honor as a god, / and he handed down secret rites and ceremonies to those under him.
14:16 Then, as time went on, the ungodly custom took firm hold and was obeyed like a law. / Absolute rulers gave orders, and carved images were worshiped.
14:17 Some people lived too far away to honor their rulers in person. / So they formed a picture of how the distant king looked / and made an image of him that everyone could see, / eager to flatter the absent king as if he were among them.
14:18 The craftsman's love of fame then pushed the worship higher, / even among people who had never known the king.
14:19 Maybe he wanted to win the favor of someone in power, / so he used his skill to force the likeness into something finer than life.
14:20 The crowds were drawn in by how charming the work was, / and the man they had honored only a little earlier / they now treated as an object of worship.
14:21 This became an ambush for human life, / because people who were slaves to misfortune or to an absolute ruler / gave to stones and wood the Name that can be shared with no one.
## What Idolatry Leads To
14:22 Going wrong about the knowledge of God did not satisfy them. / They live in a vast war of ignorance, / and they give the name peace to evils so great.
14:23 For they hold rites where children are killed, or secret mysteries, / or crazed revels with outlandish rules.
14:24 Neither their lives nor their marriages stay pure any longer. / One man lies in wait and kills another, / or wounds him by adultery.
14:25 Everything is a mix of blood and murder, theft and deceit, / corruption, faithlessness, riot, perjury, / the harassing of good people,
14:26 forgetting of kindness, defiling of souls, / the mixing up of kinds, disorder in marriage, / adultery and shameless indecency.
14:27 Worship of idols too vile to name / is the start of every evil, its cause and its end.
14:28 They either rave like madmen when they celebrate, / or they prophesy lies, / or they live unrighteously, / or they are quick to swear false oaths.
14:29 Since they trust in lifeless idols, / they swear falsely and do not expect to be hurt for it.
14:30 But justice will catch up with them for both things: / first, they had wrong ideas about God and gave their attention to idols; / second, they swore unrighteous oaths to deceive, with contempt for holiness.
14:31 What always pursues the wrongdoing of the unrighteous / is not the power of what they swear by, / but the penalty that sinners have earned.
## But We Are Yours
15:1 But you, our God, are kind and true. / You are slow to anger, and you govern all things with mercy.
15:2 Even if we sin, we are yours, because we know your might. / But knowing we are counted as yours, we will not sin.
15:3 Knowing you is complete righteousness, / and knowing your might is the root from which immortality grows.
15:4 We were not led astray by the evil art that people invent, / or by painters' wasted labor, / a figure smeared with different colors.
15:5 The sight of such things leads fools into disgrace, / and they long for the breathless form of a lifeless image.
15:6 Lovers of evil, and worthy of such hopes, / are those who make them, those who long for them, and those who worship them.
## The Potter
15:7 A potter kneads soft earth / and with hard work shapes each thing for our use. / From the same clay he forms / both the pots that serve clean purposes and those for the opposite, / all in the same way. / But what each one will be used for, / the clay-worker is the judge.
15:8 Then, with wicked toil, he shapes a useless god from the same clay. / A short time ago he himself came from the earth, / and in a little while he will go back to the earth he was taken from, / when the debt of his soul is demanded back.
15:9 But his worry is not that he will grow weary, / or that his life is short. / He competes with goldsmiths and silversmiths, / he copies the bronze workers, / and he thinks it a glory to make fakes.
15:10 His heart is ashes. / His hope is worth less than soil, / and his life is more worthless than clay,
15:11 because he did not know the one who shaped him, / who breathed into him a soul that acts / and blew into him a life-giving breath.
15:12 Instead he thought our life was a game / and our time on earth a fair for making money. / "A person has to make a profit wherever he can," he says, "even from evil."
15:13 More than anyone, this man is aware that he is sinning / when he turns earthy stuff into breakable pots and carved images.
## The Gods of Israel's Enemies
15:14 But all the enemies of your people who oppressed them are utterly foolish, / more wretched than the soul of a baby.
15:15 For they thought all the idols of the nations were gods, / yet these cannot use eyes for seeing, / or noses for drawing breath, / or ears for hearing, / or the fingers of their hands for touching, / and their feet are no good for walking.
15:16 A human being made them; / one whose own breath is on loan shaped them. / Not one human being can shape a god that matches himself.
15:17 Being mortal, he works with lawless hands and produces a dead thing. / He is worth more than the objects of his worship: / he lived once, but they never did.
15:18 They also worship animals, the most hateful kinds, / which, when you compare their stupidity, are worse than all the rest.
15:19 There is no beauty in them to make anyone long for them, / as can happen when a person looks at animals. / God's praise and God's blessing have both passed them by.
