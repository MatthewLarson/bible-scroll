## Quail instead of vermin
16:1 So they were punished, as they deserved, by creatures of the same kind, / and tormented by swarms of vermin.
16:2 In place of that punishment, you did good to your people: / to satisfy their craving you made ready quail, / a food with a strange new taste.
16:3 So on one side the Egyptians, though they wanted food, / would turn away even from the hunger they could not help, / because of what was shown them in the creatures sent against them; / while your people, after going short for a little while, / would get a share of a strange new taste.
16:4 For a want they could not escape had to come on those who played the tyrant, / but your people only had to be shown how their enemies were being tormented.
## The snakes and the token of salvation
16:5 Even when the terrible fury of wild beasts came on your people, / and they were dying from the bites of twisting snakes, / your anger did not last to the end.
16:6 They were troubled for a little while as a warning, / and they had a token of salvation / to remind them of what your law commands.
16:7 The one who turned to it was not saved by the thing he looked at, / but by you, the Savior of all.
16:8 By this, too, you convinced our enemies / that you are the one who rescues from every evil.
16:9 The bites of locusts and flies killed them, / and there was no cure for their souls, / because they deserved to be punished by such things.
16:10 But your sons were not overcome even by the fangs of poisonous serpents, / for your mercy met them and healed them.
16:11 They were stung to remind them of your sayings, / and they were quickly saved, / so that deep forgetting would not swallow them / and leave them unmindful of your kindness.
16:12 It was no herb or soothing ointment that cured them, / but your word, Lord, which heals all things.
16:13 You hold authority over life and death; / you bring down to the gates of Hades, and you bring back up.
16:14 A human being may kill in his wickedness, / but he cannot bring back the breath that has gone out, / or release a soul that has been taken.
## Hail with fire, and bread from heaven
16:15 But no one can escape your hand.
16:16 The ungodly refused to know you, / so your strong arm whipped them. / Strange rains and hail and storms chased them, and they could not escape, / and fire ate them up.
16:17 The most amazing thing was this: / in water, which puts out everything, / the fire burned all the more. / For the world itself fights for the righteous.
16:18 At one time the flame was tamed, / so that it would not burn up the living things sent upon the ungodly, / but they would see it and know / that God's judgment was driving them on.
16:19 At another time, even in the middle of water, it blazed hotter than fire's own power, / to wreck what an unrighteous land had grown.
16:20 In place of this, you fed your people the food of angels. / You sent them bread from heaven, already made, with no work on their part, / with the power to give every kind of pleasure / and to be in tune with every taste.
16:21 For your provision showed how sweet you are to your children. / Fitting itself to the longing of whoever ate it, / it was changed into whatever each one wished.
16:22 Snow and ice held up under fire and did not melt, / so that your people would know / that it was fire that ruined their enemies' crops, / fire blazing in the hail and flashing through the rain.
16:23 But this same fire, so that the righteous could be fed, / forgot even its own power.
16:24 For creation serves you, its Maker. / It tightens for punishment against the unrighteous, / and slackens to do good for those who trust in you.
16:25 That is why, even then, it took on every shape / to serve your gift that feeds everyone, / just as the needy wanted.
16:26 So that your sons, Lord, the ones you loved, would learn this: / a human being is not fed by crops springing up, / but your word keeps safe those who believe in you.
16:27 For the food that fire could not destroy / melted as soon as a brief sunbeam warmed it,
16:28 to teach that we must be up ahead of the sun to thank you, / and meet with you in prayer as the daylight rises.
16:29 For the hope of the ungrateful will melt like winter frost / and run off like useless water.
## Darkness over Egypt
17:1 Your judgments are great and hard to tell of; / that is why undisciplined souls went astray.
17:2 Lawless men thought they could hold a holy nation down by force. / But they themselves lay prisoners of darkness, / chained by a long night and locked in under their own roofs, / exiles from the providence that never ends.
17:3 They thought their secret sins would go unseen, / but they were scattered under a lightless veil of forgetting, / dreadfully afraid and shaken by ghostly shapes.
17:4 Not even the inner room that hid them kept them from fear. / Noises boomed all around and shook them, / and gloomy phantoms with grim, unsmiling faces appeared.
17:5 No force of fire could give them light, / and the bright flames of the stars could not bear to light up that hateful night.
17:6 All that shone through to them was a blaze that lit itself, full of terror. / In their fright, once that sight was gone, / they judged the things they had seen to be worse still.
17:7 The tricks of their magic arts fell flat, / and their boast of wisdom was shown up to their shame.
17:8 The ones who claimed they could chase fears and troubles away from a sick soul / were themselves sick with a fear that was laughable.
17:9 For even when nothing frightening was there to scare them, / they were startled by vermin creeping past and snakes hissing,
17:10 and they were perishing, trembling, / refusing even to look at the air, which no one can escape from anywhere.
17:11 For wickedness, when it is condemned, testifies by itself that it is a coward; / gripped by its conscience, it always imagines the worst.
17:12 For fear is nothing but reason's help betrayed.
17:13 And when the hope inside is weak, / it counts not knowing the cause of the torment / as worse than the cause itself.
17:14 All through that night, a night truly without power, / rising against them out of the deepest corners of powerless Hades, / they slept one and the same sleep,
17:15 at times chased by phantoms like monsters, / at times made limp by their own souls' betrayal; / for a fear came on them, sudden and unforeseen.
17:16 So whoever was there dropped where he was / and was held under guard, locked in a prison without iron bars.
17:17 Whether he was a farmer, or a shepherd, / or a laborer at his toil out in the wilds, / he was caught off guard and had to bear a fate he could not escape.
17:18 For all were bound with one chain of darkness. / A whistling wind, / or birds singing sweetly among the spreading branches, / or the pulse of water rushing hard,
17:19 or the grim rumble of rocks crashing down, / or animals bounding along out of sight, / or the howling of the wildest beasts, / or an echo bouncing back from hollow places in the mountains, / turned them weak with terror.
17:20 For the whole world was bright with shining light / and went about its work with nothing to stop it.
17:21 Only over them was spread a heavy night, / a picture of the darkness that was going to take them in. / But they were a heavier weight to themselves than the darkness.
## Light for your holy ones
18:1 But your holy ones had the brightest light. / The Egyptians heard their voices but could not see their shapes, / and they called them blessed because they had not suffered too.
18:2 Your people had been wronged before, yet they were not harming them, / and for this the Egyptians gave thanks / and begged their favor for having been at odds with them.
18:3 In place of the darkness, you gave your people a pillar of blazing fire, / to guide them on a journey they did not know, / a sun that did no harm on their noble journey abroad.
18:4 For they deserved to lose the light and be jailed in darkness, / those who had kept your sons shut up, / your sons, through whom the world was going to receive / the law's imperishable light.
## The night of the firstborn
18:5 They had made up their minds to kill the babies of your holy ones, / and one child was put out to die and was saved. / So to convict them you took away their children in great numbers, / and wiped them out together in the mighty water.
18:6 Our fathers knew about that night beforehand, / so that they would take heart, knowing for sure / what oaths they had put their trust in.
18:7 Your people waited for it: / salvation for the righteous and ruin for their enemies.
18:8 For by the same means you used to punish our opponents, / you summoned us to your side and made us glorious.
18:9 The holy children of good parents sacrificed in secret, / and of one mind they made the divine law binding on themselves: / that the holy people would take the same share of good things and of dangers alike. / And already the fathers were leading the songs of praise.
18:10 But from their enemies came an answering cry, all out of tune, / and the pitiful wailing for children being mourned spread far and wide.
18:11 Slave and master paid the same penalty, / and commoner and king suffered alike.
18:12 All of them together had dead beyond counting, / all by one kind of death. / There were not even enough living to bury them, / for in a single moment their most prized offspring were destroyed.
18:13 They had refused to believe anything because of their sorceries, / but when the firstborn were destroyed, they admitted that this people was God's son.
## The all-powerful word
18:14 While still silence lay over everything, / and night, hurrying on its own course, had reached its middle,
18:15 your all-powerful word, a warrior without mercy, / sprang down from the heavens, from the royal thrones, / into the heart of the land marked for ruin.
18:16 Your command, which was no empty threat, it carried as a sharp sword. / It took its stand and filled all things with death. / It reached to heaven, and yet it had set foot on the earth.
18:17 Then at once dreadful dream-visions threw them into panic, / and fears they did not expect came on them.
18:18 Flung down here and there, half dead, / each one made known the reason he was dying.
18:19 For the dreams that troubled them had warned them of this beforehand, / so they would not die without knowing why they were suffering.
## Aaron stands between
18:20 The righteous, too, were touched by a trial of death, / and a plague broke out on the crowd in the wilderness. / But the anger did not last long.
18:21 For a blameless man rushed to fight in their defense. / He brought the weapon of his own priestly service: / prayer, and incense to make atonement. / He stood against the fury and brought the disaster to a stop, / proving himself to be your servant.
18:22 He overcame the turmoil, not with bodily strength or the power of weapons. / With a word he brought the punisher under control, / by calling to mind the oaths and covenants made with the fathers.
18:23 The dead already lay in heaps, one fallen on another, / when he stepped in between, beat back the anger, / and blocked its road to the living.
18:24 For the whole world was on his ankle-length robe, / the fathers' glories were cut into the four rows of stone, / and your majesty sat on the crown of his head.
18:25 To these the destroyer gave way; these were what they feared. / For just a taste of the anger was enough.
## Through the sea
19:1 But on the ungodly a merciless fury pressed to the very end, / for he knew ahead of time what they would do next:
19:2 that after they had changed course and let your people go, / and even hurried them on their way, / they would change their minds and chase after them.
19:3 For while they were still busy with their mourning / and wailing over the tombs of those who had died, / they took up another foolish plan. / The people they had begged to leave and had thrown out, / they now hunted as runaways.
19:4 The fate they deserved dragged them to this end / and made them forget what had happened, / so that they would fill up the punishment still missing from their torments,
19:5 and so that your people would make a journey beyond belief, / while the others found death of a strange kind.
19:6 For the whole creation, each part in its own kind, was shaped again from the start, / obeying the commands meant for it, / to keep your children from harm.
19:7 The cloud was seen casting its shade over the camp, / and dry land rising up out of what had been water. / Out of the Red Sea came a clear road, / and out of the wild surf a green plain.
19:8 Along it the whole nation passed through, / sheltered by your hand, / and they watched amazing wonders.
19:9 They roamed like horses at pasture, and they skipped like lambs, / praising you, Lord, their rescuer.
## Old wonders and new
19:10 For they still remembered what happened in their time as foreigners: / how, instead of breeding animals, the ground brought out gnats, / and instead of water creatures, the river belched up a swarm of frogs.
19:11 Later they also saw a new way for birds to come into being, / when, carried away by craving, they asked for rich foods;
19:12 for to comfort them, up from the sea came quail.
## Worse than Sodom
19:13 Punishments came on the sinners, / not without the signs that came with them, the violent crashes of thunder. / They suffered justly for their own wicked deeds, / for they had practiced a harsher hatred of strangers.
19:14 Others refused to take in strangers they did not know when they arrived, / but these turned guests who had helped them into slaves.
19:15 And not only that: there will surely be a visitation for those others, / because they gave foreigners a hostile welcome.
19:16 But these welcomed with feasting those who already shared the same rights as they did, / and then crushed them with cruel labor.
19:17 They were struck with blindness too, / like the men at the righteous man's doors, / when, wrapped in a vast darkness, / each one groped for the way through his own door.
## Creation tuned anew
19:18 For the elements were re-tuned among themselves, / the way notes on a harp change what the melody is called / and yet keep their own sound. / Anyone who looks closely at what happened can work this out exactly.
19:19 Creatures of dry land became creatures of the water, / and swimmers crossed over onto the land.
19:20 Fire in water was stronger than its own power, / and water forgot its power to put fire out.
19:21 The flames, in turn, did not shrivel the bodies / of perishable living things that walked about in them, / nor did they thaw the ice-like kind of ambrosial food, / so quick to melt.
19:22 For in every way, Lord, you have made your people great and given them glory. / You have not looked past them, / but have stood by them at every time and in every place.
