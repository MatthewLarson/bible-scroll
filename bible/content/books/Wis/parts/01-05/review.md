# Hostile review: Wisdom of Solomon 1-5 (part 01-05)

## Verdict

The part is sound. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Wis/01-05` gives 0 errors and 0 warnings (102 verses, 79 notes, 20 variants, 40 glosses, 15 topic contributions). I read all 102 verses against `texts/orig/Wis.json` and found no mistranslation that changes meaning, and no invented source, fragment or reading (critical: none). The Plain English Translation stays close to the Greek, and the wording departs from the KJV and Douay base. Four things need fixing before the part counts as reviewed. (1) The chapter 3 card has two rows (Catholic, Reformed) whose cited sources answer a neighboring question, not "by nature or by gift". (2) The Orthodox row gives one Father's words as "the Orthodox" answer, and its best quote is about angels. (3) The 2:23 variant leans on a second-hand report, the apparatus in `texts/src` has no entry for it, and the drift audit says the Greek "forces" a wording that Swete's own text does not have. (4) The Enoch identification and the Rashi parallel are stated more firmly than the evidence allows, and Rashi was not opened. Minor items follow. Checked in this session: the Greek and the Swete apparatus, the Latin file, John of Damascus 2.3 and 2.12 (newadvent.org/fathers/33042.htm: "not by nature but by grace", "of the grace of the Creator"), and Aquinas ST I q.75 a.6 ad 2 (newadvent.org/summa/1075.htm: "the power of ceasing to sustain existence"). Not checked, so no confirmation: Rashi on Genesis 5:24 (Sefaria did not load; web search gave only secondary summaries), the Bullard and Hatton handbook (not found), Lateran V, WCF 32, SDA 26 and jw.org (taken from the writer's sources-checked.md only).

## Findings

### Major

1. **Major. `chapters.json` ch. 3 `traditions`, Reformed and Catholic rows: the sources answer a different question.** The card asks whether the soul is immortal "by its own nature" or "by God's gift". WCF 32.1 ("neither die nor sleep", "an immortal subsistence") is aimed at soul sleep and the state of the dead. The card's own question text says that is asked elsewhere (Revelation 6, 2 Corinthians 5). Lateran V (1513) condemns the view that the soul is mortal. It does not say whether immortality is natural or given. CCC 366 likewise says the soul "does not perish", which is survival, not nature versus gift. Only the Aquinas clause (incorruptible by nature, yet God could cease to sustain it; verified, ad 2) and the Adventist row ("God alone is immortal") hit the question. Fix: keep Aquinas as the Catholic answer and say plainly that Lateran V and CCC 366 settle that the soul survives, not how. For the Reformed row either find a Reformed text on nature or gift, or label WCF 32.1 as answering "do souls survive and stay conscious", and drop it if no row text addresses the card's question. Brief rule: "each row's source must address the question itself".

2. **Major. Same card, Orthodox row.** The row says "The soul is immortal, but as a gift" as if it were the Orthodox position. It rests on one Father, John of Damascus, whom the church honors but who is not a confession. The sharp quote ("not by nature but by grace", 2.3) is about angels, as the row admits. The soul quote (2.12, "of the grace of the Creator") is about the soul's natural qualities in general. The step to "the soul is immortal as a gift" is the writer's inference. Fix: word it as "John of Damascus, a Father the Orthodox honor, writes...", say the angel line is by analogy, and say that Orthodox teaching has no council definition on this point, unless a source can be opened. Note also that the Aquinas "God sustains it" clause makes the Catholic and Orthodox rows closer than the card shows; add a clause saying so.

3. **Major. `variants.json` 2:23, plus the drift audit.** (a) The witness line says "Most manuscripts, as reported by Bullard and Hatton". "As reported" is a banned hedge (STYLE rule 2), and no manuscript is named. `texts/src/Wis.app.json` has no entry for 2:23 beyond an unrelated `επ] επι ℵ`, so the A, ℵ and other witnesses for ἀϊδιότητος cannot be confirmed from anything on disk. I could not open the handbook. (b) The `why` says a copyist dropped a letter "after the same letters in the first word", which is a guess. (c) `.scratch/wis-01-05/drift-audit.md` lists "2:23 image of his own eternity" under "Kept (the Greek forces the wording)", but Swete's text at 2:23 is ἰδιότητος, so the Plain English Translation is not forced and departs from the base. Fix: name witnesses only from a source opened (Rahlfs apparatus or the handbook text), or say "the reading of Rahlfs's text and the KJV; Swete prints ἰδιότητος (B)", and mark the copyist explanation as the writer's suggestion. Also add the Latin ("likeness", the Douay line in the row already covers it) and reword the `p` line.

4. **Major. Enoch (`notes.json` 4:10 and 4:10 `views`; `chapters.json` ch. 4 `deep` "Enoch, the model of the young righteous"; `topics.json` `enoch`).** Three claims are too firm. (a) "Wisdom never names Enoch, but every phrase points to him" and "Here Enoch ... becomes the model" are stated as fact. Wisdom 4:10-14 is widely read of Enoch, but the author does not say so, and the Plain English Translation's "One man" is the writer's reading (the Greek has no "one"; the participles are singular, so "a man" is the most that is forced; see finding 9). Label it the common view, with the verbal parallel to Genesis 5:24 LXX as the evidence. (b) The note says Rashi "reads Genesis 5:24 the same way". Rashi (from Genesis Rabbah) says Enoch was righteous but easily turned to evil, so God took him early. That is similar to Wisdom 4:11, but Rabbah also has rabbis who judge Enoch more harshly. Present Rashi as one Jewish reading, not "Jewish tradition". (c) Rashi's quoted line ("took him away quickly and made him die before his full time", ch. 4 `J` view) and the translation credit (Rosenbaum and Silbermann, read at sefaria.org) were not verified. Sefaria returned an empty page for me, and the web search only gave secondary summaries that agree on the gist. Fix: quote Rashi only from a text opened in the session, or paraphrase without quotation marks and drop the translator credit if it cannot be shown.

### Minor

5. **Minor. Second-hand and thin sources.** `sources.json` and sources-checked show: Bullard and Hatton "as quoted" (variants 2:23, 5:14; two entries cite it for "other manuscripts" reports, again "as reported"); the Orthodox Vespers readings from one parish site (churchmotherofgod.org: it names the Forty Martyrs, George, Boris and Gleb, St Nicholas, but is not a typikon or lectionary); the Pentecost Introit from canticanova.com; JW teaching from one jw.org page. For the Vespers claim, say "at many parishes" or find the Typikon or Menaion text. The JW row's "Ezekiel 18:4" parenthesis should not be read as the JW source quoting it unless the jw.org page does.

6. **Minor. `variants.json` 2:7 and 5:23: witness claims cannot be confirmed from the apparatus.** For 2:7, the Swete note reads `αερος] εσρος A` (OCR-damaged), so "Codex Alexandrinus reads ἔαρος" is an inference from a garbled line. The departure is reasonable (the KJV and Latin point the same way), but say "A (the apparatus line is damaged)" or confirm in another source. For 5:23, `πασαν BᵃᵇℵA` supports the variant entry as written. The Plain English Translation departs from Swete in all three places (2:7, 2:23, 5:23), as the writer reports, and each has a variant entry. I found no other departure from Swete in the 102 verses.

7. **Minor. Missing variants a careful reader should know.** (a) 3:9: the Vulgate and Douay read "grace and peace" for Greek "grace and mercy"; the entry covers only the KJV addition. (b) 2:9: a corrector's margin in B glosses ἀγερωχίας with ὑψηλοφροσύνης (in the Swete apparatus), relevant to "wild partying". (c) 4:12 margin of B on μεταλλεύει (the notes use this but there is no variant entry). (d) 5:15 (Vulgate numbering), 4:14 `λαοὶ ἄλλοι A`. Add (a) and (b) at least.

8. **Minor. Reading and drift.** 2:6 "use the world hungrily" (ἐπισπουδαίως is "eagerly, earnestly") and 5:22 "Seawater will boil with anger" (ἀγανακτήσει is "will be indignant") both overstate. 2:12 renders παιδείας as "training" while 1:5, 3:5 and 3:11 say "discipline", and the term list renders παιδεία as "discipline"; the note at 2:12 gives the reason, but the Plain English Translation should be consistent or the note should say why it differs. 5:7 "until we were stuffed full" smooths a Greek that is awkward ("we were filled with the paths of lawlessness"). None of these is a mistranslation of substance.

9. **Minor. 4:10 "One man pleased God".** The Greek has no numeral. "A man who pleased God" is closer. "One man" also pushes toward the Enoch reading (finding 4).

10. **Minor. Notes: claims from memory or unsourced.**
   - 1:1 note: "The phrase comes from the Greek Psalm 2:10". It echoes it; "comes from" asserts a quotation.
   - 2:24 note: "Some interpreters take a different view: the 'devil' ... could be Cain". No named interpreter or source is given. Name one (checked in this session) or drop the line. The same line is in `topics.json` `satan`.
   - 1:6 note: "Greek writers praised kings and gods as philanthrōpos". No source, and the sentence "Wisdom gives the title to wisdom" is garbled.
   - 1:8 note: Greek poets and Dikē as "daughter of Zeus ... reported wrongdoers" has no source (Hesiod is the usual reference).
   - ch. 1 `J` view: "the rabbis did not preserve it" and ch. 3 `J` view repeat it with no source.
   - ch. 2 `J` view: "belongs to the Judaism of the Maccabean age and after" is unsourced.
   - ch. 3 `deep`: "Justin Martyr's teacher" is the old man of the Dialogue (chs. 3-6), a literary figure; say so.
   - ch. 2 `H` view and note 2:1: the Epicurus link is hedged well, but the "scholars" are unnamed.
   Under STYLE rule 2 each should be sourced from something opened or cut.

11. **Minor. `glosses.json` 5:23.** "whirlwind = tornado" over-narrows λαῖλαψ (a storm wind). The 1:16 gloss "death's team" and the 2:11 gloss "the weak are losers" are slangy and the second echoes the speakers' contempt without marking it. Consider "a violent storm wind" and a neutral wording.

12. **Minor. `soul.json` proposal.** (a) The topic does not duplicate an existing one (checked titles and `aka` in `content/topics/` and all proposals; `intermediate-state` covers soul sleep and is correctly separated, with "soul sleep" an alias only there). (b) The sources list includes `cullmann-immortality`, but sources-checked.md has no entry for Cullmann, and the key is defined only in the 2 Corinthians part. It is not cited in the body. Remove it from `sources` or show that it was opened. (c) "Many scholars therefore say the Hebrew Bible sees a human being as a unity of body and breath" is a consensus claim with no named source. (d) The statement that conditional immortality is taught by "some other Protestants" is fair but unsourced. (e) The soul/immortality aliases ("psyche", "nephesh") overlap with likely word topics; check once the full set is merged.

13. **Minor. Chapter 3 card coverage.** The Lutheran row was dropped for lack of a primary text; this is correct under the rule that an unsourced view is dropped. But Baptist (the 1689 London confession follows the Westminster wording on souls), Anglican, and Methodist are also absent, and the card gives no sign that the list is partial. Add one line: "Other churches, such as the Lutheran, are not shown because no text was opened for them", or add rows from sources opened.

14. **Minor. Fairness and consistency.** The ch. 2 `P` view says some caution that Matthew 27:43 "draws mainly on Psalm 22". This is fair, but ch. 2 `C` says "the church accepts that the passage first speaks of the righteous sufferer" with no source (Augustine is one theologian, not the church). Give a catechism or document or say "many Catholic readers". Chapter 5 `C` and `O` views assert that the Orthodox read "5:15-6:3" at Vespers on the Forty Martyrs. The sources-checked file records 3:1-9 and 4:7-15 for the Vespers checks only; confirm 5:15-6:3 against the same page or cut it.

## Checked and fine

- The Plain English Translation follows the Greek in names, numbers, tenses, who-does-what. Terms agree with the brief: "incorruption" (ἀφθαρσία) never "immortality" for it, ἀθανασία "immortality" at 3:4 and 4:1, "visitation" at 2:20, 3:7, 3:13, 4:15, "the ungodly", singular "the righteous man" in 2:12-20 and 5:1, "child of the Lord" at 2:13 with a servant note.
- Drift toward NRSV, NABRE, RSV, NJB and NETS: I found no long runs that match any of them. The nearest likenesses are short and forced by the Greek: "Love righteousness" (1:1), "God did not make death" (1:13), "righteousness is immortal" (1:15), "image of his own eternity" (2:23, one word differs from the KJV), "their hope is full of immortality" (3:4). The writer's audit lists 47 verses rebuilt; none I tested still has a shared run longer than five words.
- Quotes of the 66 in notes match the site's current Plain English Translation (the build reports no mismatch). Wisdom 3:1-9 and 4:7-15 in the Catholic funeral lectionary were recorded by the writer from liturgyoffice.org; not re-opened by me.
- Audience and setting cards are present for the opening address, the ungodly's speech, the author's answer, chs. 3-4, the confession and the Lord's armor. The audience is named exactly and the date range given as a range. I have no complaint beyond the wording in finding 10.
- Works naming: Sirach, 1 Clement, 2 Maccabees, 4 Maccabees and 1 Enoch are named in the notes as in `works.json` (the build raises no warning).
