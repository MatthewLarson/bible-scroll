## Esther Goes In to the King
5:1 On the third day Esther dressed herself as queen. She stood in the inner court of the king's palace, across from the king's hall. The king was sitting on his royal throne inside the royal hall, across from the doorway of the building.
5:2 When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, she gained his favor. The king held out to Esther the gold scepter that was in his hand, and Esther came near and touched the top of the scepter.
5:3 The king said to her, "What is the matter, Queen Esther? What do you want? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be given to you."
5:4 Esther said, "If the king thinks it good, let the king come today with Haman to the feast that I have made for him."
5:5 The king said, "Hurry and get Haman, to do as Esther has said." The king came with Haman to the feast that Esther had made.
5:6 At the wine feast the king said to Esther, "What are you asking for? It will be given to you. What do you want? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done."
5:7 Esther answered, "Here is what I ask, and what I want.
5:8 If I have won the king's favor, and if the king thinks it good to give me what I ask and to do what I want, let the king and Haman come to the feast that I will make for them. Then tomorrow I will do what the king asks of me."
## Haman's Pole
5:9 That day Haman left cheerful, with a glad heart. Then he saw Mordecai in the king's gate. Mordecai did not get up, and did not shake with fear in front of him, and Haman was full of rage at Mordecai.
5:10 But Haman kept control of himself and went home. He sent for his friends and his wife Zeresh to gather with him.
5:11 Haman went over for them the splendor of his riches and the number of his sons. He told them all that the king had done to make him great, and how he had lifted him above the officials and the king's servants.
5:12 Haman went on, "Not only that. Queen Esther brought no one but me with the king to the feast she gave. She has asked me to come again tomorrow, along with the king.
5:13 Yet all this is not enough for me, any time I see Mordecai the Jew sitting in the king's gate."
5:14 His wife Zeresh and all his friends told him, "Have them make a pole fifty cubits tall. Speak to the king first thing in the morning, and let Mordecai be hanged on it. Then you can go happily to the feast with the king." Haman liked the plan, and he had the pole built.
## The King Cannot Sleep
6:1 That night sleep ran away from the king. He ordered them to bring the book of remembrances, the daily records, and they were read aloud to the king.
6:2 It was found written there that Mordecai had reported Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who kept watch at the entrance. They had tried to raise a hand against King Ahasuerus.
6:3 The king asked, "What honor and what high rank was given to Mordecai for this?" The king's young men who served him said, "Not a thing has been done for him."
6:4 The king said, "Who is out in the court?" Haman had just come into the palace's outer court. He had come to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the pole he had readied for him.
6:5 The king's servants answered, "Look, it is Haman, standing in the court." "Have him come in," said the king.
6:6 Haman came in, and the king said to him, "What is to be done for the man the king takes pleasure in honoring?" Haman told himself, "Who else could the king take pleasure in honoring, more than me?"
6:7 So Haman said to the king, "For the man the king takes pleasure in honoring,
6:8 let them bring clothes fit for a king, which the king has worn, and a horse the king has ridden, whose head has been decked with a royal crown.
6:9 Put the clothes and the horse in the hands of one of the king's officials, one of the nobles. Have them dress the man the king takes pleasure in honoring. Have them set him on the horse and lead him through the open square of the city, and let them shout ahead of him, 'So it is done for the man the king takes pleasure in honoring!'"
6:10 The king told Haman, "Quick! Take the clothes and the horse, just as you said, and do all of it for Mordecai the Jew, the one who sits in the king's gate. Do not drop one word of all you have said."
6:11 So Haman took the clothes and the horse. He put the clothes on Mordecai and set him on the horse to ride through the open square of the city, and he shouted ahead of him, "So it is done for the man the king takes pleasure in honoring!"
6:12 Mordecai returned to the king's gate. Haman hurried away home. He was in mourning, and he had covered his head.
6:13 Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends the whole story of what had happened to him. Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said, "You have started to fall before Mordecai. If he comes from the Jewish people, you will not get the better of him. You are sure to fall before him."
6:14 They were still speaking with him when the king's eunuchs came. They rushed Haman off to the feast that Esther had made.
## The Second Feast
7:1 The king and Haman came to drink with Queen Esther.
7:2 On the second day too, at the wine feast, the king said to Esther, "What are you asking for, Queen Esther? It will be given to you. What do you want? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done."
7:3 Queen Esther answered, "O king, if I have won your favor, and if the king thinks it good, then my life is what I ask: give it to me. My people are what I want.
7:4 For we have been sold, I and my people, to be annihilated, killed and destroyed. If we had only been sold as slaves, men and women, I would have said nothing. That trouble would not have been worth bothering the king."
7:5 King Ahasuerus spoke. He said to Queen Esther, "Who is this man, and where is he, the one who has been so bold as to do this?"
7:6 Esther said, "The adversary, the enemy, is this evil man, Haman!" Haman was struck with terror in front of the king and the queen.
7:7 In his rage the king rose from the wine feast and went out to the palace garden. Haman stayed where he was, asking Queen Esther to save his life, for he could see that the king had settled on doing him harm.
7:8 The king came back from the palace garden to the hall of the wine feast. Haman had fallen across the couch Esther was on. The king said, "Would he even force himself on the queen, with me here in the house?" The words were hardly out of the king's mouth when Haman's face was covered.
7:9 Harbona, one of the eunuchs who stood before the king, said, "There is also the pole Haman made for Mordecai, the man whose words did the king good. It stands at Haman's house, fifty cubits tall." The king said, "Hang him on it."
7:10 So they hanged Haman on the pole he had readied for Mordecai, and the king calmed down from his rage.
## Esther Pleads for Her People
8:1 On that day King Ahasuerus handed over to Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. Mordecai now came in before the king, for Esther had made known to the king what Mordecai was to her.
8:2 The king slipped off the signet ring he had taken back from Haman and gave it to Mordecai. Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman's house.
8:3 Esther spoke before the king a second time. She threw herself down at his feet in tears. She pleaded for his mercy, that he would put away the evil of Haman the Agagite and the plot he had plotted against the Jews.
8:4 The king stretched out the gold scepter toward Esther. She got up and stood in front of the king.
8:5 She said, "If the king thinks it good, and if I have won his favor, if the king thinks the thing is right, and if I am good in his eyes, then let something be written to turn back the letters. Those letters were the plot of Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews throughout the king's provinces.
8:6 How could I stand to watch the evil that will find my people? How could I stand to watch my kin destroyed?"
8:7 King Ahasuerus answered Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew, "Haman's house I have given to Esther, as you see. And he has been hanged on the pole, because he raised his hand against the Jews.
8:8 So you write whatever seems good to you about the Jews. Write it in the king's name and seal it with the king's signet ring. What is written in the king's name and sealed with his ring no one can turn back."
## Mordecai's Letter
8:9 So the king's scribes were called in at that time. It was the twenty-third day of the third month, Sivan. They wrote down all that Mordecai ordered. It went to the Jews, and to the satraps, the governors and the officials of the provinces, the 127 provinces from India all the way to Cush. It went province by province in its writing and people by people in its tongue, and to the Jews in their writing and their tongue.
8:10 Mordecai wrote the letters in King Ahasuerus's name and stamped them with the king's signet ring. He sent them by runners on horses. They rode fast steeds that served the crown, foals of the mares.
8:11 In these letters the king allowed the Jews in each city to gather and to stand up for their lives. They could annihilate, kill and destroy every army of any people or province that comes against them, little children and women, and their property could be taken as plunder.
8:12 They could do it on a single day in every province of King Ahasuerus, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar.
8:13 A copy of the text was to be given out as law in each province and shown openly to all the peoples. The Jews were to be ready for that day, to get vengeance from their enemies.
8:14 The runners on the swift royal horses set out in haste, rushed along by the king's word. And the law was given out in the fortress at Susa.
8:15 Mordecai came out from the king wearing royal clothes of blue-violet and white, with a great crown of gold and a cloak of fine linen and purple. The city of Susa cheered and was glad.
8:16 For the Jews there was light and joy, gladness and honor.
8:17 Wherever the king's word and his law came, in each province and each city, the Jews had joy and gladness, with feasting and a holiday. Many people from the peoples of the land made themselves Jews, since dread of the Jews had come down on them.
## The Day Turned Upside Down
9:1 The twelfth month is Adar. On its thirteenth day the time came for the king's word and his law to be put into effect. The enemies of the Jews had hoped that on this day they would rule over them. But it was turned upside down. The Jews themselves ruled over those who hated them.
9:2 In their cities, in every province of King Ahasuerus, the Jews came together to raise a hand against anyone who was looking to do them harm. Nobody held his ground before them, since dread of them had come down on all the peoples.
9:3 All the officials of the provinces, along with the satraps, the governors and the men who carried out the king's business, were backing the Jews, because dread of Mordecai had come down on them.
9:4 Mordecai was a great man in the king's palace. Word about him was going out to every province, as the man Mordecai kept on growing greater.
9:5 So the Jews cut down all their enemies. It was a blow of the sword, of slaughter and destruction. They treated those who hated them however they wanted.
9:6 In the fortress at Susa the Jews killed five hundred men, destroying them.
9:7 And they killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,
9:8 Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,
9:9 Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha,
9:10 the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews. But they kept their hands off the plunder.
## A Second Day in Susa
9:11 On that day the count of the dead in the fortress at Susa came before the king.
9:12 The king said to Queen Esther, "In the fortress at Susa the Jews have killed five hundred men, destroying them, and the ten sons of Haman. And in the rest of the king's provinces, what have they done! Now what are you asking for? It will be given to you. What more do you want? It will be done."
9:13 Esther said, "If the king thinks it good, let the Jews in Susa be given tomorrow as well, to do what today's law allows. And let Haman's ten sons be hung on the pole."
9:14 The king said it should be done that way. The law was given out in Susa, and Haman's ten sons were hung up.
9:15 On the fourteenth of the month of Adar the Jews in Susa came together once more, and in Susa they killed three hundred men. But they kept their hands off the plunder.
9:16 The other Jews, in the king's provinces, also came together and stood up for their lives. They had rest from their enemies. Of those who hated them they killed seventy-five thousand. But they kept their hands off the plunder.
## Rest and Feasting
9:17 All this was done on the thirteenth of the month of Adar. They rested on its fourteenth day and turned it into a day of feasting and joy.
9:18 The Jews in Susa, though, came together on both the thirteenth and the fourteenth. They rested on the fifteenth and turned it into a day of feasting and joy.
9:19 This is why the village Jews, who live in the towns of the open country, keep the fourteenth of the month of Adar with joy and feasting. It is a holiday, a day for sending portions of food to one another.
## Mordecai's Letter About Purim
9:20 Mordecai wrote these things down. He sent letters out to the Jews in every province of King Ahasuerus, those near and those far away.
9:21 He made it their duty to keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and its fifteenth day, year after year.
9:22 These were the days when the Jews found rest from their enemies. It was the month that changed for them, from grief to joy and from mourning to a holiday. So they were to make these days of feasting and joy, sending portions of food to one another and presents to the needy.
9:23 So the Jews took on as their own what they had started doing and what Mordecai had written them.
9:24 This was because Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted to destroy the Jews. He had cast Pur, that is, the lot, to rout them in panic and destroy them.
9:25 But when the matter came before the king, he gave orders in writing: the evil plot Haman had plotted against the Jews must come back on his own head, and he and his sons must be hung on the pole.
9:26 So these days were named Purim, from the name Pur. And so, because of every word of this letter, and what they had seen in this matter, and what had come upon them,
9:27 the Jews set it up and took it on as binding for themselves, for their children after them and for everyone who would join them. It would not lapse: they would keep these two days as written, at their set time, every year.
9:28 These days were to be remembered and kept generation after generation, clan by clan, province by province, city by city. These days of Purim must not drop away from among the Jews, and their memory must not come to an end among their children.
## Esther's Second Letter
9:29 Queen Esther, Abihail's daughter, wrote with Mordecai the Jew, using all her authority, to make this second letter about Purim binding.
9:30 He sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of Ahasuerus's kingdom, with words of peace and truth,
9:31 to make these days of Purim stand at their set times. Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had laid this on them, just as the Jews had laid on themselves and their children the rules about the fasts and their cries for help.
9:32 Esther's word made these Purim rules binding, and it was written in the book.
## The Greatness of Mordecai
10:1 King Ahasuerus put a tax on the land and on the islands of the sea.
10:2 All the deeds of his power and strength, and the full story of Mordecai's greatness, to which the king raised him, are written in the book of the daily records of the kings of Media and Persia, are they not?
10:3 For Mordecai the Jew was next in rank after King Ahasuerus. He was a great man to the Jews and was welcomed by the crowd of his brothers. He looked out for his people's good and spoke peace to all his offspring.
