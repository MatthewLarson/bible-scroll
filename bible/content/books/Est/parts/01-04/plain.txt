## The king's great feasts
1:1 These events took place in the time of Ahasuerus. This was the Ahasuerus who was king from India all the way to Cush, over 127 provinces.
1:2 At that time King Ahasuerus was ruling from the throne of his kingdom in the fortress at Susa.
1:3 In his third year as king, he held a feast for all his officials and his servants. The forces of Persia and Media, the nobles, and the officials of the provinces were all there before him.
1:4 He put the wealth of his glorious kingdom on show, and the dazzling honor of his greatness, for many days: 180 days in all.
1:5 After those days ended, the king gave another feast, for seven days, to everyone who was in the fortress at Susa, great and small alike. He held it in the courtyard of the palace garden.
1:6 White and blue-violet cotton cloth hung there, held by cords of fine linen and purple wool that ran through silver rings and up marble columns. Couches of gold and silver stood on a paved floor of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl and colored stone.
1:7 They poured the drinks into gold cups, and no two cups were alike. Royal wine flowed freely, as the king's hand allowed.
1:8 The drinking went by the law: no one was to be pushed. The king had laid this down for every head steward in his palace: do as each man pleases.
1:9 Vashti the queen held a feast for the women as well, in the royal house that belonged to King Ahasuerus.
## Queen Vashti refuses
1:10 On the seventh day, when wine had put the king in a happy mood, he spoke to Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar and Carcas. These were the seven eunuchs who waited on King Ahasuerus in person.
1:11 They were to bring Queen Vashti to the king in her royal crown, so that he could let the peoples and the officials see her beauty, because she was a striking woman.
1:12 Queen Vashti, though, would not come at the king's word that the eunuchs carried to her. The king grew furious, and rage flared up inside him.
1:13 So the king spoke with the wise men, who were skilled in reading the times. That was the way the king dealt with any matter: he laid it before all who knew law and judgment.
1:14 His inner circle was Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media. They had the right to see the king face to face, and they held the first seats in the kingdom.
1:15 He asked, "What does the law say we must do with Queen Vashti? She did not carry out King Ahasuerus's command, which came to her by the eunuchs."
1:16 Memucan spoke up before the king and the officials. "It is not only the king that Queen Vashti has wronged, but every official, and the peoples in every province King Ahasuerus rules.
1:17 The queen's deed will get out to all the women, and it will make their husbands look small in their eyes. They will say, 'King Ahasuerus said to bring Queen Vashti to him, and she did not come.'
1:18 Today, even, the great ladies of Persia and Media who hear about the queen will start talking this way to all the king's officials. There will be more than enough scorn and anger.
1:19 If the king thinks it good, let a royal word go out from him, and let it be recorded in the laws of Persia and Media, never to be undone. It shall say that Vashti may not come before King Ahasuerus again. Then let the king give her place as queen to a neighbor of hers who is better than she.
1:20 Once people hear the king's ruling in every part of his kingdom, great as it is, every wife will honor her husband, high or low."
1:21 The king and the officials thought the plan was good, so the king followed Memucan's word.
1:22 He sent letters out to all the provinces of the king, province by province in its writing and people by people in its tongue. They said that every man was to rule his own household and to speak his own people's language.
## The search for a new queen
2:1 Some time after this, King Ahasuerus calmed down from his anger. Then he thought of Vashti, of what she had done, and of the ruling made against her.
2:2 The young men who waited on the king said, "Someone should look for young women for the king, virgins who are good-looking.
2:3 The king should name agents in each province of his kingdom. They can bring together every good-looking young virgin to the fortress at Susa, to the house for the women. There Hegai, the king's eunuch who keeps the women, will take charge of them and give them their beauty ointments.
2:4 The girl the king likes best can then reign in Vashti's place." The king liked the proposal, and that is what he did.
## Mordecai and Esther
2:5 In the fortress at Susa lived a Jewish man whose name was Mordecai. He was the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, a man of Benjamin,
2:6 who had been exiled from Jerusalem with the group of exiles who went with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon sent into exile.
2:7 He was the guardian of Hadassah, also called Esther, the daughter of his uncle, since she had lost both her father and her mother. The young woman was shapely and good-looking. After her parents died, Mordecai took her in to be his daughter.
2:8 So the king's word and law went out and were heard, and many girls were brought together to the fortress at Susa and handed over to Hegai. Esther was one of those taken into the king's house, into the hands of Hegai, keeper of the women.
2:9 Hegai liked the young woman, and she won his loyal love. He hurried to give her the things for her beauty care and her share of food. He also gave her seven maids picked from the king's house, and he moved her and her maids into the best rooms of the women's house.
2:10 Esther did not let anyone know which people or family she came from. Mordecai had told her to keep it to herself.
2:11 Day after day Mordecai paced up and down outside the court of the women's house. He wanted to know whether Esther was well and how they were treating her.
## Esther becomes queen
2:12 When each girl's turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus, she had first spent twelve months under the law for the women. That was the full time for their beauty care: six months of myrrh oil, then six months of perfumes and the creams women use.
2:13 This is the way each girl went in to the king. Anything she named, she could take along with her when she went from the women's house to the king's house.
2:14 She went in at evening and came back at morning, this time to the second women's house, under Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch who kept the concubines. After that she did not go to the king any more, unless he wanted her and sent for her by name.
2:15 Then it was Esther's turn to go in to the king. She was the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle, and Mordecai had taken her for his daughter. She asked for nothing except what Hegai said she should take. He was the king's eunuch, the keeper of the women. Everyone who saw Esther was taken with her.
2:16 So Esther was brought to King Ahasuerus at his royal house. It was the tenth month, Tebeth, in the seventh year he was king.
2:17 The king loved Esther above all the women. More than any of the virgins, she gained his favor and his loyal love. He placed a royal crown on her head and made her queen in Vashti's place.
2:18 The king threw a great feast for all his officials and servants, a feast in Esther's honor. He gave the provinces a release and handed out gifts as the king's hand allowed.
## Mordecai uncovers a plot
2:19 The virgins were brought together a second time. Mordecai was then sitting in the king's gate.
2:20 Esther was still keeping quiet about her family and her people, as Mordecai had instructed her. She carried out Mordecai's word the same way she had when she was in his care.
2:21 During that time, with Mordecai sitting in the king's gate, two of the king's eunuchs who kept watch at the entrance, Bigthan and Teresh, became angry. They looked for a way to raise a hand against King Ahasuerus.
2:22 The plan became known to Mordecai, and he passed it on to Esther the queen. Esther spoke to the king about it in Mordecai's name.
2:23 When the matter was investigated, it proved true, and the two of them were hanged on a pole. It was recorded in the book of the daily records, in front of the king.
## Haman's rise and Mordecai's refusal
3:1 Some time later, King Ahasuerus made Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite a great man. He lifted him high and gave him a seat above every other official around him.
3:2 The king's servants in the king's gate all went down on their knees and bowed low to Haman, because the king had given that order about him. Mordecai, however, did not go down on his knees, and he did not bow.
3:3 So the king's servants in the king's gate asked Mordecai, "Why are you going against what the king commanded?"
3:4 Day after day they spoke to him, but he would not listen to them. So they reported it to Haman, to find out if Mordecai's reasons would hold up. He had explained to them that he was a Jew.
3:5 Haman saw it: Mordecai would not kneel to him or bow down. Rage filled Haman.
3:6 To raise a hand against Mordecai alone seemed too small to him, since they had told him what people Mordecai belonged to. Haman set out to annihilate every Jew in Ahasuerus's entire kingdom, all of Mordecai's people.
## The lot and the decree
3:7 In the first month, Nisan, in King Ahasuerus's twelfth year, Pur, that is, the lot, was thrown before Haman, day by day and month by month, down to the twelfth month, the month of Adar.
3:8 Haman told King Ahasuerus, "One people lives scattered here and there, kept apart, among the peoples in every province of your kingdom. Their laws are unlike any other people's, and the king's laws they do not obey. The king gains nothing by leaving them be.
3:9 If the king thinks it good, let an order be written to destroy them. I will weigh out 10,000 talents of silver and hand it to the men in charge of the work, to bring into the king's treasuries."
3:10 The king slipped the signet ring off his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.
3:11 "The silver is yours," the king told Haman, "and so is the people. Do with them whatever looks good to you."
3:12 The king's scribes were called in during the first month, on its thirteenth day. Everything Haman ordered was put in writing for the king's satraps, for the governors over each province and for the officials of each people, province by province in its writing and people by people in its tongue. It went out over King Ahasuerus's name, stamped with the king's signet ring.
3:13 Runners carried the letters to every province of the king. The orders were to annihilate, kill and destroy every Jew, from the young to the old, little children and women, all in one day: the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar. Their property was to be taken as plunder.
3:14 The text was to be given out as law in each province, with a copy shown openly to all the peoples, so they would be prepared when the day came.
3:15 At the king's word the runners hurried out, and the law was given out in the fortress at Susa. Then the king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city of Susa was in turmoil.
## Mordecai mourns
4:1 Mordecai found out everything that had happened. He ripped his clothes, dressed himself in sackcloth and ashes, and went out through the city, letting out a loud, bitter cry.
4:2 He came right up to the king's gate, but no further, since a man in sackcloth could not go in through the king's gate.
4:3 Throughout the provinces, in every place the king's word and law arrived, the Jews mourned deeply. They fasted, wept and cried aloud, and many made their beds on sackcloth and ashes.
4:4 Esther's maids and eunuchs came and told her about it, and the queen was in anguish. She sent clothes for Mordecai to put on, so that he would take the sackcloth off, but he refused them.
4:5 Esther sent for Hathach, a eunuch of the king's whom the king had put at her service. She gave him orders about Mordecai: find out what this is about and what lies behind it.
4:6 Hathach went out to meet Mordecai in the city square that lay before the king's gate.
4:7 Mordecai told him all that had come upon him. He also gave him the precise figure of the silver Haman had said he would weigh out to the king's treasuries for the Jews, to have them destroyed.
4:8 He handed Hathach a copy of the written law that had been given out in Susa to annihilate them, so he could show it to Esther and tell her about it. He was to order her to go in to the king, to plead for his grace and make her request to him for her people.
4:9 Hathach came and gave Esther Mordecai's message.
## Esther decides
4:10 Esther answered Hathach and sent him back to Mordecai with orders:
4:11 "Everyone knows it, from the king's servants to the people of his provinces: if a man or a woman goes in to the king in the inner court uninvited, one law applies, and that is death. The only exception is the one the king holds the gold scepter out to. That person lives. As for me, no one has called me in to the king for thirty days now."
4:12 Esther's words were reported to Mordecai.
4:13 Mordecai told them to take this answer back to Esther: "Don't tell yourself that living in the king's house will let you escape when the rest of the Jews do not.
4:14 For if you hold your tongue completely at this time, relief and rescue will spring up for the Jews from somewhere else, while you and the house of your father perish. And who knows? Perhaps it was for a time like this that you came to royal power."
4:15 Esther had this answer sent back to Mordecai:
4:16 "Go and call together every Jew who can be found in Susa, and fast on my behalf. For three days, night and day, eat nothing and drink nothing. I will fast the same way, I and my maids. After that I will go in to the king, which the law does not allow. And if I perish, I perish."
4:17 Mordecai went his way and carried out all that Esther had commanded him.
