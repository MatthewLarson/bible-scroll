# Hostile review: 1 Timothy (1Ti), one part, flat folder

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-29. Files read: plain.txt, notes.json (100), variants.json (13), glosses.json, chapters.json (6 chapters, 19 settings, 7 traditions cards), book.json, topics.json (32), sources.json, and proposals/nt/topics/pastoral-letters.json, timothy.json, 1-timothy.json, proposals/nt/terms.json (epiphaneia, eusebeia, pistos ho logos). Checked against texts/orig/1Ti.json (SBL and Byzantine), the Plain English Translation of every book quoted, and sources opened this session (listed at the end). `node tools/build.js --check 1Ti`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly honest book. The Plain English Translation follows the Greek well, the quotes of other books' Plain English Translation are exact (Gen 2:7, 2:22, 3:13; 2 Cor 11:3; 1 Cor 6:9, 9:9; Luke 10:7; Exod 21:16; Deut 19:15; Acts 5:34, 8:3, 13:15, 16:1-3, 19:19, 20:30; Gal 1:13; 1 Cor 5:5; Rom 1:4; and the rest I tested all match), "men who lie with males" matches 1 Cor 6:9, and the Chrysostom quotations check out (Homilies 7, 9, 10, 11). I found no invented source or fragment, so nothing is critical. The weak points are (a) coverage and sourcing of the traditions cards: one card the notes point to does not exist, three of seven cards have missing or unsourced traditions (the writer admitted two of these gaps), and some statements are put in a tradition's mouth without its own document; (b) the authorship topic names almost no modern scholars and gives the defenders' side only in outline; (c) one real softening in the Plain English Translation (5:11); (d) several variants a reader will meet in the KJV are missing; (e) a few claims stated as settled that are contested (the Decalogue shape of 1:9-10, Polycarp's dependence, 1 Cor 7:21), and a few from memory. Count: 0 critical, 13 major, 17 minor.

## Major findings

1. **Major. chapters.json ch. 3 traditions; notes.json 3:1 (entry "Overseer, the word behind bishop"). The card the note points to does not exist, and the brief's church-order card is missing.** The 3:1 note ends "Whether bishops are a separate order above elders is the dividing question of church order: see the card." Chapter 3 has cards on celibacy (3:2), deacons (3:11) and 3:15, but none on 3:1-7 (episcopal, presbyterian, congregational), which brief-1ti.md listed first for chapter 3. The brief asked for 8 to 10 cards; there are 7, and 5:23 (wine) and 6:9-10 (wealth) are also absent (the wine note sends the reader to the topic page, which is acceptable, but the money passage has no card or pointer). Fix: add a church-order card (`top: elders`) with Catholic (Lumen Gentium 20-21, CCC 1554-1561), Orthodox, Anglican (Ordinal/Article 36), Lutheran, Reformed (Presbyterian: ruling and teaching elders), Baptist and congregational views, each from its own document opened this session; or remove "see the card" from note 43 and from the elders topic text.

2. **Major. chapters.json ch. 2, card "one mediator (2:5)"; topics.json communion-of-saints.** The writer admitted Reformed and Baptist are missing, and the card still carries the 3:11-style gap. Further: (a) the Orthodox entry has no Orthodox source. Chrysostom's comment (verified, Homily 7: "a mediator ought to have communion with both parties") is about Christ's two natures, not the saints, and the sentence "The Orthodox Church asks the saints and the Theotokos to pray for the faithful" carries only the Chrysostom key. (b) The topic text "Lutheran and Reformed churches cite the verse against calling on the saints (Augsburg Confession 21)" credits the Reformed with a Lutheran document. I confirmed Augsburg Confession 21 does say Scripture does not teach calling on the saints and sets out the one Christ as Mediator (Concordia text, bookofconcord.cph.org), but it is Lutheran. Fix: add Reformed (Heidelberg Catechism Q&A 30, or Westminster Confession 21.2 and 21.4: check the wording this session) and Baptist (BFM 2000) entries, give the Orthodox entry its own document, and fix the topic sentence. The Lutheran entry should also mention the Apology's grant that saints pray for the church in general (Apology 21; the search summary I read says it grants that angels pray for us, and the saints point needs checking in the text itself), so it is not read as denying all heavenly prayer.

3. **Major. chapters.json ch. 2, women-in-ministry card (2:11-15).** The most sensitive card, with these problems:
   - Orthodox: "Only men are ordained to the priesthood" has no source; the only key is Chrysostom, a Father of the 4th century. Give an Orthodox document or teacher (checked this session), or say it is the practice and name the source.
   - Anglican is missing, though brief-1ti.md listed it. Anglican provinces differ sharply (priests since 1994 in England, bishops since 2015; others do not ordain women): add with sources.
   - Pentecostal: "The Assemblies of God has ordained women since 1927." I could not confirm 1927 anywhere. What I found: women were credentialed from the 1914 founding (Christian History and Christianity Today say 1914; one says as evangelists and missionaries, not elders). The cited source is the AG position paper (1990, revised 2010); the date must come from it or be removed.
   - Baptist: "Other Baptist bodies ordain women" is unsourced and unnamed. Name one (for example American Baptist Churches USA or the Baptist Union of Great Britain) with its own document, or cut the sentence.
   - Reformed: "Divided" but only the PC(USA) is cited for the women-ordaining side. The source key `pcusa-1956` says "Presbyterian Church in the U.S.A." (the 1956 church); the card says "The Presbyterian Church (USA)", which is the 1983 merged body. Use the 1956 name. Add a confessional conservative body (for example PCA, which does not ordain women) so both Reformed answers are in their own documents.
   - Evangelical: the Danvers Statement is not a church; it is placed beside churches as if it were a tradition. Affirmation 6 is correctly quoted (verified at cbmw.org). Add an egalitarian statement in its own words (for example the CBE statement) so the egalitarian side is not only paraphrased.
   - Wesley's note 2:12 "By public teaching" is quoted correctly (Bible Hub search extract), but the same Wesley note on 2:13 (the woman "originally the inferior... more easily deceived") is not mentioned anywhere. Methodist-as-egalitarian is the card's impression; Wesley's own reasoning is partly the opposite. Either add the sentence or say that Wesley's gloss narrows 2:12 to public teaching while holding the creation argument.

4. **Major. chapters.json ch. 3, card on 3:15.** The Reformed entry ends "The church holds up the truth as a pillar holds a roof it did not make." That sentence is the writer's own, set under a Reformed label next to a Westminster Shorter Catechism quote (Q. 2) that does not mention the church or 3:15. The Protestant answer is thereby put in the reader's mouth, not in the church's own words. Replace with a Reformed document that speaks of the church and the truth (Westminster Confession 1.10 and 25, or Belgic Confession art. 29: check this session). Also missing: Orthodox (the tradition that leans most on 3:15) and Lutheran. The topic scripture-and-tradition repeats "Protestants stress that the church holds up a truth it does not make" without a source.

5. **Major. chapters.json ch. 4, ordination card (4:14).** Catholic (CCC 1556), Lutheran (Apology 13.11-12) and Anglican (Article 25) only. Orthodox (ordination as a mystery), Reformed and Baptist, the three views that most differ on whether it is a sacrament, are missing. The Anglican entry quotes only Article 25's negative list; it leaves out that the Anglican Ordinal ordains by prayer and laying on of hands and that Anglo-Catholics hold it sacramental; "not to be counted for Sacraments of the Gospel" alone presents a one-sided picture. The Catholic quote and the claim that CCC 1556 cites 1 Tim 4:14 could not be confirmed from a page I opened (the vatican.va page returned did not contain 1556); the search extract said the paragraph describes the passing on of the Spirit by laying on of hands and ties it to 1 Tim 4:14 only through a cross-reference list. Verify the exact footnote.

6. **Major. plain.txt 5:11 (and note 74).** "When their desires grow restless against Christ." The Greek is *katastrēniasōsin tou Christou*; the site's own word list glosses it "they may grow wanton against" (texts/orig/1Ti.json). The verb is about sensuous or wanton impulse; "restless" drops the sexual sense that 5:11-14 turns on (the widows "want to marry"), and softens the verse. The note then says the word "suggests pulling away from a rein" and the short text says it "was used of an animal pulling at the reins": I found no source for that image in anything I opened, and it is a checkable lexical claim from memory (STYLE rule 2). Fix: "when their desires pull them away from Christ" or "when sensual desire draws them away from Christ" (check BDAG/LSJ for the sense in this session), and remove or source the rein image.

7. **Major. proposals/nt/topics/pastoral-letters.json (authorship).** The brief required the critical majority and the traditional defence fairly, with scholars named and checked. The topic names Schleiermacher and nobody else in the modern debate: no Harrison, Johnson, Dibelius-Conzelmann, Marshall, Mounce, Towner, Collins, or Quinn-Wacker. (Harrison and Johnson are on the book page.) The critical case is set out with its reasons; the defence is given as "Defenders answer each point" without the answers (the stylometric objections to Harrison, the genre and secretary arguments, the personal details and itinerary, the second imprisonment evidence in 2 Tim 4:16-17 and 1 Clement 5). Missing altogether: ancient pseudonymity and its ethics (the brief's "views, fairly"; book.json has two sentences), the date range for each side, and Polycarp as used in finding 11. The traditional view also lacks a named modern defender. Fix: name and source two or three scholars per side from works opened this session, state the defenders' replies, add a section on pseudonymity. The 1 Clement 5 quote "the extreme limit of the west" is exact in the translation I opened (newadvent.org/fathers/1010); P46 and Kenyon are accurate (Kenyon's leaf count and Duff, NTS 44, 1998, confirmed in search summaries; the writer's "others answer that the scribe could have added leaves" matches Duff).

8. **Major. variants.json: missing entries.** The brief required 1:4 (the KJV "godly edifying" is a visible difference: the TR in the site's word list reads *oikonomian*, but the Byzantine text reads *zētēseis* "controversies" against *ekzētēseis* "speculations", texts/auto/1Ti.json and texts/orig). None of the following is in variants.json although each shows in the KJV or auto list and changes the English: 1:1 (Byzantine "Lord Jesus Christ our hope"); 1:4 (*ekzētēseis* / *zētēseis*); 2:14 (*exapatētheisa* / Byzantine *apatētheisa*: the Plain English Translation's "fooled through and through" translates the prefix, which the variant removes, so the reader should be told); 5:21 (Byzantine "Lord Jesus Christ"); 6:5 (*diaparatribai*: *paradiatribai*: KJV "perverse disputings"); 6:11 (*praupathian* against *praotēta*; the Plain English Translation's "gentle spirit" follows the first); 6:13 (*zōogonountos* against *zōopoiountos*, KJV "quickeneth"). All except 6:5 are in the auto list. Add, or say in the report why they are left out. Also 3:16: the Wikipedia article the writer relied on (summary read this session; I could not read the apparatus itself) records a reading *hō* (uncial 061) that the entry omits, and the entry lists "Gregory" without saying which Gregory (the article says Gregory of Nyssa).

9. **Major. notes.json 1:9 note and chapters.json ch. 1 "The vice list and the Ten Commandments"; topics ten-commandments, law-of-moses, same-sex-relations.** The note says the list "is not random... follow[s]... the second half of the Ten Commandments in order" and ends "The point is that the law's lawful use is the one God gave at Sinai." The chapter page says the match is "close" and gives a one-to-one mapping. This is one scholarly proposal (the brief said "views"), stated as fact in four places with no named scholar and no source key. The mapping is also strained at points: "men who lie with males" and "those who commit sexual sin" both go to the seventh, "slave dealers" to the eighth only by Exod 21:16, and coveting is "dealt with in 6:6-10", which is an addition. The same-sex topic then builds a placement argument on it. Fix: say "many commentators see..." and name two or three from books opened this session, state the objection, and keep the placement of *arsenokoitai* neutral as note 12 already does.

10. **Major. notes.json 6:1 (entry "Slaves in the church").** "Paul does not call for an end to slavery here, unlike 1 Corinthians 7:21, where he tells a slave to take freedom if he can." The site's own note on 1 Cor 7:21 (1Co parts/05-08) says the Greek *mallon chrēsai* ("rather use") has no object and gives two readings, including "stay a slave even if you could be free". The 1Ti note states one reading as fact and uses it to contrast the two letters. Fix: "where, on one reading of the Greek, he tells a slave to take freedom if he can (the note there gives the other reading)". Also "A large part of the people in Roman cities were slaves" has no source.

11. **Major. notes.json 6:7 (Polycarp) and book.json "Traditional view".** "Polycarp... says 'the love of money is the root of all evils'... which shows the letter was known by then." The quotation is accurate in the older English translation (Polycarp 4:1), but the inference is given as settled. A search this session found that most recent scholars rate dependence on 1 Timothy as probable or near certain, but the direction has also been argued the other way (1 Timothy depends on Polycarp, or the two share common sayings), and the pastoral-letters topic uses the echo as evidence for early authorship. Fix: "Polycarp's letter has very close wording; most scholars think he used 1 Timothy; others think the sayings were in circulation or that the Pastorals used Polycarp". The 1Ti book.json "echoes" should become "has wording close to".

12. **Major. plain.txt 2:12 and note 37 (authentein).** The Plain English Translation says "hold authority over a man", and the note says this "keeps the main sense without settling which kind." It does not: "hold authority" is the neutral-positive reading (Knight's), and a reader of the Plain English Translation only gets that sense; the negative readings ("domineer", "assume authority", "usurp") are offered only in the note. Fix with a gloss on "hold authority" giving the other options, or choose the rendering that honestly covers both ("take authority", "exercise authority over") and say so. The note's papyrus claim is unchecked: "A papyrus letter of 27 BC (BGU 1208)... a case George Knight (1984) called the crux of the question." A search this session returned nothing on BGU 1208 and *authentein*, so I cannot confirm the date, the meaning given ("got his own way with another") or that Knight called it the crux. The brief also asked for the Wolters studies; they are not cited, though they are the main counter to Knight. Remove the BGU sentence or verify it against the papyrus text (papyri.info) and the Knight and Baldwin articles.

13. **Major. chapters.json ch. 2, election card (2:4).** (a) The Catholic entry quotes CCC 1037 ("God predestines no one to go to hell"), which answers a different question than "why are not all saved?" and does not mention 2:4; it should quote what the Catechism says on this verse (CCC 74, 1058, or 605; check this session). (b) The Reformed entry is Calvin's commentary plus Augustine, not a Reformed confession (Westminster Confession 3, Canons of Dort): so the Reformed view is given by one man, while Catholic and Baptist are given by documents. (c) Lutheran is absent (Formula of Concord, art. 11), as is the Orthodox church's own text (the Orthodox entry is again only Chrysostom). (d) The Baptist entry quotes BFM article V ("consistent with the free agency of man"), which I confirmed through a search summary, but "Divided between Calvinist and non-Calvinist readings" has no source. The Calvin quote "relates to classes of men, and not to individual persons" is reported in the CCEL volume (ccel.org/c/calvin/comment3/comm_vol43/htm/iii.iv.htm) but I could not open that page's text this session, so I record it as plausible, not verified.

## Minor findings

14. **Minor. notes.json 2:2 (entry "Godliness").** "Ten times in 1 Timothy alone" is wrong. texts/orig/1Ti.json has *eusebeia* at 2:2, 3:16, 4:7, 4:8, 6:3, 6:5, 6:6, 6:11 (eight) plus the verb *eusebein* at 5:4: nine. The text of the note lists the same eight. Change to "eight times (nine with the verb in 5:4)".

15. **Minor. notes.json 3:11 (entry "Deacons' wives, or women deacons?").** "The list matches the deacons' list point for point" is overstated: 3:11 has four items (serious, not slanderous, clear-headed, trustworthy in everything) against the deacons' four (serious, not double-tongued, not devoted to wine, not chasing shameful profit); only "serious" and the sobriety item match. Say "the same kind of list". Pliny's "two slave women, who were called ministrae" (Letters 10.96.8) is as the standard text reads, but I did not open it; and "probably the Latin for female deacons" is an unsourced opinion from the writer (Latin *ministra* is an ordinary word for a female attendant; its link to the church office is disputed). Mark as a view or source it. The same goes for the topic `deacons`.

16. **Minor. notes.json 5:13.** "ta periergа" contains a Cyrillic "а" (U+0430) in the last letter. Retype in Latin.

17. **Minor. Claims from memory with no source opened (STYLE rule 2).** The following cannot be confirmed from anything cited in the book and should be sourced or cut: note 15 ("the blessed God... a phrase Greek writers used of the gods' untroubled life"); note 26 (*enteuxis* "the word used for a request presented to a king"); note 53 ("readers in Ephesus had the huge columns of the temple of Artemis before their eyes" as a reason for "pillar"); note 77 ("later Reformed church order took as teaching elders beside ruling elders"); note 82 (Thomas Welch, a Methodist, pasteurized grape juice in 1869 for the Lord's Supper; repeated in the `wine` topic; I did not verify this); note 88 ("Greek moralists had similar proverbs"); note 43 (Ignatius "about AD 110"); chapters 1:H and 4:H general claims about the period; note 6:1 (see 10). Also the `wealth` topic's "the Bible's longest passage on money in the letters" is a claim nobody could check; cut it.

18. **Minor. variants.json, several `b` fields.** The entries say "Witness lists as gathered from the published apparatus on the Wikipedia page..." (2:7, 3:1, 3:16, 4:10, 6:7, 6:21). Wikipedia is a secondary source and the phrase advertises it inside the content. Under brief-1ti.md the witnesses had to be "verified this session" against editions. A summary-level check this session found no contradiction in the main lists for 3:16, 4:10 and 6:21, except the omitted 061 reading (see 8). But the 6:21 entry's claim about plural against singular "you" has no witness list behind it (the Wikipedia article's variant at that verse is only "Amen"); the writer says it comes "from the editions in this site's word list", which shows the reading in the editions but not the manuscripts. Either name only the editions for that variant or check NA28/UBS. Replace the Wikipedia attributions with the apparatus actually consulted, or say in plain words that the lists come from Wikipedia and have not been checked against NA28 or ECM.

19. **Minor. plain.txt: echoes of the NIV/ESV (checked in my head only).** The structure "Be a model for the believers instead: in what you say, in how you live, in love, in faith, in purity" (4:12) follows the common NIV/ESV arrangement of speech, conduct, love, faith, purity. "A root of every kind of evil" (6:10) and "rest their hope on riches, which are so unsure" (6:17) are close to the NIV's pattern; "This saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance" (1:15, 4:9) is almost the NIV's whole formula, though this is a lead-decided term. "Hold authority over" (2:12) and "saved through the bearing of children" (2:15) are close to the ESV. None is a copy of a verse and each is defensible from the Greek; consider varying 4:12, 6:10 and 6:17 where the Greek allows.

20. **Minor. plain.txt 2:8.** "free of anger and argument" for *dialogismou*. The Greek word is "reasoning, doubt, dispute"; a number of translators and commentators take "doubting" (the other well-known reading). Add a short note or gloss.

21. **Minor. glosses.json.** No gloss at 1:10 for "sound teaching" though the terms entry and brief-pauline2 require a gloss "healthy" at first use; the note covers it, but the Plain English Translation page does not show a gloss. Also no gloss for "the husband of one wife" at 3:2 (the note exists).

22. **Minor. book.json authorship, "Critical view".** "Since Friedrich Schleiermacher (1807), most critical scholars have held that the three Pastoral Letters come from a follower." The topic text itself says Schleiermacher questioned 1 Timothy; the extension to all three came later. Reword as the topic does.

23. **Minor. notes.json 1:1 (entry "Did Paul write 1 Timothy?").** Names Johnson as the sole defender and Harrison as the sole critical scholar; a reader will think those are the only voices. Add one defender and one critic (Marshall, Mounce, Towner, Collins, Quinn-Wacker, Dibelius-Conzelmann: each checked this session) or send the reader to the topic once it names them.

24. **Minor. notes.json 2:11 (entry "Quietness, not silence").** "Greek has another word for not speaking (sigē, as in 1 Corinthians 14:28, 30, 34)." Those verses use the verb *sigaō* ("keep silent"); *sigē* is the noun and does not occur there. Say "the verb *sigaō*". The note also moves quickly to "may be to the teaching, or to the church's order" for "submission", a suggestion without a named supporter; say who holds it or label it as a possibility.

25. **Minor. chapters.json, repeated settings text.** The "Traditional / Critical" views entries in settings 1:1-2, 2:1-7 and 4:6-16 are the same two sentences word for word ("Paul writes to the real Timothy..." and "A follower of Paul writes in his name late in the first century..."). Fine once; in 2:1-7 and 4:6-16 they say nothing about the section. Replace with views that fit the section, or cut.

26. **Minor. chapters.json ch. 3 celibacy card.** The question names "the remarried" but the Catholic entry says nothing about what 3:2 means for remarriage, and Lutheran, Reformed and Baptist (the churches that rejected compulsory celibacy, with Lutheran Augsburg Confession 23) are missing. The Orthodox entry cites the OCA text `oca-divorce-ordination`; I did not open it and cannot confirm the quoted phrases. Catechism 1579 and 1580 are quoted accurately (confirmed at vatican.va, footnotes Mt 19:12, 1 Cor 7:32, PO 16).

27. **Minor. chapters.json ch. 3 deacons card.** The Methodist entry reads 3:11 as "deacons' wives" and gives the 1956 clergy-rights date, which says nothing about deaconesses. The Methodist and the Anglican/Lutheran/Orthodox deaconess orders (the Orthodox diaconate of women has been revived in parts of the church in recent decades; check) are the real subject. The Catholic entry says nothing on women deacons beyond the ban on ordination, though the question is at the centre of recent Catholic discussion; add what the Church has formally said, from a document opened this session.

28. **Minor. notes.json 1:4 (entry "God's plan") and 5.** The note says "God's training" is a possible sense of *oikonomia* ("see 3:15"). No lexicon is named, and the claim in note 52 that "the same idea is behind 'God's plan'" links two words (*oikos* and *oikonomia*) by sound rather than evidence. Soften.

29. **Minor. notes.json 6:13, 6:14, etc.** "This is the only place in the letters of Paul where he [Pilate] is named" is true of the Pauline letters as the site arranges them, and says nothing of the authorship question; qualify with "in the letters in Paul's name".

30. **Minor. proposals/nt/terms.json.** The `eusebeia` entry lists 2 Tim 3:5, 12 and Titus 1:1, 2:12 but not *theosebeia* (1 Tim 2:10), which the Plain English Translation renders "honor God"; add a line saying that word is kept apart. The `epiphaneia` entry lists 2 Thess 2:8, which belongs to a book still to be reviewed; fine, but note that the 2Th Plain English Translation must match. The `pistos ho logos` entry is correct (five places, two with "full acceptance").
