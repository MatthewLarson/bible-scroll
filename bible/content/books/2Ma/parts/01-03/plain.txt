## The first letter to Egypt
1:1 To our brothers, the Jews in Egypt, greetings, from your brothers, the Jews in Jerusalem and in the land of Judea. May you have good peace.
1:2 May God be good to you, and may he remember his covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, his faithful servants.
1:3 May he give all of you a heart to worship him and to do his will with a great heart and a willing soul.
1:4 May he open your hearts to his law and his commands, and make peace.
1:5 May he hear your prayers and be reconciled with you. In an evil time may he not desert you.
1:6 Here and now we are praying for you.
1:7 In the reign of Demetrius, in the year 169, we Jews wrote to you in the trouble and the crisis that struck us in those years, after Jason and his people broke away from the holy land and the kingdom.
1:8 They burned the gateway and shed innocent blood. Then we prayed to the Lord, and we were heard. We offered sacrifice and fine flour, we brought out the lamps, and we set out the loaves.
1:9 So now keep the days of the Feast of Booths in the month of Kislev.
## The first letter's date, and the second letter
1:10 In the year 188, at Jerusalem. Those in Judea, with the council of elders and Judas, to Aristobulus, the teacher of King Ptolemy, who comes from the family of the anointed priests, and to the Jews in Egypt: greetings and good health.
1:11 God has saved us from great dangers, and we thank him greatly, as men who stood in battle against a king.
1:12 For he himself flung those who drew up for battle in the holy city out into Persia.
## How Antiochus died
1:13 When the leader got there, with an army around him that seemed unbeatable, they were cut down in the temple of Nanea by a trick of Nanea's priests.
1:14 Antiochus came to the place with the Friends who were with him, as if he would marry her, so as to take more of the money as a dowry.
1:15 Nanea's priests laid the money out, and he entered the walled enclosure of the shrine with a few men. When Antiochus had come in, they locked the temple.
1:16 They opened a hidden door in the ceiling, threw stones, and struck the leader down as if with thunderbolts. They cut them limb from limb and threw the heads out to the people outside.
1:17 Blessed be our God in every way, who handed over the ones who acted ungodly.
## The fire hidden in the well
1:18 We are about to keep the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth of Kislev. So we thought we must make it known to you, so that you too may keep a Feast of Booths, and the feast of the fire, when Nehemiah offered sacrifices after he had built the temple and the altar.
1:19 For when our ancestors were being led away to Persia, devout priests secretly took some of the fire from the altar. They hid it in a hollow in a well that had no water, and they kept it safe there, so that no one knew the place.
1:20 Many years later, when God saw fit, Nehemiah was sent by the king of Persia. He sent the descendants of the priests who had hidden the fire to fetch it.
1:21 They told us that they had found no fire, only thick water, and he ordered them to scoop some out and bring it. When the things for the sacrifices had been brought up, Nehemiah ordered the priests to sprinkle the water on the wood and on what lay on it.
1:22 This was done, and some time passed. The sun had been behind the clouds, and when it shone out, a huge blaze was kindled, and everyone was amazed.
1:23 As the sacrifice burned, the priests prayed, and everyone with them. Jonathan began, and the others joined in, as Nehemiah did.
## The prayer at the altar
1:24 This is how the prayer ran: "Lord God, maker of all things, fearsome and strong, just and merciful, the only King, the kind one,
1:25 the only giver, the only just one, the Almighty, the eternal one, who rescues Israel from every evil, who made our ancestors chosen and made them holy:
1:26 accept the sacrifice offered for all your people Israel. Keep your portion safe and make it holy.
1:27 Bring our dispersed people together. Set free those who are slaves among the nations. Look on those who are despised and loathed. Let the nations know that you are our God.
1:28 Bring torment on the oppressors and on those who are insolent in their arrogance.
1:29 Plant your people in your holy place, as Moses said."
1:30 The priests sang the hymns to the harp.
## The sacred place in Persia
1:31 When the sacrifice had been burned up, Nehemiah ordered the water that was left to be poured on some large stones.
1:32 When this was done, a flame was kindled. But it was swallowed up by the light that shone back from the altar.
1:33 The matter became widely known. The king of the Persians was told that in the place where the deported priests had hidden the fire, water had appeared, and that Nehemiah and his people had purified the sacrifices with it.
1:34 The king fenced the place in and made it sacred, once he had tested the matter.
1:35 The king received large revenues, and he gave a share of them to these men.
1:36 Nehemiah's people gave it the name Nephthar, which in translation is "Purification." Most people call it Nephthar.
## Jeremiah hides the ark
2:1 It is found in the records that Jeremiah the prophet told the people being carried off to take some of the fire, as was shown above.
2:2 They also show how the prophet gave them the law when he gave orders to the deported. He told them not to forget the Lord's commands, and not to let their minds be led astray when they saw images of gold and silver and the finery on them.
2:3 He said other things like these, and he urged them not to let the law go from their hearts.
2:4 The writing also said that the prophet, after a message came to him from God, ordered the tent and the ark to go along with him. Then he set out for the mountain that Moses had climbed when he saw God's inheritance.
2:5 When Jeremiah got there, he found a room like a cave. He carried the tent, the ark and the altar of incense into it, and he blocked up the entrance.
2:6 Some of his companions went up to mark out the road, but they were not able to find it.
2:7 When Jeremiah found out, he blamed them and said, "The place will stay unknown until God gathers his people in, and mercy comes.
2:8 And then the Lord will bring these things to light. The glory of the Lord will be seen, and the cloud, as they were made visible in Moses' day, and as when Solomon prayed for the place to be made greatly holy."
2:9 The writing also made clear how Solomon in his wisdom offered a sacrifice of dedication and of the temple's completion.
2:10 Just as Moses prayed to the Lord and fire came down from heaven and burned up the sacrifice, so Solomon prayed to the Lord, and the fire came down and burned up the burnt offerings.
2:11 Moses said, "Because the sin offering had not been eaten, it was burned up."
2:12 In the same way Solomon kept the eight days.
## Nehemiah's library
2:13 These same things were set out in the records, and in the memoirs from Nehemiah's time. They tell too how he laid the foundation of a library. He collected the books about the kings and prophets, and David's books, and letters from kings about gifts to the temple.
2:14 Judas did the same. He gathered up what had been scattered because of the war we went through, and it is here with us.
2:15 If you need any of them, then, send messengers to carry them back to you.
2:16 Since we are about to celebrate the purification, we have written to you. You will do well to keep the days.
2:17 God rescued all his people. He restored the inheritance to every one of them, with the kingdom, the priesthood and the holiness,
2:18 just as he promised in the law. Our hope rests on God: soon he will show us mercy and bring us together from all that lies under heaven to the holy place. He has already rescued us from great evils and purified the place.
## The writer's preface
2:19 This is the story of Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, of the purification of the greatest of temples and the dedication of the altar,
2:20 and of the wars against Antiochus Epiphanes and his son Eupator.
2:21 It tells of the appearances from heaven given to the men who did brave deeds for Judaism, eager for honor, so that though they were few they plundered the whole country and drove the barbarian crowds before them.
2:22 They recovered the temple whose fame went around the whole world, they set the city free and put back in force the laws that were about to be done away with, for the Lord was gracious to them with all kindness.
2:23 Jason of Cyrene told all this in five books, and we will try to boil it down into one.
2:24 For we saw the flood of numbers, and how hard it is for those who want to plunge into the accounts of the history, because there is so much material.
2:25 We have taken care to give enjoyment to those who want to read, ease to those eager to fix it in memory, and benefit to everyone who comes across it.
2:26 For us, who have taken on the hard labor of shortening it, the task is not easy. It is a matter of sweat and sleepless nights,
2:27 just as it is not easy for someone who prepares a banquet and aims at what will do other people good. Still, to earn the thanks of many, we will gladly bear the hard labor.
2:28 We leave it to the historian to work out every point exactly, and we wear ourselves out keeping to the outlines of a summary.
2:29 The chief builder of a new house has to plan the whole framework, while the one who sets out to finish it and paint it must look for what suits its decoration. That is how I think it is with us.
2:30 To go into the matter deeply, to walk all around it in words and to busy oneself with every detail is the task of the one who first wrote the history.
2:31 But the one who retells it must be allowed to aim at a short telling and to give up a full working out of the subject.
2:32 So let us begin the story here, after adding this much to what has been said. For it is foolish to run on at length before the history and then cut the history itself short.
## Simon betrays the treasury
3:1 People lived in the holy city in complete peace, and the laws were kept as well as they could be, because of the godliness of Onias the high priest and his hatred of evil.
3:2 As a result even the kings honored the place, and they made the temple glorious with the best gifts.
3:3 Even Seleucus, king of Asia, paid from his own revenues all the costs that the services of the sacrifices required.
3:4 But one Simon, from the tribe of Benjamin, the appointed overseer of the temple, came into conflict with the high priest over the running of the city market.
3:5 Since he could not get the better of Onias, he went to Apollonius son of Thrasaeus, who at that time was governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
3:6 He told him that the treasury at Jerusalem was crammed with more money than words could tell, so much that no one could count the funds. These had nothing to do with the cost of the sacrifices, he said, and they could be made to pass into the king's hands.
3:7 Apollonius had an audience with the king and passed on what he had been told about the money. The king singled out Heliodorus, his chief minister, and sent him with orders to remove it.
3:8 Heliodorus set out at once. He made it look as if he were touring the cities of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, but his real aim was to do what the king intended.
3:9 When he reached Jerusalem, the high priest of the city gave him a friendly welcome. He told him about the information that had been given and made clear why he was there. He asked whether it was all true.
3:10 The high priest explained that there were deposits belonging to widows and orphans,
3:11 and some belonging to Hyrcanus son of Tobias, a man of very high rank. It was not as the impious Simon had falsely said. In all there were four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold.
3:12 To wrong people who had relied on the holiness of the place, he said, could not be done at all. They had trusted the dignity of a temple honored all over the world, and its right of sanctuary.
## Heliodorus in the temple
3:13 But the other man had royal orders, and he insisted that the money had to go into the king's treasury, no matter what.
3:14 On a day he had fixed, he entered to manage the inspection. All through the city there was great anguish.
3:15 The priests, in their priestly robes, threw themselves down before the altar. They called to heaven on the one who gave the law about money left in trust, asking him to keep it safe for its owners.
3:16 One look at the high priest was enough to wound the heart. His face and his changed color showed how he agonized inside.
3:17 Fear and a shuddering of the body had come over the man, and through them the pain in his heart was plain to those who watched.
3:18 People poured out of their houses in flocks to join a prayer of the whole people, because the place was about to be treated with contempt.
3:19 Women wrapped sackcloth below their breasts and filled the streets. Of the unmarried girls, who were usually kept shut in, some ran to the gateways, some to the walls, and some leaned out of the windows to look.
3:20 All of them stretched out their hands toward heaven and made their prayer.
3:21 It was pitiful to see the crowd of every kind lying face down on the ground, and the dread of the high priest in his great anguish.
3:22 So they kept calling on the all-ruling Lord to guard the deposits, safe and fully secure, for the people who had deposited them.
3:23 But Heliodorus carried out his decision.
3:24 He was already there with his bodyguards at the treasury, when the Sovereign of spirits and of all authority caused a great appearance. All who had dared to come in with him were struck with terror at God's power, and they turned faint and afraid.
3:25 For there appeared to them a horse with a fearsome rider, decked out in splendid trappings. It charged headlong and kicked at Heliodorus with its front hooves. The rider seemed to have armor of gold.
3:26 Two other young men appeared to him as well, outstanding in strength, very beautiful in their glory, and splendid in their clothing. They stood on either side of him and flogged him without stopping, laying many blows on him.
3:27 All at once he fell to the ground, and thick darkness wrapped around him. They seized him and laid him on a litter.
3:28 The man who had just gone into that treasury with a great crowd of attendants and his whole bodyguard, they now carried away. He had become completely helpless, and he had plainly come to know the sovereign power.
3:29 Struck by the work of God, he lay on the ground speechless, with no hope and no rescue.
3:30 But the people blessed the Lord, who had shown his own place to be marvelous. The temple, which a little before had been full of fear and turmoil, was filled with joy and gladness, now that the Almighty Lord had appeared.
## Onias prays for Heliodorus
3:31 At once some of Heliodorus's intimates begged Onias to call on the Most High and give life back to a man who was breathing his last.
3:32 The high priest grew uneasy that the king might suspect the Jews of some crime against Heliodorus. So he brought a sacrifice for the man's rescue.
3:33 While the high priest was making the atonement, the same young men appeared again to Heliodorus, dressed in the same clothes. They stood there and said, "Be very grateful to Onias the high priest. It is because of him that the Lord has given you your life.
3:34 And you, who have been flogged from heaven, tell everyone how great is the power of God." When they had said this, they vanished.
3:35 Heliodorus offered a sacrifice to God and made very great vows to the one who had saved his life. He parted from Onias on good terms and led his force back to the king.
3:36 He told everyone what he had seen with his own eyes: the works of the greatest God.
3:37 The king asked Heliodorus what sort of man would be fit to send to Jerusalem once more, and he said,
3:38 "If you have any enemy, or anyone scheming against the state, send him there. He will come back to you flogged, if he comes out alive at all. For a power of God truly surrounds that place.
3:39 He who has his home in heaven is himself the overseer and helper of that place. Any who come there to do harm, he strikes and destroys."
3:40 That is how things turned out with Heliodorus and the protection of the treasury.
