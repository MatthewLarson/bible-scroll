## Judas gathers an army
8:1 Judas, who was also called Maccabeus, and his men went into the villages without being seen and called their kinsmen to join them. They took in those who had held on to Judaism, and they brought together as many as six thousand.
8:2 They called on the Lord to look on the people whom everyone was oppressing, and to pity the temple that ungodly men had profaned.
8:3 They prayed him to show mercy to the city as it was being ruined, soon to be flattened to the ground, and to hear the blood that was crying out to him.
8:4 They asked him to remember the babies who had done no sin and were put to death against the law, and the blasphemies spoken against his name, and to show that he hates wickedness.
8:5 When Maccabeus had a band gathered around him, the nations found him impossible to resist, because the Lord's anger had now turned to mercy.
8:6 Arriving when no one expected him, he set towns and farmland on fire. He seized the places that mattered and drove off no small number of the enemy.
8:7 Above all he used the nights as his partners for raids like these, and the story of his bravery rang out on every side.
## Nicanor is sent to sell the Jews
8:8 Philip watched the man moving forward bit by bit, his successes coming more and more often. He wrote to Ptolemy, the governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, asking him to come to the aid of the king's cause.
8:9 Ptolemy wasted no time. He chose Nicanor son of Patroclus, one of the First Friends, and sent him off at the head of no fewer than twenty thousand men from a mix of nations, with orders to wipe out the entire race of Judea. Gorgias went with him as well, a general well tried in the business of war.
8:10 Nicanor's plan was to pay off what the king owed the Romans in tribute, two thousand talents, out of the Jews he would capture.
8:11 He sent word straight away to the cities by the sea, calling on buyers for Jewish slaves and offering ninety slaves for one talent. He had no idea of the punishment from the Almighty that was soon to follow him.
## Judas prepares his men
8:12 Judas learned that Nicanor was coming, and he told the men with him that the army was near.
8:13 Then the cowards, who had no faith in God's justice, slipped away and took themselves off to other places.
8:14 The rest sold off whatever they still owned and pleaded with the Lord to save them, since the impious Nicanor had sold them even before he had met them.
8:15 They asked this, if not because of who they were, then because of the covenants with their ancestors, and because his holy and majestic name had been called over them.
8:16 Maccabeus called together the men around him, six thousand strong. He told them not to be terrified of the enemy or afraid of the crowds of nations marching on them so unjustly. They were to fight like men of noble spirit,
8:17 keeping in view the lawless outrage those men had done to the holy place, the cruel treatment of the city they had mocked, and on top of that the overthrow of the way of life handed down from their ancestors.
8:18 "Those men rely on their weapons and their daring," he said, "but our trust is in God Almighty. With one nod he can overpower the men who are marching on us, and the whole world as well."
8:19 He went over with them the times their ancestors had received help, and the help in Sennacherib's day, when a hundred and eighty-five thousand were destroyed,
8:20 and the battle against the Galatians in Babylonia, when all who went into the fight were eight thousand, with four thousand Macedonians. When the Macedonians were at their wits' end, the six thousand destroyed a hundred and twenty thousand, because of the help that came to them from heaven, and they took a great deal of plunder.
8:21 These words made them bold and willing to die for the laws and their homeland. Next he split the army into four divisions.
8:22 Over each division he set one of his brothers, Simon, Joseph and Jonathan, with fifteen hundred men under each one,
8:23 and Eleazar as well. Then he read aloud from the holy book and gave the watchword, "God's help." He led the first unit himself and engaged Nicanor.
## Victory and the Sabbath
8:24 The Almighty fought with them as their ally, and they slaughtered over nine thousand of the enemy. Most of Nicanor's army they left wounded and maimed in their limbs, and they made every one of them run.
8:25 They took the money of the men who had come to buy them. They chased the enemy a good distance, but they turned back because they were running out of time,
8:26 for it was the day before the Sabbath, and for that reason they did not keep up the chase.
8:27 Once they had gathered the enemy's weapons and stripped the dead of their spoils, they gave themselves to the Sabbath. Over and over they blessed and praised the Lord, who had brought them safely to this day and had made it the start of his mercy toward them.
8:28 When the Sabbath was over, they handed out part of the spoils to the tortured, the widows and the orphans. The rest they shared out among themselves and their children.
8:29 That done, they all prayed together, asking the merciful Lord to be at peace with his servants once and for all.
## Timothy and Bacchides defeated
8:30 They also met the troops of Timothy and Bacchides in battle and killed over twenty thousand of them, and they very easily made themselves masters of high strongholds. They split up a great deal more plunder, making the tortured, the orphans and the widows, and the old people too, equal in share with themselves.
8:31 They gathered the enemy's arms with care and laid them all up in places of importance. The rest of the spoils they took to Jerusalem.
8:32 They killed the troop commander under Timothy, a most unholy man who had done the Jews much harm.
8:33 While they were celebrating the victory in their homeland, they burned the ones who had set fire to the holy gateways: Callisthenes, who had fled into a little house. They set it alight, and he received the pay his impiety deserved.
## Nicanor's flight
8:34 As for Nicanor, that thrice-cursed villain who had brought three thousand merchants to the sale of the Jews,
8:35 he had been brought low, with the Lord's help, by men he thought were nobodies. He threw off his fine clothes of rank and, like a runaway slave, left himself without companions and went through the inland country to Antioch. His one great success was the ruin of his own army.
8:36 And the man who had taken on the task of paying the Romans' tribute out of Jerusalem's captives now went about announcing that the Jews had someone fighting for them. For this reason, he said, the Jews could not be wounded, because they kept the laws that this Champion had laid down.
## Antiochus driven out of Persepolis
9:1 It was at about this time that Antiochus pulled out of the lands of Persia in disarray.
9:2 He had marched into the place called Persepolis and set about plundering its temples and seizing the city. But the crowds rose and took up arms, and the people of the country routed Antiochus, so that he made a disgraceful retreat.
9:3 When he was near Ecbatana, he got the report of what had happened to Nicanor and Timothy's army.
9:4 Lifted up by his fury, he meant to make the Jews pay for the shame dealt him by the people who had chased him away. He told his charioteer to drive on without a stop and finish the journey, though the judgment from heaven was already traveling with him. For he had boasted, "I will go there and turn Jerusalem into a mass grave of Jews."
## God strikes the king
9:5 But the Lord who sees everything, the God of Israel, struck him a blow that was unseen and beyond healing. The words were hardly out of his mouth when a pain in his bowels took hold of him that nothing could ease, and bitter torments within him.
9:6 It was perfectly just: he had tortured other people's bowels with many strange torments.
9:7 Even so, his haughtiness did not let up in the least. He grew still fuller of pride, breathed fire at the Jews in his anger, and kept ordering more speed. Then, as the chariot raced along with a roar, he was thrown from it, and the fall was so bad that every limb of his body was twisted.
9:8 A little before, in pride beyond what is human, he had thought he could give orders to the waves of the sea and set the mountain peaks on a scale. Now he had fallen to the ground and was being carried on a stretcher, a plain display to all of the power of God.
9:9 Worms came boiling up out of the impious man's body. He was still alive, in agony and pain, when his flesh began to fall away, and his rotting smell weighed on the whole army.
9:10 Shortly before, he had fancied he could reach up and touch the stars of heaven. Now the stench was so unbearable that no one could carry him.
9:11 So there, shattered, he began to let go of most of his pride. Under God's whip he started to come to knowledge, as his pains grew sharper moment by moment.
9:12 When he could not stand even his own smell, he said, "It is right to submit to God. No one who is mortal should think arrogant thoughts."
## The king's vow
9:13 Then the vile man made a vow to the Master, who was no longer going to have mercy on him. This is what he promised:
9:14 He would set free the holy city, the city he had been rushing to flatten and fill with graves.
9:15 As for the Jews, he had decided they did not deserve even burial; he had meant to wipe them out with their babies and leave them as food for birds and beasts. Now he would make them all the equals of the Athenians.
9:16 He would deck the holy temple he had stripped earlier with the most beautiful gifts, return all the sacred vessels many times over, and pay out of his own revenues for the costs of the sacrifices.
9:17 Besides all this, he would become a Jew, and he would travel to every inhabited land telling of God's might.
## The king's letter to the Jews
9:18 His pains did not ease in the slightest, because God's righteous judgment had come upon him. With no hope left for himself, he wrote the Jews the letter set out below. It was written as a petition, and this is what it said:
9:19 "To the worthy Jews, the citizens, many greetings, good health and prosperity, from Antiochus, king and general.
9:20 Good health to you and to your children; your affairs will go as you wish. Keeping my hope fixed on heaven,
9:21 with warm affection I have kept in mind the honor and goodwill you have shown me. As I was returning from Persia, I came down with a painful illness, and I have judged it necessary to plan for the safety of all of you together.
9:22 Not that I have given up on myself; I have every hope of shaking off this illness.
9:23 But I have taken note that my father too, at the times when he took his army into the upper regions, named the one who would succeed him.
9:24 That way, if something surprising took place and some further trouble came, the people of the land would not be alarmed, because they would know into whose hands affairs had been put.
9:25 I also keep in view the rulers who sit along our borders, neighbors of the kingdom who lie in wait for the right moment and watch for what will come. For this reason I have named my son Antiochus king. Many times, when I set out in haste for the upper provinces, I put him in the care of most of you and spoke for him. I have written to him the letter copied below.
9:26 So I appeal to you and ask you to remember the kindnesses I did you, as a people and one by one, and each of you to keep the goodwill you have now toward me and my son.
9:27 I am sure he will hold to my purpose and deal with you fairly and humanely, in harmony with you."
## The death of Antiochus
9:28 So the murderer and blasphemer suffered the worst, just as he had treated others, and in a foreign land, among the mountains, his life came to a most wretched end.
9:29 Philip, his companion from childhood, escorted the body. Then, afraid of Antiochus's son, he crossed over to Ptolemy Philometor in Egypt.
## The temple purified
10:1 The Lord went before Maccabeus and his men, and they recovered the temple and the city.
10:2 They tore down the altars that the foreigners had built in the marketplace, and the shrines too.
10:3 They made the temple clean and built a new altar. They heated stones, took fire from them, and offered sacrifices, the first in two years. They set out incense and lamps, and they laid out the Presence Bread.
10:4 After this they lay on the ground face down and pleaded with the Lord never to let them fall into such evils again. If they ever did sin, they asked that he would correct them himself, gently, and not give them over to blasphemous, barbarian nations.
10:5 And it came about that the cleansing of the temple fell on the very day the foreigners had profaned it, the twenty-fifth of the same month, Kislev.
10:6 With gladness they kept eight days as one keeps the Feast of Booths, for they remembered that a short time before, at the Feast of Booths, they had been living like wild animals in the mountains and the caves.
10:7 So, holding leafy wands and beautiful branches, and palm fronds too, they gave thanks to the one who had let them succeed in purifying his own place.
10:8 They also passed a decree, by a public order and a vote, for the whole nation of the Jews: these days were to be kept every year.
## Eupator, Lysias and Ptolemy Macron
10:9 This, then, is how things went with the death of Antiochus, who was called Epiphanes.
10:10 Next we will set out the events under Antiochus Eupator, the son of that ungodly man, cutting short the account of the evils that kept falling on the cities.
10:11 On taking the throne he made a man named Lysias head of the government and chief governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
10:12 This came about because Ptolemy, known as Macron, had been the first to treat the Jews fairly, in view of the wrongs done to them, and he had tried to manage his dealings with them in peace.
10:13 For this the Friends brought charges against him before Eupator. Everywhere he turned he was called a traitor, since he had left Cyprus, which Philometor had put in his hands, and had gone over to Antiochus Epiphanes. He could not make his high post an honorable one, so he poisoned himself and died.
## War with the Idumeans
10:14 Gorgias, now governor of the region, hired foreign soldiers and kept up the fighting against the Jews at every chance.
10:15 The Idumeans joined in. They held strongholds in key places and gave the Jews no rest, and they took in men driven out of Jerusalem and worked to keep the war alive.
10:16 Those with Maccabeus held a solemn prayer, asking God to be their ally, and then stormed the Idumean strongholds.
10:17 They pressed the attack hard and won the places. They drove back everyone fighting on the walls, cut down those who came their way, and killed at least twenty thousand.
10:18 At least nine thousand escaped together into two towers, very strong, with all they needed to hold out against a siege.
10:19 Maccabeus left Simon and Joseph behind to besiege them, along with Zacchaeus and his men, who were enough for the task, and went himself to places that were pressing.
10:20 But Simon's men loved money, and some of the men in the towers won them over with silver. They took seventy thousand drachmas and let a number of them get away.
10:21 Maccabeus heard what had been done. He called the leaders of the people together and charged those men with selling their brothers for silver, because they had let loose the enemy to fight them.
10:22 Then he put the traitors to death and took the two towers straight away.
10:23 His arms brought success to all he set his hand to, and in the two strongholds he killed more than twenty thousand.
## Five riders from heaven
10:24 Timothy, whom the Jews had beaten earlier, brought together foreign troops in great numbers and a large force of Asian horses, and arrived planning to take Judea as a prize of war.
10:25 As he came closer, Maccabeus's men turned to pleading with God. They threw earth on their heads and tied sackcloth around their waists,
10:26 and they fell down on the platform facing the altar and pleaded with him to be merciful to them, to be the enemy of their enemies and the foe of their foes, as the law plainly states.
10:27 When the prayer was over, they took their arms and moved out a long way from the city. Near the enemy they stopped and stayed by themselves.
10:28 As the first light spread, both sides charged. One army had its refuge in the Lord as the pledge of success and victory, together with its courage. The other put its fury in command of the battle.
10:29 The battle was raging when, out of heaven, five splendid men on horses with golden bridles appeared to the enemy. Two of them were leading the Jews.
10:30 They placed Maccabeus in the middle, sheltered him under their own armor, and guarded him so that no wound touched him. At the enemy they flung arrows and lightning bolts, and the enemy, struck blind and thrown into confusion, full of terror, were cut down.
10:31 Twenty thousand five hundred were slaughtered, and six hundred horsemen.
## The fall of Gazara
10:32 Timothy himself ran for safety to a fort named Gazara, a stronghold with a strong guard, commanded by Chaereas.
10:33 Maccabeus's men gladly besieged the fort for twenty-four days.
10:34 Inside, the defenders put their faith in how strong the place was. They kept up a stream of blasphemy and shouted things no one should say.
10:35 When the light of the twenty-fifth day was showing, young men in Maccabeus's army, on fire with rage over the blasphemies, threw themselves at the wall. They fought like true men, with the fury of wild animals, and struck down whoever came in their way.
10:36 Others climbed up after them in the same way and, while the defenders were caught up with the first attackers, set the towers on fire. They built fires and burned the blasphemers alive, while still others hacked through the gates, brought in the rest of the troops, and seized the town.
10:37 Timothy was hiding in a cistern. They slaughtered him there, and his brother Chaereas, and Apollophanes.
10:38 After all this, they blessed the Lord with hymns and songs of thanks, for he is the great benefactor of Israel and grants them victory.
## Lysias at Beth-zur
11:1 Only a very short time later, Lysias was badly upset by what had happened. He was the king's guardian and Kinsman, and he ran the government.
11:2 He brought together about eighty thousand men and all the cavalry, and marched on the Jews. His plan was to turn the city into a place where Greeks would live,
11:3 to make the temple pay silver, as the shrines of the other nations did, and to sell the high priesthood year by year.
11:4 He never stopped to reckon with God's might. His head was turned by his foot soldiers in tens of thousands, his horsemen in thousands, and his eighty elephants.
11:5 He invaded Judea and came up to Beth-zur, a fortified place about five schoinoi from Jerusalem, and he pressed it hard.
11:6 Maccabeus's men heard that he was besieging the strongholds. With all the crowds they wept and wailed and begged the Lord to send a good angel to bring rescue to Israel.
11:7 Maccabeus took up his weapons first of all, and he called on the others to face the danger alongside him and help their brothers. With one mind and eagerly they set out.
11:8 They were still near Jerusalem when a rider on horseback appeared, going ahead of them. He was dressed in white, and he shook golden armor as he rode.
11:9 All together they blessed the merciful God, and their hearts grew strong. They were ready to cut down not only men, but the fiercest beasts and walls of iron.
11:10 So they moved forward ready for battle. They had an ally from heaven, because the Lord had taken pity on them.
11:11 Like lions they burst in on the enemy. They brought down eleven thousand of them, and sixteen hundred horsemen, and drove the whole army to flight.
11:12 Most of them came out alive, but wounded and stripped of their arms. Lysias himself saved his life by running away in disgrace.
## Lysias makes peace
11:13 Lysias was no fool. He thought over the loss he had suffered, and he saw that the Hebrews could not be conquered while God in his power was their ally. So he sent to them
11:14 and persuaded them to come to terms on everything that was just, and he promised to persuade the king as well, pressing him to become their friend.
11:15 Maccabeus, with an eye to what was best, nodded his assent to everything Lysias asked. For whatever Maccabeus had handed to Lysias in writing about the Jews, the king granted.
## The letter of Lysias
11:16 The letters written to the Jews were these. The one from Lysias said: "Lysias to the people of the Jews, greetings.
11:17 John and Absalom, whom you sent, have handed me the document written below and asked about the matters set out in it.
11:18 Whatever also had to be laid before the king, I made clear to him, and he granted all that could be granted.
11:19 So if you hold on to your goodwill toward the government, I will try from now on to be a cause of good for you.
11:20 I have given these men, and my own people too, orders to talk the details over with you.
11:21 Farewell. In the year 148, on the twenty-fourth of Dioscorinthius."
## The king's letter to Lysias
11:22 The king's letter read like this: "King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greetings.
11:23 Our father has gone over to the gods. It is our wish that the people of our kingdom be free of disturbance and able to look after their own affairs.
11:24 We hear that the Jews did not go along with our father's plan to bring them over to Greek ways. They would rather keep their own way of life, and they ask to be allowed their own customs.
11:25 We want this nation, too, to live without disturbance. So we rule that the temple is to be given back to them, and that they are to govern their lives by the customs of their ancestors.
11:26 It would be good of you, then, to send to them and give them your right hand. Then they will know our mind, take heart, and get on contentedly with their own business."
## The king's letter to the Jews
11:27 The king's letter to the nation was this: "King Antiochus to the council of elders of the Jews and to the rest of the Jews, greetings.
11:28 Good health to you; that would be just as we wish. We ourselves are well.
11:29 Menelaus has told us that he wants you to come home and take up your own business again.
11:30 So those who go home by the thirtieth of Xanthicus will have our right hand, with a promise of safety:
11:31 the Jews may use their own foods and their own laws, as before, and no one of them will be harassed in any way over what was done unknowingly.
11:32 I have also sent Menelaus to encourage you.
11:33 Farewell. In the year 148, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus."
## The letter of the Romans
11:34 The Romans also sent them a letter. It said: "Quintus Memmius and Titus Manius, envoys of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greetings.
11:35 What Lysias, the king's Kinsman, granted you, we also approve.
11:36 Look into the things he judged must be put before the king, and send someone without delay, so that we may handle them as is fitting for us, since we are moving on toward Antioch.
11:37 Hurry, then, and send men, so that we also may learn what your thinking is.
11:38 Good health to you. In the year 148, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus."
