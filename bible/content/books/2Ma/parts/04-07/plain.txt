## Simon slanders Onias
4:1 The Simon already mentioned, who had become an informer about the money and against his homeland, began to slander Onias. He said that Onias was the one who had stirred things up with Heliodorus and had brought about these troubles.
4:2 He dared to call the man who did good to the city, who looked after his own people and was zealous for the laws, a plotter against the state.
4:3 The hatred grew so great that one of Simon's trusted men even carried out murders.
4:4 Onias saw how harsh the quarrel had become. He saw too that Apollonius son of Menestheus, the governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, was adding to Simon's wickedness.
4:5 So he took himself to the king. He went not as an accuser of his fellow citizens, but with an eye to what would benefit the whole people, all together and each one.
4:6 For he saw that unless the king stepped in, there could be no peace in public life, and Simon would never stop his madness.
## Jason buys the high priesthood
4:7 Seleucus departed this life, and Antiochus, who was called Epiphanes, received the kingdom. Then Jason, Onias's brother, crept into the high priesthood by underhanded means.
4:8 He went to the king and promised him three hundred sixty talents of silver, plus eighty talents out of some other income.
4:9 Besides these he pledged to pay a hundred fifty more, if he was granted the right, by the king's authority, to set up for himself a gymnasium and a training corps for youths, and to register the Antiochenes in Jerusalem.
4:10 The king nodded his consent. As soon as Jason took hold of the office, he moved his own people over to the Greek stamp.
4:11 He pushed aside the favors the kings had granted the Jews, won through John the father of Eupolemus, the man who went as envoy to the Romans to seek friendship and alliance. He did away with the lawful ways of life and started new practices that broke the law.
4:12 He was glad to found a gymnasium at the very foot of the citadel. He made the best men among us submit, and he put them under the broad-brimmed hat.
4:13 This was how the Greek way came to its height and foreign ways advanced: through the overwhelming impurity of Jason, ungodly and no high priest.
4:14 The priests were no longer eager to serve at the altar. They looked down on the temple and neglected the sacrifices, and once the discus gave its call, they rushed to join in the lawless spectacle staged in the wrestling school.
4:15 They counted the honors of their fathers as nothing and thought Greek distinctions the finest of all.
4:16 That is why hard times closed in around them. In the very people whose ways they envied, and whom they wanted to be like in everything, they found enemies and avengers.
4:17 For it is no light thing to be ungodly toward the divine laws. But the time that follows will make this plain.
## A gift for Heracles
4:18 The games held every fifth year were being held in Tyre, and the king was there.
4:19 The foul Jason sent envoys, as Antiochenes from Jerusalem, to bring three hundred silver drachmas for the sacrifice to Heracles. But the men who carried the money asked that it not be used for a sacrifice,
4:20 but, because of the circumstances, for fitting out warships.
4:21 Apollonius son of Menestheus was sent to Egypt for the crowning of King Philometor. Antiochus heard that the king had turned against his affairs, and he gave thought to keeping himself safe. That is why he came to Joppa and then arrived at Jerusalem.
4:22 Jason and the city received him lavishly, and he was brought in by torchlight and with shouts. After that he marched his army off to Phoenicia and camped there.
## Menelaus outbids Jason
4:23 Three years later Jason sent Menelaus, the brother of the Simon named before, to deliver the money to the king and to finish reports on pressing matters.
4:24 But when Menelaus was introduced to the king, he flattered him with a show of authority. He brought the high priesthood round to himself, topping Jason's offer by three hundred talents of silver.
4:25 He came back holding the king's orders. He brought nothing fit for the high priesthood, only the tempers of a cruel tyrant and the fury of a wild beast.
4:26 So Jason, who had cheated his own brother out of office, was cheated out of it by another. He was forced to flee as an exile into the land of Ammon.
4:27 Menelaus held the office, but he paid none of the money he had promised the king on time,
4:28 though Sostratus, the commander of the citadel, kept demanding it, since the revenue was his to collect. For this reason the king summoned both of them.
4:29 Menelaus left his brother Lysimachus to stand in for him in the high priesthood, and Sostratus left Crates, the commander of the Cyprians.
## The murder of Onias
4:30 Meanwhile it came about that the people of Tarsus and Mallus rose in revolt, because they had been handed to Antiochis, the king's concubine, as a present.
4:31 The king hurried off to calm things down, and he left as his stand-in Andronicus, one of the men of rank.
4:32 Menelaus thought he had found his chance. He helped himself to some of the gold vessels of the temple and gave them to Andronicus as a present. Others he had already sold in Tyre and the cities nearby.
4:33 Onias found this out beyond doubt and reported it. He had withdrawn to a place of refuge at Daphne, which lies near Antioch.
4:34 For this reason Menelaus got Andronicus alone and pressed him to lay hands on Onias. Won over to the treachery, Andronicus came to Onias. He took his hand with oaths and gave his own right hand, and though Onias was wary, he coaxed him out of the refuge. At once he killed him, caring nothing for what was right.
4:35 This is why many people of other nations, and not Jews alone, were indignant and heavy-hearted over the man's unjust murder.
4:36 The king returned from the parts of Cilicia, and the Jews of the city brought him their plea, with the Greeks sharing their disgust at the wickedness: Onias had been killed for no reason.
4:37 Antiochus felt the sorrow deep in his soul and was moved to mercy. He shed tears over the dead man's self-control and his great good order.
4:38 Burning with anger, he at once stripped Andronicus of his purple and tore off his clothes. He led him around through the whole city to the very place where he had sinned against Onias, and there he removed the murderer from the world. So the Lord gave back to him the penalty he had earned.
## The crowd kills Lysimachus
4:39 Lysimachus, with Menelaus's consent, kept robbing sacred things in the city, and word of it got out. By then many gold vessels had already been carried off, and the people massed against Lysimachus.
4:40 As the crowds rose up and grew full of fury, Lysimachus armed about three thousand men and struck the first unjust blows. Their leader was one Auranus, a man far along in years and just as far in ignorance.
4:41 When the people saw what Lysimachus was attempting, some snatched up stones, others thick sticks of wood, and some grabbed handfuls of the ash lying close by. They flung them all in a jumble at Lysimachus's men.
4:42 So they left many of them wounded and some dead, and they chased them all away. The temple robber himself they killed beside the treasury.
## Menelaus on trial
4:43 Menelaus was put on trial over these events.
4:44 When the king arrived at Tyre, three men sent by the council of elders argued the case before him.
4:45 Menelaus already saw he was beaten. He promised Ptolemy son of Dorymenes plenty of money if he would persuade the king.
4:46 So Ptolemy led the king off into a covered walk, as if to let him cool down, and turned him around.
4:47 The king let Menelaus, the cause of all the trouble, go free of the charges. But the wretched men, who would have walked away blameless even if they had spoken before Scythians, he condemned to death.
4:48 So the men who had spoken up for the city, the people and the holy vessels quickly paid that unjust penalty.
4:49 Even the people of Tyre were disgusted at the wrong, and for that reason they gave the men a splendid funeral at their own cost.
4:50 Through the greed of those in power, Menelaus kept his office. Wickedness grew on him, and he stood as the great schemer against his own people.
## Riders in the sky
5:1 About this time Antiochus got ready for his second march into Egypt.
5:2 It happened that for nearly forty days people all over the city saw horsemen running through the air. They wore robes shot with gold and were armed with lances, in companies,
5:3 with swords being drawn, squadrons of horses lined up, charges and rushes made by each side, shields swinging, spears in crowds, darts flying, gold ornaments flashing, and every kind of breastplate.
5:4 So everyone prayed that the appearance meant good.
## Jason's attack and end
5:5 A lying report went around that Antiochus had departed this life. Jason gathered at least a thousand men and launched a surprise attack on the city. The men on the wall were pushed back, and as the city was finally falling, Menelaus escaped into the citadel.
5:6 Jason kept butchering the people of his own city without sparing them. He never stopped to think that a good day won against one's own kin is the worst day of all. To him it seemed that he was raising victory monuments over enemies, not over his own nation.
5:7 Still, he did not win the ruling power. His scheme ended by bringing him shame, and he went off once more as a fugitive to the land of Ammon.
5:8 So in the end he found a wretched finish. He was shut in by Aretas, the ruler of the Arabs. He ran from city to city, hunted by everyone, hated as a deserter of the laws, and loathed as the butcher of his country and his people. He was thrown out into Egypt.
5:9 He who had driven many from their homeland died in a foreign land. He had sailed to the Spartans, hoping to find shelter there because they were related.
5:10 The man who had flung out crowds to lie unburied was not mourned. He received no burial of any kind and had no part in the rites of his fathers' law.
## Antiochus sacks Jerusalem
5:11 Reports of these events came to the king, and he judged that Judea was rebelling. So he pulled out of Egypt, savage as a wild beast in spirit, and took the city at spear point.
5:12 He told his soldiers to strike down without sparing anyone who fell into their hands, and to butcher those who climbed up inside the houses.
5:13 Young men and old men were killed off; boys, women and children were wiped out; young girls and babies were slaughtered.
5:14 Eighty thousand were destroyed in three days all told. Forty thousand fell in combat, and no fewer than those who were slaughtered were sold.
5:15 That was not enough for him. He made bold to go into the temple holiest of all on earth, and his guide was Menelaus, the man who had turned traitor to the laws and to his homeland.
5:16 He seized the holy vessels with his foul hands. The gifts many kings had set up for the growth, glory and honor of the place, he dragged off with his unholy hands.
5:17 Antiochus's head swelled with pride. He did not grasp that the Master had grown angry for a short while over the sins of the city's people; that was why the place was being overlooked.
5:18 Had they not been tangled in many sins, he would have been whipped the moment he stepped forward and thrown back from his daring, as happened to Heliodorus, whom King Seleucus sent to look over the treasury.
5:19 But the Lord did not choose the nation for the sake of the place; he chose the place for the sake of the nation.
5:20 That is why the place itself, having shared the people's disasters, later took part in their good fortune too. Overtaken when the Almighty was angry, it was set upright again with all glory when the great Master made peace.
5:21 Antiochus took eighteen hundred talents out of the temple and left quickly for Antioch. He was so proud that he imagined he could make dry land fit for ships and the open sea fit to march across, because his heart was so carried away.
5:22 He left officials behind to do harm to the race: in Jerusalem Philip, a Phrygian by birth, with ways more savage than the one who placed him there;
5:23 at Gerizim, Andronicus; and besides them Menelaus, who raised himself above the citizens more harshly than the others. He bore a hateful attitude toward the Jews, his fellow citizens.
## Apollonius strikes on the Sabbath
5:24 He sent Apollonius, the commander of the Mysians, with a force of twenty-two thousand, and told him to butcher everyone of fighting age and to sell the women and the young.
5:25 Apollonius arrived at Jerusalem and played the man of peace. He held off until the holy day of the Sabbath. Then, catching the Jews at rest, he gave orders to those under him for an armed display.
5:26 Everyone who came out to watch the show he had stabbed, and charging into the city with the armed men, he posted large forces there.
5:27 Judas, also called Maccabeus, withdrew, one of about ten. With those who were with him he lived like the wild beasts in the mountains, and they went on eating plants for food, so as to have no part in the pollution.
## The temple given to Zeus
6:1 Not long after, the king sent an elderly Athenian to make the Jews give up their ancestral laws, so that they no longer governed their lives by the laws of God.
6:2 He was also to defile the temple in Jerusalem and name it for Olympian Zeus, and to name the one on Gerizim for Zeus, Protector of Guests, as befitted those who dwelt there.
6:3 The onset of this evil was harsh and hard for everyone to bear.
6:4 The temple was filled with wild living and drunken revels by the Gentiles, who amused themselves with prostitutes and went to bed with women within the walls around the holy places. They also brought inside things that did not belong there.
6:5 The altar was loaded with unholy things, the kind the laws rule out.
6:6 It was not allowed to keep the Sabbath, or to guard the feasts of the fathers, or simply to say, "I am a Jew."
6:7 Each month, on the king's birthday, they were led off under bitter compulsion to eat the sacrificial organs. When the festival of Dionysus came, they were made to march in Dionysus's parade carrying ivy.
6:8 At Ptolemy's suggestion, a decree went out to the neighboring Greek cities that they should treat the Jews the same way and make them eat the sacrifices,
6:9 and kill anyone who would not choose to switch to the Greek way. So the present wretchedness was there for anyone to see.
6:10 Two women were brought up for having circumcised their children. They hung the babies from their mothers' breasts, led the women around the city for all to see, and flung them over the wall.
6:11 Others had gathered in caves nearby to keep the seventh day in secret. Philip was told about them, and they were burned up together, since they held back in fear of God from helping themselves, to keep the honor of the most solemn day.
## Why the Lord disciplines his people
6:12 So I appeal to anyone who picks up this book: do not be disheartened by these calamities. Count the punishments as meant, not for ruin, but for the correction of our people.
6:13 For when the impious are not let go for a long time, but meet their penalties right away, that is a mark of great kindness.
6:14 With the other nations, the Master holds back in patience and waits to punish them until they have come to the point where their sins are full. He has not judged it right to do this with us,
6:15 so that he would not have to punish us afterward, when our sins had run their full course.
6:16 That is why he never removes his mercy from us. He trains his own people by misfortune, yet he never leaves them.
6:17 Let this much be said by us to remind you. In a few words, we must get back to the account.
## The death of Eleazar
6:18 There was Eleazar, one of the foremost scribes, a man getting on in years and with a very fine face. They pried his mouth open and tried to force him to eat pig's meat.
6:19 But he chose a death with good name over a life with defilement. Freely he walked toward the torture frame,
6:20 spitting it out first, the way those must come forward who stand firm to fight off what is forbidden to taste, even out of love of life.
6:21 The men assigned to the lawless sacrificial meal had known him from long ago. They took him aside and called on him to bring in meat he was permitted to use, prepared by himself, and to act as though he were eating the portions of the sacrifice that the king had ordered.
6:22 If he did this he would be let off from death, and because of his old friendship with them he would find kindness.
6:23 But he took up a fine line of reasoning, worthy of his years, of the dignity of old age, of the distinguished way of life he had gained, of his excellent conduct from childhood, and still more of the holy law made by God. So he answered at once, telling them to send him on to Hades.
6:24 "Playacting is not worthy of a man of my age," he said. "Many young people would suppose that Eleazar, ninety years old, had changed over to foreign ways.
6:25 Through my pretending, for the sake of a short and passing bit of life, they would be led astray because of me, and I would bring stain and disgrace on my old age.
6:26 Even if for now I escape the punishment of men, I will not slip out of the hands of the Almighty, alive or dead.
6:27 So if I give up my life bravely now, I will show myself worthy of my old age,
6:28 and to the young I will leave behind a fine pattern: to meet a good death eagerly and bravely for the solemn and holy laws." After saying this, he went at once to the torture frame.
6:29 The men leading him turned the kindness they had felt a moment before into hostility, because they took what he had said to be madness.
6:30 When he was about to die under the blows, he groaned and said, "To the Lord, who holds holy knowledge, it is plain that I could have been let off from death. In my body I bear cruel pains from the whip, but in my soul I suffer this with joy, because of my fear of him."
6:31 That was how this man departed life. He left his death behind as a pattern of nobility and a reminder of virtue, for the young, and for most of the nation too.
## Seven brothers and their mother
7:1 It also happened that seven brothers were arrested along with their mother. The king was trying to force them to touch the forbidden pork, and they were tortured with whips and cords.
7:2 One of them made himself their speaker. "Are you going to question us and find out from us?" he said. "We are ready to die sooner than break the laws of our fathers."
7:3 The king flew into a rage and ordered griddles and kettles to be heated red-hot.
7:4 When they were glowing, the king gave orders: cut out the tongue of the one who had been the spokesman, strip off his scalp the way the Scythians do, and lop off his hands and feet, while his other brothers and his mother stood by watching.
7:5 When he was useless in the rest of his body, the king commanded that he be brought to the fire alive and fried. As the fumes from the pan drifted a long way, they called on one another, with their mother, to meet the end bravely, saying,
7:6 "The Lord God sees, and he is truly moved to pity for us. Moses showed this through the song that testifies to the people's faces, where he says, 'And he will show his servants pity.'"
7:7 After the first had passed away like this, they led the second out for their cruel game. They peeled the skin and hair off his head and kept asking, "Will you eat, before your body is tortured one limb at a time?"
7:8 He replied in his fathers' tongue, "No." So he, too, received the torment that came next, like the first.
7:9 As he drew his last breath he said, "You fiend, you are releasing us from the life we have now. But the King of the world will raise us, who died for his laws, to live again forever."
7:10 After him they tormented the third for their fun. Asked for his tongue, he stuck it out at once, and he stretched out his hands without fear.
7:11 He said with noble spirit, "From Heaven I have these. For the sake of his laws I look past them, and from him I hope to receive them back."
7:12 So the king and his men were amazed at the young man's courage, for he treated the pain as though it were nothing.
7:13 When this one too had departed, they put the fourth to the same torture and mangled him.
7:14 Coming near the end, he spoke like this: "It is worth choosing, to pass from among men and look for the hopes God holds out, that he will raise us again. For you, though, there will be no resurrection to life."
7:15 Right after, they led up the fifth and tortured him.
7:16 He fixed his eyes on the king and said, "You hold power over people and do whatever you like, mortal though you are. Yet do not imagine that our race has been deserted by God.
7:17 Hold on a while and watch his great strength, how he will torment you and your offspring."
7:18 After him they led in the sixth, and when he was near death he said, "Do not go on deceiving yourself. We suffer this because of ourselves; we sinned against our own God. Things to marvel at have come about.
7:19 But you, do not suppose that you will get off free, after you have set out to fight against God."
## The mother speaks
7:20 The mother was good beyond all measure, and she is worth remembering for good. She watched seven sons perish within one day and bore it with a steady heart, because of the hopes she had in the Lord.
7:21 She spoke encouragement to each of them in their fathers' tongue. A noble resolve filled her; she stirred her womanly thinking with a manly heart. She told them,
7:22 "How you showed up in my womb, I cannot say. I was not the one who granted you breath and life, and the make-up of each of you was not put together by me.
7:23 That is why the Creator of the world, who formed how a human being is born and worked out how all things come to be, will give you back the breath and the life in his mercy, just as you now disregard yourselves for the sake of his laws."
7:24 Antiochus thought he was being looked down on, and he eyed the scolding voice with suspicion. While the youngest still lived, the king did more than plead with him in words. He gave his oath as well: he would make him both rich and a man to be envied, once he had left his ancestral ways, and he would have him as a Friend and hand him important tasks.
7:25 The young man would not listen in the least, so the king summoned the mother and pressed her to counsel the boy toward saving his life.
7:26 He pressed her with many words, and she took it on herself to win her son over.
7:27 Bending down to him and laughing at the savage tyrant, she said in their fathers' tongue, "Son, take pity on me. I bore you in my belly nine months. I nursed you three years. I raised you and led you to this age, and I carried the load of caring for you.
7:28 Child, I ask you to look up at the sky and the earth and to see all that is in them. Know that God made them, not from things that were, and the human race comes to be in the same way.
7:29 Have no fear of this executioner. Show yourself worthy of your brothers and welcome death, so that in the mercy to come I may receive you back with your brothers."
## The youngest son's answer
7:30 She was just finishing when the young man said, "Who are you waiting for? I am not listening to the king's order. I listen to the order of the law given to our fathers through Moses.
7:31 You have become the inventor of every wickedness against the Hebrews, but you will not get away from the hands of God.
7:32 We suffer for our own sins.
7:33 If our living Lord has become angry for a short time to correct and train us, he will make peace again with his own servants.
7:34 But you, unholy one, filthiest of all mankind, do not swell up in empty pride, quivering with hopes that come to nothing, as you lift your hand against the servants of Heaven.
7:35 You have not yet run clear of the judgment of God, the Almighty, who sees all.
7:36 For our brothers, after bearing a short pain, have now fallen under God's covenant of ever-flowing life. But you, under God's judgment, will carry off the fitting penalty for your pride.
7:37 Like my brothers, I hand over my body and my life for our fathers' laws. I call, as they did, on God to be gracious soon to the nation, and by torments and whippings to make you admit that he alone is God,
7:38 and in me and my brothers to stop the anger of the Almighty, the anger rightly brought down on our whole race."
7:39 The king, furious, dealt with this one more cruelly than with the rest, for he could not bear being mocked.
7:40 So this one too died pure, trusting fully in the Lord.
7:41 After her sons, last of all, the mother met her end.
7:42 So much, then, for the sacrificial meals and the savage tortures beyond measure; let what has been shown be enough.
