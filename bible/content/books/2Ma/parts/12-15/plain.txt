## New attacks after the treaties
12:1 Once these agreements were concluded, Lysias left for the king, and the Jews were busy working the land.
12:2 But among the regional governors, Timothy and Apollonius son of Gennaeus, then Hieronymus and Demophon, and Nicanor the Cypriarch on top of them, gave the Jews no chance to settle down and enjoy quiet.
## Judas punishes Joppa and Jamnia
12:3 The people of Joppa carried out an impious deed this great: they called on the Jews living in their city to step into boats they had set ready, wives and children too, as though no hostility at all stood between them.
12:4 After the city's common decree, and once the Jews had agreed, since they meant to live at peace and had no thought of danger, the men of Joppa took them out and sent them to the bottom, at least two hundred of them.
12:5 Word reached Judas of the savage thing done to men of his own nation. He gave his men orders,
12:6 called on God the just Judge, and came against those who had shed his brothers' blood. By night he set the harbor ablaze and burned up the boats, and he ran through those who had fled there for safety.
12:7 The town was shut up, so he left, planning to return and uproot the entire citizen body of Joppa.
12:8 He heard too that the people in Jamnia wanted to deal in the same way with the Jews settled there.
12:9 So he fell on the Jamnians at night and set their harbor alight, ships and all. The gleam of the fire showed all the way to Jerusalem, two hundred forty stadia off.
## Battles east of the Jordan
12:10 When they had moved off nine stadia from there, marching against Timothy, Arabs attacked him, no fewer than five thousand, with five hundred horsemen.
12:11 The fighting was fierce, but through God's aid Judas's side came off best. Worsted, the nomads asked Judas for his right hand, pledging to give livestock and to be of use to his men in everything else.
12:12 Judas judged that they truly would be of service in many ways, and he consented to be at peace with them. Hands were clasped, and they went away to their tents.
12:13 He also moved against a certain town, building a causeway to reach it. It was strong, walled all around, and lived in by people of every nation mixed together. Its name was Caspin.
12:14 The people inside trusted in the strength of their walls and in their store of food. They treated Judas's men with contempt, mocking them, blaspheming as well, and saying things no one should say.
12:15 But Judas's men called on the great Sovereign of the world, who brought Jericho crashing down in Joshua's day without rams or machines of war, and they threw themselves at the wall like wild beasts.
12:16 They took the town by the will of God and made a slaughter beyond words, so that the lake beside it, two stadia wide, seemed to be filled with the blood that ran down into it.
12:17 From there they went on seven hundred fifty stadia and reached the stockade, among the Jews who are called Tubians.
12:18 Timothy they did not catch in those parts. He had pulled out of the area by then with nothing to show for it, though he had left behind a garrison, and a very strong one, in a certain place.
12:19 Dositheus and Sosipater, officers of Maccabeus, went out and wiped out the men Timothy had left in the stronghold, over ten thousand of them.
12:20 Maccabeus drew up the army around him company by company and set them over the companies. Then they rushed against Timothy, who had with him a hundred twenty thousand foot soldiers, and two thousand horsemen on top of seven thousand.
12:21 Hearing of Judas's advance, Timothy sent the women and the rest of the baggage on ahead to the place called Carnion, a spot hard to besiege and hard to get at, since all the ground around it was so cramped.
12:22 As soon as Judas's leading company came into view, the enemy were seized with dread. Fear came upon them from the appearance of the one who sees all things, and they broke and ran, scattering every which way, so that many times their own comrades wounded them and they were run through on the tips of their own swords.
12:23 Judas chased them all the more fiercely, stabbing the villains as he went, and he killed some thirty thousand men.
12:24 Timothy himself ran into the forces of Dositheus and Sosipater. With a great show of cunning he pleaded with them to release him unharmed: he had the parents of many of them, he said, and the brothers of others, and it would go hard with these.
12:25 Once he had sworn to it many times over, that he would hand them back without harm, they released him, to keep their brothers safe.
12:26 Judas went out against Carnion and the shrine of Atargatis, and he cut down twenty-five thousand people.
12:27 When these had been turned back and destroyed, he went to war against Ephron too, a fortress town with crowds of every race inside. Sturdy young men were posted before the walls, defending them with vigor, and there was a large supply of machines and missiles on hand.
12:28 They called on the Sovereign who crushes the strength of enemies with his might, and they took the town into their power. Of those inside they struck down some twenty-five thousand.
12:29 Moving on from there, they pressed on to Scythopolis, six hundred stadia from Jerusalem.
12:30 But the Jews settled there spoke up for the people of Scythopolis: they had been well disposed toward them and had met them gently when bad times came.
12:31 They gave thanks, and further urged them to stay kind toward the Jewish people in time to come. Then they arrived in Jerusalem, with the Feast of Weeks just ahead.
## The battle with Gorgias
12:32 After the feast called Pentecost, they set out in haste against Gorgias, governor of Idumea.
12:33 He came out with three thousand foot soldiers and four hundred horsemen.
12:34 They drew up for battle, and as it turned out a few of the Jews fell.
12:35 One Dositheus, of Bacenor's men, a mounted man and a strong one, kept hold of Gorgias. He seized his cloak and was hauling him along with all his strength, wanting to take the accursed man prisoner alive. But a Thracian horseman charged him and took off his shoulder, and Gorgias got away to Marisa.
12:36 Esdris's men had fought on and on and were exhausted, and Judas called on the Lord to appear as their ally, to appear as the one who goes ahead of them in the war.
12:37 He began the shout, with hymns, in his fathers' tongue. Then he rushed on Gorgias's men before they could expect it, and routed them.
## Prayer and sacrifice for the fallen
12:38 Judas took his army and came to the town of Adullam. Since the week was coming round to its seventh day, they made themselves clean according to custom and spent the Sabbath in that place.
12:39 The next day, since the need had now arisen, Judas's men came to recover the bodies of the fallen and to restore them, with their kin, to their family tombs.
12:40 But on every one of the dead, beneath the tunic, they found objects consecrated to the idols of Jamnia, things the law keeps the Jews away from. To all of them it was plain that this was why these men had fallen.
12:41 So they all blessed what the Lord had done, the just Judge who brings hidden things into the open,
12:42 and they turned to pleading. They asked that the sin that had been done be wiped away entirely. And the noble Judas called on the people to keep clear of sin, for they had seen right in front of them what came about through the sin of the fallen.
12:43 He also collected money from each man, amounting to two thousand silver drachmas, and sent it to Jerusalem to present a sacrifice for sin. In doing so he did a very fine and gracious thing, for he had the resurrection in mind.
12:44 For if he had not expected the fallen to rise again, it would have been pointless and silly to pray for the dead.
12:45 But if he had his eyes on the finest gift of thanks, the one stored up for those who fall asleep in godliness, then it was a holy and godly thought. That is why he had atonement made for the dead, to free them from the sin.
## The death of Menelaus
13:1 In the year 149 the news came to Judas and his men that Antiochus Eupator was advancing on Judea with masses of troops,
13:2 and Lysias with him, the king's guardian and chief minister. Each brought an army of Greeks: a hundred ten thousand on foot, five thousand three hundred on horseback, twenty-two elephants and three hundred scythed chariots.
13:3 Menelaus threw in his lot with them too, and with much false show he egged Antiochus on, not to save his homeland, but in the belief that he would be set up in office.
13:4 But the King of kings woke Antiochus's fury against the villain. Lysias pointed him out as the cause of every evil, and the king commanded that he be brought to Beroea and done away with there, by the custom of that place.
13:5 There is a tower in that place fifty cubits high, full of ashes. It had a device, round in shape, that fell away sheer on every side into the ash.
13:6 Anyone found guilty of robbing a temple, or of going to the limit in some other crime, is pushed off there by everyone, to his destruction.
13:7 By such a fate the renegade Menelaus came to die, without even being given earth for a grave.
13:8 And it was perfectly fair. He had sinned much around the altar, whose fire was pure, and its ash too, so in ash he received his death.
## Victory near Modein
13:9 The king was on his way, heavy with arrogant purposes, to let the Jews see the very worst of what had happened under his father.
13:10 Learning of this, Judas told the crowd to cry to the Lord by day and by night, that if ever before, then now too, he would come to the aid of
13:11 a people about to be robbed of the law, of their homeland and of the holy temple, and that he would not allow those who had barely caught their breath to be made subject to the blaspheming nations.
13:12 All of them did so together, pleading with the merciful Lord through weeping and fasting and lying on the ground for three days on end. Then Judas spoke to encourage them and told them to report for duty.
13:13 Taking counsel apart with the elders, he resolved to go out and decide the issue with the help of God, before the royal army could break into Judea and become master of the city.
13:14 He left the outcome to the Lord of the world. He urged his men to fight nobly, to the death, for the laws, the temple, the city, the homeland and their way of life, and he set up camp near Modein.
13:15 He passed the password "God's victory" to those around him. Then with the finest of the young men, chosen men, he struck at the royal quarters by night. He wiped out as many as two thousand men in the camp, and he laid low the chief of the elephants, together with the man in its housing.
13:16 Finally they left the whole camp full of panic and uproar, and they got away with success.
13:17 It was all done as day was beginning to show, by the shelter of the Lord, which stood by him.
## Beth-zur and the peace
13:18 Having now tasted Jewish boldness, the king probed their positions with schemes.
13:19 He came at Beth-zur, a small Jewish fort. He moved up, was turned back, struck again, came off worse.
13:20 Judas sent in to the men inside what they needed.
13:21 But Rhodocus, from the Jewish ranks, gave away secrets to the enemy. He was hunted down, caught and locked up.
13:22 The king talked a second time with the people in Beth-zur. He gave his right hand, took theirs, went away,
13:23 made an attack on Judas's men, and got the worse of it. He heard that Philip, left at Antioch over the government, had rebelled in desperation. He was thrown into confusion. He pleaded with the Jews, gave way, and took an oath on all fair terms. He was reconciled and brought a sacrifice. He paid honor to the temple and the place, and showed kindness.
13:24 He welcomed Maccabeus, and he left Hegemonides as governor from Ptolemais as far as the Gennerians.
13:25 He came to Ptolemais. The Ptolemaians took the agreement badly; they were frightened beyond measure, and they wished to set its terms aside.
13:26 Lysias stepped up to the platform and argued his case as well as it allowed. He persuaded them, softened them, put them in a good humor, and went off to Antioch. So went the king's march in and his march out.
## Alcimus accuses Judas
14:1 After a span of three years, news came to Judas and his men: Demetrius son of Seleucus had put in at the port of Tripolis with a powerful army and a fleet,
14:2 had become master of the land, and had killed Antiochus and his guardian Lysias.
14:3 There was one Alcimus, high priest before, who of his own free will had made himself unclean in the days of keeping apart. He knew that he had no way at all to be safe, and no road back to the holy altar.
14:4 So in about the year 151 he presented himself to King Demetrius, bringing him a golden crown and a palm, and some of the temple's customary branches as well. For that day he said nothing.
14:5 But he got a chance to further his own madness: Demetrius summoned him to the council and asked him how the Jews were disposed and what they meant to do. His reply was this:
14:6 "The Jews they call Hasideans, led by Judas Maccabeus, feed the war and breed rebellion, and they will not allow the kingdom to settle down.
14:7 That is why I, robbed of my ancestral glory, I mean the high priesthood, have now come a second time,
14:8 for one thing, because my loyalty to what belongs to the king is sincere, and for another, because I have my own countrymen in view. The thoughtlessness of the people I spoke of has brought our entire race into no little misery.
14:9 You, O king, now know every one of these facts. Look out for the land and for our people, pressed on all sides, with the ready kindness you show to everyone you meet.
14:10 While Judas survives, public affairs cannot come to peace."
14:11 These were his words, and at once the other Friends, who bore Judas ill will, fanned Demetrius's anger hotter.
## Nicanor and Judas
14:12 Without delay he picked out Nicanor, once master of the elephants, named him governor of Judea and dispatched him,
14:13 giving him letters: he was to kill Judas himself, disperse his followers, and make Alcimus high priest of the greatest temple.
14:14 Then the Gentiles of Judea who had run from Judas came over to Nicanor in herds. They imagined that Jewish ruin and disaster would be their own success.
14:15 Hearing of Nicanor's advance and the Gentiles' attack, the Jews threw earth over themselves and pleaded with the one who set up his people to last for all time, and who always helps his own portion with an appearance.
14:16 Their leader gave the order. He broke camp at once and closed with them at the village of Dessau.
14:17 Simon, Judas's brother, had joined battle with Nicanor, but he was slow about it, and he stumbled because the enemy were suddenly struck speechless.
14:18 All the same, Nicanor had heard of the bravery of Judas's men and their high spirit in the fights for their country, and he was wary of having the issue decided in blood.
14:19 So he sent Posidonius, Theodotus and Mattathias to give and receive right hands.
14:20 There was long study of the matter. The leader laid it before the troops, and when their verdict proved to be the same, they nodded assent to the agreement.
14:21 They fixed a day for the two to come together privately. From each side a seat was carried forward, and chairs were put down.
14:22 Judas had armed men stationed, ready, at the right points, in case the enemy suddenly tried something wicked. The talks they held were fitting.
14:23 Nicanor spent time in Jerusalem and did nothing improper, and he dismissed the herds of people that had collected.
14:24 He kept Judas with him all the time. He had grown fond of the man with all his heart.
14:25 He pressed him to take a wife and raise a family. Judas wed, lived quietly, and took his part in everyday life.
14:26 But Alcimus, seeing how well the two got on, took the written terms and went to Demetrius. Nicanor, he said, had aims foreign to the state's interests, for he had appointed Judas, who schemed against his kingdom, as successor.
14:27 The king was beside himself, goaded by the lies of that thoroughly evil man. He wrote to Nicanor, declaring that he took the agreement very badly and ordering him to ship Maccabeus to Antioch quickly, in chains.
14:28 When this reached Nicanor he was shaken. It weighed on him to cancel what had been agreed, though the man had committed no offense.
14:29 Opposing the king was out of the question, so he kept looking for the right moment to get it done by a ruse.
14:30 Maccabeus could see that Nicanor was colder in his dealings and blunter in their usual meetings, and he guessed that this sternness meant nothing good. He drew together a good number of his men and kept out of Nicanor's sight.
## Nicanor threatens the temple
14:31 The other realized that he had been well outgeneraled by the man. He came to the great and holy temple at the hour the priests were bringing the proper sacrifices, and told them to surrender the man.
14:32 They swore that they had no idea where the man he wanted might be.
14:33 He held out his right hand toward the temple and swore: "If you do not give Judas up to me in chains, I will make this precinct of God a bare field. I will dig the altar out, and in this very spot I will put up a temple to Dionysus for all to see."
14:34 After saying all this he left. The priests lifted their hands toward heaven and called on the one who at all times has been our nation's champion, saying:
14:35 "You, Lord of all things, who lack nothing, chose with pleasure to have a temple for your dwelling among us.
14:36 And now, holy Lord of every holy thing, keep this house, newly cleansed, from all defilement for all time."
## The death of Razis
14:37 Someone informed Nicanor about Razis, an elder of Jerusalem. He was a man devoted to his people, with a very high reputation, and because of the kindness he showed he was known as father of the Jews.
14:38 For back in the days of keeping apart he had been brought to trial for Judaism, and he had staked body and soul on Judaism with all his strength.
14:39 Nicanor wished to put on show his hostility toward the Jews, and he dispatched over five hundred soldiers to seize him.
14:40 He reckoned that capturing that man would bring disaster on them.
14:41 The crowds were on the point of seizing the tower, pressing hard on the courtyard gate, and shouting for fire to set the doors alight. Hemmed in on every side, Razis turned his sword on himself.
14:42 He wanted a noble death sooner than to become the prisoner of villains and suffer insults that his high birth did not deserve.
14:43 But in the hurry of the fight the stroke went wrong. As the throng burst inside the doorways, he raced bravely up onto the wall and flung himself off, like a true man, into the throng below.
14:44 They pulled back fast, leaving a gap, and he came down in the middle of the open ground.
14:45 Breath was still in him, and he was on fire with fury. He got to his feet, with blood gushing out of him in streams and his wounds dreadful, ran right through the crowds, and stood on a rock that dropped away sharply.
14:46 By now he was completely drained of blood. He pulled his bowels out, seized them with both hands, and flung them at the crowds. He called on the one who rules over life and spirit to give these back to him again. And so he passed from life.
## Nicanor's boast
15:1 Nicanor got word that Judas's forces were in the districts around Samaria, and he planned to fall on them on the day of rest, in perfect safety.
15:2 The Jews who were made to march with him said, "Do not slaughter them in so wild and barbarous a way. Pay honor to the day that the one who sees all things has set in honor before others, and made holy."
15:3 Then the thrice-cursed villain demanded to know whether there was a Sovereign in heaven who had ordered the Sabbath day to be observed.
15:4 They answered plainly, "There is: the Lord, living, himself a Sovereign in heaven, who told us to practice the seventh day."
15:5 The other said, "And I am a sovereign on earth, ordering you to bear arms and get the king's work done." Yet he did not manage to carry out his cruel plan.
## Judas's dream
15:6 Nicanor, holding his head high in all his self-assurance, had made up his mind to set up a public trophy over Judas and his men.
15:7 Maccabeus, though, kept up his trust without a break, and his full hope, that he would win support from the Lord.
15:8 He told his men not to be cowards before the advancing Gentiles. They should bear in mind the aid from heaven they had already had, and they should count on it now: victory would come to them from the Almighty.
15:9 He gave them heart from the Law and the Prophets, praised to them besides the contests they had carried through, and so roused them to greater zeal.
15:10 With their spirits fired up, he gave them their orders, while he also laid bare the Gentiles' lack of faith and their breaking of oaths.
15:11 He equipped every one of them, not with the protection of shield and spear, but rather with the encouragement good words give. He told them a dream that deserved belief, and it filled them all with joy beyond measure.
15:12 This is what he had seen. Onias, the former high priest, a man truly good, shy in meeting people and mild in his ways, apt in his speech, and from boyhood schooled in all that belongs to virtue: he had his hands held out and was praying for the whole Jewish community.
15:13 Next, in the same way, a man had appeared who stood out by his white hair and his glory, and the greatness that surrounded him was wonderful and full of splendor.
15:14 Onias spoke up: "This is the lover of his brothers, the one who prays much for the people and the holy city, Jeremiah the prophet of God."
15:15 Jeremiah reached out his right hand and passed Judas a sword of gold, and as he handed it over he spoke these words:
15:16 "Take the holy sword, a gift from God. With it you will smash your enemies."
## Victory over Nicanor
15:17 Judas's words, very fine and able to spur men to courage and make young souls manly, lifted them. They resolved not to wage a long campaign, but to throw themselves bravely into the attack, to grapple with the enemy with every bit of courage and so decide the matter, because the city, the holy place and the temple were at risk.
15:18 Fear for wives and children, and for brothers and kin as well, counted for less with them. The biggest fear, and the first, was for the hallowed temple.
15:19 And the people held fast inside the city felt no slight anguish, upset as they were about the clash out in the open.
15:20 By now everyone was waiting for the coming trial. The enemy had made contact, their force was marshaled, the beasts were posted where they would be most useful, and the horse was ranged on the flanks.
15:21 Maccabeus took in the presence of the masses, the varied equipment of their weapons and the savagery of the beasts. He lifted his hands up to heaven and called on the Lord who does marvels. He knew that it does not depend on arms: as the Lord himself judges, so he secures the victory for the deserving.
15:22 This was the way he called on him: "You, Master, sent your angel when Hezekiah was king of Judea, and he did away with a hundred eighty-five thousand from the camp of Sennacherib.
15:23 So now, Sovereign of the heavens, send a good angel ahead of us, to bring dread and shaking.
15:24 Let the greatness of your arm strike terror into those who come against your holy people with blasphemy." And there he stopped.
15:25 Nicanor's forces moved up to the sound of trumpets and war chants.
15:26 Judas's men, though, closed with the enemy with cries to God and with prayers.
15:27 With their hands they fought, and with their hearts they prayed to God, and they brought down no less than thirty-five thousand. God's care for them made them very glad.
15:28 When they were done with the work and were heading back in joy, they found that Nicanor had fallen, armor and all.
15:29 Shouts and uproar broke out, and they blessed the Sovereign in their fathers' tongue.
15:30 Then the man who in every way, body and soul, stood in the front line for his fellow citizens, and who had kept the warm feeling of his young days for his own people, gave orders to cut off Nicanor's head, and his hand with the shoulder, and take them to Jerusalem.
15:31 He got there, called his countrymen together, placed the priests in front of the altar, and summoned the people from the Citadel.
15:32 He displayed the head of the foul Nicanor, and the hand of that blasphemer, which he had held out in his bragging against the holy house of the Almighty.
15:33 He cut out the impious Nicanor's tongue and said it should be fed to the birds bit by bit, and that the pay for his madness should be hung up facing the temple.
15:34 Every one of them sent up blessing toward heaven to the Lord who appears, and they said, "Blessed be the one who has kept his own place free from defilement!"
15:35 He tied Nicanor's head up on the Citadel for everyone to see, a plain token of the Lord's help.
15:36 All of them voted together and made it law that this day should by no means go by without a mark of honor. They would keep the thirteenth of the twelfth month as a special day (in the Syrian language the month is called Adar), one day before Mordecai's Day.
## The writer's farewell
15:37 That is the way things went with Nicanor. From those times on the Hebrews held the city. And so I, for my part, will stop my account right here.
15:38 If it is well written, and its arrangement hits the mark, that is what I myself was hoping for. If it is thin and middling, that was all within my reach.
15:39 Wine taken on its own is bad for you, and water on its own likewise. But wine blended with water is pleasant and brings real delight. In the same way, a well-built story charms the ears of those who come across the work. Here, then, will be the ending.
