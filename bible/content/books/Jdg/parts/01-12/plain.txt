## Judah goes up first
1:1 After Joshua died, the Israelites asked the LORD, "Who will go up for us at the start, to fight the Canaanites?"
1:2 "Judah is the one to go up," said the LORD. "Look, I have handed the land over to him."
1:3 Judah said to his brother Simeon, "Come up with me into the land that fell to me by lot, and we will fight the Canaanites. After that I will go with you into your lot as well." And Simeon went along with him.
1:4 Judah went up, and the LORD put the Canaanites and the Perizzites in their power. They struck them down at Bezek, ten thousand men.
1:5 At Bezek they came upon Adoni-bezek. They fought him there and struck down the Canaanites and the Perizzites.
1:6 Adoni-bezek fled. They ran after him and seized him, and they cut off the thumbs of his hands and the big toes of his feet.
1:7 Adoni-bezek said, "Seventy kings, the thumbs of their hands and feet cut off, went gathering under my table. What I did to others, God has now done to me." They took him to Jerusalem, where he died.
1:8 The people of Judah made war on Jerusalem and captured it. They struck it down with the sword and sent the town up in flames.
## Caleb, Achsah and the Kenites
1:9 Later the people of Judah went down to make war on the Canaanites of the hill country, the Negev and the Shephelah.
1:10 Judah marched against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron. Hebron used to be called Kiriath-arba. They struck down Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.
1:11 From there Judah went on against the people living in Debir. Debir used to be called Kiriath-sepher.
1:12 Caleb said, "Whoever attacks Kiriath-sepher and captures it, I will give him my daughter Achsah as his wife."
1:13 Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother, captured it. So Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah as his wife.
1:14 When she came to him, she pressed him to ask her father for a field. She got down from her donkey, and Caleb said to her, "What is it?"
1:15 She answered, "Give me a blessing. You have given me Negev land, so give me pools of water too." So Caleb gave her the upper pools and the lower pools.
1:16 The family of the Kenite who was Moses' father-in-law came up with the people of Judah from the City of Palms to the wilderness of Judah, in the Negev of Arad. There they settled down among the people.
1:17 Then Judah went with his brother Simeon, and they struck down the Canaanites who lived in Zephath. They put the town under the ban, and so it was named Hormah.
1:18 Judah captured Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, and Ekron and its territory.
1:19 The LORD was with Judah, and Judah took possession of the hill country. But there was no driving out the people who lived in the valley, for they had iron chariots.
1:20 As Moses had said, they gave Hebron to Caleb, and he drove the three sons of Anak out of there.
1:21 As for the Jebusites living in Jerusalem, the Benjaminites did not drive them out, and the Jebusites have stayed on among the Benjaminites in Jerusalem until today.
## Bethel, and the towns not taken
1:22 Then the house of Joseph went up too. Their target was Bethel, and the LORD was with them.
1:23 The house of Joseph had Bethel scouted. The town used to be called Luz.
1:24 The lookouts spotted a man leaving the town and said to him, "Show us, please, where the way in is, and we will treat you with loyal love."
1:25 He showed them the way in, and they struck the town with the sword. But the man and all his clan they let go.
1:26 The man went off to Hittite country and built a town, giving it the name Luz. It is still called that today.
1:27 Manasseh did not take possession of Beth-shean and its villages or Taanach and its villages. Nor did it drive out the people of Dor and its villages, the people of Ibleam and its villages, or the people of Megiddo and its villages. The Canaanites were set on living in that land.
1:28 As Israel gained strength, they made the Canaanites do forced labor. But as for driving them out, they did not do it.
1:29 Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites of Gezer either, and the Canaanites lived on in Gezer in Ephraim's midst.
1:30 Zebulun did not drive out the people of Kitron or the people of Nahalol. The Canaanites lived on among Zebulun, and they became forced laborers.
1:31 Asher did not drive out the people of Acco or the people of Sidon, or of Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphek or Rehob.
1:32 Since Asher did not drive them out, the Asherites made their home in the middle of the Canaanites who lived in the land.
1:33 Naphtali did not drive out the people of Beth-shemesh or the people of Beth-anath. Naphtali settled in the middle of the Canaanites who lived in the land, and the people of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath were put to forced labor for them.
1:34 The Amorites crowded the Danites into the hill country. They would not let them come down into the valley.
1:35 The Amorites were set on living in Mount Heres, in Aijalon and in Shaalbim. But the hand of the house of Joseph pressed heavy on them, and they became forced laborers.
1:36 The border of the Amorites ran from the Scorpion Pass, from Sela, and on upward.
## Weeping at Bochim
2:1 The angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, "I am the one who brought you up from Egypt and took you into the land I promised your ancestors with an oath. I said, 'I will not break my covenant with you, ever.
2:2 As for you, make no covenant with the people of this land. Pull down their altars.' But you did not listen to my voice. What is this you have done?
2:3 So I tell you now: I will not drive them out ahead of you. They will become barbs in your sides, and their gods will become a trap for you."
2:4 The angel of the LORD spoke these words to all the Israelites, and the people raised their voices in weeping.
2:5 They gave the place the name Bochim, and they sacrificed to the LORD there.
## A generation that did not know
2:6 Joshua then let the people go, and the Israelites went, each to his own inheritance, to take possession of the land.
2:7 As long as Joshua was alive, the people served the LORD. They went on serving him as long as the elders who lived on after Joshua were alive, men who had seen the whole great work the LORD had done for Israel.
2:8 Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died. He had lived a hundred and ten years.
2:9 They buried him within the borders of his own inheritance, at Timnath-heres in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.
2:10 That whole generation, too, was gathered to its ancestors. Then came a generation after them that knew nothing of the LORD, or of the work he had done for Israel.
## The pattern of the judges
2:11 Then the Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD: they served the Baals.
2:12 They left the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They went after other gods, gods of the peoples all around them. They bowed down to those gods and made the LORD angry.
2:13 They left the LORD and served Baal and the Ashtoreths.
2:14 Then the LORD's anger blazed against Israel. He put them in the power of raiders, who looted them. He sold them to the enemies around them, and they could no longer hold their ground against their enemies.
2:15 Wherever they went, the hand of the LORD was against them for harm, as the LORD had warned and as he had sworn to them, and their distress was very great.
2:16 The LORD would raise up judges, and the judges would rescue them from their looters' power.
2:17 Yet even to their judges they would not listen. Instead they went after other gods like a prostitute and bowed down to them. They soon turned off the road their ancestors had walked, the road of obeying the LORD's commands. They did not do as their ancestors had done.
2:18 When the LORD raised up judges for them, he stood with each judge, and all the judge's days he rescued them from their enemies' power. The LORD was moved to pity by their groaning under those who crushed them and shoved them around.
2:19 Once the judge was dead, they would go back and behave worse than their ancestors, going after other gods, serving them and bowing down to them. They dropped none of their practices or their stubborn path.
2:20 So the LORD's anger blazed against Israel. He said, "This nation has broken my covenant that I laid on their ancestors, and they have not listened to my voice.
2:21 I for my part will drive out from before them not one more of the nations Joshua left behind at his death.
2:22 Through these nations I will test Israel: will they keep to the way of the LORD and walk in it, as their ancestors did, or not?"
2:23 That is why the LORD let these nations stay. He did not drive them out quickly, and he had not handed them over to Joshua.
## Nations left to test Israel
3:1 Here are the nations the LORD left in place to test Israel with, all the Israelites who had not been through any of the wars for Canaan.
3:2 He did it only so that the generations of Israel would come to know war, to teach it to those who had not known it before.
3:3 They were the five rulers of the Philistines and all the Canaanites, along with the Sidonians and the Hivites who lived on Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon to Lebo-hamath.
3:4 They were there to put Israel to the test, to find out whether they would listen to the commands the LORD gave their ancestors by Moses' hand.
3:5 And so the Israelites made their homes in the middle of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.
3:6 They married those peoples' daughters, handed their own daughters to those peoples' sons, and served their gods.
## Othniel
3:7 The Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD. Forgetting the LORD their God, they served the Baals and the Asherahs.
3:8 The LORD's anger blazed against Israel, and he sold them into the power of Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim. The Israelites served Cushan-rishathaim for eight years.
3:9 Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD. The LORD raised up a rescuer for them, and he rescued them. It was Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.
3:10 Othniel had the Spirit of the LORD on him, and he judged Israel. When he went out to war, the LORD put Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram in his power, and he got the upper hand over Cushan-rishathaim.
3:11 So the land was quiet for forty years. Then Othniel son of Kenaz died.
## Ehud and the king of Moab
3:12 Once again the Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD. Because they did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, the LORD made Eglon king of Moab strong against Israel.
3:13 Eglon brought the Ammonites and Amalek in with him. He came and struck Israel, and they took possession of the City of Palms.
3:14 For eighteen years the Israelites served Eglon king of Moab.
3:15 The Israelites cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a rescuer for them: Ehud son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a man hampered in his right hand. The Israelites sent their tribute to Eglon king of Moab by him.
3:16 Ehud had made himself a sword with two edges, a short cubit long, and he belted it on under his clothes, over his right thigh.
3:17 He handed over the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Eglon was a very fat man.
3:18 When he was done presenting it, Ehud sent off the men who had carried the tribute.
3:19 He himself, though, turned around at the carved stones by Gilgal and came back. "O king," he said, "I have a secret word for you." The king said, "Hush!" and all who stood by him went out.
3:20 Ehud came in to him. The king was sitting alone in his cool upper room. Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you." The king got up from his throne.
3:21 With his left hand Ehud reached out, took the sword off his right thigh, and drove it into his belly.
3:22 The hilt followed the blade in, and the fat shut around the blade, because Ehud did not draw the sword back out of his belly. And the waste came out.
3:23 Ehud slipped out into the porch, shutting the doors of the upper room behind him and locking them.
3:24 Once he was gone, the king's servants came in and looked: the doors of the upper room were locked. "He must be covering his feet in the cool room," they said.
3:25 They waited until they felt ashamed, yet the doors of the upper room stayed shut. So they took the key and opened them, and there lay their master on the ground, dead.
3:26 While they hung back, Ehud got clear. He went past the carved stones and made his escape to Seirah.
3:27 On arriving, he sounded the ram's horn in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites came down from the hills with him, Ehud in front.
3:28 He told them, "Come after me! The LORD has put your enemies, Moab, in your power." They went down behind him and took control of the Jordan fords toward Moab, and they let no one cross.
3:29 They killed about ten thousand men of Moab then, every one of them fat and strong. Not a man got away.
3:30 So Moab was brought low that day under the hand of Israel, and the land was quiet for eighty years.
3:31 Next after Ehud came Shamgar son of Anath. With an ox goad he struck down six hundred Philistine men, and he as well was a rescuer of Israel.
## Deborah and Barak
4:1 Ehud was dead, and once again the Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
4:2 So the LORD sold them to Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. His army commander was Sisera, and Sisera lived in Harosheth-haggoyim.
4:3 The Israelites cried out to the LORD, because Jabin had nine hundred iron chariots, and he had crushed the Israelites by force for twenty years.
4:4 Now Deborah, a woman and a prophet, the wife of Lappidoth, was the one judging Israel at that time.
4:5 She used to sit beneath Deborah's Palm, between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites would go up to her for judgment.
4:6 She sent word and called Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali. She said to him, "The LORD, the God of Israel, has given you an order, hasn't he? 'Go, draw your men toward Mount Tabor, and take ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun with you.
4:7 I will draw Sisera, the commander of Jabin's army, out to you at the Kishon River, with his chariots and his troops, and I will put him in your power.'"
4:8 Barak said to her, "If you go with me, I will go. But if you do not go with me, I will not go."
4:9 She answered, "I will go with you, of course. But on this road you are taking, the glory will not be yours, because the LORD will sell Sisera into a woman's hands." So Deborah set out and went with Barak to Kedesh.
4:10 Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together at Kedesh. Ten thousand men went up behind him, and Deborah went up with him.
4:11 Heber the Kenite had separated himself from the Kenites, the descendants of Hobab, Moses' father-in-law, and had pitched his tent as far off as the great tree at Zaanannim, near Kedesh.
4:12 Sisera got word that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up on Mount Tabor.
4:13 So Sisera called out all his chariots, nine hundred iron chariots, and all the troops with him, from Harosheth-haggoyim to the Kishon River.
4:14 Deborah said to Barak, "Get up! Today is the day the LORD has put Sisera in your power. The LORD has marched out ahead of you, hasn't he?" So Barak came down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men came after him.
4:15 Before Barak the LORD threw Sisera into panic, with all his chariots and all his army, under the sword. Sisera jumped down from his chariot and fled on foot.
4:16 Barak chased the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth-haggoyim. The whole army of Sisera went down under the sword; not even one was left.
## Jael and the tent peg
4:17 But Sisera had run off on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, because there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.
4:18 Jael came out to meet Sisera. "Turn aside, my lord," she said to him. "Turn aside to me. Do not be afraid." He turned aside to her, into the tent, and she covered him with a rug.
4:19 "Please, a little water to drink," he said to her. "I am thirsty." She opened the skin of milk, let him drink from it, and covered him again.
4:20 He said to her, "Stand in the tent door. If someone comes and asks you, 'Is there a man in here?' tell him, 'No.'"
4:21 Then Jael, Heber's wife, took a tent peg and put the hammer in her hand. She came to him quietly and drove the peg into his temple until it went down into the ground. He had been fast asleep, worn out, and he died.
4:22 Just then Barak came up, chasing Sisera, and Jael went out to meet him. "Come," she told him, "and I will show you the man you are looking for." He came to her, and there lay Sisera, dead, with the peg in his temple.
4:23 So on that day God brought Jabin king of Canaan low before the Israelites.
4:24 And the hand of the Israelites bore down ever harder on Jabin king of Canaan, until they had cut Jabin king of Canaan off.
## The Song of Deborah
5:1 Then Deborah sang on that day, with Barak son of Abinoam. They said:
5:2 When hair flies loose in Israel, / when the people offer themselves freely, / bless the LORD!
5:3 Listen, you kings! Pay attention, you rulers! / I, yes I, will sing to the LORD; / I will make music for the LORD, the God of Israel.
5:4 O LORD, as you came out of Seir, / as you strode from the land of Edom, / the earth quaked, the skies dripped, / the clouds dripped with water.
5:5 Mountains ran like water at the LORD's presence, / the One of Sinai, / at the presence of the LORD, the God of Israel.
5:6 In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, / in the days of Jael, the caravans stopped; / those who walked the roads took crooked side paths.
5:7 Village life stopped in Israel, it stopped, / till I rose up, Deborah, / rose up, a mother in Israel.
5:8 They chose new gods; / then the fighting reached the gates. / Could a shield be seen, or a spear, / among forty thousand in Israel?
5:9 My heart goes out to Israel's leaders, / the volunteers among the people. / Bless the LORD!
5:10 You who ride on tawny donkeys, / you who sit on fine rugs, / you who walk the road, tell it out!
5:11 At the sound of those who share out water at the wells, / there they retell the LORD's just deeds, / the just deeds of his villagers in Israel. / Then the people of the LORD went down to the gates.
5:12 Up, up, Deborah! / Up, up, speak out a song! / Rise up, Barak, / and lead off your captives, son of Abinoam!
5:13 Then the survivors marched down to the nobles; / the people of the LORD marched down for me against the warriors.
5:14 From Ephraim they came, whose root is in Amalek. / Benjamin came behind you, among your troops. / From Machir came down commanders, / and from Zebulun those who wield the marshal's staff.
5:15 My princes in Issachar were with Deborah, / and as Issachar was, so was Barak, / sent down into the valley at his heels. / In Reuben's divisions / great were the plans of the heart.
5:16 Why did you stay sitting between the saddlebags, / listening to the pipes played for the flocks? / In Reuben's divisions / hearts searched and searched.
5:17 Gilead camped on the far side of the Jordan. / And Dan, why did he hang around the ships? / Asher sat still by the shore of the seas / and stayed by his harbors.
5:18 Zebulun was a people who threw their lives away to the death, / and so was Naphtali, on the high fields.
5:19 Kings came, they fought; / then the kings of Canaan fought / at Taanach, by the waters of Megiddo, / but no silver did they carry off.
5:20 The stars fought from heaven; / from their highways they fought against Sisera.
5:21 The Kishon River swept them away, / the river of old, the Kishon River. / Tread on, my soul, with strength!
5:22 Then the horses' hooves pounded: / the galloping, the galloping of his stallions.
5:23 "Curse Meroz," says the angel of the LORD. / "Curse, yes curse, the people who live there, / for they did not come to the LORD's aid, / to the LORD's aid against the strong."
5:24 Blessed above women be Jael, / the wife of Heber the Kenite; / above women in the tent may she be blessed.
5:25 Water he asked; milk she gave. / In a bowl fit for chiefs she brought him cream.
5:26 She put out her hand for the peg, / her right hand for the laborer's mallet. / She struck Sisera, she smashed his head, / she broke through and split his temple.
5:27 Between her feet he bent down, he fell, he lay; / between her feet he bent down, he fell. / Where he bent down, there he fell, ruined.
5:28 From the window she looked down and wailed, / Sisera's mother, through the lattice: / "What keeps his chariot from coming? / What holds back the hoofbeats of his chariots?"
5:29 Her wise noblewomen answer her, / and she too repeats her own words to herself:
5:30 "Aren't they finding the plunder and sharing it out? / A womb or two for every man; / plunder of dyed cloth for Sisera, / plunder of dyed cloth, embroidered, / dyed cloth, two pieces embroidered, / for the necks of the plunder?"
5:31 So may all your enemies die, O LORD! / And may those who love him be like the sun / as it goes out in its strength. / And the land was quiet for forty years.
## Midian's raids, and a prophet
6:1 The Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD handed them over to Midian for seven years.
6:2 Midian's hand was too strong for Israel. Because of Midian the Israelites made themselves dens in the mountains, as well as caves and strongholds.
6:3 Each time Israel sowed seed, up came Midian and Amalek and the people of the East. They came up against Israel.
6:4 They would pitch camp against them and ruin what the land produced, as far as the edge of Gaza. They left Israel nothing to live on, and no sheep, cattle or donkeys.
6:5 For they would come up with their cattle and their tents, coming in as thick as a swarm of locusts. Neither they nor their camels could be counted, and they entered the land to ruin it.
6:6 Midian brought Israel very low, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.
6:7 And when the Israelites cried out to the LORD over what Midian was doing,
6:8 the LORD sent a prophet to the Israelites. He said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I brought you up from Egypt. I brought you out of the house of slaves.
6:9 I rescued you from the power of Egypt and from the power of all who oppressed you. I drove them out ahead of you and gave you their land.
6:10 I told you, 'I am the LORD your God. Do not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you are living.' But you have not listened to my voice."
## The angel under the oak
6:11 The angel of the LORD came and sat under the oak at Ophrah, the one that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite. His son Gideon was beating out wheat in the winepress, to keep it hidden from Midian.
6:12 The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said, "The LORD is with you, you mighty fighter."
6:13 "Pardon me, my lord," Gideon answered him, "but if the LORD is with us, why has all this found us? And where are all his wonders? Our ancestors told us of them: 'The LORD brought us up out of Egypt, didn't he?' But now the LORD has let us go and handed us over to Midian."
6:14 The LORD turned to face him and said, "Go with this strength you have, and you will rescue Israel from the power of Midian. I am the one sending you, am I not?"
6:15 "Pardon me, Lord," he said to him, "how am I to rescue Israel? Look, my clan is the poorest in Manasseh, and in my father's house I count for least."
6:16 The LORD answered, "I will be with you. You will strike down Midian as if it were one man."
6:17 Gideon said to him, "Please, if I have found favor in your eyes, then do me a sign that you are the one speaking with me.
6:18 Do not leave here, please, until I come back to you and bring out my gift and set it before you." He said, "I will stay until you return."
6:19 Gideon went in and got ready a young goat, with flat bread made from an ephah of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, brought them out to him under the oak, and set them before him.
6:20 The angel of God said to him, "Lay the meat and the flat bread on this rock, and pour the broth out." And he did so.
6:21 The angel of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff in his hand and touched the meat and the flat bread. Fire went up from the rock and ate up the meat and the flat bread, and the angel of the LORD went out of his sight.
6:22 Gideon saw that it had been the angel of the LORD. "Oh no, Lord GOD!" Gideon said. "Now I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!"
6:23 The LORD said to him, "Peace to you. Do not be afraid. You will not die."
6:24 Gideon built an altar to the LORD there and named it "The LORD Is Peace." It is in Ophrah of the Abiezrites even now.
## Gideon pulls down Baal's altar
6:25 That same night the LORD said to him, "Take your father's young bull, the second bull, seven years old. Tear down your father's altar to Baal, and chop down the Asherah pole that stands beside it.
6:26 Build an altar to the LORD your God on top of this stronghold, with its stones set in rows. Then take the second bull and offer it up as a burnt offering on the wood of the Asherah pole once you have cut it down."
6:27 Gideon picked ten of his servants and carried out what the LORD had told him. But he feared his father's family and the men of the town, so he did not act by day; he acted by night.
6:28 When the men of the town got up early next morning, there was Baal's altar torn down, the Asherah pole beside it cut down, and the second bull offered up on the altar that had been built.
6:29 "Who did this thing?" they said to one another. They asked around and searched, and then they said, "Gideon son of Joash did this thing."
6:30 The townsmen told Joash, "Bring your son out here, so he can be put to death! He pulled down Baal's altar and chopped down the Asherah pole that stood by it."
6:31 But Joash said to all who stood against him, "Are you the ones to take up Baal's case? Will you be the ones to rescue him? Whoever takes up his case will die before morning! If he is a god, let him take up his own case, because it is his altar someone tore down."
6:32 So that day Gideon was called Jerubbaal, which means, "Let Baal contend against him," because he tore down Baal's altar.
## The Spirit and the fleece
6:33 Then all Midian and Amalek and the people of the East gathered together, crossed over, and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.
6:34 But the Spirit of the LORD put Gideon on like clothing. He blew the ram's horn, and the Abiezrites were called out to follow him.
6:35 He sent messengers all through Manasseh, and they too were called out to follow him. He also sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, and they came up to meet them.
6:36 Gideon said to God, "If you really mean to rescue Israel through me, as you promised,
6:37 I am putting a wool fleece out here on the threshing floor. If dew is on the fleece alone, while all the ground stays dry, I will know you are going to rescue Israel through me, just as you promised."
6:38 So it happened. Early the next morning he pressed the fleece and drained the dew out of it, a full bowl of water.
6:39 Then Gideon said to God, "Do not let your anger blaze against me. Let me speak just this once more. Please let me test just this once more with the fleece. This time let the fleece alone be dry, and let dew be on all the ground."
6:40 God did so that night: the fleece alone stayed dry, and on all the ground there was dew.
## Three hundred men
7:1 Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, got up early with all his troops, and they camped by the spring of Harod. Midian's camp lay to their north, by the hill of Moreh in the valley.
7:2 The LORD said to Gideon, "The people with you are too many for me to put Midian in their power. Israel might glorify itself at my expense and say, 'My own hand rescued me.'
7:3 So now call out in the people's hearing: 'Whoever is scared and shaking, let him turn back and be off from Mount Gilead.'" Twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand stayed.
7:4 The LORD said to Gideon, "The army is still too big. Bring them down to the water, and I will refine them for you there. Anyone I tell you, 'This one will go with you,' will go with you. And anyone I tell you, 'This one will not go with you,' will not go."
7:5 So Gideon brought the people down to the water, and the LORD said to him, "Put everyone who laps up the water with his tongue, the way a dog laps, in one group, and everyone who kneels down to drink in another."
7:6 Three hundred men lapped from their hands held to their mouths. All the others knelt on their knees to drink the water.
7:7 Then the LORD told Gideon, "By the three hundred men who lapped I will rescue you. I will put Midian in your power. Send the rest of the people off, each one to his own place."
7:8 They took the people's food and their rams' horns in hand. All the other men of Israel he sent away, each to his tent, but he kept the three hundred. Midian's camp lay below him in the valley.
## A dream in the enemy camp
7:9 That night the LORD said to him, "Up! Go down into the camp, for I have put it in your power.
7:10 But if you are afraid to go down, then take your servant Purah and go down to the camp.
7:11 You will hear what they say, and after that your hands will be strong to go down into the camp." So he and his servant Purah went down to the edge of the armed men in the camp.
7:12 Midian, Amalek and all the people of the East were sprawled over the valley like a swarm of locusts. Their camels were past counting, as many as the grains of sand on the shore of the sea.
7:13 Gideon came, and there was a man telling his neighbor a dream. "Listen," he said, "I dreamed a dream. A round loaf of barley bread was rolling over and over into Midian's camp. It came up to the tent and struck it, and the tent fell. It flipped the tent upside down, and the tent lay flat."
7:14 His neighbor answered, "This is nothing else than the sword of Gideon son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has put Midian and the whole army in his power."
7:15 Hearing the dream told and its meaning, Gideon bowed down in worship. Then he went back to Israel's camp. "Up!" he said. "The LORD has put Midian's camp in your power."
## Horns, jars and torches
7:16 He split the three hundred men into three companies and gave every man a ram's horn and an empty jar, with a torch inside the jar.
7:17 He told them, "Keep your eyes on me and copy me. When I reach the edge of the camp, whatever I do, you do too.
7:18 I will blow the ram's horn, I and all who are with me. Then you too blow your horns all around the camp and shout, 'For the LORD and for Gideon!'"
7:19 Gideon came to the edge of the camp with his hundred men at the start of the middle watch, when the guards had only just been set. They blew the rams' horns and smashed the jars they were holding.
7:20 All three companies sounded the horns and broke the jars. In their left hands they gripped the torches, and in their right the horns for blowing, and they cried, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"
7:21 Every man kept his place in a ring around the camp, while the whole enemy camp ran about. They screamed and ran away.
7:22 As the three hundred blew their horns, the LORD turned each man's sword against his comrade all through the camp. The army fled as far as Beth-shittah, toward Zererah, up to the border of Abel-meholah, by Tabbath.
7:23 The men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, from Asher and from all Manasseh, and they chased Midian.
7:24 Gideon sent messengers through all the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Come down to meet Midian, and take control of the waters ahead of them as far as Beth-barah, and of the Jordan." So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they took control of the waters as far as Beth-barah, and of the Jordan.
7:25 They captured the two commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb at the Rock of Oreb, and Zeeb they killed at the Winepress of Zeeb. They kept on chasing Midian, and they brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon across the Jordan.
## Ephraim's complaint
8:1 The men of Ephraim said to him, "What is this you have done to us, not calling us when you went to fight Midian?" And they argued with him fiercely.
8:2 He said to them, "What have I done that compares with you? Isn't what Ephraim picks up after the harvest better than Abiezer's whole grape harvest?
8:3 It was into your hands that God gave the commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb. What could I do that compares with that?" Once he said this, their temper toward him cooled down.
## Succoth and Penuel
8:4 Gideon came to the Jordan and crossed over, he and the three hundred men with him, worn out but still chasing.
8:5 He said to the men of Succoth, "Please give some round loaves to the people at my heels, for they are worn out, and I am in pursuit of Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian."
8:6 But the officials of Succoth said, "Is the hand of Zebah and Zalmunna in your hand right now, that we should give bread to your army?"
8:7 Gideon answered, "For that, once the LORD puts Zebah and Zalmunna in my hands, I will thresh your bodies with thorns from the wilderness and with briers."
8:8 From there he went up to Penuel and spoke to them the same way, and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered.
8:9 So he said to the men of Penuel too, "When I come back safe, I will tear down this tower."
8:10 Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with their armies, about fifteen thousand men. Those were what remained of the whole army of the people of the East, for a hundred and twenty thousand sword-bearing men had fallen.
8:11 Gideon went up by the road of the tent dwellers, east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and he attacked the army while it felt safe.
8:12 Zebah and Zalmunna fled, but he chased them and captured the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and he threw their whole army into panic.
8:13 Then Gideon son of Joash came back from the battle by the Pass of Heres.
8:14 He laid hold of a young fellow from Succoth and put questions to him, and the youth listed in writing for him the officials of Succoth and its elders: seventy-seven men.
8:15 Then he came to the men of Succoth. "Look," he said, "here are Zebah and Zalmunna. You mocked me over them and said, 'Is the hand of Zebah and Zalmunna in your hand right now, that we should give bread to your tired men?'"
8:16 He took the elders of the town, and thorns from the wilderness and briers, and with them he made the men of Succoth learn.
8:17 And Penuel's tower he tore down, and he killed the men of the town.
8:18 Then he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, "What about the men you killed at Tabor?" They said, "They were like you. Each one looked like a king's son."
8:19 "They were my brothers," Gideon said, "my mother's sons. As surely as the LORD lives, had you let them live, I would not be killing you."
8:20 He told Jether his firstborn, "Up, kill them!" But the youth would not draw his sword, because he was afraid; he was still young.
8:21 Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, "You get up and fall on us yourself! A man's strength matches the man." So Gideon got up and killed Zebah and Zalmunna. He took the moon-shaped ornaments from their camels' necks.
## Gideon refuses a crown
8:22 The men of Israel said to Gideon, "Be our ruler, you, your son and your grandson too, since you rescued us from Midian's power."
8:23 But Gideon said to them, "I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you. The LORD will rule over you."
8:24 Then Gideon said to them, "Let me ask one thing of you: each of you give me an earring from his plunder." They had gold earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.
8:25 "We will give them, gladly," they said. They spread out a garment, and each man threw on it an earring from his plunder.
8:26 The weight of the gold earrings he asked for came to seventeen hundred shekels of gold. On top of that were the crescents and the drops and the purple clothes the kings of Midian had on, and the bands around their camels' necks.
8:27 Gideon made it into an ephod and put it in his town, Ophrah. All Israel went after it there like a prostitute, and it became a trap for Gideon and his house.
8:28 So Midian was brought low before the Israelites, and they did not raise their heads again. The land was quiet for forty years in the days of Gideon.
## Gideon's sons
8:29 Jerubbaal son of Joash went home and lived in his own house.
8:30 Gideon had seventy sons of his own body, because he had many wives.
8:31 His concubine in Shechem bore him a son as well, and he gave him the name Abimelech.
8:32 Gideon son of Joash died in a good old age. They buried him in his father Joash's tomb at Ophrah of the Abiezrites.
8:33 As soon as Gideon was dead, the Israelites turned back and went after the Baals like a prostitute. They made Baal-berith their god.
8:34 The LORD their God, who had rescued them from the power of all the enemies around them, the Israelites did not remember.
8:35 Nor did they show loyal love to the family of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, for all the good he had done for Israel.
## Abimelech made king
9:1 Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, to his mother's brothers. He spoke to them and to the whole clan of his mother's family. He said,
9:2 "Please say this in the hearing of all the lords of Shechem: 'Which is better for you, to have seventy men, all Jerubbaal's sons, rule over you, or to have one man rule over you?' Remember, I am your own bone and flesh."
9:3 So his mother's brothers spoke for him and said all these things in the hearing of all the lords of Shechem. Their hearts swung over to Abimelech, because they said, "He's our brother."
9:4 They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the temple of Baal-berith. With it Abimelech hired empty, reckless men, and they followed him.
9:5 Then he went to his father's house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, the sons of Jerubbaal, seventy men, on a single stone. Only Jotham, Jerubbaal's youngest son, was left, because he had hidden himself.
9:6 Then all the lords of Shechem and all Beth-millo gathered and went and made Abimelech king, by the oak of the pillar at Shechem.
## Jotham's fable
9:7 Word came to Jotham, and he went and stood on top of Mount Gerizim. He raised his voice and called out to them, "Listen to me, you lords of Shechem, so that God may listen to you!
9:8 The trees went out once to anoint a king over themselves. They said to the olive tree, 'Be king over us.'
9:9 The olive tree answered them, 'Am I supposed to stop making my rich oil, which brings honor to gods and humans, just to go wave back and forth over the trees?'
9:10 Then the trees said to the fig tree, 'You come, be king over us.'
9:11 The fig tree answered them, 'Am I supposed to give up my sweetness and my good fruit, just to go wave over the trees?'
9:12 Then the trees said to the vine, 'You come, be king over us.'
9:13 The vine answered them, 'Am I supposed to give up my new wine, which cheers gods and humans, just to go wave over the trees?'
9:14 Then all the trees said to the thornbush, 'You come, be king over us.'
9:15 The thornbush said to the trees, 'If in good faith you are anointing me king over you, come, shelter in my shade. If not, may fire go out from the thornbush and devour the cedars of Lebanon!'
9:16 "So now, did you act in good faith and integrity when you made Abimelech king? Did you deal well with Jerubbaal and his house, and do to him what his deeds deserved?
9:17 My father fought for you. He threw his life out in front of you and rescued you from the power of Midian.
9:18 But today you have risen up against my father's family. You have killed his sons, seventy men, on a single stone, and you have made Abimelech, the son of his slave woman, king over the lords of Shechem, because he is your brother.
9:19 If you have acted in good faith and integrity toward Jerubbaal and his family today, then be happy with Abimelech, and may he be happy with you too.
9:20 But if not, may fire break out of Abimelech and eat up the lords of Shechem and Beth-millo, and may fire break out of the lords of Shechem and Beth-millo and eat up Abimelech!"
9:21 Then Jotham ran away. He fled to Beer and lived there, out of reach of his brother Abimelech.
## Shechem turns on Abimelech
9:22 Abimelech held power over Israel for three years.
9:23 Then God sent a harmful spirit between Abimelech and the lords of Shechem, and the lords of Shechem broke faith with Abimelech.
9:24 This happened so that the violence done to Jerubbaal's seventy sons would come back, and their blood would be laid on their brother Abimelech, who killed them, and on the lords of Shechem, who strengthened his hands to kill his brothers.
9:25 The lords of Shechem set men in ambush against him on the mountaintops, and they robbed everyone who passed them on the road. Abimelech was told about it.
9:26 Gaal son of Ebed came along with his brothers and passed into Shechem, and the lords of Shechem put their trust in him.
9:27 They went out into the field, gathered the grapes of their vineyards and trod them, and they made a feast of praise. Then they went into the house of their god, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.
9:28 Gaal son of Ebed said, "Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Jerubbaal's son, with Zebul as his deputy, is that not who he is? It is the men of Hamor, Shechem's father, you should serve! Why should we serve him?
9:29 Who will put this people in my hands? Then I would remove Abimelech." And he said to Abimelech, "Make your army big and come out!"
9:30 When Zebul, the ruler of the town, heard the words of Gaal son of Ebed, his anger blazed.
9:31 He sent messengers to Abimelech in secret, saying, "Look, Gaal son of Ebed and his brothers have come to Shechem, and they are turning the town against you.
9:32 So now, get up by night, you and the troops with you, and lie in wait in the fields.
9:33 In the morning, as soon as the sun comes up, rise early and rush on the town. When he and the people with him come out against you, do to him whatever you can."
9:34 So Abimelech and all the troops with him got up by night and lay in wait against Shechem in four companies.
9:35 Gaal son of Ebed went out and stood at the entrance of the town gate. Then Abimelech and the troops with him rose up from their hiding place.
9:36 Gaal saw the troops and said to Zebul, "Look, there are people coming down off the mountaintops!" Zebul told him, "Those are the shadows of the mountains. They just look like men to you."
9:37 Once more Gaal said, "There, people are coming down from the Navel of the Land, and one company is on its way by the road of the Diviners' Oak."
9:38 Then Zebul said to him, "Where is that mouth of yours now, you who said, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' This is the army you sneered at, isn't it? Now get out there and fight them!"
9:39 So Gaal went out at the head of the lords of Shechem and fought Abimelech.
9:40 Abimelech chased Gaal, and Gaal fled from him. Many fell wounded all the way up to the gate's entrance.
9:41 Abimelech stayed at Arumah, and Zebul drove Gaal and his brothers out, so they could not live in Shechem.
## Shechem destroyed
9:42 The next day the people went out into the fields, and Abimelech was told.
9:43 He took the people, made three companies of them, and lay in wait in the field. When he saw the people coming out of the town, he rose up against them and struck them down.
9:44 Abimelech and the companies with him charged forward and took their stand at the entrance of the town gate, while two of the companies charged everyone in the field and struck them down.
9:45 Abimelech fought against the town all that day. He captured it and killed the people in it, then tore the town down and sowed it with salt.
9:46 When all the lords of the Tower of Shechem heard this, they went into the stronghold of the temple of El-berith.
9:47 Abimelech was told that all the lords of the Tower of Shechem had gathered there.
9:48 So Abimelech went up Mount Zalmon, he and all the troops with him. Abimelech took an ax in his hand, cut a branch from the trees, lifted it and put it on his shoulder. He said to the troops with him, "What you have seen me do, hurry and do just as I have done!"
9:49 So all the troops too cut down each man his branch and followed Abimelech. They laid the branches over the stronghold and set it on fire above them, and all the people of the Tower of Shechem died as well, about a thousand men and women.
## A millstone at Thebez
9:50 Then Abimelech went to Thebez. He camped against Thebez and captured it.
9:51 There was a strong tower in the middle of the town, and every man and woman fled there, all the lords of the town. They shut the door behind them and went up onto the roof of the tower.
9:52 Abimelech came to the tower and fought against it. He came near the tower's entrance to burn it with fire.
9:53 But a woman threw an upper millstone down on Abimelech's head and smashed his skull.
9:54 At once he shouted to the young man who carried his armor, "Pull out your sword and finish me off, so no one can say about me, 'A woman killed him.'" So the young man stabbed him through, and he died.
9:55 When the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, each of them went back to his own place.
9:56 So God paid back the evil Abimelech had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers.
9:57 God also made all the evil of the men of Shechem come back on their own heads. The curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.
## Tola and Jair
10:1 When Abimelech was gone, Tola son of Puah, son of Dodo, a man of Issachar, stood up to rescue Israel. His home was Shamir, in the hill country of Ephraim.
10:2 He judged Israel for twenty-three years. Then he died and was buried in Shamir.
10:3 After him rose up Jair the Gileadite, who judged Israel for twenty-two years.
10:4 He had thirty sons who rode on thirty donkeys, and they had thirty towns, which are called Havvoth-jair until today; they are in the land of Gilead.
10:5 Jair died and was buried in Kamon.
## Israel confesses
10:6 Once again the Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD. They served the Baals and the Ashtoreths, the gods of Aram, the gods of Sidon, the gods of Moab, the gods of the Ammonites and the gods of the Philistines. They left the LORD and did not serve him.
10:7 So the LORD's anger blazed against Israel, and he sold them into the power of the Philistines and of the Ammonites.
10:8 That year they shattered and crushed the Israelites, and for eighteen years so it went for all the Israelites beyond the Jordan, in the land of the Amorites, in Gilead.
10:9 The Ammonites even crossed the Jordan to make war on Judah and Benjamin and the house of Ephraim. Israel was in very great distress.
10:10 The Israelites cried out to the LORD, "We have sinned against you! We left our God and served the Baals."
10:11 The LORD said to the Israelites, "Didn't I rescue you from Egypt, from the Amorites, from the Ammonites and from the Philistines?
10:12 When the Sidonians, Amalek and Maon oppressed you and you cried out to me, I rescued you from their power.
10:13 Yet you are the ones who left me and served other gods. That is why I will rescue you no more.
10:14 Go and cry to the gods you have picked. They can rescue you in your time of trouble!"
10:15 The Israelites said to the LORD, "We have sinned. Do to us all that seems good in your eyes, only rescue us today, please."
10:16 So they put away the foreign gods from among them and served the LORD. And the LORD had had enough of Israel's misery.
10:17 The Ammonites were summoned to arms, and they pitched camp in Gilead. The Israelites came together and pitched camp at Mizpah.
10:18 The people, the leaders of Gilead, said to one another, "Whoever starts the fight against the Ammonites will be head over all the people of Gilead."
## Jephthah called home
11:1 Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty fighter, but he was the son of a prostitute. Gilead was Jephthah's father.
11:2 Gilead's wife bore him sons, and when the wife's sons grew up, they drove Jephthah out. "You will get no inheritance in our father's house," they told him, "because you are the son of another woman."
11:3 So Jephthah fled from his brothers and settled in the land of Tob. Empty men gathered around Jephthah and went out with him.
11:4 After a while the Ammonites made war on Israel.
11:5 When the Ammonites made war on Israel, the elders of Gilead went to bring Jephthah back from the land of Tob.
11:6 They said to Jephthah, "Come and lead us as commander, and we will fight the Ammonites."
11:7 Jephthah answered the elders of Gilead, "You are the ones who hated me and chased me out of my father's house, aren't you? So why come to me now, just when you are in trouble?"
11:8 The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "That is why we have turned back to you now. Come with us and fight the Ammonites, and you will be our head, over all the people of Gilead."
11:9 Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "If you bring me back to fight the Ammonites and the LORD hands them over before me, I myself will be your head."
11:10 The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "The LORD will be the one who hears between us. We will surely do as you say."
11:11 So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people set him over them as head and commander. And Jephthah spoke all his words before the LORD at Mizpah.
## Jephthah's letter to Ammon
11:12 Then Jephthah sent messengers to the Ammonite king to say, "What is there between you and me, that you have come against me to make war on my land?"
11:13 The king of the Ammonites answered Jephthah's messengers, "Because Israel took my land when they came up from Egypt, from the Arnon to the Jabbok and to the Jordan. So now give it back peacefully."
11:14 Once more Jephthah sent messengers to the Ammonite king,
11:15 and said to him, "This is what Jephthah says: Israel did not take the land of Moab or the land of the Ammonites.
11:16 When they came up from Egypt, Israel went through the wilderness to the Sea of Reeds and came to Kadesh.
11:17 Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom: 'Please let me pass through your land.' The king of Edom would not hear of it. To the king of Moab they sent as well, and he refused. So Israel stayed at Kadesh.
11:18 Then they went on through the wilderness and went around the land of Edom and the land of Moab. They came to the east side of the land of Moab and camped across the Arnon. Into Moab's territory they did not go, for the Arnon was Moab's border.
11:19 Next Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, king of Heshbon, and said to him, 'Please let us pass through your land to our place.'
11:20 But Sihon did not trust Israel to cross his territory. He gathered all his people, camped at Jahaz, and fought with Israel.
11:21 The LORD, the God of Israel, put Sihon and all his people in Israel's power, and they struck them down. So Israel took possession of all the land of the Amorites who lived there.
11:22 They took possession of all the territory of the Amorites, from the Arnon to the Jabbok and from the wilderness to the Jordan.
11:23 So now the LORD, the God of Israel, has driven out the Amorites ahead of his people Israel. And are you going to take possession of it?
11:24 Whatever your god Chemosh gives you to possess, you take possession of, don't you? So whatever the LORD our God has driven out from before us, we will take possession of.
11:25 And now, are you so much better than Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever pick a quarrel with Israel? Did he ever make war on them?
11:26 For three hundred years Israel has lived in Heshbon and its villages, in Aroer and its villages, and in all the towns by the banks of the Arnon. Why did you not recover them in all that time?
11:27 I have not sinned against you. It is you who wrong me by fighting against me. Let the LORD, the Judge, be judge today between the Israelites and the Ammonites."
11:28 But the king of the Ammonites would not listen to the words Jephthah sent him.
## Jephthah's vow
11:29 Jephthah now had the Spirit of the LORD on him. He passed through Gilead and Manasseh, passed through Mizpah of Gilead, and from Mizpah of Gilead he passed on toward the Ammonites.
11:30 Jephthah made a vow to the LORD. He said, "If you will truly put the Ammonites in my power,
11:31 then whatever comes out, whatever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me as I come home safe from the Ammonites, will be the LORD's, and I will offer it up as a burnt offering."
11:32 Jephthah went on to the Ammonites to make war on them, and the LORD put them in his power.
11:33 He struck them from Aroer to the area of Minnith, twenty towns, and as far as Abel-keramim, a very great defeat. So the Ammonites were brought low before the Israelites.
11:34 Jephthah came to Mizpah, to his home, and look! His daughter was coming out to meet him, with tambourines and dancing. She was his one and only child. Apart from her he had neither son nor daughter.
11:35 As soon as he saw her, he ripped his clothes. "Oh, my daughter!" he said. "You have brought me to my knees! You are the one bringing me ruin! I opened my mouth to the LORD, and I cannot go back on it."
11:36 She answered him, "Father, you opened your mouth to the LORD. Do to me what came out of your mouth, since the LORD has paid back your enemies, the Ammonites, for you."
11:37 And she said to her father, "Let this one thing be done for me. Leave me alone for two months, and let me go down into the hills and weep over my virginity, I and my friends."
11:38 "Go," he said. He sent her away for two months, and she went with her friends and wept over her virginity on the hills.
11:39 At the end of two months she came back to her father, and he did to her the vow he had made. She had never slept with a man. So it became a custom in Israel
11:40 that from year to year the daughters of Israel go to retell the story of the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite, four days in the year.
## Shibboleth
12:1 The men of Ephraim were summoned, and they crossed over toward Zaphon. "Why did you cross over to make war on the Ammonites," they said to Jephthah, "and not call us to go with you? We will burn your house down over you!"
12:2 Jephthah said to them, "My people and I had a great quarrel with the Ammonites. I called you, and you did not rescue me from their hands.
12:3 When I saw you were no rescuer, I put my life in my palm and crossed over against the Ammonites, and the LORD put them in my power. So why have you come up against me today, to fight me?"
12:4 Then Jephthah gathered all the men of Gilead and fought Ephraim. The men of Gilead struck Ephraim down, because Ephraim had said, "You are runaways from Ephraim, you Gileadites, in the middle of Ephraim and Manasseh."
12:5 Gilead held the fords of the Jordan against Ephraim. Any runaway of Ephraim who said, "Let me cross over," the men of Gilead questioned: "Are you an Ephraimite?" If he said, "No,"
12:6 they would say to him, "Say 'Shibboleth.'" He would say "Sibboleth," for he could not manage to say it right. Then they would seize him and slaughter him at the fords of the Jordan. Forty-two thousand of Ephraim fell at that time.
12:7 Jephthah judged Israel for six years. Then Jephthah the Gileadite died and was buried in one of the towns of Gilead.
## Ibzan, Elon and Abdon
12:8 After him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel.
12:9 He had thirty sons and thirty daughters. The daughters he sent outside, and thirty daughters he brought in from outside for his sons. He judged Israel seven years.
12:10 Then Ibzan died and was buried in Bethlehem.
12:11 After him Elon the Zebulunite judged Israel. He judged Israel for ten years.
12:12 Elon the Zebulunite died. They buried him at Aijalon in the land of Zebulun.
12:13 After him Abdon son of Hillel the Pirathonite judged Israel.
12:14 He had forty sons and thirty sons' sons, who rode on seventy donkeys. He judged Israel eight years.
12:15 Then Abdon son of Hillel the Pirathonite died and was buried at Pirathon in the land of Ephraim, in the hill country of the Amalekites.
