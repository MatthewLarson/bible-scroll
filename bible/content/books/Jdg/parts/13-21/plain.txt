## The birth of Samson
13:1 Once again the Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD handed them over to the Philistines for forty years.
13:2 In Zorah lived a man named Manoah, from the clan of the Danites. His wife was barren. She had never had a child.
13:3 The angel of the LORD appeared to the woman. "Look," he told her, "you are barren and have never had a child. But you will conceive, and you will have a son.
13:4 So now please be on your guard. Do not drink wine or other strong drink, and do not eat anything unclean.
13:5 You are going to be pregnant and have a son. No razor is to touch his head, for the boy will be God's Nazirite from the womb. He is the one who will begin to rescue Israel from the grip of the Philistines."
13:6 The woman went to her husband and said, "A man of God came to me. He looked like God's angel, truly frightening to see. I did not ask him where he was from, and he did not tell me his name.
13:7 He told me, 'Look, you are going to conceive and have a son. So now, keep away from wine and other strong drink, and eat nothing unclean. The boy will be God's Nazirite from the womb to his dying day.'"
13:8 So Manoah prayed to the LORD. "Oh, Lord, please," he said, "the man of God you sent, let him come to us one more time. Let him teach us what to do for the boy who is about to be born."
13:9 God heard what Manoah asked, and the angel of God came back to the woman. She was sitting out in the field, and her husband Manoah was not with her.
13:10 The woman hurried and ran to tell her husband. "Look!" she said. "The man who came to me that day is here. He has shown himself to me."
13:11 Manoah rose and went after his wife. He came up to the man and asked him, "Are you the one who spoke with the woman?" He answered, "I am."
13:12 Manoah said, "Now, when your words come about, what will be the rule for the boy, and what is he to do?"
13:13 The angel of the LORD said to Manoah, "Everything I told the woman, she must take care to keep.
13:14 Anything the grapevine produces, she must not eat. Wine and other strong drink she must not drink, and nothing unclean may she eat. Every command I gave her, she must keep."
13:15 Manoah said to the angel of the LORD, "Please stay with us a while, and let us cook a young goat for you."
13:16 The angel of the LORD answered Manoah, "Even if you keep me here, I will not eat your food. If you make a burnt offering, though, it is to the LORD you must offer it." Manoah did not know that this was the angel of the LORD.
13:17 Manoah said to the angel of the LORD, "What is your name? Then, when your words come about, we can give you honor."
13:18 "Why are you asking for my name?" the angel of the LORD said. "It is wonderful, beyond grasping."
13:19 Manoah took the young goat and the grain offering and offered them up on the rock to the LORD. And something wonderful was done while Manoah and his wife looked on.
13:20 When the flame rose from the altar toward the sky, the angel of the LORD rose with it, up in the altar's flame. Manoah and his wife saw it and threw themselves face down on the ground.
13:21 The angel of the LORD never showed himself to Manoah and his wife again. That was when Manoah knew it had been the angel of the LORD.
13:22 "We are certain to die," Manoah told his wife. "It is God we have seen!"
13:23 But his wife answered him, "If it had been the LORD's wish to kill us, he would not have taken a burnt offering and a grain offering from us. He would not have let us see all this, or let us hear such things as we heard just now."
13:24 The woman had a son, and she named him Samson. As the boy grew up, the LORD blessed him.
13:25 The Spirit of the LORD began to prod him at Mahaneh-dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.
## Samson's Philistine wife
14:1 Samson went down to Timnah, and in Timnah he saw a woman, one of the Philistines' daughters.
14:2 He came back up and told his father and mother. "I saw a woman in Timnah, one of the Philistines' daughters," he said. "Go now and get her to be my wife."
14:3 His father and his mother said to him, "Is there no woman among the daughters of your relatives, or among all my people, that you go and take a wife from the Philistines, the uncircumcised?" Samson told his father, "Get her for me. She is right in my eyes."
14:4 His father and mother did not know this was from the LORD, for he was looking for a pretext to strike at the Philistines. In those days the Philistines ruled over Israel.
14:5 So Samson went down to Timnah, and his father and mother too. They reached the vineyards of Timnah, and there was a young lion, roaring as it came to meet him.
14:6 The Spirit of the LORD swept down on him, and with nothing in his hand he ripped the lion apart, as you would rip apart a young goat. He did not tell his father or his mother what he had done.
14:7 He went down and spoke with the woman, and she was right in Samson's eyes.
14:8 After a while he came back to take her as his wife. He left the road to see the lion's carcass, and he found a swarm of bees inside it, and honey.
14:9 He dug the honey out into his palms and kept walking, eating as he went. He reached his father and mother and shared it with them, and they ate. He did not tell them the honey had come out of a dead lion's body.
## A riddle at the wedding feast
14:10 Then his father went down to the woman, and Samson put on a feast there, since that is what young men did.
14:11 As soon as they saw him, they brought thirty companions to be with him.
14:12 Samson said to them, "Let me pose a riddle for you. If you really can work it out and tell me the answer within the seven days of the feast, I will give you thirty linen wraps and thirty sets of fine clothes.
14:13 If you cannot tell me, then you will be the ones to give me thirty linen wraps and thirty sets of fine clothes." "Go ahead," they said. "Riddle us your riddle. We are listening."
14:14 He said to them, "Out of the eater, food came out; / out of the strong, sweetness came out." Three days went by, and they could not explain the riddle.
14:15 On the seventh day they said to Samson's wife, "Lure your husband into telling us the riddle, or we will set fire to you and to your father's household. Did you invite us here to make us poor? Isn't that it?"
14:16 Samson's wife wept over him and said, "You only hate me. You don't love me. You put a riddle to my people, and to me you haven't explained it." He said to her, "Look, to my father and my mother I haven't explained it. And to you I should?"
14:17 She went on weeping over him through the seven days of their feast. On the seventh day he told her, because she gave him no rest. Then she told the answer to her people.
14:18 On the seventh day, before the sun went down, the men of the town said to him, "What is sweeter than honey? / What is stronger than a lion?" He said to them, "Had you not plowed with my heifer, / you would not have cracked my riddle."
14:19 The Spirit of the LORD swept down on him. Down he went to Ashkelon and struck thirty men of theirs dead. He took what they had on them and handed the changes of clothes to those who had told the riddle. His anger burning, he went up to his father's house.
14:20 And Samson's wife became the wife of his companion, the one who had been his groomsman.
## Foxes and fire
15:1 A while afterward, during the wheat harvest, Samson came to see his wife, bringing a young goat. "Let me go in to my wife, into her room," he said. But her father refused to let him in.
15:2 "I told myself you must surely hate her," her father said, "so I gave her to your companion. Isn't her little sister better than she is? Let her be yours instead."
15:3 Samson said to them, "This time no one can blame me for what I do to the Philistines. I am going to do them harm."
15:4 Samson went out and captured three hundred foxes. He got torches, turned the animals tail to tail, and set one torch between every two tails.
15:5 He set the torches on fire and sent the foxes into the Philistines' fields of grain. Everything burned, from the piled-up sheaves to the grain still standing, and even the vineyards and the olive trees.
15:6 "Who has done this?" the Philistines asked. People said, "Samson did, the Timnite's son-in-law, because the man took his wife and handed her to his companion." So the Philistines went up and set fire to her and to her father.
15:7 Samson told them, "If you do things like this, I will not rest until I have had my revenge on you. After that I will stop."
15:8 He struck them down, leg upon thigh, a great slaughter. Then he went down and lived in the cleft of the rock of Etam.
## The jawbone at Lehi
15:9 The Philistines came up and set up camp in Judah, and they fanned out at Lehi.
15:10 The people of Judah said, "What brings you up here against us?" "We came up to tie up Samson," they said, "and to treat him the way he treated us."
15:11 So three thousand men from Judah went down to the cleft in the rock of Etam. They said to Samson, "Don't you know the Philistines are our masters? Look what you have done to us!" He said to them, "As they did to me, so I have done to them."
15:12 "We have come down to tie you up," they told him, "so we can turn you over to the Philistines." "Give me your oath," Samson said, "that you will not set on me yourselves."
15:13 They told him, "No, we will just tie you up firmly and give you to them. We are not going to kill you." They bound him with two new ropes and took him up out of the rock.
15:14 He was just coming to Lehi when the Philistines ran to meet him, shouting. The Spirit of the LORD swept down on him, and the ropes on his arms turned into flax that people have put to the fire, and the bonds dissolved from his hands.
15:15 He came across the jawbone of a donkey that had died not long before. He reached down, picked it up, and killed a thousand men with it.
15:16 Then Samson said, "With a donkey's jaw, / a heap, two heaps! / With a donkey's jaw / I struck down a thousand men."
15:17 When he had finished saying this, he tossed the jawbone out of his hand, and he named that place Ramath-lehi.
15:18 Then he was terribly thirsty. He called to the LORD and said, "By your servant's hand you have brought about this great rescue. Am I now to die of thirst and fall into the power of the uncircumcised?"
15:19 God broke open the hollow at Lehi, and water poured out of it. Samson drank, his spirit returned, and he revived. That is why he named it En-hakkore. It is at Lehi to this day.
15:20 Samson judged Israel for twenty years, during the days of the Philistines.
## The gates of Gaza
16:1 Samson went to Gaza. He saw a woman there, a prostitute, and slept with her.
16:2 Word went to the people of Gaza: "Samson has come here!" They closed in around him and waited in ambush all night by the town gate. All night they kept still, telling themselves, "At daybreak we will kill him."
16:3 Samson lay down till midnight. At midnight he got up, took hold of the doors of the town gate and the two gateposts, and tore them loose, bar and all. He put them on his shoulders and carried them up to the top of the hill in front of Hebron.
## Samson and Delilah
16:4 Later on Samson loved a woman who lived in the Sorek Valley. Delilah was her name.
16:5 The rulers of the Philistines came up to her. "Lure him," they told her, "and find out what makes his strength so great, and how we can get the better of him. We want to tie him up and bring him low. Each of us will give you eleven hundred pieces of silver."
16:6 Delilah said to Samson, "Please tell me, what makes your strength so great? How could someone tie you up and bring you low?"
16:7 Samson answered, "If they tie me up with seven new cords of gut that have not yet dried out, I will grow weak and be like anyone else."
16:8 So the rulers of the Philistines brought up to her seven new gut cords that had not dried out, and she tied him up with them.
16:9 Men lay hidden in the room, waiting on her word. She said to him, "Samson! The Philistines are on you!" He broke the cords as a strand of tow breaks at a whiff of fire. So no one learned what made him strong.
16:10 Delilah said to Samson, "Look, you have played me for a fool and lied to me. Now please, tell me what you can be tied up with."
16:11 "If they tie me tight with new ropes that have never been put to work," he said, "I will grow weak and be like anyone else."
16:12 So Delilah got new ropes and tied him up with them. Then she said, "Samson! The Philistines are on you!" The men lying in wait were sitting in the inner room. He tore the ropes off his arms like so much thread.
16:13 Delilah said to Samson, "Up to now you have mocked me and told me lies. Tell me, how can you be tied up?" He said to her, "If you weave the seven braids of my head in with the web, and beat them in with the pin against the wall, I will be weak and be like any other man."
16:14 She put him to sleep and wove the seven braids of his head into the web. She beat it in with the pin and said, "Samson! The Philistines are on you!" He woke from his sleep and tore out the pin of the loom, and the web with it.
16:15 Then she said to him, "How can you say 'I love you' and keep your heart away from me? Three times you have played me for a fool. You still haven't told me what makes your strength so great."
16:16 Day after day she kept at him with her talk and pushed him, until he was so fed up he could have died.
16:17 So he opened his whole heart to her. He said, "A razor has never touched my head, because I have been God's Nazirite since I was in my mother's womb. If I am shaved, my strength will go away from me. I will grow weak and be like everyone else."
16:18 Delilah saw that he had opened his whole heart to her. She sent for the rulers of the Philistines with a message: "Come up this time. He has opened his whole heart to me." So the rulers of the Philistines came up to her, and they brought the silver with them.
16:19 She lulled him to sleep on her knees and called in a man, and she shaved off the seven braids of his head. She began to bring him low, and his strength left him.
16:20 Then she said, "Samson! The Philistines are on you!" He woke from his sleep and said, "I'll go out as at the other times and shake myself free." He did not know that the LORD had left him.
16:21 The Philistines grabbed him and dug out his eyes. They took him down to Gaza and bound him in double chains of bronze, and he spent his days grinding grain in the prison house.
16:22 Yet once it was shaved, the hair of his head started growing back.
## Samson's death
16:23 The rulers of the Philistines came together to make a great sacrifice to their god Dagon and to have a celebration. They said, "Our god has handed us Samson, our enemy!"
16:24 When the people saw him, they gave praise to their god, because they said, "Our god has put in our hand / the enemy who wrecked our land, / who piled up our dead."
16:25 When their hearts were glad, they said, "Send for Samson so he can make us laugh!" They sent for Samson from the prison house, and he made sport in front of them. They had him stand between the pillars.
16:26 Samson said to the boy who was leading him by the hand, "Let go of me, and let me touch the pillars the building stands on, so I can lean on them."
16:27 The building was packed with men and women, and all the rulers of the Philistines were there. Up on the roof were some three thousand more, men and women, watching Samson make sport.
16:28 Samson cried out to the LORD, "Lord GOD, remember me, please! Give me strength, please, just this one time, O God, and let me take one revenge on the Philistines for my two eyes."
16:29 Samson gripped the two pillars in the middle, the ones the building rested on, and pressed against them, one with his right hand and the other with his left.
16:30 "Let my life end with the Philistines!" Samson said. He bent forward with all his strength, and the building fell on the rulers and on all the people inside. The dead he killed in his death outnumbered those he had killed in his life.
16:31 His brothers and his father's whole household came down and carried him away. They took him up and laid him in his father Manoah's tomb, between Zorah and Eshtaol. Samson had judged Israel for twenty years.
## Micah's idol
17:1 A man named Micah lived in the hill country of Ephraim.
17:2 He said to his mother, "Those eleven hundred pieces of silver that someone took from you? You laid a curse over them, and I heard you say it. Well, look, the silver is here with me. I am the one who took it." His mother said, "My son, may the LORD bless you!"
17:3 So he gave the eleven hundred pieces back to his mother. His mother said, "I hereby set this silver apart for the LORD. From my own hand it goes to my son, to make a carved idol and a cast idol. So now I am handing it back to you."
17:4 He returned the silver to his mother, and she took two hundred pieces of it and handed them to a silversmith. He made it into a carved idol and a cast idol, and it stood in Micah's house.
17:5 The man Micah had a house of God. He made an ephod and household gods and ordained one of his sons, who became a priest for him.
17:6 There was no king in Israel in those days. Everyone did whatever was right in his own eyes.
## A Levite for hire
17:7 A young man from Bethlehem in Judah, of Judah's clan, was a Levite who was staying there as a foreigner.
17:8 He set out from his town of Bethlehem in Judah to live as a foreigner wherever he could find a place, and while on the road he reached Micah's house in the hill country of Ephraim.
17:9 "Where have you come from?" Micah asked him. He answered, "I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah. I'm on my way to live wherever I can find a place."
17:10 "Stay here with me," Micah said. "Be my father and my priest. I will pay you ten pieces of silver a year, plus a set of clothes and your keep." So the Levite went.
17:11 The Levite was willing to settle down with the man, and the young man was treated like one of his own sons.
17:12 Micah put the Levite in office, and the young man became his priest. He lived in Micah's house.
17:13 Micah said, "Now I am sure the LORD will treat me well, since I've got a Levite for my priest."
## The Danites look for land
18:1 There was no king in Israel in those days. And in those days the Danite tribe was searching for an inheritance to settle in, because until then nothing had come to them by lot as their inheritance among Israel's tribes.
18:2 The Danites picked five men out of their whole tribe, tough fighters from Zorah and Eshtaol, and sent them to scout the land and look it over. "Go and look the land over," they said. The men reached Micah's house in the hill country of Ephraim and stayed the night there.
18:3 As they came near Micah's house, they knew the voice of the young Levite. They went over to him and asked, "Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place? What have you got here?"
18:4 "Micah has done such-and-such for me," he told them. "He hired me, and I'm his priest."
18:5 "Then ask God for us, please," they said, "so we can know whether this trip we are on will turn out well."
18:6 The priest told them, "Go in peace. The road you are taking lies before the LORD."
18:7 The five men went on and came to Laish. They saw how the people there were living free from worry, in the Sidonian way, calm and trusting. No one in the land was doing anything to harm them, and no one held power over them. They lived far from the Sidonians and had nothing to do with anyone.
18:8 They came back to their kinsmen at Zorah and Eshtaol, and their kinsmen asked them, "Well, what news?"
18:9 "Come on, let's go up and attack them," they answered. "We saw the land, and it is excellent. Will you just sit there? Don't drag your feet. Go on in and take possession of the land.
18:10 When you go, you will come to a people who feel safe, and the land is wide on both sides. God has given it into your hand. It is a place where nothing on earth is missing."
## Dan takes Micah's gods
18:11 So six hundred men from the Danite clan set out from there, from Zorah and Eshtaol, with their weapons of war strapped on.
18:12 They went up and made camp at Kiriath-jearim in Judah. That is why people call that place Mahaneh-dan to this day. It lies just west of Kiriath-jearim.
18:13 From there they crossed into the hill country of Ephraim and arrived at Micah's house.
18:14 The five men who had gone to scout the land of Laish spoke up and said to their kinsmen, "Did you know there is an ephod in these buildings, and household gods, and a carved idol and a cast idol? So now, think about what you are going to do."
18:15 They turned off the road there and went to the young Levite's house, the house of Micah, and asked how he was.
18:16 Meanwhile the six hundred men of Dan stood at the entrance of the gate, wearing their weapons of war.
18:17 The five men who had scouted the land went up and walked in. They took the carved idol, the ephod, the household gods and the cast idol. The priest was standing at the gate's entrance with the six hundred men in their battle gear.
18:18 These men went into Micah's house and took the carved idol, the ephod, the household gods and the cast idol. "What are you doing?" the priest asked them.
18:19 "Hush!" they told him. "Put your hand over your mouth and come along with us. Be our father and priest. Which is better for you: to be the priest for one man's household, or the priest for a whole tribe and clan in Israel?"
18:20 The priest's heart was pleased. He took the ephod, the household gods and the carved idol, and he went off in the middle of the people.
18:21 They turned and went on their way, with the children, the livestock and their belongings out in front.
18:22 They had gone a good way from Micah's house when the men who lived in the houses near Micah's were called together. They caught up with the Danites
18:23 and shouted after them. The Danites turned around and said to Micah, "What is wrong with you, that you've gathered a crowd?"
18:24 "My gods that I made, you've taken them," he said, "and the priest too, and walked off. What do I have left? And you ask me, 'What is wrong with you?'"
18:25 The Danites told him, "Don't let us hear another word from you. Otherwise some men with bitter tempers will fall on you, and you will throw away your life and the lives of your household."
18:26 The Danites went their way. Micah saw that they were too strong for him, and he turned back to his house.
## The fall of Laish
18:27 They took what Micah had made and the priest who had been his, and came on Laish, a people quiet and trusting. They struck them with the sword and burned the town with fire.
18:28 There was no rescuer, because it was far from Sidon and they had no dealings with anyone. It lay in the valley that belongs to Beth-rehob. They built the town again and lived in it.
18:29 They gave the town the name Dan, after their forefather Dan, Israel's son. But Laish had been the town's name before.
18:30 The Danites put up the carved idol for themselves. Jonathan, son of Gershom, son of Moses, served as priest for the Danite tribe, he and his sons after him, until the day the land was taken into exile.
18:31 So they kept Micah's carved idol, the one he had made, standing for themselves all the time that the house of God stood at Shiloh.
## The Levite and his concubine
19:1 In those days, with no king in Israel, there was a Levite staying as a foreigner in the far reaches of the hill country of Ephraim. He took a woman from Bethlehem in Judah to be his concubine.
19:2 But his concubine broke faith with him, and she left him and went to her father's house in Bethlehem in Judah. She stayed there for four months' time.
19:3 Her husband set out and went after her, to speak to her heart and bring her back. He took his servant along and a pair of donkeys. She brought him into her father's house, and when the young woman's father saw him, he was glad to meet him.
19:4 His father-in-law, the young woman's father, held on to him, and he stayed with him three days. They ate and drank, and they slept there.
19:5 On the fourth day they were up early in the morning, and he stood up to leave. But the young woman's father told his son-in-law, "Hold up your strength with a bit of bread. After that you can go."
19:6 The two men sat down and ate and drank together. Then the young woman's father said to the man, "Do agree to stay the night. Let your heart feel good."
19:7 The man rose to go, but his father-in-law kept urging him, so he turned back and spent the night there.
19:8 On the fifth day he rose early in the morning to leave. The young woman's father said, "Please, hold up your strength. Stay on until the day wears down." And the two of them ate.
19:9 Then the man got up to leave, he and his concubine and his servant. But his father-in-law, the young woman's father, said to him, "Look, the daylight is sinking into evening. Please stay the night. See, the day is setting. Stay the night here and let your heart feel good. Tomorrow you can rise early for your trip and go home to your tent."
## Night at Gibeah
19:10 But the man refused to stay another night. He set off and traveled until he came near Jebus, which is Jerusalem. He had his pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.
19:11 By the time they reached Jebus, the day was far gone. The servant said to his master, "Please, let's leave the road and go into this Jebusite town and stay the night there."
19:12 His master told him, "We will not turn aside into a town of foreigners, people who are not of Israel. We will go on to Gibeah."
19:13 He said to his servant, "Come on, let's get close to one of these places and spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah."
19:14 They kept on traveling, and the sun went down as they came alongside Gibeah, the town of Benjamin.
19:15 They left the road there to go into Gibeah for the night. The Levite went in and sat down in the town square, but nobody offered to bring them home for the night.
19:16 Then, look, an old man was coming in from his work, from the field, at evening. The man was from the hill country of Ephraim and was staying in Gibeah as a foreigner. The men of the place were Benjaminites.
19:17 The old man raised his eyes and saw the man on the road, there in the town square. "Where are you going?" he said. "Where do you come from?"
19:18 He told him, "We are passing through, from Bethlehem in Judah to the far reaches of the hill country of Ephraim. I am from there. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to the house of the LORD. No one is taking me into his home.
19:19 We even have straw and feed for our donkeys, and there is bread and wine for me and for your servant woman, and for the young man who is with your servants. We are not short of anything."
19:20 "Be at peace," the old man said. "Leave whatever you need to me. Just don't stay the night out in the square."
19:21 He brought him into his home and gave the donkeys fodder. The travelers washed their feet, and they ate and drank.
19:22 While they were making their hearts merry, look: the men of the town, worthless men, closed in around the house, pounding on the door. They said to the old man, the master of the house, "Bring out the man who came to your house, so we can know him."
19:23 The man, the master of the house, went out to them and said, "Don't, my brothers, please don't do evil. Now that this man has entered my house, do not do this disgraceful thing.
19:24 Here is my daughter, the virgin, and his concubine. Let me bring them out to you. Dishonor them and do to them whatever looks good to you. But do no such disgraceful thing to this man."
19:25 But the men would not listen to him. So the man seized his concubine and brought her outside to them. They knew her and abused her all night until morning, and they let her go as the dawn came up.
19:26 As morning turned, the woman came back. At the entrance of the man's house, where her master was, she fell, and she lay there until it grew light.
19:27 Her master rose in the morning, opened the doors of the house and went out to go his way. And there was the woman, his concubine, fallen at the entrance of the house, with her hands on the threshold.
## Twelve pieces
19:28 "Up," he told her. "We're going." No one answered. He lifted her onto the donkey, and the man set off for his own place.
19:29 He reached his house, took the knife, seized his concubine, and cut her up, bone by bone, into twelve pieces. Then he sent her to every corner of Israel.
19:30 All who saw it said, "Nothing like this has been done or seen from the day the Israelites came up from the land of Egypt to this day. Set your minds on it, talk it over, and speak out!"
## Israel gathers at Mizpah
20:1 All the Israelites came out, from Dan to Beersheba, and from the land of Gilead too. The community came together like one man before the LORD at Mizpah.
20:2 The pillars of the whole people, of all the tribes of Israel, came forward into the gathering of the people of God: four hundred thousand men on foot, drawing the sword.
20:3 Now the Benjaminites heard that the Israelites had gone up to Mizpah. And the Israelites said, "Speak. How did this evil come about?"
20:4 The Levite, husband of the woman who had been murdered, spoke up. "I came to Gibeah of Benjamin, I and my concubine, to stay the night," he said.
20:5 "The leading men of Gibeah rose up against me. They surrounded the house at night to get at me. Me they meant to kill, and my concubine they dishonored, and she died.
20:6 I took hold of my concubine, cut her in pieces and sent her through the whole land that Israel inherited, because they committed a vile act, a disgraceful thing in Israel.
20:7 Look, you are all Israelites. Put your word and your counsel forward, here and now."
20:8 Every one of the people stood up together, like one man. "Not one of us will go off to his tent," they said. "No one will turn back home.
20:9 Here is what we will do to Gibeah: we will go up against it as the lot directs.
20:10 From all the tribes of Israel we will take ten men out of every hundred, a hundred out of every thousand, and a thousand out of every ten thousand, to bring supplies for the troops. The rest, when they reach Gibeah of Benjamin, will pay it back for every disgraceful thing it has done in Israel."
20:11 All the men of Israel gathered against the town, joined together like one man.
20:12 The tribes of Israel sent men through all the tribes of Benjamin to say, "What is this evil that has happened among you?
20:13 So now hand over the men, the scoundrels in Gibeah, so we can put them to death and sweep the evil out of Israel." But the Benjaminites refused to listen to their brothers, the Israelites.
20:14 The Benjaminites gathered out of their towns to Gibeah, to march out to war with the Israelites.
20:15 That day the Benjaminites counted twenty-six thousand men from their towns who carried the sword, not counting the people of Gibeah, who mustered seven hundred picked men.
20:16 Out of all this army, seven hundred picked men were hampered in the right hand. Each of them could hit a single hair with a slingstone, never missing.
20:17 The men of Israel, not counting Benjamin, numbered four hundred thousand men who carried the sword, every one a fighting man.
## War on Benjamin
20:18 The Israelites set out and went up to Bethel, and they asked God, "Which of us should go up first to do battle with the Benjaminites?" "Judah goes first," the LORD said.
20:19 Next morning the Israelites rose and pitched camp against Gibeah.
20:20 The men of Israel marched out to battle with Benjamin and lined up for battle against them at Gibeah.
20:21 The Benjaminites came out of Gibeah, and that day they struck twenty-two thousand men of Israel to the ground.
20:22 But the people, the men of Israel, took courage, and they formed the battle line again in the place where they had formed it the first day.
20:23 And the Israelites went up and wept before the LORD until evening, and asked of the LORD, "Shall I come near again for battle with the Benjaminites, my brother?" The LORD said, "Go up against him."
20:24 On the second day the Israelites moved in close against the Benjaminites.
20:25 And on that second day Benjamin came out of Gibeah to meet them and struck to the ground eighteen thousand more of the Israelites, all of them men who carried the sword.
20:26 Then all the Israelites, all the people, went up and came to Bethel. They wept and sat there before the LORD, and that day they fasted until evening. They offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.
20:27 The Israelites consulted the LORD. The ark of the covenant of God was there in those days,
20:28 and Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, served before it in those days. They asked, "Should I go out to war yet again against my brother Benjamin, or should I stop?" The LORD said, "Go up. Tomorrow I will hand him over to you."
## The ambush at Gibeah
20:29 So Israel posted men in hiding all around Gibeah.
20:30 On the third day the Israelites went up against the Benjaminites and lined up against Gibeah as they had the other times.
20:31 The Benjaminites came out to meet the people and were drawn away from the town. As at the other times, they began to strike some of the people down dead on the highways (one goes up to Bethel, the other to Gibeah) and in the field: about thirty men of Israel.
20:32 "We have them beaten, just like the first time!" the Benjaminites said. But the Israelites had said, "Let's run and pull them away from the town onto the highways."
20:33 All the men of Israel rose from their place and formed up at Baal-tamar, while Israel's ambush was bursting out of its place, from the open ground of Geba.
20:34 Ten thousand chosen men from all Israel came at Gibeah from the front. The battle grew heavy, and Benjamin did not know that disaster was about to touch them.
20:35 The LORD routed Benjamin in front of Israel. That day the Israelites cut down twenty-five thousand one hundred men of Benjamin, all of them men who carried the sword.
20:36 Then the Benjaminites saw it: they were defeated. The men of Israel had given Benjamin room, trusting the ambush they had set against Gibeah.
20:37 The ambush moved fast and made a dash at Gibeah. The ambush marched in and struck the whole town with the edge of the sword.
20:38 The men of Israel and the ambush had agreed on a signal: the ambush would make a huge signal of smoke go up from the town.
20:39 The men of Israel turned back in the battle. Benjamin had started to strike down Israelites, about thirty men, and they were saying, "They are beaten in front of us, beaten like in the first battle!"
20:40 Then the signal began to rise from the town, a pillar of smoke. Benjamin looked back, and there was the whole town going up toward the sky.
20:41 Then the men of Israel turned around, and the men of Benjamin were struck with panic, for they saw that ruin had reached them.
20:42 They turned before the men of Israel toward the wilderness road, but the battle caught up with them, and those coming out of the towns were destroying them in its midst.
20:43 They surrounded Benjamin. They chased them and trod them down where they rested, as far as opposite Gibeah toward the sunrise.
20:44 Eighteen thousand men of Benjamin fell, all of them strong fighting men.
20:45 The rest turned and ran toward the wilderness, to the Rock of Rimmon. The Israelites gleaned five thousand of them on the highways, kept on close behind them as far as Gidom, and struck down two thousand of them.
20:46 The total of Benjamin's fallen that day came to twenty-five thousand men who carried the sword, all of them strong fighting men.
20:47 Six hundred men turned and fled toward the wilderness, to the Rock of Rimmon, and they lived at the Rock of Rimmon four months.
20:48 Then the men of Israel turned back against the Benjaminites. They put them to the sword, town after town, people and animals and everything that turned up. Every town they came to, they set on fire.
## Wives for Benjamin
21:1 Now the men of Israel had sworn at Mizpah, "None of us will give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife."
21:2 The people came to Bethel and sat there until evening before God. They lifted up their voices and wept, long and hard.
21:3 They said, "O LORD, God of Israel, what has made this happen in Israel, so that Israel is short one tribe today?"
21:4 The next day the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings.
21:5 And the Israelites said, "Who is there from all the tribes of Israel who did not go up in the assembly to the LORD?" For the great oath had been made about anyone who did not go up to the LORD at Mizpah: "He must surely be put to death."
21:6 The Israelites felt sorry for Benjamin their brother. They said, "One tribe has been chopped off from Israel this day."
21:7 "What can we do to find wives for the ones who are left? We swore by the LORD that we would not give them any of our daughters to marry."
21:8 So they asked, "Which of the tribes of Israel did not come up to the LORD at Mizpah?" And it turned out that no one had come into the camp, to the assembly, from Jabesh-gilead.
21:9 When the people were counted, not one of those who lived in Jabesh-gilead was there.
21:10 So the community sent twelve thousand of its strongest fighters there with these orders: "Go and put the people of Jabesh-gilead to the sword, women and little ones too.
21:11 Here is what you must do: every male, and every woman who has lain with a man, you are to put under the ban."
21:12 Among those living in Jabesh-gilead they found four hundred young women, virgins who had not known a man by lying with a male. They brought them to the camp at Shiloh in the land of Canaan.
21:13 Then the whole community sent messengers to speak with the Benjaminites at the Rock of Rimmon and called out to them, offering peace.
21:14 Benjamin came back at that time, and the Israelites gave them the women they had spared from Jabesh-gilead. But even so there were too few for them.
21:15 The people felt sorry for Benjamin. The LORD had made a breach in the tribes of Israel.
## The daughters of Shiloh
21:16 The elders of the community said, "What can we do about wives for the ones who are left? The women have been wiped out of Benjamin."
21:17 They said, "Benjamin's survivors must keep their holding, so that no tribe is wiped out from Israel.
21:18 But we cannot give them wives from our own daughters, because the Israelites swore, 'A curse on anyone who gives a wife to Benjamin!'"
21:19 Then they said, "Look, the LORD's festival is held year by year at Shiloh, north of Bethel, on the east side of the highway that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah."
21:20 They gave the Benjaminites these orders: "Go and lie in wait in the vineyards.
21:21 Keep watch. When you see the daughters of Shiloh coming out to dance in the circle dances, come out of the vineyards. Each of you grab a wife for himself from the daughters of Shiloh and go home to the land of Benjamin.
21:22 When their fathers or their brothers come to us to complain, we will say to them, 'Be gracious to us about them. We did not take a wife for each man in the war. And you did not give them to them, or by now you would be guilty.'"
21:23 The Benjaminites did so. They carried off wives to match their number, from the dancers they had seized. Then they went back to their inheritance, built the towns up again and lived there.
21:24 The Israelites then scattered from there, each man to his own tribe and clan. They went out from there, each man to his own inheritance.
21:25 There was no king in Israel in those days. Everyone did whatever was right in his own eyes.
