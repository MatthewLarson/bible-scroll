## The title
1:1 The Song of Songs, the best of songs, of Solomon.
## Her longing
1:2 Let him kiss me, kisses from his mouth! / Your lovemaking is better than wine.
1:3 The smell of your oils is good, / and your name is oil emptied out. / That is why young women love you.
1:4 Pull me along behind you, and let us run! / The king has taken me into his private rooms. / We will celebrate and be glad in you. / We will remember your lovemaking more than wine. / With good reason they love you.
## Dark and lovely
1:5 I am dark, and I am lovely, / you daughters of Jerusalem, / like the tents of Kedar, / like the tent curtains of Solomon.
1:6 Do not stare at me because I am dark, / because the sun has looked at me. / My mother's sons burned with anger at me. / They made me the guard of the vineyards, / but I have not guarded the vineyard that is mine.
## Where do you pasture?
1:7 Tell me, you whom I love with my whole self: / where do you pasture your flock? / Where do you let them rest at noon? / Why should I be like a woman wrapped in a veil / beside your companions' flocks?
1:8 If you do not know, / you beautiful one among women, / go out on the tracks of the flock / and pasture your young goats / beside the shepherds' camps.
## Praise for each other
1:9 Among Pharaoh's chariots, my darling, / I compare you to my mare.
1:10 Your cheeks are lovely with their rows of jewels, / your neck with its strings of beads.
1:11 We will make you jewelry of gold / with dots of silver.
1:12 While the king was at his dining couch, / my nard gave off its scent.
1:13 A pouch of myrrh is my lover to me. / He spends the night between my breasts.
1:14 A cluster of henna blossoms is my lover to me, / in the vineyards of En-gedi.
1:15 Look at you, my darling! You are beautiful. / Look at you! You are beautiful. / Your eyes are doves.
1:16 Look at you, my lover! You are handsome, / and pleasant too. / Our bed is fresh and green.
1:17 Cedars are the beams of our house, / and cypresses are our rafters.
## The crocus and the apple tree
2:1 I am a crocus of the Sharon plain, / a lily of the valleys.
2:2 A lily among thorns, / that is my darling among the daughters.
2:3 An apple tree among the trees of the woods, / that is my lover among the young men. / I longed for his shade and sat down, / and his fruit was sweet to my mouth.
## The banquet house
2:4 He has brought me to the house of wine, / and love is his banner over me.
2:5 Hold me up with raisin cakes, / revive me with apples, / for love has made me sick.
2:6 His left hand is under my head, / and his right hand holds me close.
2:7 I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, / by the gazelles or by the does of the field: / do not wake love and do not stir it up / until it wants to.
## Spring: get up, my darling
2:8 Listen! It is my lover! / Look, he is coming, / springing over the mountains, / skipping over the hills.
2:9 My lover is like a gazelle or a young deer. / Look, he is standing behind our wall, / looking in through the windows, / peering through the lattices.
2:10 My lover spoke up and said to me, "Get up, my darling, my beautiful one, / and come along.
2:11 Look, the winter has passed. / The rain has finished and gone away.
2:12 The blossoms have shown themselves in the land. / The time for pruning has come, / and the cooing of the turtledove / is heard in our land.
2:13 The fig tree is ripening its early figs, / and the vines in blossom give off their scent. / Get up, my darling, my beautiful one, / and come along.
2:14 My dove, in the clefts of the rock, / in the hiding place of the cliff, / show me how you look, / let me hear your voice. / For your voice is sweet, / and how you look is lovely."
## The little foxes
2:15 Seize the foxes for us, / the foxes, the small foxes / that wreck the vineyards, / now that our vineyards are in blossom.
2:16 My lover belongs to me, and I belong to him, / the one who grazes among the lilies.
2:17 Until the day breathes and the shadows run away, / turn, my lover, / and be like a gazelle or a young deer / on the mountains of Bether.
## The night search
3:1 Night after night on my bed / I searched for the one I love with my whole self. / I searched for him, but I did not find him.
3:2 I will get up now and go around the city, / through its streets and its open squares. / I will search for the one I love with my whole self. / I searched for him, but I did not find him.
3:3 The watchmen found me, / those who go around the city. / "Have you seen the one I love with my whole self?"
3:4 I had just passed them / when I found the one I love with my whole self. / I clung to him and would not let him go / until I had taken him to my mother's house, / to the room of the woman who conceived me.
3:5 I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, / by the gazelles or by the does of the field: / do not wake love and do not stir it up / until it wants to.
## Solomon's litter
3:6 Who is this coming up out of the wilderness, / in columns of smoke, / sweet with myrrh and frankincense, / with all the powders a trader sells?
3:7 Look, it is Solomon's bed! / Sixty warriors are around it, / from among Israel's warriors.
3:8 All of them grip swords. / They are trained in war. / Each man has his sword on his thigh / against the dread of the nights.
3:9 King Solomon built himself a litter / from Lebanon's wood.
3:10 Its posts he made of silver, / its support of gold, / its seat of purple cloth. / Its inside is fitted out with love / by the daughters of Jerusalem.
3:11 Go out and look, daughters of Zion, / at King Solomon, / at the crown his mother put on him / on his wedding day, / on the day his heart was glad.
## His praise of her
4:1 Look at you, my darling! You are beautiful. / Look at you! You are beautiful. / Behind your veil your eyes are doves. / Your hair is like a flock of goats / streaming down Mount Gilead.
4:2 Your teeth are like a flock of ewes just shorn, / coming up from the washing. / Every one of them is bearing twins, / and not one of them is left without her young.
4:3 Like scarlet thread are your lips, / and lovely is your mouth. / The side of your face behind your veil / is like a slice of pomegranate.
4:4 Your neck is like David's tower, / built in tiers, / with a thousand shields hung on it, / every shield a warrior's.
4:5 Your breasts, both of them, are like two fawns, / twins of a gazelle, / grazing among the lilies.
4:6 Until the day breathes and the shadows run away, / I will go to the mountain of myrrh / and to the hill of frankincense.
4:7 All of you is beautiful, my darling, / and you have no blemish.
## My sister, my bride
4:8 With me from Lebanon, bride, / with me from Lebanon, come. / Come down from the top of Amana, / from the top of Senir and Hermon, / from the lions' dens, / from the leopards' mountains.
4:9 You have taken my heart, my sister, my bride. / You have taken my heart / with just one of your eyes, / with just one chain of your necklace.
4:10 How lovely your lovemaking is, my sister, my bride! / How much better your lovemaking is than wine, / and the smell of your oils than all spices!
4:11 Your lips drip honey from the comb, my bride. / Under your tongue are honey and milk, / and the smell of your clothes is like the smell of Lebanon.
## The locked garden
4:12 My sister, my bride, is a garden with a lock on it, / a pool with a lock on it, / a spring with a seal on it.
4:13 What grows from you is an orchard: pomegranates, / choice fruits, / henna and nard,
4:14 nard and saffron, / calamus and cinnamon, / with every kind of frankincense tree, / myrrh and aloes, / with all the finest spices.
4:15 A spring for gardens, a well of living water, / streams flowing down from Lebanon.
## The garden opened
4:16 Wake up, north wind! Come, south wind! / Make my garden breathe, / and let its spices flow out. / Let my lover come into his garden / and eat its choice fruits.
5:1 I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride. / I have gathered my myrrh and my spice. / I have eaten my honeycomb and my honey. / I have drunk my wine and my milk. / Eat, friends! Drink, and be drunk with lovemaking!
## The knock at the door
5:2 I was asleep, but my heart was awake. / Listen! My lover is knocking: "Open to me, my sister, my darling, / my dove, my perfect one, / for my head is full of dew, / my hair is damp from the drops of the night."
5:3 I have taken off my tunic: how could I put it on again? / I have washed my feet: how could I get them dirty?
5:4 My lover sent his hand in through the opening, / and my insides churned for him.
5:5 I got up to open to my lover, / and myrrh dripped from my hands, / liquid myrrh from my fingers, / onto the handles of the bolt.
5:6 I opened to my lover, / but my lover had slipped away and was gone. / My life went out of me when he spoke. / I searched for him and could not find him. / I called out to him, and he gave me no answer.
5:7 The watchmen found me, / those who go around the city. / They hit me and bruised me. / They lifted my shawl off me, / those watchmen of the walls.
5:8 I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem: / if you find my lover, what will you tell him? / That love has made me sick.
## What is your lover like?
5:9 What makes your lover better than another lover, / you beautiful one among women? / What makes your lover better than another lover, / that you make us swear like this?
5:10 My lover is bright and ruddy, / standing out above ten thousand.
5:11 His head is gold, the finest gold. / His locks are curls, / black as a raven.
5:12 His eyes are like doves / beside channels of water, / bathing in milk, / sitting beside the full water.
5:13 His cheeks are like garden beds of spice, / like towers of sweet herbs. / His lips are lilies / dripping with liquid myrrh.
5:14 His hands are rods of gold / set with stones from Tarshish. / His belly is a plate of ivory / covered with sapphires.
5:15 His legs are pillars of marble / standing on bases of fine gold. / He looks like Lebanon, / as choice as the cedars.
5:16 His mouth is full of sweet things, / and everything about him is desirable. / This one is my lover, and this one is my friend, / daughters of Jerusalem.
## He has gone down to his garden
6:1 Where has your lover gone, / you beautiful one among women? / Which way has your lover turned, / so that we can look for him with you?
6:2 My lover has gone down to his garden, / to the beds of spice, / to graze in the gardens / and to pick lilies.
6:3 I belong to my lover, and my lover belongs to me, / the one who grazes among the lilies.
## His praise again
6:4 You are beautiful, my darling, like Tirzah, / lovely as Jerusalem, / as fearsome as troops with banners.
6:5 Turn your eyes away from me, / for they overpower me. / Your hair is like a flock of goats / streaming down from Gilead.
6:6 Your teeth are like a flock of ewes / coming up from the washing. / Every one of them is bearing twins, / and not one among them has lost her young.
6:7 The side of your face behind your veil / is like a slice of pomegranate.
6:8 Sixty are the queens and eighty the concubines, / and young women beyond counting.
6:9 One is my dove, my perfect one. / She is her mother's only one. / She is the pure one of the woman who bore her. / Young women saw her and called her happy. / Queens and concubines praised her.
6:10 Who is this looking down like the dawn, / lovely as the moon, / clear as the sun, / fearsome as troops with banners?
## The nut garden and the dance
6:11 I went down to the nut garden / to look at the green shoots in the stream bed, / to see if the vine had budded / and the pomegranates had flowered.
6:12 I did not know it. My longing set me / among the chariots of my noble people.
6:13 Come back, come back, Shulammite! / Come back, come back, so that we may look at you. / Why do you look at the Shulammite / as at the dance of the two camps?
## Praise from the feet up
7:1 How lovely your steps are in your sandals, / you daughter of a nobleman! / The curves of your thighs are like jewels, / the work of a skilled craftsman's hands.
7:2 Your navel is a round bowl. / May it never run short of mixed wine. / Your belly is a pile of wheat, / fenced in with lilies.
7:3 Your breasts, both of them, are like two fawns, / twins of a gazelle.
7:4 Your neck is like a tower of ivory. / In Heshbon, by the gate of Bat-rabbim, / your eyes are pools. / Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, / watching toward Damascus.
7:5 Your head upon you is like Mount Carmel, / and the hair of your head is like purple cloth. / A king is bound by its tresses.
7:6 How beautiful you are, how sweet, / love, among the delights!
7:7 You are as tall as a palm tree, / and your breasts are like bunches.
7:8 I said, "I will climb up the palm tree / and take hold of its fruit-stalks." / May your breasts be like bunches from the vine, / and may the smell of your breath be like apples,
7:9 and your mouth like the finest wine, / going down smoothly for my lover, / gliding over the lips of sleepers.
## Let us go to the fields
7:10 I belong to my lover, / and his desire is toward me.
7:11 Come, my lover, let us go out to the open country. / Let us spend the night in the villages.
7:12 Let us get up early and go to the vineyards, / to see if the vine has budded, / if the blossom has opened, / if the pomegranates have flowered. / There I will give you my lovemaking.
7:13 The mandrakes give off their scent, / and at our doors is every choice fruit, / new ones and old ones, / that I have saved up for you, my lover.
## If you were my brother
8:1 If only someone would make you my brother, / who nursed at my mother's breasts! / I would find you outside and kiss you, / and no one would look down on me.
8:2 I would lead you and bring you to my mother's house, / and you would teach me. / I would let you drink spiced wine, / the juice of my pomegranates.
8:3 His left hand is under my head, / and his right hand holds me close.
8:4 I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem: / do not wake love and do not stir it up / until it wants to.
## The seal and the flame
8:5 Who is this climbing up from the wilderness, / leaning on her lover? / Under the apple tree I woke you. / There your mother went into labor with you. / There the woman who bore you went into labor.
8:6 Put me like a seal on your heart, / like a seal on your arm. / Because love is strong, like death, / and jealousy is hard, like Sheol. / Its sparks are sparks of fire, / a flame of Yah.
8:7 Many waters cannot put out love, / and rivers cannot wash it away. / If a man gave for love all the wealth in his house, / people would only sneer at him.
## The little sister
8:8 We have a little sister, / and she has no breasts. / What shall we do for our sister / on the day she is spoken about?
8:9 If she is a wall, / we will build a silver battlement on her. / If she is a door, / we will shut her in with a plank of cedar.
8:10 I am a wall, / and my breasts are like towers. / So in his eyes I became / like one who finds peace.
## Two vineyards
8:11 A vineyard belonged to Solomon at Baal-hamon. / He gave the vineyard to those who guard it. / Each man would bring in a thousand silver pieces for its fruit.
8:12 My own vineyard is in front of me. / The thousand are yours, Solomon, / and two hundred go to those who guard its fruit.
## Hurry, my lover
8:13 You who live in the gardens, / companions are listening for your voice. / Let me hear it.
8:14 Hurry, my lover, / and be like a gazelle or a young deer / on the mountains of spices.
