## Critical findings

1. CRITICAL. notes.json #51 (5:5, "their priests sang to flutes and drums, were wreathed in ivy"), plus topics.json `vine`. The note says "The golden vine is not named in Josephus's description of the temple in the passages read for this part", and the vine topic says the vine "was a golden object visitors saw, which he does not tie to any Jewish teaching". Josephus does name it. War 5.5.4 (the temple's front gate): "it had also golden vines above it, from which clusters of grapes hung as tall as a man's height". Antiquities 14.3.1 says Aristobulus sent Pompey "a golden vine" worth 500 talents, which Strabo saw in Rome. This is the best Josephus link to Tacitus's "vitis aurea templo reperta", and the note denies it exists. Fix: replace the sentence with the War 5.5.4 and Antiquities 14.3.1 evidence. Remove the "passages read for this part" wording. Add the Bible's vine for Israel (Psalm 80:8-16; Isaiah 5:1-7; John 15:1) as a link, not a proof.

2. CRITICAL. notes.json #27 (5:4), topics.json `golden-calf`, chapters.json deep "What a Roman got right": "The Bible's only animal image in Israel's worship is Aaron's calf", "the only animal image in Israel's worship in the Bible". False on the site's own text. 1 Kings 12:28-29: Jeroboam "made two calves of gold... placed one calf in Bethel, and the other he put in Dan". 2 Kings 18:4: the bronze snake Moses made (Numbers 21:8-9) was burned to until Hezekiah crushed it. The temple itself had bronze oxen under the Sea (1 Kings 7:25) and the commanded cherubim (Exodus 25:18-22; 1 Kings 6:23-28). Fix: say "the Bible's best-known animal image in Israel's worship is Aaron's calf (Exodus 32), repeated by Jeroboam (1 Kings 12:28-29), and the bronze snake (2 Kings 18:4)". Then note that the cherubim over the ark are commanded images, and that Tacitus's "no image" does not mean "no sculpture at all".

3. CRITICAL. notes.json #85 (5:1 "the two kings", Bernice) and the berenice contribution. The note says "The Titus link rests only on that editor's note, so it is background here." Suetonius's own text (Titus 7 in the Thomson/Forester text the writer opened) says "his well-known attachment to queen Berenice, who received from him, as it is reported, a promise of marriage", and that he "immediately sent away Berenice from the city". The editor's footnote 786 adds the Xiphilinus detail. The claim contradicts a primary source the writer had open. Fix: cite Suetonius Titus 7 as the source for the attachment and its end, and keep footnote 786 as the editor's addition. The berenice core (lead in proposals/world/topics/berenice.json, "a tie with Titus that Roman writers talked about") already agrees with Suetonius, so this note is the one that is wrong.

4. CRITICAL. notes.json #6 (5:1): "Tacitus is the only Roman historian whose surviving pages bring a Roman army to the walls of Jerusalem". Cassius Dio, a Roman senator who wrote in Greek, narrates Pompey's assault on Jerusalem and the temple in book 37, chapters 15-17 (LacusCurtius). His account of Pompey says the temple was taken with difficulty and that the Jews "never had any statue of him even in Jerusalem itself". That also supports Tacitus 5:9 and weakens the H lens's "Tacitus errs ... says Pompey found an empty sanctuary". I checked Dio only for Pompey. I did not check the Titus books. Fix: delete the sentence. Add Dio 37 to the sources and as a check on 5:9. The writer listed Dio 40.3 as "left out because unchecked", but Dio 37 is an easily opened source.

## Major findings

8. MAJOR. notes #80, #82, chapters.json "Felix and Drusilla" and the variant on 5:9 (Drusilla). The two Drusilla "options" are presented as rivals, but Whiston's footnote to Antiquities 20.7.2 is both: "It is probable that Tacitus may say true, when he informs us that this Felix ... did once marry such a grandchild of Antonius and Cleopatra; and finding the name of one of them to have been Drusilla, he mistook her for that other wife". So Whiston and the Thomson/Forester footnote 537 agree on two wives. The only difference is whether Tacitus knew it. Fix: state it that way. The chapter's "Whiston ... thought Tacitus mixed them up" is accurate, but the note's option (2) is wrong as a separate view. Also, "Against: nothing" in #80 is too strong: Annals 12.54 has Felix already governing Samaria while Cumanus governed Galilee, while Josephus (Antiquities 20.6-7.1, read in the same text) sends Felix after Cumanus is banished. Add that difference. The "Spec" about royal families reusing names has no lines it rests on; give them or drop it.

9. MAJOR. Unchecked negatives about scholarship ("I know no scholar who...", "Nobody else I have read..."): notes #11, #20, #94, #109, #110, and the box "The Exodus told backward". These claim novelty without a search. STYLE rule 2 forbids unchecked claims. Fix: either search and state what you found, or drop the sentence and keep the `spec` label.
