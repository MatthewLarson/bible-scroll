Severity used here: **critical** = the English says the opposite of the Latin or gives the wrong person, place or event as the doer or subject (wrong facts in the reader's head); **major** = dropped or added thought, name, number, office or place, or a garbled sentence; **minor** = voice, slang, name form, punctuation, small bend.

## Verdict
The draft is NOT fit to publish as it stands. It is readable and mostly follows the order of events, but it is a loose paraphrase that, in dozens of places, reverses or scrambles who does what (Galba's own adoption speech, Otho's speeches, the deaths of Galba, Vinius and Piso, Poppaea and Octavia, Laco, the Helvetii, Vienna's disarming). Several sentences are repeated across the model's 75-word chunk seams (1:4, 1:18, 1:35, 1:50, 1:84), whole thoughts are dropped (1:1, 1:2, 1:26, 1:40, 1:48, 1:50, 1:90), and the voice slides between a good conversational register and slang that Tacitus's subject cannot carry ("sucked up", "ratted out", "get this", "total chicken", "fake news", "rebrands"). It cannot be called reviewed until every item below is applied and the whole chapter is re-read against the Latin. The summary of what to do: apply the numbered fixes, remove slang, close or open every quotation, then add headings.

## Findings by verse

### 1:1 to 1:8

1. **1:1 (critical).** "I never met Galba, Otho, or Vitellius, so I've got no reason to praise or blame them." The Latin is `mihi Galba Otho Vitellius nec beneficio nec iniuria cogniti` ("known to me by neither favor nor injury"). Not that he never met them. Fix: "Galba, Otho and Vitellius did me no favors and no harm, so I have no reason to praise or blame them."
2. **1:1 (major).** Dropped: Tacitus calls the Nerva and Trajan period "a richer and less worrying subject" (`uberiorem securioremque materiam`). Added: "wise Trajan" (Latin has only `imperium Traiani`). Fix: "If I live long enough, I'm saving for my old age a richer and less anxious subject: the reign of the divine Nerva and the rule of Trajan, a rare and happy time when you can think what you like and say what you think."
3. **1:1 (major).** "We naturally avoid writers who kiss up, but we love those who speak ill, because flattery feels like cowardice, while harsh words seem honest." Latin: `ambitionem scriptoris facile averseris, obtrectatio et livor pronis auribus accipiuntur` (you easily turn away from a flatterer; spite and malice get a ready ear). "Love" is too strong, and "cowardice" is wrong: flattery carries `foedum crimen servitutis` (the ugly charge of servility), spite `falsa species libertatis`. Fix: "We turn away easily from a writer who flatters, but we listen eagerly to spite and malice, because flattery carries the ugly charge of servility, while malice looks like honesty."
4. **1:1 (minor).** "When everyone agreed that one person should rule" is added; the Latin says it was `pacis interfuit`, in the interest of peace. "wrote ... with both skill and honesty" for `pari eloquentia ac libertate` (equal eloquence and freedom). Fix: "...when it became necessary for peace that all power go to one man". "skill and honesty" -> "eloquence and freedom".
5. **1:1 (minor).** Slang: "got messed up", "sucked up", "kiss up", "gonna", "get the ball rolling". Replace per G4: "History was damaged too, in many ways", "flattered", "Vespasian started my career, Titus advanced it, and Domitian took it further".
6. **1:2 (major).** Dropped: "even in peace full of horrors" (`ipsa etiam pace saevum`); the clause "the refusal or the acceptance of office" is cut to "refusing a job" (Latin `omissi gestique honores`: honors both skipped and held). "Wild behavior among the elite" for `magna adulteria` (great adulteries). Fix the opening: "I'm starting a history rich in disasters, terrible in its battles, torn by revolts, savage even in peace." Fix: "Being noble, rich, or holding office or turning it down was grounds for a charge". Fix: "There were sacred rites defiled and great adulteries among the elite."
7. **1:2 (minor).** "Suebi" -> "Suevi", "Sarmatians" -> "Sarmatæ" (G5). "Dacia saw its share of victories and defeats" -> "The Dacians made a name for themselves by the losses both sides suffered." "swallowed by the earth" is not in the Latin (`haustae aut obrutae urbes`): "cities in the richest coast of Campania were swallowed up or buried". "Rome burned" -> "Rome was ravaged by fires". "The people who turned others in got rewards just as bad as their crimes" -> "The informers' rewards were as hated as their crimes". "Parthians were ready to march, tricked by a fake Nero" -> "the Parthians almost went to war, fooled by a fake Nero". Slang: "sucked", "chaos" are fine, "so-called friends" -> "friends".
8. **1:3 (critical).** "the gods don't care about our well-being, only our suffering." Latin `esse ultionem`: only about punishing (vengeance). Fix: "that the gods care nothing for our safety, only for punishing us."
9. **1:3 (major).** "Mothers went into hiding with their sons" -> Latin `comitatae profugos liberos matres`: mothers went along with sons who were fleeing. Fix: "Mothers went with their sons into flight." Also "We saw men pushed to their limits": the Latin has `supremae clarorum virorum necessitates fortiter toleratae` (famous men bore their last extremity bravely): "Famous men faced their last hours bravely."
10. **1:4 (critical).** "freedmen and slaves of exiles" -> Latin `clientes libertique damnatorum et exulum`: clients (dependents) and freedmen. Fix: "the dependents and freed slaves of condemned and exiled men".
11. **1:4 (critical).** The last sentence: "they grabbed onto any rumor, desperate for hope after Nero's death." Latin `maesti et rumorum avidi`: gloomy and hungry for rumors. They were dejected, not hopeful. Fix: "The dirty crowd that lived at the circus and the theater, the worst slaves, and men who had gone through their money and lived off Nero's shameful excesses, were gloomy and hungry for any rumor."
12. **1:4 (major).** Repetition: "When Nero died, Romans were overjoyed." and then "When Nero died, senators breathed easier." Also added "News traveled fast", and "even the city's soldiers" with "the people" vague. Fix the middle: "Nero's death was welcome at first and set off joy, but it also stirred up all sorts of feelings, not only in Rome among the senators, the people and the city's soldiers, but in every legion and commander, because the secret was out: an emperor could be made somewhere other than Rome." Then "The senators felt free to speak as they pleased, since the new emperor was new to power and away from Rome."
13. **1:5 (major).** "Galba didn't give them their promised bonus": the Latin (via base) has the bonus promised in Galba's name; fine. But the chain "They realized that this new emperor's favor was already taken up with those who'd helped him get there" is correct in meaning; however the soldiers' reasoning about peace and war ("great services and great rewards had not the same room in peace as in war") is cut. Fix the second sentence: "They also saw that in peacetime there's less room than in war for big services and big rewards, and that the favor of an emperor made by the legions had already been spoken for."
14. **1:5 (minor).** "Nymphidius" attempt: "The soldiers got riled up when their prefect... tried to grab power" is fine in sense but the order is wrong and "got riled up" is slang. "The tough discipline ... turned into a burden. They'd grown used to ... loving his vices just like they'd once admired his good qualities" -> "loving their emperors' vices as much as they had once respected their virtues". "Most of his actions didn't follow that approach" -> "His other acts were not like that."
15. **1:6 (major).** "It was like they were innocent." Garbled. Latin `inauditi atque indefensi tamquam innocentes perierant`: they died unheard and undefended, like innocent men. Fix: "Both died without a hearing or a defense, as if they were innocent men."
16. **1:6 (major).** Dropped "ill-omened" and the cause: the Latin has his entry into the city `trucidatis tot milibus inermium militum` (after thousands of unarmed soldiers were butchered), `infaustus omine` (ill-omened), `formidolosus` to the very killers. Fix: "Galba entered the city after thousands of unarmed soldiers had been cut down. It was a bad omen, and even the men who had done the killing were afraid of it."
17. **1:6 (minor).** "worthless fool" -> "the worst of men"; "Galba's march ... slow and bloody" fine; "He marched his Spanish legion into Rome, while Nero's naval legion still held the city" -> "He brought the Spanish legion in, while the legion Nero had raised from the fleet was still there."
18. **1:7 (major).** Trebonius Garutianus was a procurator (Latin `procurator`), not "the local governor". Fix: "the procurator Trebonius Garutianus, acting on Galba's order".
19. **1:7 (critical).** "pushing him to rebel when they couldn't win over his troops": Latin `a legatis bellum suadentibus, postquam impellere nequiverint`: after the legates urged him to war and could not push HIM into it. Fix: "...that the legates themselves urged him to take up arms, and when they couldn't push him into it, they invented a charge against him."
20. **1:7 (major).** "his slaves were grabbing all the money they could get, hoping to get rich while their master was still alive": Latin `tamquam apud senem festinantes`: in a hurry because their master was an old man. Fix: "and the slaves grabbed at quick gains, hurrying to get rich with an old man for a master."
21. **1:7 (critical).** "Even Galba's reign was a joke to those who'd known Nero's youthful rule." Latin `ipsa aetas Galbae inrisui ac fastidio erat`: Galba's old age itself was laughed at and found disgusting. Fix: "Galba's very age drew laughter and disgust from people used to Nero's youth, who, as the crowd does, judged their emperors by their looks and good figure."
22. **1:7 (minor).** "took care of him" for `interfecerant` -> "killed him"; "undoubtedly" in "undoubtedly fomenting sedition" was dropped (Latin `haud dubie turbantem`): "who was clearly stirring up trouble".
23. **1:8 (critical).** "He'd been pulled back supposedly because he was too close to Nero." Latin `abducto Verginio per simulationem amicitiae`: Verginius was taken away under the pretext of friendship (with the Emperor Galba, per the base). Not Nero. Fix: "Verginius had been called away on the pretext of friendship with the emperor."
24. **1:8 (critical).** "Even people who hadn't been wronged by Capito's execution were angry about it." Latin `etiam qui queri non poterant, tamen indignabantur`: even those who could not complain (it was not an injustice to them) were angry. Fix: "Even people who couldn't call Capito's execution unjust were angry about it."
25. **1:8 (major).** "Galba had just made them Roman citizens" overstates `recenti dono Romanae civitatis`; "had recently given Roman citizenship". "taxes": "lower taxes" is fine. "Some thought he wasn't interested" -> "it was unclear whether he had refused to rule". "slap in the face" is added: "they took it as an accusation against themselves". "At Rome, that's how things stood, as much as you can say about so many people" -> "That was the mood in Rome, as far as you can say of such a crowd." "Spain was run by" fine.

### 1:9 to 1:20

Nero ... deposuerat ... mox suspectum ... seposuit`).
