Scope: notes.json (152 notes), glosses.json (44), variants.json (21), chapters.json (1 page, 16 settings), topics.json (48 contributions), the part's sources.json, and the topic cores in content/proposals/world/topics/ that this part created or fed (year-of-four-emperors, galba, otho, vitellius, vespasian, titus-emperor, domitian, josephus, tacitus, praetorian-guard, roman-senate, roman-army, roman-omens, roman-citizenship, roman-governors, roman-knights, judea, plus the shared cores it touches). ## Verdict

The context layer is thorough and mostly accurate: dates, Latin forms, Josephus quotations, the Suetonius and Plutarch parallels, the Bible quotations and the date arithmetic (Kalends, Nones, Ides) all check out, and no explicit sexual or gory detail is repeated. It is not ready to publish. Three things are false or unsupported in a way the rules call critical: five quotations given in quotation marks as Plutarch's words are not his (one of them also contradicts Thayer's note on the golden milestone); the page says in three places that Tacitus agrees with Josephus on two facts that Tacitus does not state anywhere in the Histories; and four notes (and one topic core) credit Wikipedia's Equites page with sesterce figures and a conversion it does not contain. Beyond that, the hedge/process statements the rules forbid are still in the content (two notes, one variant, one setting), one note still says "the Plain translation", a note on the Latin of 1:1 contradicts its own anchor, two variants are not variants (toggling them puts Suetonius's or another author's text into Tacitus), the daggered word at 1:2 has no variant, the timeline dates the night riot to "March" with no source, and several obvious Bible links (the speaking ox and Balaam's donkey, Dalmatia, Gaul/Galatia, Spain) are missing while the chapter page claims links the notes do not carry.

Findings: 3 critical, 13 major, 12 minor.

## Critical

**1. (critical) Quotations in quotation marks that are not Plutarch's words, and a milestone claim Thayer's note contradicts.** File: notes.json. Notes 85 (1:27 "How many soldiers?"), 86 (1:27 "The golden milestone"), 87 (1:28 Martialis), 90 (1:32 "The advisers' debate"), 100 (1:42 "Vinius's last words"); glosses.json 1:27 "golden milestone".
Evidence (Plutarch, Galba, Loeb, as printed at Thayer):
- Note 85 prints "only twenty-three soldiers" (Galba 25.1). Plutarch: "were no more than twenty-three."
- Note 86 prints Plutarch calling it "the milestone where the Italian roads end" (Galba 24.4). Plutarch: "to where a gilded column stood, at which all the roads that intersect Italy terminate." The word "milestone" and the quoted phrase are not in Plutarch. Worse, Thayer's own note 11 on Suetonius, Otho 6, which the writer cites for the same milestone, says: "The Roman roads were supposed to converge at that point, but the distances on them were reckoned from the gates." So the note's lead ("marked where Italy's roads were counted from"), its last sentence ("Rome itself was the starting point of the empire's roads") and the gloss ("where the roads of Italy were counted from") say the opposite of the source the writer read.
- Note 87 prints "the tribune Martialis" in quotes (25.2-3). Plutarch: "Martialis, the military tribune in charge of the watch at the time."
- Note 90 prints "Vinius argues against Galba leaving the palace, while Celsus and Laco urge him to go out" (26.1). Plutarch: "as Galba purposed to go forth, and Vinius would not permit it, while Celsus and Laco urged it and vehemently chided Vinius."
- Note 100 prints "claiming to be against Otho's wishes," (27.4). Plutarch: "he admitted himself a party to the conspiracy against Galba by crying out that he was put to death contrary to the wishes of Otho." (This also drops Plutarch's own point that Vinius thereby admitted being in the plot, which the note's link strength "strong. Both give the claim" misses.)
The facts behind notes 85, 87, 90 and 100 are right; the quotation marks are false. STYLE rule 2: never invent a quotation. The two "quotations" that do match Plutarch (Galba 26.2 "Who gave thee thy orders?" and 27.1 "Do your work, if this is better for the Roman people") show the writer could copy exactly when it mattered.
Fix: remove the quotation marks from notes 85, 87, 90, 100 and paraphrase, or copy Plutarch's words exactly. In note 86 and the gloss write: "a gilded column in the Forum where, the story went, the roads of Italy met (the distances were reckoned from the city gates, Thayer's note on Suetonius, Otho 6)", and delete the sentence "Rome itself was the starting point of the empire's roads". Name the translation (Loeb, Perrin, 1926, public domain) in the plutarch-lives source entry.

**2. (critical) The page says Tacitus agrees with Josephus on facts Tacitus does not state.** Files: chapters.json (views.J), topics.json (josephus), notes.json (note 132, 1:76 "Judea's army swears to Otho").
- chapters.json views.J: Josephus "agrees with Tacitus that Vespasian waited, that Titus turned back, and that Galba reigned seven months." topics.json josephus: Josephus "agrees with Tacitus on Vespasian's pause, Titus's turning back and the length of Galba's reign."
- Evidence: I searched all of texts/en/His.json (key cb, Books 1 to 5) for "seven months" and "seventh month": no hit. Tacitus nowhere gives the length of Galba's reign. In Book 1 (the only book this part covers) Titus is sent to Galba (1:10) but does not turn back; that is told at 2:1-2, where Tacitus gives a different reason (ambition, or love of Berenice) from Josephus's "by a Divine impulse" (Jewish War 4.9.2). Nor does Tacitus say in Book 1 that Vespasian paused the war: he says only that Vespasian "had no wish or intention against Galba" (1:10) and had his army swear Otho's oath (1:76). Josephus (4.9.2, Whiston) says Vespasian "put off at first his expedition against Jerusalem, and stood waiting" and that Galba "reigned seven months and as many days." So these are Josephus's statements, not agreements.
- Note 132 (1:76) calls the pause "Link strength: strong" and "Tacitus and Josephus agree that Vespasian waited and did not move" (its preview), although its own body quotes only Josephus for the pause. The chapter-page spec box (deep[3]) has the same weakness ("Tacitus is the writer who fills in why the pause lasted": Tacitus 1:10 and 1:76 do not).
Fix: rewrite as "Josephus says Vespasian paused the war and that Galba reigned seven months and as many days (Jewish War 4.9.2); Tacitus has Vespasian sending Titus to Galba (1:10) and the Judean army swearing Otho's oath (1:76), and tells Titus's turning back in Book 2 (2:1-2) with a different reason." Change note 132 to "Link strength: the oath is Tacitus's; the pause is inferred from Josephus", and change the preview.

**3. (critical) Sesterce figures and a conversion credited to Wikipedia's Equites page, which has none of them.** Files: notes.json 24 (1:4 "Roman Knights"), 71 (1:20 "How much is 2,200 million sesterces?"), 79 (1:24), 141 (1:82); proposals/world/topics/roman-knights.json.
- Note 24: "Wikipedia says a Roman Knight wore a gold ring and met a property test of 400,000 sesterces." Note 71: "Four sesterces made a denarius (Wikipedia, Equites: 60,000 sesterces equal 15,000 denarii)." Notes 79 and 141 build their arithmetic on it. The core roman-knights repeats "(400,000 sesterces after Augustus)" as Wikipedia's.
- Evidence (Wikipedia, Equites, raw text): the property requirement is "100,000 denarii" (Augustus' legislation, line "meeting the property requirement of 100,000 denarii to use the title of eques and wear the narrow-striped tunic and gold ring"). The page never says 400,000 sesterces and never gives a sesterce-to-denarius rate. "60,000 sesterces" appears only as the salary class of the sexagenarii ("receiving 300,000, 200,000, 100,000, and 60,000 sesterces per annum"), and "15,000" denarii only as the lowest procurator salary, in different sentences. The writer joined two unrelated numbers into a "page says" equation.
- The arithmetic itself is right: Wikipedia, Denarius, gives 1 aureus = 25 denarii = 100 sestertii, so 4 sesterces to the denarius; 100,000 denarii = 400,000 sesterces; 2,200,000,000 / 400,000 = 5,500; / 4 = 550 million; 100 sesterces = 25 denarii; 5,000 sesterces = 1,250 denarii.
Fix: say "Wikipedia, Equites: a property requirement of 100,000 denarii" and "Wikipedia, Denarius: a denarius was worth four sesterces, so 400,000 sesterces"; add the key `wiki-denarius`; delete the "60,000 sesterces equal 15,000 denarii" parenthesis; fix the core roman-knights the same way.

## Major

**4. (major) Capitol fire told as Vespasian's men's act, against the primary text and the writer's other Wikipedia page.** notes.json 14 (1:2 "The Capitol on fire").
The note says the Year of the Four Emperors page "says Vespasian's men burned the temple" and then, "He [Tacitus] counts it as the worst shame of the war." The Year page does say "they burned down the temple of Jupiter" (Vespasian's men "caught and killed" Vitellius), but the Domitian page the note also cites says "On 19 December, Vitellianists burst onto the Capitol", and Tacitus 3:71 (texts/en/His.json, available in the site) says "A doubt arises at this point, whether it was the assailants who threw lighted brands on to the roofs, or whether, as the more general account has it, the besieged thus sought to repel the assailants", and the Capitol was "neither defended by friends, nor spoiled by a foe". The reader gets one side of a dispute, and Tacitus's own doubt is left out.
Fix: "Who set the fire is disputed. Tacitus says the Vitellianists were the attackers and the Flavian besieged under Sabinus, and that it was uncertain whether the attackers or the defenders threw the first brands, the more general story blaming the defenders (3:71); Wikipedia's page on the year puts it as 'they burned', its page on Domitian as Vitellianists storming the hill." Use the Tacitus text, not Wikipedia, for the fact.

**5. (major) Note 2 contradicts its own anchor and misreads "prioris aevi".** notes.json 2 (1:1 "Counting from the city's founding").
(a) The note says the translation "keeps the number and drops the label 'of the earlier age'". The anchor, taken from plain.txt, is "The earlier 820 years since Rome was founded": the translation keeps "earlier". (b) The note says Tacitus "sets the free age of the Republic against what came after Actium" with the label "of the earlier age", and that the label is "easy to miss". Wikipedia, Ab urbe condita: AD 1 is AUC 754, so 820 years after the founding is about AD 67 or 68, Nero's last year, and AD 69 is AUC 822. The 820 years therefore include about a hundred years of the Principate; Actium (31 BC) comes in the middle of them, and the Latin puts the contrast after the colon ("postquam bellatum apud Actium ... magna illa ingenia cessere"). "Prioris aevi" cannot mean "the Republic".
Fix: delete both claims; say only "the 820 years run from the founding (traditionally 753 BC) to Nero's death; Tacitus's own period begins after them. Wikipedia, Ab urbe condita." Do not give an interpretation of "prioris aevi" that is not from an opened commentary.

**6. (major) Note 10 states, unsourced, what the wars of 1:2 are.** notes.json 10 (1:2 "Which four emperors?").
"The wars he goes on to name were the three civil wars of that year and the revolt on the Rhine." The Latin is "trina bella civilia, plura externa ac plerumque permixta: ... Galliae nutantes ..." (texts/orig/His.json 1:2): Tacitus does not say "of that year", does not identify the three, and names no "revolt on the Rhine" (he has "the Gauls wavered" and later "a Sarmatian and Suevian rising"). The note's other sentence, that readers "usually count" Galba, Otho and Vitellius, has no source; the writer's own sources-checked.md lists "who the fourth emperor is" under "left out because I could not check them" and then the note half-answers it.
Fix: replace with "Tacitus says four emperors were killed by the sword and three civil wars were fought; he does not name them here. Galba, Otho and Vitellius all died in 69 (1:41, Wikipedia on Otho and Vitellius). Which fourth he means is not given in the sentence." Delete the Rhine sentence.

**7. (major) Two variants are not variants; the toggle would put another author's text into Tacitus; one `why` reverses its own reading order.** variants.json 1:22 "Ptolemaeus or Seleucus?" and 1:41 "Galba's last words".
- 1:22: the second reading is "Seleucus", Suetonius's name. The entry admits it is "a difference between two authors, not between manuscripts of Tacitus." STYLE: variants.json records manuscript witnesses. A reader who taps it gets "Seleucus" inside Tacitus's sentence, a reading Tacitus's text never had. The same content is already in note 77 (same verse, same title): STYLE says never explain the same point twice.
- 1:41: reading 0 is Tacitus's "more common" report (the offered throat), reading 1 is Tacitus's other report (pleading), reading 2 is Suetonius. All three are accounts, not readings of a manuscript; switching puts Suetonius's story, or a duplicate of Tacitus's own previous sentence, into the translation. The `why` says Suetonius's general report "agrees with Tacitus's first": Suetonius's "more general account" (Galba 20.1: "he offered them his neck without resistance, urging them to do their duty and strike") agrees with Tacitus's second report in the text (the offered throat), and the first report in Tacitus's order (the plea for time to pay the bonus) is nearer Suetonius's other report (he cried "What mean you, fellow soldiers? I am yours and you are mine," and "promised them largess"). The variant's Suetonius reading leaves out the promise of largess.
- The Suetonius reading carries `"orig": "(Latin not opened)"` (see finding 9).
Fix: delete both entries. Keep notes 77 and a new note at 1:41 for the three accounts (notes already carry 99 and 91). If a variant is wanted at 1:41, use only a true textual difference, and only from an apparatus actually opened.

**11. (major) Unsourced chronology.** chapters.json deep[0] and settings[14]; notes.json none.

## Minor
