# Review 2: Histories of Tacitus, Book 3 (His 03-03), the context layer

Scope: notes.json (84), glosses.json (32), variants.json (17), chapters.json, topics.json (35 contributions), sources.json, and the topic cores in `content/proposals/world/topics/` that this part created (primus-antonius, flavius-sabinus, capitoline-hill, cremona). The translation is out of scope; two possible translation errors are under "Translation flags". `node tools/build.js --check His/03-03` and `--check His`: 0 errors, 0 warnings (the check does not look at topic-core `sources`, see finding 8).

Severity: critical = false or unsupported claim, a Bible verse that does not say what the note says, explicit detail. major = a rule break, a fairness failure, a missing required piece. minor = polish, exactness.

Everything below was checked in something opened in this session: Thayer's Suetonius (Vitellius, Domitian, Vespasian, Nero), Thayer's Cassius Dio (Cary, books 64 and 65), Whiston's Josephus (War and Antiquities, Gutenberg texts), Thayer's Plutarch (Publicola), the Latin and Church and Brodribb in `texts/`, the site's own plain.txt for every quoted Bible verse, Wikipedia pages (Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, Marcus Antonius Primus, Second Battle of Bedriacum, Year of the Four Emperors, Polemon II of Pontus, Titus Flavius Sabinus (consul AD 69), Musonius Rufus), vatican.va (CCC 2309, 2311), bookofconcord.org (Augsburg XVI), a University of Washington reprint of the Schleitheim Confession, and the Biblica abstract page for Parker (2001). Where I could not open something I say so.

## Verdict

The layer is much better than a first draft. Every Bible quotation I compared (about 45 verses) matches the site's Plain English Translation word for word, the Latin words in the `word` notes are right, the 3:5 crux and the 3:86 verbless sentence are described correctly, and most Suetonius, Josephus and Dio quotations are exact. The problems are of three kinds. First, a handful of statements are false against a source the writer cited or could have opened: the 3:28 note says Tacitus calls Pliny "the better source" when Tacitus says he cannot decide; the chapter page and a topic say Josephus does not mention Cremona when Josephus gives the figures for it; the "pair of fires" theory says "the same army" burned the Capitol and destroyed the Jerusalem temple, which is not so; the Capitol note says Jesus foretold a "third fire", which Mark 13:2 does not say; and Primus Antonius's legion is taken from Wikipedia against Tacitus 2:86. Second, rule-2 and rule-4 failures: first-person "I did not find" hedges, a Wikipedia-only attribution to Wiedemann, an uncited and disputed Pontus date, a Domitian "own idea" that published scholarship already holds and disputes, undefined source keys in three topic cores, and a Schleitheim quotation that is Wikipedia's summary, not the Confession. Third, the chapter page leaves out settings for about nine speeches and letters, and the war/soldiers card has three traditions. No explicit sexual, crude or graphic detail is repeated in any note, gloss, title or topic, and I propose no `sensitive.json` entry.

Count: critical 5, major 13, minor 19.

## Critical

**1. notes.json, 3:28 "Messalla reports" (Tacitus names his sources). Tacitus is made to say something he does not.**
Note: "Gaius Plinius, whom Tacitus calls the better source, blames Antonius."
Evidence: Latin 3:28 `Hormine id ingenium, ut Messala tradit, an potior auctor sit C. Plinius, qui Antonium incusat, haud facile discreverim` = "whether it was Hormus's idea, as Messalla reports, or whether Pliny, who blames Antonius, is the better authority, I could not easily decide." The plain.txt line says the same ("I can't easily decide"). The same misreading feeds the topic `tacitus` ("and Gaius Plinius for the other"), which is fine, but the note's "whom Tacitus calls the better source" is not.
Fix: replace with "Tacitus cannot decide between them: Messalla says it was Hormus's idea, and Gaius Plinius, who blames Antonius, may be the better authority, but Tacitus says he cannot easily tell."

**2. chapters.json deep "How the other ancient writers tell it" ("He does not mention Cremona in the sections I read") and topics.json `josephus` ("In the sections I read he does not mention Cremona"). False.**
Evidence: Whiston's War, Book 4 chapter 11 sections 2-3 (about Niese 4.634-637; Gutenberg text, lines "Antonius about Cremona in Gall" and "fled to Cremona"): Josephus has Caecina find Antonius "about Cremona", the army desert and then repent, the fight, "fled to Cremona; then did Primus take his horsemen, and cut off their entrance into the city ... and fell into the city together with the rest, and gave leave to his soldiers to plunder it", and gives "Vitellius's whole army, being thirty thousand and two hundred, while Antonius lost no more ... than four thousand and five hundred". So Josephus gives the Cremona figures and puts the plunder on Antonius's permission, which bears directly on the 3:49 and 3:32 judgments of Antonius. The writer's sources-checked file lists "Josephus on Cremona (not found in sections opened)": the sections opened were the wrong ones (the page cites 4.633 and 4.636 as individual lines but never reads 4.634-637 as a whole).
Fix: delete the "does not mention Cremona" sentences. Add to the Josephus paragraph and to the cremona core: "Josephus (War 4.634-637) tells a shorter and different story: Caecina's army deserts and repents, Antonius storms Cremona, and Josephus has him allow the plunder; he gives 30,200 Vitellianist dead against 4,500 of Antonius's men. Tacitus puts 40,000 armed men into the city at the sack (3:33)." Mark the numbers as Josephus's, not checked against anything else.

**3. notes.json 3:72 spec "Claude's theory: a pair of fires" and chapters.json deep spec box. False statement of fact.**
Note: "The same army, under Vespasian's son Titus, was sent there (Josephus, War 4.658 ...)"; box: "The Jerusalem temple fell the next year to the same army."
Evidence: Tacitus 3:71-73 and Josephus 4.649 say the Capitol was burned by Vitellius's soldiers ("the soldiers that came from Germany", Josephus 4.646-649); Dio 64.17.3 says "the soldiers of Vitellius". Titus went to Jerusalem with "a select part of his army" from the East (War 4.658), the army that had been in Judea and Egypt. The two fires were started by opposite sides of the civil war.
Fix: delete "the same army" in both places. Say only that the temple in Jerusalem fell the next year to Titus's army, and that the Capitol had been burned by Vitellius's German soldiers in a war that Vespasian's side won. The theory (a Jewish or Christian reader setting the two losses side by side) survives without the false claim. Also drop "I found no ancient source that links the two" or replace it with a source-based statement (see minor 24).

**4. notes.json 3:71 "So the Capitol burned to the ground with its gates shut". Mark 13:2 is made to foretell a fire.**
Note: "Jesus foretold a third fire for the temple of his day: 'not one stone will be left on a stone' (Mark 13:2)."
Evidence: the site's Mark 13:2 reads "Here not one stone will be left on a stone. Every one will be knocked down." Luke 21:6 the same. Neither says fire. "Third" is also unexplained (586 BC, AD 69 Capitol, AD 70 Jerusalem are three different fires of two different temples). Josephus, who tells the burning of the Jerusalem temple, is not cited here.
Fix: "Jesus foretold that the buildings of the temple would be knocked down (Mark 13:2; Luke 21:6). Josephus tells that the temple burned in AD 70 (cite the exact War passage after opening it)." Drop "third fire".

**5. notes.json 3:2 "Primus Antonius" (and topic core primus-antonius "Who he was"). Contradicts Tacitus.**
Note: "Galba brought him back and gave him the 13th legion in Pannonia." Taken from the Wikipedia page ("placed him in command of the Legio XIII Gemina").
Evidence: Tacitus 2:86 (texts/orig/His.json): `praepositus a Galba septimae legioni` = "set by Galba over the 7th legion" (Church and Brodribb: "appointed by Galba to command the 7th legion"). The 13th is the legion at Petovio in 3:1, not Antonius's command from Galba. This is a Wikipedia claim used where the primary text is in the site itself.
Fix: "Galba restored him to the Senate and gave him the 7th legion (Tacitus 2:86; Wikipedia says the 13th)". Do the same in the primus-antonius core. Also in the core, the Becco line is from Suetonius, Vitellius 18, not Vespasian (see finding 8).

## Major

**6. notes.json 3:74 spec "Claude's theory: Domitian, 'Our Lord and God'" and topic core domitian `claude` box. Provenance wrong, strongest objection missing.**
The note says "This is my own idea, and I did not open a source that argues it"; the topic says "I did not open a scholar who argues this." STYLE rule 4 requires saying where a theory overlaps published scholarship, and rule 2 forbids the "I did not open" hedge. The idea is not new. A search turned up Floyd O. Parker, "'Our Lord and God' in Rev 4,11: Evidence for the Late Date of Revelation?", Biblica 82 (2001) 207-231 (opened: bsw.org abstract page). Parker says the Domitian reading is "a commonly-held belief", and argues against it: the pair "Lord and God" has a Jewish origin in the Septuagint and other Jewish sources and, in his words, was used of rulers in the Greek East more than a century before Domitian, so it cannot date Revelation. The note gives only the Domitian side and treats the late date as the condition, without the objection.
Fix: relabel as "Claude's reading of an old idea". Say the Domitian reading of Revelation 4:11 is common in commentaries on the late date and that Parker (Biblica 82, 2001) argues the title is Jewish and older than Domitian. Keep Suetonius, Domitian 13 (exact quote verified: "Our Master and our God bids that this be done", from the formal letter written for his procurators) and say plainly that it does not date Revelation. Remove the "did not open a source" sentences. The note's `r` lists Revelation 13:4, which the body does not use; remove it or use it (Parker finds the link to chapter 13 weak).

- 3:9 Caecina's letter to the Flavianist legions and the Flavianist leaders' replies, read to the army.
- 3:10 the letter from Vespasian that met Flavianus on the road (a line, but it saves his life).
- 3:36-37 Vitellius's praise of the soldiers' loyalty to a crowded assembly (3:36) and his "speech composed for grandeur" to the Senate (3:37).
- 3:41 Valens's letter to Vitellius asking for help.
- 3:52 Mucianus's letters to Primus, Varus and Plotius Griphus.
- 3:56 Vitellius addressing the troops when the birds and the bull appeared (the omen note anchors here).
- 3:63 and 3:78 the offers of safety and money sent to Vitellius, and the secret letters he is said to have sent Antonius.
- 3:82 Antonius calling the legions to an assembly at the Mulvian Bridge.
Fix: add a card for each (to, when, why, readers). Many can share the existing `readers` text. For 3:78 give the two accounts Tacitus gives (treachery, or invention to please Mucianus) as `views`.

**8. Topic cores cite source keys that are defined nowhere.**
`primus-antonius.json` lists `suetonius-vespasian-thayer`; `capitoline-hill.json` and `flavius-sabinus.json` list `josephus-war-4-lexundria`. `grep` finds neither key in content/sources.json or any sources.json. `build.js` (line 602) shows an unknown key as its raw text, so the readers would see "suetonius-vespasian-thayer" as a source. `--check` does not test topic-core `sources`, which is why it passes. The Becco nickname is also from Suetonius, Vitellius 18 (checked: "who was born at Tolosa and in h[is] ... bore the surname Becco"), not Vespasian.
Fix: use the existing keys `suetonius-caesars` and `josephus`, and name the passage in the text (Vitellius 18; War 4.645, 4.649).

**9. chapters.json (H lens, deep "A map of the book") and topics.json `year-of-four-emperors` `uncommon`: Wiedemann attributed from a Wikipedia footnote.**
Wikipedia says (opened): "Some scholars argue that the new regime distorted the events, especially through the writings of the historian Tacitus", footnote "Wiedemann 2010, pp. 280-282". The writer did not open Wiedemann. STYLE rule 2: what a named scholar argues goes in only if checked in an opened source. Also the "especially through the writings of the historian Tacitus" half of Wikipedia's sentence is left out, which is the half this site's reader most needs. The H lens is then left with no named holder who argues anything.
Fix: write "Wikipedia's article on the year says some scholars argue that the Flavian regime distorted the events, especially in Tacitus's writing." Delete the Wiedemann name and pages unless the book is opened. The H lens should name one or two checked scholars or say plainly that none was checked.

**10. notes.json 3:47 "a freedman of Polemon" and topic core berenice: "Pontus became a province in 62".**
The Wikipedia page says 62 with no citation and the note says so. A second search shows the date is disputed: Smith's 1890 dictionary and English Wikipedia say 62; a numismatic dealer's page and omniatlas say 64 or 65 (search summaries only; I did not open those pages). Suetonius, Nero 18 (opened) says only that Nero added "the realm of Pontus, when it was given up by Polemon", with no year.
Fix: drop the year. Write "under Nero, Pontus was made a province when Polemon gave it up (Suetonius, Nero 18)". Remove "no citation given". The identification of Tacitus's Polemon with Berenice's husband rests on that Suetonius line plus Josephus (Ant. 20.145, "Poleme, king of Cilicia"); say so instead of "the page identifies one king", because the Wikipedia page does not mention Tacitus 3:47 (opened; it says nothing of Anicetus).

**11. notes.json 3:71 "Who set the Capitol on fire? Four ancient accounts" and topic core capitoline-hill: Suetonius understated.**
Note: "Suetonius has the temple of Jupiter set on fire in the fighting, with Vitellius watching." Topic: "Josephus, Suetonius and Dio put it on the Vitellianists."
Evidence (Thayer, Vitellius 15): "Then he set fire to the temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus and destroyed them, viewing the battle and the fire from the house of Tiberius, where he was feasting." Suetonius puts the act on Vitellius himself, not on the fighting. This is the strongest contrast with Tacitus, who leaves open that the defenders did it. Plutarch (Publicola 15.2, opened) also tells of the burning "during the troublous times of Vitellius" without naming a cause, and says Vespasian's rebuilt temple burned "upon the death of Vespasian" (a fifth account, `plutarch-lives` already exists).
Fix: "Suetonius makes Vitellius himself the one who set it alight, watching from the house of Tiberius." Retitle "Five ancient accounts" or add Plutarch. In the core: "Josephus and Dio put it on Vitellius's soldiers, Suetonius on Vitellius."

**12. notes.json 3:86 "He was completing his 57th year" and variants.json 3:86. The reader is left to weigh a number that Suetonius's own date refutes.**
Note: "The two Latin writers agree and Dio's epitome differs." Suetonius (Vitellius 3, opened) gives his birth as "the eighth day before the Kalends of October, or according to some, on the seventh day before the Ides of September, in the consulship of Drusus Caesar and Norbanus Flaccus"; those two were the consuls of AD 15 (two Wikipedia pages in a search; Suetonius gives no year). Born 24 September AD 15, he was 54 on 20 December AD 69, which is Dio's "fifty-four years and eighty-nine days" (Dio 64.22, opened). "Septimum et quinquagensimum annum" is the loose Latin count, and Suetonius's own birth date shows it is too high.
Fix: add one sentence to the note and the variant `b`: "Suetonius's own birth date (24 September, in the consulship of Drusus Caesar and Norbanus Flaccus, AD 15) makes him 54, as Dio says. The 57th-year count in both Latin writers does not fit it." Also say Church and Brodribb have "nearly completed", Latin `explebat`.

**13. Hedges and first-person scope statements in reader-facing text (STYLE rule 2).**
The rule bars an unchecked detail behind a hedge and bars "I" notes. Present: notes.json 3:1 ("I did not find the council at Petovio in the Josephus sections I read"), variants.json 3:5 `why` ("I did not open an apparatus"), notes.json 3:5 Sextilius ("No source I opened ties them"), 3:54 Agrestis ("no source I opened connects them"), 3:70 Martialis ("nothing in the sources I opened links them"), 3:72 spec ("I found no ancient source that links the two fires"), 3:74 spec ("I did not open a source"), 3:84 ("I did not find the name in the others' sections that I read"), 3:33 ("my arithmetic"), 3:47 ("with no citation given"); chapters.json H lens ("the page ... that I read", "the page names no one else"); topics.json `josephus`, `domitian` and others.
I checked three of these negatives: Josephus does mention Cremona (finding 2), so that hedge hid a false claim; "Julius Placidus" is not in Dio 64, Suetonius's Vitellius, or the Josephus passage on Vitellius's death (grep, my own texts); Josephus does not name Petovio in what I read, but I did not search the whole War. Where the fact is a negative about a whole work, either search the whole work and say so plainly, or leave it out.
Fix: delete each "I did not find / I read" sentence. Keep a same-person verdict only where the reason is stated ("Felix was a common name; the two men are on different frontiers"), without "no source I opened".

**14. variants.json / notes.json: 3:86 and 3:7 (missing variant and an unsupported literal).**
(a) Note 3:86 "Luceria was his native place" is typed `word`/`var` with no variants.json entry, although its point is a text problem. Fisher prints `Patrem illi Luceriam`; Church and Brodribb translate "Luceria was his native place", which requires a different word (a reading like "patria" for "patrem"). Say that Church and Brodribb's English follows a different Latin reading, and add a variant entry (`Patrem` / the reading behind "native place"). The "literally, 'To him a father, Luceria'" is not a literal rendering of the Latin as printed (both nouns are accusative) and should go.
(b) 3:7: Latin `septima Galbiana, tertia decima Gemina` = the 7th (Galba's) and the 13th (Gemina). Church and Brodribb and the plain.txt both have "eighteenth/18th". This is a real difference between the English and the Latin of the same kind as the 3:13 entry (which the writer did record), and it is a plain error against 3:1 and 3:27 (13th). Add a variants entry and fix plain.txt (see Translation flags).

**15. notes.json 3:37 "Compared with the Bible: authorities, flatterers and a bad emperor". Contested reading stated as the site's voice.**
"These words ... carry no exception for a bad ruler." Romans 13:1-7 and 1 Peter 2:13-17 are read differently by the traditions (limits on rulers, resistance to tyranny, the Barmen-type refusal); the sentence that follows (Acts 5:29) already limits it. A flat "no exception" ranks one reading above the others and contradicts the next sentence.
Fix: "These words were written to Christians who lived under emperors, and they say nothing there about exceptions. The same apostles also say, 'Obeying God has to come before obeying people' (Acts 5:29). The topic page on church and state sets out how traditions weigh the two." Same fix in the `church-and-state` contribution.

**16. chapters.json `traditions` card: Schleitheim quotation is not from the Confession, and the card has three traditions.**
The Anabaptist cell says "its summary is that 'the weapons of a Christian are spiritual'". That string is the Wikipedia article's summary ("their weapons are worldly, but the weapons of a Christian are spiritual"), not the Confession. The Confession's Article VI (opened, the University of Washington reprint of the Wenger translation) begins "The sword is ordained of God outside the perfection of Christ. It punishes and puts to death the wicked, and guards and protects the good", and says "the Christians' weapons are spiritual, against the fortification of the devil". The card also omits that the Confession itself says the sword is "ordained of God" for the magistrate.
Fix: quote the Confession (name the translation), including "ordained of God outside the perfection of Christ". The Catholic (CCC 2309, 2311, verified word for word on vatican.va) and Lutheran (Augsburg XVI, verified: "it is right for Christians to bear civil office, to sit as judges ... to engage in just wars, to serve as soldiers") cells are accurate. Add Reformed, Orthodox, Quaker and Baptist cells only from a confession or catechism opened in the session (I did not open any, so I give no wording); if none is added, retitle "Three views".

**17. Wikipedia used for dates where the primary text is in the site's own `texts/` (notes 3:7, 3:13, 3:68, 3:86; year-of-four-emperors core; primus-antonius core).**
Dates taken from Wikipedia while Tacitus is in the site: Galba's death 15 January (Tacitus 1:27 `Octavo decimo kalendas Februarias`), Vespasian's proclamation 1 July (Tacitus 2:79 `kalendis Iuliis`, at Alexandria, by Tiberius Alexander's oath), Antonius's banishment (Tacitus 2:86 `falsi damnatus`, "convicted of forgery"; Wikipedia says forging a will). The notes 3:7, 3:13, 3:68 and 3:86 and the year-of-four-emperors core cite Wikipedia for them. Wikipedia is needed only for 24 October (battle) and 20 December (death), neither of which I found in Tacitus's text of Book 3.
Fix: cite Tacitus 1:27, 2:79 and 2:86 for those three.

**18. notes.json 3:13 "Compared with the Bible: tribunes and centurions". Overstatement of Acts 22:28.**
"the same Roman habit of buying and selling rank and rights." Acts 22:28 is about buying citizenship, and the tribune says so. Nothing in it or in Tacitus 3:9 is about rank being bought. Fix: "the habit of selling rights, here citizenship".
