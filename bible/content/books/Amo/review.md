# Hostile review: Amos (Amo)

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent. Reviewed against `texts/orig/Amo.json`, `node tools/literal.js Amo 1-9`, Swete (`tools/lxx.py`), the ETCBC scroll printout (`tools/dss.py`), `texts/auto/Amo.json`, the site's Plain English Translation of the books quoted, and web search for the Damascus Document. `node tools/build.js --check Amo`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

A careful, well-made book. The Plain English Translation is a real rebuild from the Hebrew with no copying of NIV or ESV sentences, the hard places (1:1, 6:12, 7:14, 9:12) are weighed honestly, the citations I checked are mostly real and correctly placed, and the 4QFlorilegium, Damascus Document, Targum, Makkot, Sanhedrin and Avodah Zarah references hold up. It is not ready to publish yet, for four kinds of fault. (1) Three variants cite Dead Sea scroll readings that the printout shows are restored (bracketed), which the brief forbids. (2) The Jewish lens is thin: not one medieval commentator (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi) appears anywhere, and lens J is missing from chapters 6 to 8. (3) A cluster of counting and attribution slips ("six nations" followed by five names, fire "eight times", the "ninth" oracle, the cows of Bashan attributed both ways to Paul), plus the false claim that Amos 4:6-11 follows the order of the covenant curses. (4) Four notes on books already on the site use "own rendering" instead of the site's Plain English Translation, and 6:2 and 6:3 have a missing Greek variant and a Plain English Translation/note contradiction. No critical findings (nothing invented). Counts: critical 0, major 10, minor 27.

## Checks the lead asked for (results)

- **4QFlorilegium (4Q174) and Amos 9:11**: confirmed. 4Q174 1-3 i 12 quotes "I will raise up the booth of David that is fallen" and applies it to the Branch of David. Note 128 is right.
- **Damascus Document (CD 7)**: confirmed that CD 7 quotes Amos 5:26-27 and 9:11 and explains the "booth of the king" as the books of the Torah and Kiyyun as the books of the prophets. But the quotation differs from the Masoretic Text in more than the vowels: web sources give CD as "I will exile (the) Sikkuth of your king and Kiyyun of your images from my tent (to) Damascus", so the verb is first person and "beyond" has become "from my tent". The book reports only "booth". See finding 21.
- **Targum Jonathan on 9:11** ("the kingdom of the house of David that has fallen"): correct.
- **Makkot 23b-24a** (613 commandments reduced to "Seek me and live", then Habakkuk): correct as told. **Sanhedrin 96b-97a** (Bar Nafle): correct. **Avodah Zarah 4a** (Amos 3:2): correct.
- **Hazor and Gezer**: the book claims Yadin tied Hazor Stratum VI to the earthquake (correct, Yadin, *Hazor*, 1975) and that Austin, Franz and Frost (2000, *International Geology Review* 42) used Dead Sea sediments (correct: the Ein Gedi seismite). It makes no Gezer earthquake claim; the only Gezer item is the Gezer calendar at 8:1 (correct, month of summer fruit, 10th century). Both earthquake claims are hedged ("neither match is accepted by everyone"). Minor: finding 27.
- **4Q78 at 2:16 and 4:2**: both readings are in the printout (2:16 «ומוצא» for ואמיץ; 4:2 «בסופוד» for בסירות). Reported accurately in notes 28 and 41.
- **dss.py and lxx.py readings**: Swete's Greek is quoted accurately in every variant checked (1:11, 1:15, 2:7, 3:9, 3:11, 3:12, 4:3, 4:13, 5:26, 6:12, 7:1, 7:7, 7:14, 8:3, 8:14, 9:12). The scroll claims are wrong in four places: findings 1 to 3.
- **Acts 7:42-43 and 15:15-18** quotations match the site's Acts Plain English Translation word for word. Finding 22 covers the Acts/LXX differences.
- **"Changed his mind" at 7:3, 7:6**: matches the site's Exodus 32:14, Jeremiah 18:8, Joel 2:13-14 and Jonah 3:9-10 exactly. The wording is consistent. Only the sentence in note 92 about "relent" is confusing (finding 25).
- **Numbering**: English numbering throughout; no stray Hebrew numbers remain (Numbers 17:4, Hosea 13:16, Joel 3:16-18 are English). Good.
- **Joel pairs (on disk), for the alignment pass**:
  - Amos 1:2: "He said: / The LORD roars from Zion / and sends out his voice from Jerusalem. / The shepherds' meadows go dry, / and Carmel's summit withers."
  - Joel 3:16: "The LORD roars from Zion / and gives out his voice from Jerusalem. / Heaven and earth shake. / But the LORD is a refuge for his people / and a stronghold for the Israelites."
  - The Hebrew is identical in these two lines (`yitten qolo`), so Amos 1:2 should read "gives out his voice" to match Joel.
  - Amos 9:13: "Look, days are coming, declares the LORD, / when the plowman will catch up with the reaper / and the grape treader with the one who sows the seed. / The mountains will drip with sweet wine, / and all the hills will flow with it."
  - Joel 3:18: "On that day sweet wine will drip from the mountains, / milk will run down the hills, / and water will run in every streambed of Judah."
  - The Hebrew matches only in `yitpu/vehitipu heharim 'asis` ("the mountains ... drip ... sweet wine"); the verbs differ (Amos hiphil, Joel qal) and the second clause is different (Amos `titmogagnah`, Joel `telachnah chalav`). The Amos clause may stay as is, with "the mountains will drip sweet wine" for the shared words if the lead wants the match closer.

## Findings

### Major

1. **Major. variants.json, 7:1 ("the king's cutting, or Gog"), `why`: "The Hebrew is read by two scrolls."** The printout shows 4Q78 reads `אחר [גזי] המלכ` with `gizzei`, the contested word, in brackets (wholly or partly restored), and 4Q82 stops at `[לקש]` before it. The word that decides Hebrew against "Gog" is not visible in either scroll. Citing a restored word as the scroll's reading is forbidden by the brief. Fix: say that 4Q78 preserves `ha-melekh` and traces consistent with `gizzei`, that the word itself is restored, and that no scroll can be cited for it. Change the witness line likewise.

2. **Major. variants.json, 6:12, witness line and `why`: "4Q82 (4QXIIg) has the same words ... The scroll supports the Masoretic consonants."** In the printout 4Q82 has `הירצון [בסלע] סוסים אם [יחרוש] [בבקרים]`: the three words that matter (`basela'`, `yacharosh`, `babbeqarim`, and so the `ba-yam baqar` proposal) are all in brackets. Only `hayeratsun` and `susim 'im` are visible. The scroll cannot be said to support the Masoretic division. Fix: state that the key words are restored and drop the claim that the scroll supports the consonants; the argument against the emendation then rests on the lack of a manuscript, which is still true.

3. **Major. variants.json, 8:14 (`'ashmat`), witness line and `why`: "4Q82 and Mur88 agree ... support from two scrolls".** In 4Q82 the whole phrase `[הנשבעים] [באשמת] [שמרון]` is restored. Only Mur88 shows `באשמת שמרון`. Fix: "Mur88 (4Q82 restored)". The same entry for `derekh` (8:14b) is fine: `דרך` is visible in both. Also variants 12 (7:7): Mur88 shows only `והנה ובידו אנך`, so it cannot be cited for `חומת אנך` ("a wall of anak"); the entry says all three scrolls agree. Fix: cite 4Q78 (visible) and 4Q82 (`חומת` restored, `אנך` visible); say Mur88 preserves only the second `anak`.

4. **Major. Whole book: the Jewish lens (J) has no Jewish commentary.** The brief and lens J promise "Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum". A search of the folder finds Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Kimhi only in the lens description in book.json. No note, view, chapter J line or card gives any of their readings. The J lines that exist are: the Talmud (ch3, ch5, ch9), the Targum (ch1, ch9), the Damascus Document (ch5, ch9), `teshuvah` (ch4), and in ch2 a list of Torah cross references that is not a Jewish reading at all. Chapters 6, 7 and 8 have no J key. Fix: add real J entries for 6, 7, 8 and rewrite ch2's. Candidates the writer can verify before citing: the medieval readings of 7:14 ("I am no prophet" in Rashi and Kimhi), of 3:2, of 5:24-26, of Edom as Rome in 1:11-12, and of 9:11-12 (Kimhi's messianic reading); the Targum's renderings at 1:2 and 7:14; and the haftarot (Amos 2:6-3:8 and 9:7-15 are used in the cycle; verify from a printed source or omit). Do not cite any of this unless it is checked.

5. **Major. variants.json (missing entry) and notes.json #80, 6:2: Calneh.** Swete's Greek at 6:2 is `διάβητε πάντες καὶ ἴδετε`, "cross over, all of you", with no Calneh; it also has Gath "the strongest of all these kingdoms". The Hebrew `כלנה` is Calneh or `kullanah`, "all of them". The brief lists 6:2 among the Greek differences. Note 80 builds a dating argument (Calneh taken in 738, so 6:2 is later than Amos) on the place name, while the oldest translation does not read it. Fix: add a variant entry at 6:2 and qualify note 80 and ch6 H ("Calneh, if that is the right reading").

6. **Major. plain.txt 6:3 against notes.json #81 and the Hebrew.** Hebrew `shevet chamas` is "seat of violence" (literal line: "a seat of | violence"). The Plain English Translation says "you bring the rule of violence near" yet note 81 says "The Plain English Translation keeps the picture of a throne". The Plain English Translation has no throne or seat. Either the note is wrong or the Plain English Translation drifted to the English of a modern version. Fix: Plain English Translation "you bring the seat of violence near" (or "violence's throne"), or rewrite the note to say the Plain English Translation takes `shevet` as "rule".

7. **Major. notes.json #9, #37, #103, #130: "own rendering" quotations of books already on the site.** The rule is that a note quoting another book's verse uses that verse's current Plain English Translation. These are all on the site: 2 Kings 13:7 (site: "made them like dust to be trampled"; the note's "like dust at threshing" is closer to the Hebrew, so the lead may want to fix 2 Kings instead), Exodus 22:13 (site: "let him bring what is left as a witness"; note: "bring it as evidence"), 2 Samuel 7:8 (site: "took you off the grazing land, from walking behind the flock"), Leviticus 26:5 (site: "Your threshing will last until the grape harvest, and the grape harvest until sowing time"; the note's version has the sense but different words). The build did not warn because of the label. Fix: quote the site's Plain English Translation exactly, or paraphrase without quotation marks. Zechariah 3:2 and 14:5 are off the site and fine.

8. **Major. Chapter 1 summary and notes: counting errors.** (a) chapters.json ch1 `sum`: "Six nations are named in turn, Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom and Ammon in this chapter": that is five. (b) notes.json #10 `p`: "Eight times the same punishment comes": fire falls seven times (1:4, 7, 10, 12, 14; 2:2, 5). Israel's oracle (2:13-16) has no fire. The ch1 deep note says each of the eight has "the fire", also wrong. (c) notes.json #17 `b`: "as the ninth oracle against Israel will show (2:6)": Israel is the eighth. (d) chapters.json ch2 C: "the original series ended with Israel as the eighth oracle" (if Judah is dropped, Israel is the seventh). Fix each. Note also book.json `lead` says "seven nations and then Israel" while the Amos topic and ch1 deep say "eight nations"; pick one convention.

9. **Major. Cows of Bashan (4:1): contradictory attribution and a settled audience.** Note 40 says "Some readers think the verb forms are used for all the elite, men included (Paul)." The ch4 settings card puts Paul on the other side: "The cows are women ... the wives of the rich (Wolff, Paul)", and "the elite" is given to "some critics, with Andersen and Freedman as cautious". One of them is wrong about Paul. Verify in Paul's Hermeneia and fix both places. In addition, book.json `audience.why` states "the cows of Bashan are real women of Samaria" and the ch4 `to` line says "Ruling-class women of Samaria first" as fact, though the card itself shows the view is debated. Fix: soften both to "most likely".

10. **Major. "The five disasters follow the order of the covenant curses": notes.json #45, chapters.json ch4 H and ch4 deep, topics.json `covenant`.** The claim is stated four times. Neither Leviticus 26 nor Deuteronomy 28 has the sequence famine, drought, blight and locust, plague and war, overthrow (Leviticus 26 runs terror and disease, defeat, drought, beasts, sword and pestilence, famine, exile; Deuteronomy 28 runs city and field, pestilence, drought, defeat, and so on). Ch4 H says the curses "list them in that order". The fair statement is that the disasters are the kinds of covenant curse. Note 45 also lists six disasters for "five". Fix: drop "order" and "in that order"; count war and plague as one (4:10).

### Minor

11. **Minor. notes.json #43, 4:4.** "The Greek has 'on the third day'." Swete's Greek is `εἰς τὴν τριημερίαν`, "for the three-day period". Fix: say so (it supports "every three days" better than "on the third day").

12. **Minor. notes.json #109, 8:4.** The data (`texts/auto/Amo.json`) shows the Ketiv (written) is `'anwei` ("humble") and the Qere (read) is `'aniyyei` ("afflicted"). The note lists `'aniyyei` first as if it were the Ketiv. Fix: reverse the order and say the Plain English Translation follows the Qere.

13. **Minor. notes.json #113, 8:8 (and plain.txt 8:8).** The Masoretic text is `ועלתה כאר כלה`, "will rise like *the light* (`ka'or`), all of it" (the literal line says "like the light"); the Plain English Translation's "swell like the Nile" silently reads `ka-ye'or`, as 9:5 has. The note speaks only of the Ketiv/Qere on `nishqah/nishq'ah` and never says the first word is written `ka'or`. The Greek (`ὡς ποταμὸς συντέλεια`) also differs. Fix: add the `ka'or` / `ye'or` point to the note and a variant if the lead agrees.

14. **Minor. notes.json #61, 5:8, and variants.json (missing entry).** The note says "the Greek tradition ... take them as the Pleiades and Orion". Swete's Greek of Amos 5:8 names neither constellation (`ὁ ποιῶν πάντα καὶ μετασκευάζων`); the translation exists for Job 9:9 and 38:31 but not here. Fix: say so, with a short variant entry (the sense changes: no stars named).

15. **Minor. notes.json #7.** Sirach 25:7 is a nine-and-ten saying and Job 5:19 six-and-seven, listed as examples of "three ... and four". Fix: call the pattern "a number and the next number" and add Sirach 26:5 (three and four); keep Micah 5:5 (seven and eight).

16. **Minor. notes.json #99, 7:12.** "Gad and Hanan" as seers. The seer of 2 Chronicles 16:7 is Hanani. Fix.

17. **Minor. notes.json #122, 9:6.** Says Psalm 104:3 uses "the same noun, `aliyyah`" for `ma'alot`. `'aliyyah` and `ma'alah` are different nouns from the same root. Fix: "a related noun".

18. **Minor. notes.json #41, 4:2.** The note says 4Q78 "reads a different word for 'fishhooks'". The printout shows `בסופוד דוגה` for `בסירות דוגה`. Accurate, but name the reading ("a different, unclear word, `sopod`") or it cannot be checked.

19. **Minor. plain.txt 1:11, "smothered his compassion".** Hebrew `shichet` is "destroyed / ruined / corrupted" (the Plain English Translation's own variant entry gives "he destroyed his compassion" as the English of the Hebrew, and the Greek has `ἐλυμήνατο`, "ruined"). "Smothered" is an unexplained new rendering and reads close to the well-known English "stifled". Fix: "destroyed" or "cast off ruinously", with the note giving why.

20. **Minor. plain.txt 5:26: "the star of your god".** The Hebrew is `kokhav 'eloheikhem`, "star of your gods" (plural form, also the literal line "gods your"). The Plain English Translation makes it singular and no note says why. The same Hebrew word is "gods" in the parallel with `Sikkuth` and `Kiyyun`. Fix: note or change.

21. **Minor. variants.json, 5:26 and notes.json #76: the Damascus Document.** Add that CD 7 does not simply "read booth" but quotes the verse as "I will exile ... from my tent (to) Damascus" (so it differs in verb person and in "beyond"). This is the strongest Hebrew witness to a text different from the Masoretic one, and it matters for the debate over "beyond Damascus / beyond Babylon". Cite Vermes or a named edition; do not give a line number unless checked.

22. **Minor. variants.json 9:12 and notes.json #129; chapters.json ch9.** Several places say Acts 15 "quotes the Greek". The Acts text departs from the LXX: it opens "After these things I will come back" where the Greek has "On that day", reads `ἀνοικοδομήσω ... ἀνορθώσω` for `ἀναστήσω`, adds "the Lord" after "seek", and closes "known from ages past" where the Greek has "who does these things". The Acts Plain English Translation on the site shows this. Fix: one sentence in the 9:12 entry saying Acts follows the LXX in the 9:12 verb and subject but not elsewhere; and the dispensational card's argument from "after these things" should be marked as Acts's wording, not Amos's.

23. **Minor. notes.json #50, #26, #110, #40.** Overstated claims. #26: Samuel is "a Nazirite from birth" (MT 1 Samuel 1:11 does not say so; Mishnah *Nazir* 9:5 and 4QSam-a do). #110: "earliest evidence outside the Law for observance of the Sabbath as a rest from trade" (2 Kings 4:23, Isaiah 1:13 and Hosea 2:11 are at least as early). #40: the cows image "was recorded in Amos's lifetime and not removed" (unprovable). #50: the survivors "are wary" (not in the text). Fix: soften or drop.

24. **Minor. notes.json #101 and variants #13, 7:14.** The Plain English Translation follows "I am", and the reason given is that "the Hebrew grammar has no past marker". A verbless clause has no present marker either; this is not a reason. The other reason ("hear the word of the LORD" shows Amos accepts the role) is the main argument of the past reading as well, so it does not decide. No medieval Jewish reading is given. The Plain English Translation also drops the connective `ki` ("for I was/am a herdsman"), which is itself part of the debate (explanatory "for" favors the past; "but rather" favors the present). Fix: give an honest reason (the Plain English Translation follows the Hebrew's lack of a verb by choosing the reading that most scholars choose today) and mention `ki`.

25. **Minor. notes.json #92, 7:3.** "Other English Bibles say 'relent' (see Joel 2:13 and Jonah 3:10)." The site's Joel 2:13 and Jonah 3:10 say "changes his mind", the same as Amos, so this reads as if the site's Joel and Jonah say "relent". Fix: "many English Bibles say 'relent'; the same Hebrew in Joel 2:13 and Jonah 3:10 is 'change his mind' on this site".

26. **Minor. notes.json #5, 1:2.** The last two sentences ("Amos 1:2 and Joel 3:16 should be read together because both set the LORD in Jerusalem while Amos speaks at Bethel.
