## Greeting
1:0 The church of God that lives as a resident alien in Rome, to the church of God that lives as a resident alien in Corinth, to those who are called and made holy by the will of God through our Lord Jesus Christ. May grace and peace from God Almighty be multiplied to you through Jesus Christ.
## A church once famous
1:1 Beloved, misfortunes and calamities have struck us suddenly, one after another, so we think we have been rather slow to turn our minds to the questions being argued among you. We mean above all the rebellion, foul and unholy, alien and foreign to God's chosen. A handful of rash and headstrong people have fanned it into such madness that your name, once honored and famous and worthy of everyone's love, has been badly slandered.
1:2 For who ever stayed with you and did not find your faith full of every virtue and firm? Who did not admire your godliness in Christ, self-controlled and forbearing? Who did not spread the word about your magnificent way of welcoming guests? And who did not call your knowledge blessed, so complete and so sure?
1:3 For you did everything without favoring anyone, and you walked in God's laws. You submitted to your leaders and gave fitting honor to the older people among you. You told the young to keep their thoughts modest and worthy of respect. You instructed the women to do everything with a blameless, respectful and pure conscience, and to love their husbands as they should. You taught them to keep to the rule of submission, to look after the work of the home with dignity, and to be thoroughly sensible.
2:1 All of you were humble, boasting of nothing, more ready to submit than to make others submit, happier to give than to receive. You were content with the supplies Christ gives for the journey. You paid close attention to his words and took them deep into your hearts, and you kept his sufferings in front of your eyes.
2:2 So all of you had been given peace, deep and rich, and a hunger to do good that was never filled. The Holy Spirit was poured out in full on everyone.
2:3 You were full of holy purpose. With good eagerness and godly confidence you reached out your hands to God Almighty and begged him to show mercy if you had sinned in anything without intending it.
2:4 You strove day and night for the whole brotherhood, so that the number of his chosen would be saved, with mercy and with conscience.
2:5 You were sincere and innocent, and you held no grudges against one another.
2:6 Every rebellion and every split was hateful to you. You mourned over your neighbors' wrongs, and you counted their failings as your own.
2:7 You had no regrets over any good you did, and you were ready for any good work.
2:8 Your way of life as citizens was full of virtue and worthy of reverence, and it was your adornment. In all things you acted in the fear of him. The Lord's commands and rulings were written on your heart's tablets.
## Jealousy and envy
3:1 Every honor and room to grow was given to you, and what is written came true: "The beloved one ate and drank, and he grew broad and grew fat, and he kicked."
3:2 This was the source of jealousy and envy, fighting and rebellion, persecution and disorder, war and captivity.
3:3 So the dishonored were stirred up against the honored, the nobodies against the well known, the senseless against the sensible, the young against the elders.
3:4 This is why righteousness and peace are far away. Each person has deserted the fear of God, and the eyes of faith in him have grown weak. People do not walk in the laws of his commands or live as citizens in a way fitting for Christ. Instead each one walks by the desires of his own evil heart. They have taken up an unrighteous and ungodly jealousy, the very thing through which "death came into the world."
## From Cain to Saul
4:1 For it is written like this: "After some days Cain brought some of the fruit of the earth as a sacrifice to God. And Abel for his part brought some of the firstborn of the sheep and some of their fat.
4:2 God looked with favor on Abel and on his gifts, but he paid no attention to Cain and his sacrifices.
4:3 Cain was deeply hurt, and his face fell.
4:4 God said to Cain, 'Why are you so hurt, and why has your face fallen? If you made your offering rightly but did not divide it rightly, have you not sinned?
4:5 Be still. He will turn to you, and you will rule over him.'
4:6 Cain said to Abel his brother, 'Let us go out into the open country.' And while they were in the open country, Cain rose up against Abel his brother and killed him."
4:7 You see, brothers: jealousy and envy led to a brother's murder.
4:8 Because of jealousy Jacob our father ran away out of the sight of Esau his brother.
4:9 Jealousy made Joseph a hunted man, close to death, and brought him into slavery.
4:10 Jealousy made Moses flee out of the sight of Pharaoh, king of Egypt, when he heard one of his own people say, "Who put you in charge as judge or arbiter over us? You don't mean to kill me, do you, the same way you killed the Egyptian yesterday?"
4:11 Because of jealousy Aaron and Miriam had to spend their nights outside the camp.
4:12 Jealousy sent Dathan and Abiram down living into Hades, for they had risen in rebellion against Moses, God's attendant.
4:13 Because of jealousy David drew the envy not only of the foreigners. He was also hunted by Saul, king of Israel.
## The athletes of our own time
5:1 But let us leave the examples from long ago and come to the athletes closest to our own time. Let us take up the noble examples our own generation gives.
5:2 Because of jealousy and envy the greatest and most righteous pillars were hunted down, and they kept up the contest until death.
5:3 Let us put the good apostles before our eyes.
5:4 There was Peter. He suffered under unrighteous jealousy, and his hardships were not one or two but many. Having borne his witness so, he went on to the place of glory owed to him.
5:5 Because of jealousy and fighting Paul showed the way to the prize that endurance wins.
5:6 Seven times he wore chains. He was banished, he was stoned. He became a herald in the East and in the West, and he won the noble fame of his faith.
5:7 He taught righteousness to the whole world and came to the far limit of the West. And having borne his witness before the rulers, he was set free from the world in this way and taken up into the holy place, the greatest model of endurance for others to trace.
6:1 To these men, who lived their lives in holiness, a great multitude of the chosen was gathered. They suffered because of jealousy, and through many outrages and tortures they became the finest example among us.
6:2 Because of jealousy women were hunted down as Danaids and Dirces. Terrible, unholy outrages were done to them, yet they reached the end of faith's sure race and received a noble prize, though their bodies were weak.
6:3 Jealousy has turned wives away from their husbands and has changed the saying of our father Adam: "This now is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh."
6:4 Jealousy and fighting have torn down great cities and pulled up great nations by the roots.
## A call to repent
7:1 Beloved, these things we write to warn you, and to remind ourselves as well. We stand in the same arena, and the same contest lies ahead of us.
7:2 So let us leave behind empty and useless worries, and let us come to the rule handed down to us, glorious and worthy of reverence.
7:3 Let us see what is good, what delights, what is welcome in the eyes of the one who made us.
7:4 Let us look steadily at the blood of Christ and know what great value it has for his Father. Because it was poured out for our salvation, it brought the gift of repentance to the whole world.
7:5 Let us travel back through all the generations and learn this: in every generation the Master gave a place for repentance to anyone willing to turn to him.
7:6 Noah preached repentance, and those who listened were saved.
7:7 Jonah preached overthrow to the people of Nineveh. But they repented of their sins and won God's mercy by their pleading, and they received salvation, though they were strangers to God.
8:1 Those who served God's grace spoke about repentance through the Holy Spirit.
8:2 The Master of all things himself also spoke about repentance, and he swore an oath: "As I live, says the Lord, I do not want the sinner's death so much as his repentance." And he added a kind word as well:
8:3 "Repent, house of Israel, of your lawlessness. Say to the sons of my people: Even if your sins reach from the earth to the sky, even if they are redder than scarlet and blacker than sackcloth, and you turn back to me with all your heart and say, 'Father,' I will hear you as I hear a holy people."
8:4 And in another place he says this: "Wash yourselves, become clean. Take the evil out of your souls, from before my eyes. Quit your evil ways. Learn how to do good. Go after justice. Rescue the one who is wronged. Give the orphan his rights; take up the widow's case. Come, then, let us argue it out, says the Lord. Even if your sins are like crimson, I will make them as white as snow. If they are like scarlet, I will make them as white as wool. If you are willing and you listen to me, you will eat the best the land has. But if you are not willing and do not listen to me, the sword will eat you. For the Lord's mouth has spoken these things."
8:5 So, because he wants all whom he loves to have a share in repentance, he made it firm by his almighty will.
## Obedience and faith
9:1 So let us obey his magnificent and glorious will. Let us bow down as people begging for his mercy and kindness, and turn back to his compassion. Let us leave behind useless effort, fighting, and the jealousy that leads to death.
9:2 Let us look steadily at those who gave perfect service to his magnificent glory.
9:3 Take Enoch. He was found righteous through obedience and was taken away, and his death was never found.
9:4 Noah was found faithful. Through his service he preached a new birth to the world, and through him the Master brought safely through the living things that went into the ark together in harmony.
10:1 Abraham, who was called "the friend," was found faithful because he obeyed God's words.
10:2 Because he obeyed, he went out from his land, from his kin and from his father's house. He left behind a small land, a weak family and a little house, so that he might inherit God's promises. For God says to him:
10:3 "Go away from your land, away from your kin and from your father's house, to the land that I will show you. I will make you a great nation. I will bless you and make your name great, and you will be blessed. I will bless those who bless you and curse those who curse you, and all the tribes of the earth will be blessed in you."
10:4 And again, when he had parted from Lot, God said to him, "Raise your eyes and look out from the place where you are now, north and south, east and west. For all the land that you see I will give to you and to your offspring for all time.
10:5 I will make your offspring like the sand of the earth. If anyone can count the sand of the earth, your offspring too will be counted."
10:6 And again he says: "God led Abraham outside and said to him, 'Look up to the sky and number the stars, if you can number them. So will your offspring be.' Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness."
10:7 Because of his faith and hospitality he was given a son in his old age. And because he obeyed, he offered him as a sacrifice to God on the mountain that God showed him.
## Lot and his wife
11:1 The whole region around was judged by fire and sulfur, but Lot, because of his hospitality and godliness, was saved out of Sodom. So the Master made it plain that he does not abandon those who hope in him, but those who turn aside he hands over to punishment and torment.
11:2 His wife went out with him, but she thought differently and was not in harmony with him. So she was made a sign: she became a pillar of salt to this day. This makes it known to all that the double-minded, and those who doubt God's power, become a judgment and a warning sign for every generation.
## Rahab and the scarlet sign
12:1 Because of her faith and hospitality Rahab the prostitute was saved.
12:2 Joshua son of Nun had sent spies to Jericho, and the king of the land got word that they had come to look over his people's country. He sent out men to seize them, so that once caught they could be put to death.
12:3 Rahab, a woman who welcomed guests, received them and hid them in the upper room beneath the flax stalks.
12:4 The king's men came and stood over her and said, "The spies of our land came in to you. Bring them out, for the king commands it." She answered, "Yes, the men you are hunting for came in to me, but they went off right away and are traveling on down the road." And she pointed them the wrong way.
12:5 And she said to the men, "I am sure of it: the Lord God is giving this land into your hands. Fear and trembling at you have fallen on its people. So when you come to take it, rescue me and my father's household."
12:6 They said to her, "It will be as you have said to us. So when you know that we are near, bring all your people together under your roof, and they will be saved. Everyone found outside the house will be destroyed."
12:7 They went on to give her a sign: she was to hang out something scarlet from her house. By this they made it plain that through the blood of the Lord there will be redemption for all who believe and hope in God.
12:8 You see, beloved, that the woman had not only faith but prophecy.
## Be humble
13:1 So, brothers, let us be humble and put away all bragging, conceit, foolishness and anger. Let us do what is written. For the Holy Spirit says, "A wise person should not boast about his wisdom, nor a strong person about his strength, nor a rich person about his riches. Rather, let the one who boasts boast in the Lord, by seeking him out and doing justice and righteousness." Above all, let us keep in mind the words of the Lord Jesus that he spoke when he was teaching forbearance and patience.
13:2 This is what he said: "Show mercy, so that mercy will be shown to you. Forgive, so that you will be forgiven. What you do will be done to you. What you give will be given to you. The way you judge is the way you will be judged. The kindness you show will be shown to you. The measure you use is the one that will be used to measure out to you."
13:3 By this command and these instructions let us make ourselves strong to walk in obedience to his holy words, and to be humble. For the holy word says,
13:4 "On whom will I look, if not on the one who is gentle and quiet and trembles at my sayings?"
14:1 So it is right and holy, brothers, for us to obey God rather than follow those who, in their bragging and disorder, are the leaders of a foul jealousy.
14:2 The harm we would suffer is not small. It is a great danger, if we carelessly hand ourselves over to the plans of people who hurl themselves into fighting and rebellions, to drag us away from what is good.
14:3 Let us show kindness to each other, matching the tender mercy and sweetness of the one who made us.
14:4 For it is written, "The land will be home for the kind, and the innocent will be left in it, but from it the lawbreakers will be destroyed."
14:5 And again he says, "I saw an ungodly man towering high, lifted up like the cedars of Lebanon. I went past, and there, he was gone. I looked for the place where he had been, and I did not find it. Keep yourself innocent and keep your eyes on what is upright, for the man of peace has a remnant left to him."
## Peace, not pretense
15:1 So then, let us hold fast to those who keep the peace out of godliness, and not to those who want peace only as a pretense.
15:2 For somewhere it says, "This people gives me honor with their lips, but their heart is far away from me."
15:3 And again: "With their mouth they bless, but in their heart they were cursing."
15:4 And again it says, "They loved him with their mouth, and with their tongue they lied to him. Their heart was not straight with him, and they were not faithful to his covenant."
15:5 So then, "let lying lips be struck dumb, lips that speak lawlessness against the righteous." And again: "May the Lord destroy all lying lips, the tongue that talks big, those who say, 'We will make our tongue great. Our lips are our own. Who is lord over us?'
15:6 Because of the misery of the poor and the groaning of the needy, now I will rise up, says the Lord. I will set him in safety;
15:7 I will speak out boldly for him."
## Christ the humble one
16:1 For Christ belongs to the humble, not to those who lift themselves up over his flock.
16:2 The scepter of God's majesty, the Lord Jesus Christ, did not arrive in a show of bragging or arrogance, though he had the power to. He came humble, just as the Holy Spirit had spoken about him. For it says:
16:3 "Lord, who has believed what we have heard? And the Lord's arm: to whom has it been revealed? We made our report before him, as of a little child, as of a root in thirsty ground. He has no form, no glory. We saw him, and he had no form or beauty. His form had no honor; it fell short of the form of other people. He was a man under blows and pain, who knew how to bear weakness. Because his face was turned away, he was dishonored and counted as nothing.
16:4 This is the one who carries our sins and suffers pain for us. But we thought of him as in pain, under blows and afflicted.
16:5 But he was wounded because of our sins and made weak because of our lawless deeds. The discipline that bought our peace lay on him; by his welts we were healed.
16:6 We had all strayed like sheep; each one strayed on his own way.
16:7 And the Lord handed him over for our sins, and because he has been mistreated he does not open his mouth. Like a sheep he was taken off to slaughter, and like a lamb that makes no sound in front of the one who shears it, so he does not open his mouth. When he was brought low, justice was snatched from him.
16:8 Who will tell of his generation? For his life is removed from the earth.
16:9 Because of the lawless deeds of my people he has come to death.
16:10 And I will give the wicked in return for his burial and the rich in return for his death, for he did nothing lawless, nor was trickery found in his mouth. And the Lord wants to cleanse him of his wound.
16:11 If you make an offering for sin, your soul will see offspring with long life.
16:12 And the Lord wants to take some of the toil of his soul away, to show him light and to shape him with understanding, to justify a righteous one who serves many well. And he himself will carry their sins.
16:13 For this reason he will inherit many, and he will divide the spoils of the strong, because his soul was handed over to death and he was counted among the lawless.
16:14 He bore the sins of many, and because of their sins he was handed over."
16:15 And again he himself says, "But I am a worm and not a man, a thing for people to insult, dismissed with contempt by the crowd.
16:16 All who see me make fun of me; they sneer with their lips and shake their heads: 'He hoped in the Lord. Let the Lord rescue him; let him save him, since he takes delight in him.'"
16:17 You see, beloved men, the model to trace that has been given to us. If the Lord was so humble, what should we do, who have come under the yoke of his grace through him?
## The humble saints of old
17:1 Let us be imitators too of those who went around in goat hides and sheepskins and proclaimed that Christ was coming. We mean the prophets Elijah and Elisha, and Ezekiel too, and besides them those who were given a good witness.
17:2 Abraham was given a great witness and was called God's friend. Yet looking steadily at God's glory, he says humbly, "But I am earth and ashes."
17:3 And about Job, too, it is written like this: "Job was righteous and blameless, true, a man who revered God and kept away from every evil."
17:4 Yet he accuses himself and says, "No one is clean of filth, not even if he lives for only one day."
17:5 Moses was called "faithful in all his house," and through his service God judged Egypt with the scourges and torments that came on them. Yet even he, though he was given great glory, did not talk big. When God's word came to him from the bush, he said, "Who am I, that you send me? But I am weak of voice and slow of tongue."
17:6 And again he says, "But I am steam from a pot."
## David's prayer
18:1 And what shall we say of David, who was given a good witness? God said of him, "I have found a man to suit my heart, David son of Jesse. With eternal mercy I have anointed him."
18:2 Yet he too says to God, "Have mercy on me, O God, as your mercy is great. As your compassions are many, wipe out my lawless deed.
18:3 Wash me again and again of my lawlessness, and make me pure from my sin. For I myself know my lawlessness, and my sin stands before me all the time.
18:4 Against you alone I sinned, and I did what is evil in your sight, so that you may be proved right in your words, and win your case when you are judged.
18:5 Look, I was conceived in lawless deeds, and in sins my mother conceived me.
18:6 Look, you have loved truth. The unseen and hidden things of your wisdom you have made clear to me.
18:7 You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I will be pure. You will wash me, and I will be whiter than snow.
18:8 You will let me hear gladness and joy. The bones that were brought low will leap for joy.
18:9 Turn your face away from my sins; wipe out all my lawless deeds.
18:10 Create a pure heart in me, O God, and make a straight spirit new deep inside me.
18:11 Do not fling me away from before you; and your holy spirit, do not take it from me.
18:12 Give me back the gladness of your salvation, and make me firm with a governing spirit.
18:13 I will teach your ways to the lawless, and the ungodly will turn back to you.
18:14 Save me from the guilt of blood, O God, God of my salvation.
18:15 My tongue will shout for joy about your righteousness. Lord, you will open my mouth, and my lips will make your praise known.
18:16 For if you had wanted a sacrifice, I would have given one. You will take no pleasure in whole burnt offerings.
18:17 A broken spirit is the sacrifice for God. A heart broken and humbled God will not look down on."
## The goal of peace
19:1 So many people, and such great ones, were given a good witness in this way. Through obedience their humility and their submissive spirit have made us better, and not only us but also the generations before us, all who received his sayings in fear and truth.
19:2 Since we have had a share in many great and glorious deeds, let us run back to the goal of peace that was handed down to us from the beginning. Let us look steadily at the Father and Maker of the whole world, and let us hold fast to his magnificent and surpassing gifts of peace and to his kindnesses.
19:3 Let us picture him in our minds and gaze with the eyes of the soul at his patient will. Let us grasp how free of anger he is toward all that he has made.
## The world in harmony
20:1 The heavens, moving as he directs them, submit to him in peace.
20:2 Day and night run the course he set for them, and neither gets in the other's way.
20:3 Sun and moon and the dancing choirs of stars roll through the circuits he assigned them, in harmony, as he ordered, never once straying.
20:4 The earth grows heavy with crops at its own seasons, as he wills, and makes food spring up in plenty for people and beasts and every living thing on it. It does not quarrel or change any of the things he has decreed.
20:5 The regions of the deeps, past all tracing, and the regions of the world below, past all telling, are held in place by the same commands.
20:6 The great hollow of the boundless sea, gathered into its pools when he made it, does not overstep the bars set around it. It does just as he ordered it.
20:7 For he said, "Up to here you will come, and your waves will break within you."
20:8 The ocean, which people can find no end to, and the worlds beyond it are steered by the same orders of the Master.
20:9 The seasons of spring, summer, autumn and winter hand over to one another in peace.
20:10 The winds from their stations do their service at the proper time without a stumble. Springs that never stop flowing, made for enjoyment and health, hold out their breasts to give people life, and never fail. Even the smallest creatures come together in harmony and peace.
20:11 The great Creator and Master of all things ordered all these to be in peace and harmony. He does good to all things, but far beyond measure to us who have fled for refuge to his compassion through our Lord Jesus Christ.
20:12 To him be the glory and the majesty forever and ever. Amen.
