# Hostile review: 1 Clement, part 40-65

Reviewer's scope: plain.txt, notes.json, variants.json, chapters.json (26 pages, the chapter 44 card), glosses.json, topics.json, sources.json, `content/proposals/works/topics/apostolic-succession.json`, and this part's section in `peace.json`. `node tools/build.js --check 1Cl/40-65` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings.

Sources opened this session: `texts/orig/1Cl.json` (Lake's Greek), `texts/src/1Cl.app.json` (Lake's footnotes, Beta Code read by hand), Lake's printed pages and introduction (`.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers01lake.txt`, `TheApostolicFathersV1.txt`), Lightfoot 1890 (`.cache/dl/lightfoot-clem/p1v2.txt`), Swete's Septuagint via `tools/lxx.py` (Pss 17, 31, 68, 117, 140; Prov 1; Deut 9; Num 16; Isa 26, 60; Job 5), the site's own plain.txt files (Isaiah 60:17, Sirach 6:34, 1 Peter 4:8, 1 Corinthians 13), the Catechism of the Catholic Church 861 (vatican.va; paraphrase and footnote 374 read), the 1908 Catholic Encyclopedia article "Pope St. Clement I" (newadvent), the PCA Book of Church Order part 1 (pcaac.org), the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 (bfm.sbc.net), and a search result that quotes Canon 1740 from the Vatican text. Not re-opened by me: Lumen Gentium 20, Canon 1741's list, Eusebius, Irenaeus, Sefaria.

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly faithful part. The Greek is followed closely, the forbearance decision is applied consistently (56:1, 58:2, 62:2), the quotations of the Greek Old Testament are mostly right, and most of Lake's footnotes are entered as variants with the right witnesses. I found no invented source or fragment. The real problems are narrower: one translation slip in the key verse 44:4; several note claims that are false when checked against the Greek or the Septuagint (46:3, 47:3); variants that leave out what Lightfoot says in the same pages the writer opened (41:1, 44:2, 51:1, 59:3), which matters most where the choice touches succession and the Eucharist; the chapter 44 card, which has only three rows and one of them rests on an inference; and a handful of unsourced assertions the rules forbid. The doxology "to God" for Greek "to him" is a defensible reading but is made silently.

Counts: 0 critical, 7 major, 28 minor.

## Major findings

1. **Major. plain.txt 44:4 (and its note, notes.json 44:4).** The Greek is `ἐὰν τοὺς ἀμέμπτως καὶ ὁσίως προσενεγκόντας τὰ δῶρα τῆς ἐπισκοπῆς ἀποβάλωμεν`: "if we throw out those who blamelessly and holily presented the gifts **of the office of overseer**." The genitive depends on "the gifts." The Plain English Translation has "throw out of the office of overseer those who have presented the gifts," which moves the genitive onto the verb and adds "out of." That turns "the gifts of the episcopate" (the offerings tied to the office, Lightfoot's "gifts") into a statement about being removed from office, and it hides the phrase the whole Temple argument (40:2, 41:2) rests on. Lake's English keeps it ("offered its sacrifices"). Fix: "those who have presented the gifts of the office of overseer blamelessly and in a holy way," and let the 44:4 note say the genitive is attached to "gifts."

2. **Major. notes.json 46:3 and chapters.json 46 (`views.J`).** The note says Clement "quotes exactly" Psalm 18:25-26 (Greek 17:26-27). Swete's Psalm 17:26 begins `μετὰ ὁσίου ὁσιωθήσῃ` ("with the holy you will be made holy"), and Clement 46:3 omits that line, running straight to `μετὰ ἀνδρὸς ἀθῴου ἀθῷος ἔσῃ`. The omission is notable because 46:2's "cling to the holy... will be made holy" sounds like the dropped line. Verified in Swete (lxx.py Psa 17:25-28). Fix: say Clement abridges the Greek Psalm by dropping its first line, and consider noting the possible connection to 46:2.

3. **Major. notes.json 47:3.** "He does use the same adverb of his own letter, written 'through the Holy Spirit' (63:2)." 63:2 has `διὰ τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος`, a prepositional phrase. The adverb `πνευματικῶς` occurs only at 47:3. The same sentence's "one of the earliest witnesses to a Pauline letter read with authority" has no source. Fix: say Clement claims the Spirit for his own letter in different words (63:2), and drop or source "one of the earliest."

4. **Major. variants.json 41:1 (and plain.txt 41:1).** The variant says Lake's text is "with the other witnesses (Lake's footnote names only A as differing)." Lightfoot's apparatus (p.125, opened) gives A `εὐχαριστείτω`, C and S `εὐαρεστείτω`, and his note says: "Though the reading εὐαρεστείτω is simpler, εὐχαριστείτω is doubtless correct," pointing to the same confusion at 62. The Plain English Translation follows the other reading, and the variant's `why` argues for "please" without telling the reader that Lightfoot argued the opposite, in a verse where the answer decides whether "the church's thanksgiving" (the Lord's Supper) is meant. Fairness requires Lightfoot's judgment in `why` or `b`. Also, Lake's printed text itself reads `εὐαριστείτω` (one rho; both OCR files, scan lines about 4289): that is a misprint for `εὐαρεστείτω`, so the variant's first reading's `orig` should say so.

5. **Major. chapters.json 44, the `traditions` card.** (a) Three rows only: Catholic, Reformed (the PCA alone), Baptist. Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist and Pentecostal or independent churches are absent although they have distinct answers, and nothing says they were left out for lack of a source. (b) The Baptist row's second sentence, "The confession names no body above the congregation, so decisions about its pastor rest with the congregation itself," is the writer's inference. BFM 2000 article VI (read) says the church is an autonomous congregation led through democratic processes and has two offices; it says nothing about removing a pastor. (c) The Catholic row answers a narrower question (removal of a parish priest, canons 1740-1741; I confirmed 1740's "diocesan bishop can remove him from the parish" and the card's "can remove him" is in it) than the card asks ("ordained ministers"); deacons, bishops and other priests are not covered. (d) The PCA row is verified (BCO 23-1: "If any church desires to be relieved of its pastor, a similar procedure shall be observed"; the presbytery dissolves the relation) but labelled "Reformed" for a whole family on one denomination. Fix: add sourced Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican and Methodist rows, or retitle the rows by the body quoted and state the limits; rewrite or drop the Baptist inference; say which question each row answers.

6. **Major. plain.txt 59:3, variants.json 59:3 (lacuna), notes.json 59:3.** The Greek has `ἀνοίξας` (aorist participle, "having opened"). The Plain English Translation has the imperative "Open the eyes of our heart." Supplying "Grant us, Lord," does not change that participle. Lightfoot (p.172, opened) says Hilgenfeld "gets over the difficulty in part by substituting ἄνοιξον for ἀνοίξας," and that Gebhardt and Harnack deny the text is defective at all. Lake's English also has "open." The variant entry records only the supplement and says nothing of the participle or of either dissent, so the Plain English Translation departs from Lake's Greek with no entry (the works brief requires one for every departure). Fix: add to the 59:3 variant (or a second one) the reading `ἀνοίξας` against `ἄνοιξον`, and Gebhardt-Harnack's view that there is no gap.

7. **Major. chapters.json 44 (`views.G` and the "Claude's theory" box), variants.json 44:2.** `views.G` says Lake's sense "codicil" "comes from the language of Roman wills," and the box says a codicil to a will "was a familiar thing to Roman readers" and "a grave wrong in Roman law and custom." Lake says only that `ἐπινομίς` means "codicil"; neither Lake nor Lightfoot says Roman will law, and no source on Roman wills was opened. That is a checkable claim without a source. In addition, Lightfoot (p.133) records that "codicil" was already Rothe's reading (1837), and he calls the "codicil" option, along with the other, "unwarranted"; the box says it is "my own suggestion" and the variant `why` presents Lake's sense as the Plain English Translation's ground without saying a standard commentator rejected it. STYLE rule 4 requires saying where a theory overlaps published scholarship. Fix: label the box's will analogy as Claude's with no source, say it builds on Lake's gloss and Rothe's earlier "codicil," give Lightfoot's rejection, and remove "Roman" claims or source them.

## Minor findings

8. **Minor. variants.json (missing entry), 58:2.** Lake's footnote: "ζῇ 2 CS, om. LK and quotation by Basil." The second "lives" in the oath ("and as the Lord Jesus Christ lives") is in C and S only. The oath is the passage the 58:2 note builds a Trinity discussion on; add a variant.

9. **Minor. variants.json 51:1.** The second reading is rendered "any of the enemy's people" only. Lightfoot (p.153): "by any of the wiles (or of the ministers) of the adversary," and he prints the A, C, S reading; Lake's English is "through any attacks of the adversary." Give the neuter "wiles" sense and say Lightfoot prints the reading Lake rejects.

10. **Minor. notes.json 50:5.** Lake: "This seems corrupt: a present is required." Lightfoot (p.151) explains that if the imperfect stands, "the point of time denoted in ἐσμέν must be the second advent, so that the deeds of this present life are regarded as past." The note omits that explanation, and "have been doing" is a perfect, not Lake's imperfect "were doing"; the note says the text is kept in "the past tense."

11. **Minor. notes.json 59:2.** Only Lake's "child" is given. Lightfoot (p.171) says the higher sense of "son" was soon read into `παῖς` and adds, "And so Clement seems to have used the word here." The note should report Lightfoot's view beside the "servant" rendering.

12. **Minor. notes.json 44:3 ("with no thought of gain").** Lightfoot (p.134): `ἀβαναύσως` is "unassumingly," "vulgar self-assertion." The note gives only Lake's "disinterestedly" and an unsourced definition of `banausos` ("a craftsman who worked for pay"). The Plain English Translation's "with no thought of gain" thus follows the Lake side only, while 49:5 renders `βάναυσον` "cheap." Give Lightfoot and reconcile the two renderings.

13. **Minor. chapters.json 57 (`sum`).** "From 57:6 to the start of chapter 64" Codex Alexandrinus is missing. Lightfoot (his description of Codex A in the introduction to the text, opened) says A omits "from § 57 ἀνθ᾽ ὧν γὰρ ἠδίκουν to the end of § 63," which is 57:7. Align the summary and the 57:7 note's wording with 57:7. The `views.H` claim that no Greek copy of the prayer "was known until Bryennios found" C is not in Lake's introduction (it gives only the find); cite a source or soften to what the introduction says.

14. **Minor. chapters.json 48 (`views.J`).** "Psalm 118 was sung at the great feasts as pilgrims entered the Temple gates" has no source in sources-checked.md or the files. Source it or drop it (STYLE rule 2).

15. **Minor. notes.json 47:7.** "Most read it of outsiders in general, Romans who were not Christians" has no source, and "Romans" is wrong in a letter from Romans to Corinthians. Say "non-Christians" and name who reads it so, or drop it.

16. **Minor. variants.json 45:8.** "The two Greek words differ by one letter." `αὐτοῦ` and `αὐτῶν` differ in two letters.

17. **Minor. variants.json 46:8.** "Lake prints it as the harder and less Gospel-like reading" attributes a reason to Lake that he does not give (he only prints it and gives the witnesses). State it as the writer's reasoning.

18. **Minor. chapters.json 46 (`settings`).** "Quoted from memory or tradition" for the saying at 46:8 is a claim about Clement that nothing proves, and it uses the hedge STYLE rule 2 forbids. Use "Clement's form; no Gospel has exactly these words."

19. **Minor. notes.json 46:2.** "No source opened for this site names a candidate" is contradicted by Lightfoot (p.139), which the writer opened: nearest is Sirach 6:34, with similar words in Hermas, Visions 3.6, Similitudes 8.8 and 9.20, and "perhaps another of those apocryphal quotations to which Photius alludes." Proverbs 13:20 in the note's list is the writer's own addition with no source; label it or remove it. Give the Hermas parallels.

20. **Minor. notes.json 54:4.** The note admits the Greek has no word for "city" and the Plain English Translation adds "God's city," which also invites a reader to think of the later "City of God." The Greek is "citizenship (or way of life) of God"; the note's own gloss is closer. Fix: "those who live as citizens of God's commonwealth" or "live the life of God's citizens," and keep the note.

21. **Minor. plain.txt 58:2, 64:1, 65:2 (doxologies), variants/notes.** Greek `δι᾽ οὗ ἐστὶν αὐτῷ ἡ δόξα` (58:2), `δι᾽ οὗ αὐτῷ δόξα` (64:1, 65:2) is "to him." The Plain English Translation has "to God" in all three. It is the natural reading (the writer's antecedent is the Father), and Lake's English keeps "to him" ("through whom is to him the glory"), but the change is not noted. The word "to God" should either be restored to "to him" or a one-line note at 58:2 should say the Greek has "him" and why "God" is supplied. 61:3's `σοι` is addressed to God and is fine.

22. **Minor. plain.txt 64:1 vs 58:1 and 60:1.** `μεγαλωσύνη` is "majesty" (58:1) and "greatness" (61:3, 64:1, 65:2); `μεγαλοπρέπεια` is "greatness" at 60:1 and `μεγαλοπρεπές` is "splendid" at 64:1. Two Greek words, one English word, two English words, one Greek word. Pick a fixed pair and add the terms.

23. **Minor. plain.txt 58:2.** `ἀμεταμελήτως` is "without turning back," while `ἀμεταμέλητα` two lines earlier in the same verse is "nothing to regret," and 54:4 has "no one regrets." Same root, two renderings in one verse. Use "without regret" or "without changing their mind" and keep the three consistent.

24. **Minor. plain.txt 59:3.** `ἐπαίδευσας` "taught" while `παιδεία` is "discipline" throughout 56 ("holy discipline," 56:16), the same root. Either "disciplined" or a note that the verb is the same word.

25. **Minor. plain.txt 57:7.** `νηπίους` "the helpless": Swete's `νηπίους` is "infants, the simple, the naive" (and Matthew 11:25's word). The note at 57:5 mentions it but gives no rendering reason, and the verse itself has none. Use "the simple" or "little ones" or add a note at 57:7.

26. **Minor. chapters.json 52, 50, 48 (`settings`).** The brief asks for settings for the quoted speeches (God, Moses, David). Chapter 52 (David's words, three psalm quotations), 50:4 (the "it is written" prophecy) and 48:2 (the psalm) have none, although 53, 54, 56 and 57 do. 56:3-15 (Job's friend Eliphaz, Psalm 118, Proverbs 3) is under a general setting only.

27. **Minor. notes.json 59:1, chapters.json 59.** The "Where Rome speaks with authority" note gives a Catholic source (1908 Catholic Encyclopedia, "Clement evidently writes officially, with all that authority of the Roman Church" and "No appeal seems to have been made to Rome": both verified) and Lake, but the other side is "Readers who doubt that it shows a Roman primacy point out..." with no source. The brief says to give each view only from a source that addresses 1 Clement. Name a source for the non-Catholic reading (Lightfoot's reading of the letter as one church advising another, if it fits what he says) or state that the Orthodox and Protestant readings were not found.

28. **Minor. `content/proposals/works/topics/apostolic-succession.json`.** The lead says "the churches still differ... a line of bishops by ordination, or faithfulness to the apostles' teaching," a two-way split with no source and no church named (no Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran or Reformed wording). It cites only the Catechism and Lightfoot. The 44:2 variant (ἐπιμονήν was Lightfoot's emendation, and Lightfoot's translation is a reading, not the text) should also be marked, because the core quotes Lightfoot's "provided for a succession of tried persons" as if it were the Greek. Add sourced church statements or soften the lead.

29. **Minor. `content/books/1Cl/parts/40-65/topics.json`, `elders`.** `common`: "In 1 Clement, as in Acts 20 and Titus 1, overseers and elders appear to be the same leaders." This is presented as shared ground but rests on Lightfoot alone; the writer's own card question is whether churches disagree. Source another tradition's scholar or move it to a view.

30. **Minor. chapters.json (`settings.readers`, chapters 40-65).** "About AD 170 the bishop of Corinth wrote..." The date is not in Eusebius 4.23.11 as quoted; it comes from outside the opened passage. Source or give "in the time of Soter, bishop of Rome" only if Eusebius says so. (I did not reopen Eusebius; this is a flag, not a refutation.)

31. **Minor. texts (for the lead, not the writer).** `texts/orig/1Cl.json` 46:8 has `καταπον τισθῆναι` (a line-break hyphen turned into a space; the scan has `καταπον-`/`τισθῆναι`): should be `καταποντισθῆναι`; 46:9 has a stray `·` before `εἰς` (`πολλοὺς· εἰς`). Lake's printed `τοῦ ζήλους` (63:2; scan line 6546) and `εὐαριστείτω` (41:1) are Lake's own misprints, so they are not digital slips but should be named in notes. No slip should go in variants.json.

32. **Minor. notes.json 44:2 / variants.json 44:2.** "The Syriac... approval" and `ἐπιδοκιμήν`: Lake's footnote gives "the equivalent of ἐπιδοκιμήν S." Lightfoot (p.133) translates the Syriac "in the meantime upon probation (ἐπὶ δοκιμῇ) they gave," so "probation, testing" is closer than "approval."

33. **Minor. notes.json 55:1 (type `myth`), chapters.json 55 (`views.G`).** "From Greek and Roman history" and "the stories Greek and Roman readers told of their heroes" name no example and open no source (Lightfoot gives instances). Give one named example from a source opened, or say only that Clement names none.

34. **Minor. notes.json 56:5.** The note quotes Clement's Greek-Psalm text as "the oil of a sinner" (singular, which is Swete's `ἁμαρτωλοῦ`). Clement 56:5 has `ἁμαρτωλῶν` ("sinners"), as plain.txt has it. Quote Clement's plural and note Swete's singular.

35. **Minor. chapters.json 41, 42, 44 and 45 (`views.N` / `J`).** "The list follows Hebrews 11:33-38" (45), "is the method of Hebrews 2:2-3" (41), "the sending chain is John 20:21" (42): "follows" and "is" are stronger than an echo. Use "echoes" unless a source says dependence.
