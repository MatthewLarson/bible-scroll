# Hostile review: 1 Clement 21-39

Reviewer: separate hostile reviewer (not the writer). Date of review: 2026-11-12.
Checked: every verse of `plain.txt` against Lake's Greek (`texts/orig/1Cl.json`), Lake's footnotes (`texts/src/1Cl.app.json`, Beta Code converted) and Lake's English (`texts/en/1Cl.json`); Lake vol. 1 OCR (`.cache/dl/lake-ia/`); Lightfoot 1890 (`.cache/dl/lightfoot-clem/p1v2.txt`); Rahlfs LXX (via `.scratch/1Cl-21-39/rahlfs.py`); the site's own wording of about 70 quoted verses (via `pv.py`); Eusebius 3.38 (newadvent.org, opened now); Livy 2.32 (`livy.htm`); Tertullian, Resurrection 13 (newadvent.org/fathers/0316.htm); KJV Psalm 19:3 (biblegateway.com). `node tools/build.js --check 1Cl/21-39` reports 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is a strong, careful part. The translation is faithful verse by verse; I found no mistranslation of names, numbers, tenses or who-does-what, and the quotations that follow the site's wording do match it exactly (checked by script). The ἐπιείκεια decision ("forbearance") is applied correctly in 21:7, 29:1 and 30:8. The phoenix material is accurate against Herodotus's reported claims, Lake's footnote and Tertullian. The problems are: one translation slip that contradicts the writer's own note (23:5, "and the Holy One"); one overstated claim about the Hebrew of Psalm 19:3; several note quotations that are not the site's wording; a missing Lake/Lightfoot variant at 37:2 (and a doubtful "steadily"); the 36 chapter page does not give the two directions of dependence the brief required and states a conclusion ("too close to be chance") with no source; many views are attributed to unnamed "readers" or "churches"; and a scatter of unsourced assertions about Clement's process ("from memory") and about later Christian use. Nothing is invented. No copyrighted-translation dependence found beyond one wording match flagged in finding 1.

Counts: 0 critical, 6 major, 17 minor.

## Major

1. **major. Notes quote the site's Hebrews and Psalm wording wrongly.** `notes.json`, note at 21:2 (word, "it says somewhere"). It gives Hebrews 2:6 as "someone has testified somewhere" and Hebrews 4:4 as "he has said somewhere", in quotation marks and as the site's wording. The site has "someone has declared somewhere" (Hebrews 2:6) and "Somewhere, you see, he spoke about the seventh day" (Hebrews 4:4). "Testified somewhere" is also the shape of a well-known modern translation's Hebrews 2:6 (I did not open that text this session; I flag only the three-word match). Fix: quote the site's words exactly, or drop the quotation marks and describe the phrase. The build did not catch it (it checks only some shapes). Same fault, smaller: note at 36:2 gives 1 Corinthians 13:12 as now we see "in a mirror" dimly; the site has "look through a mirror" and "a riddle" (see finding 17).

2. **major. Psalm 19:3: "the Hebrew says the opposite" is overstated and one-sided.** Note at 27:7 (title "The Greek Psalm 19:3 says the opposite", p, and b), repeated in the 27 chapter page (`views.J`: "whose third line says the opposite of the Hebrew"). The Hebrew line is ambiguous. The public-domain KJV, which I opened, reads "There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard", which is Clement's sense (every voice heard). The site's "No speech, no words; no voice of theirs is heard" is one defensible reading, not the plain content of the Hebrew. Fix: say the Hebrew can be read either way, that the site's Psalm 19 takes the silent reading and some older translations (KJV) take the "heard everywhere" reading, and that the Septuagint (and so Clement) fixes the second. Do not say "opposite".

3. **major. 23:5 drops "and" before "the Holy One".** Greek: `ἐξαίφνης ἥξει ὁ κύριος εἰς τὸν ναὸν αὐτοῦ, καὶ ὁ ἅγιος, ὃν ὑμεῖς προσδοκᾶτε`. The Plain English Translation has "The Lord will come suddenly to his temple, the Holy One whom you are waiting for", which turns a second subject into an apposition. Lake keeps the "and" ("and the Holy One for whom ye look"). Malachi 3:1 (Greek) has two figures joined by καί (the Lord; the messenger of the covenant), and the writer's own note at 23:5 and the 23 chapter page (`views.J`) say Clement "joins the two halves" and puts "the Holy One" where Malachi has "the messenger of the covenant". The text and the note disagree. Fix: "the Lord will come suddenly to his temple, and the Holy One whom you are waiting for." Leave the question of one figure or two to a note.

4. **major. Chapter 36 does not give both directions of dependence, and states a conclusion with no source.** `chapters.json` ch. 36 (`deep`, `views.N`, `views.H`) and note at 36:2. The brief (brief-1cl.md, Parallels, Hebrews 1:3-13) required "both directions of dependence and who holds them, from sources opened". The page gives only Clement-uses-Hebrews, then Eusebius. Problems: (a) "The likeness is too close to be chance" is the writer's assertion, unsourced; (b) the note at 36:2 calls this "the clearest use of Hebrews in any early writing" (note's `p`), unsourced; (c) the note says "the site's Hebrews introduction treats 1 Clement as the earliest witness to Hebrews", but `Heb/book.json` says only "Before about AD 96, when 1 Clement ... echoes it"; "earliest witness" is not stated there; (d) the dependence of Hebrews on Clement, or both on a shared tradition, is not mentioned at all, and no scholar is named for any view. I verified Eusebius 3.38.1-3 (newadvent.org): Clement "gives many thoughts drawn from the Epistle to the Hebrews", showing "most plainly that it is not a recent production"; "some say that the evangelist Luke, others that this Clement himself, translated" it; "The latter seems more probable", because the two epistles have a similar character of style. The page's summary of Eusebius is accurate. Fix: add the other directions with named holders from an opened source (Lightfoot's commentary, which is local, is the obvious place to look), drop or source "too close to be chance" and "clearest", and correct (c).

5. **major. Missing variant and doubtful rendering at 37:2 (ἑκτικῶς).** Lake's footnote (read by the writer, per `sources-checked.md`): "A reads εὐεικτ... (the rest of the word has disappeared...)", while C has ἑκτικῶς, which Lake prints. Lightfoot (`p1v2.txt` around line 14209) discusses this crux at length: he prefers εἰκτικῶς ("yieldingly, concessively") and reads ἑκτικῶς as "habitually ... readily, promptly". There is no variants.json entry for 37:2 and no note. The Plain English Translation has "steadily"; Lake has "habitual readiness", Lightfoot "readily". "Steadily" has no support in what was opened, and the three adjectives ("orderly, steadily, obediently") are not the sense Lightfoot gives. Fix: add a variant entry (A εὐεικτ-/εἰκτικῶς, C ἑκτικῶς, S as Lightfoot discusses it) with the renderings each reading yields; render ἑκτικῶς "readily" or "as a matter of habit", or say in the entry why "steadily"; no LSJ entry for ἑκτικῶς appears in `sources-checked.md`.

6. **major. Views and churches attributed to nobody.** The brief and STYLE require who holds each view. Instances: note 21:7 ("Readers who link the verse to 1 Corinthians 14:34 ..."; "Others note ..."); 30:3 and 32:4 notes and the 32 `deep` page ("Some readers", "Read one way", "Read another way", "Readers who stress 32:4 ... readers who stress 30:3"); 34:6-7 chapter `views.O` ("Many link ... to the Sanctus"); note 36:1 ("the churches read those chapters differently: some see the Eucharist ..., others prayer and praise only"); note 38:2 ("Some readers think the Corinthian rebels included people proud of their abstinence", and the `p` line "Some at Corinth may have boasted of sexual abstinence"); note 39:1 and ch. 39 `views.H` ("Many readers take both as the leaders of the revolt"). None names a scholar, church, catechism or commentator. The faith-and-works deep page points to the Romans 3, James 2 and Joshua 2 cards for churches, which is right, but the two "readings" of 30:3/32:4 are given as if they were established positions. Fix: attach a named holder from an opened source (Lightfoot, Lake, a catechism, a named scholar) to each, or label the sentence as the writer's own analysis (`spec`), or cut it. The `spec` box on 32 is correctly labelled; the unlabelled readings are not.

## Minor

7. **minor. Unsupported claims that Clement quoted "from memory".** Note 28:3 (title "Psalm 139 from memory"; "he seems to quote from memory"), note 35:5 ("Clement seems to quote Romans from memory"), note 29:3 ("made from memory"), note 26:2 ("may be quoting from memory"), and the 23 chapter setting (`settings[1].when`: "quoted from memory or from a book now lost"). No opened source says this. The differences are real; the cause is not known (a different Greek text, a lectionary form, a free adaptation). Fix: say "differs from the Septuagint and the Hebrew in these ways" and stop there. The title of note 28:3 should change.

8. **minor. Note 35:5 says the site's renderings are used where Paul's Greek is the same word; they are not.** The site's Romans 1:29 has "whisper behind backs" for ψιθυριστάς, and "slander", "hate God", "insolent, proud and boastful" in 1:30; the translation of 35:5 has "gossip" (ψιθυρισμούς, also 30:3), and nouns where Paul has adjectives. The note (also "eris is 'fighting'") should say which words match (unrighteousness, wickedness, greed, deceit, spite, slander, hatred of God) and which do not. Nothing is wrong in the translation; the note's claim is.

9. **minor. Unsourced assertions about later Christian use and the setting** (STYLE rule 2; the no-memory rule). (a) 25 `deep`: "In Christian art and writing it became a common sign of Christ's rising"; 25 `readings`: "Later Christians kept the phoenix as a symbol of resurrection long after no one believed in the bird"; (b) same page: Tertullian "about a century after Clement" (no date opened; Tertullian's date was not checked this session; the page I opened gives no date); (c) 25 `views.H`: "Clement wrote from Rome, where Egyptian and Arabian marvels were well known"; (d) 24 `views.H`: "one of the earliest Christian arguments for the resurrection outside the New Testament"; (e) note 52 (30:7): "Clement's lists of examples (chapters 4-12, 17-18, 31) are his own version of Hebrews 11", a dependence claim stated as fact; (f) note 54 and ch. 31: "as some Jewish retellings do" rests on one text (4 Maccabees 13:12), so "retellings" should be "a retelling"; (g) note 38:3 asserts the "tomb" is the womb ("Clement calls the womb a 'tomb'") and the chapter-38 summary says "dark tomb of the womb"; the Greek says only "from what sort of tomb and darkness" (ἐκ ποίου τάφου καὶ σκότους). Lightfoot (line 14715) cites only Harnack's pointer to the Greek Psalm 139:15 ("the lowest parts of the earth"), which could equally mean the earth the body was made from (38:3 begins "from what material"). Fix: source each or soften; for (g) present womb and earth as possible meanings.

10. **minor. Missing Lake apparatus entries.** Lake's footnote at 30:3 records `μή CLK, καὶ μή AS` (the text "ἔργοις δικαιούμενοι, μὴ λόγοις") on the very verse the faith-and-works page centres on; and at 39:8 `βαλόντας A, βάλλοντας CLS (LXX)`. Neither has a variant entry. The sense barely changes, but the brief says to list Lake's differences and the 30:3 reading ("and not by words") is a one-word difference in a verse readers argue about. Add both, brief.

11. **minor. Variant witness lists are partly inferred from silence.** 21:3: the entry names A, C, the Syriac and the Coptic as reading ἴδωμεν "as Lake's text"; Lake's footnote says only that L implies εἴδωμεν. 21:1 and 38:2 also infer. Whether the Coptic (K) covers chapter 21 at all was not shown. The `w` fields should say "the other witnesses, by Lake's silence" without naming K and S unless Lake does. In 22:7 `b` the "Syriac's singular" is Lake's Greek retroversion of the Syriac, not the Syriac itself; the Hebrew of Psalm 34:19 is singular and the Greek plural, which is the sourced point.

12. **minor. Note 22:7 (and ch. 22 text): "the next psalm verse (34:19)".** After 34:17 the next verse is 34:18; 34:19 is two verses on (Clement skips 34:18-19, as the note at 22:1 says). Say "a later verse".

13. **minor. Note 29:2 (Deuteronomy 32:8): "recalling Babel (Genesis 11:8), where the Hebrew says he divided them."** The site's Genesis 11:8 says "scattered", the same word the Greek Deuteronomy uses; it is Deuteronomy 32:8's Hebrew that says "split up". The sentence reads as if Genesis 11:8 says "divided". Fix the antecedent.

14. **minor. Chapter 32 `deep`: "Three sentences in five chapters"** (30:3, 32:4, 33:1 span chapters 30-33, four chapters). The `justification` topic contribution is fine.

15. **minor. 32:4 "all people from the beginning of time" resolves an ambiguity the note keeps open.** The Greek is πάντας τοὺς ἀπ᾽ αἰῶνος ("all those from of old"); the note at 32:4 says he "does not say whether every human being is meant or all the righteous of every age" and favours the second. The text should not choose the wider sense. Consider "all those from the beginning of time" or "all who have lived since the world began" (the note's own phrase).

16. **minor. 35:5 "unkindness to strangers" and the term list.** ἀφιλοξενία is the exact opposite of hospitality, which the variant entry and the `hospitality` topic both depend on; the translation hides the word. "Lack of hospitality" keeps the link and still reads at eighth-grade level. Related terms point: the existing entry for παρρησία (terms.json: "freedom of speech ... pair freedom with courage rather than 'boldness' alone") is not followed at 35:2 ("truth with boldness") or 34:1 ("head held high"); a note should say why, or align.

17. **minor. Further site-wording slips in notes.** Note 36:2: 1 Corinthians 13:12 quoted "in a mirror" ("dimly" is not in the site). Note 38:3: Psalm 139:15 quoted "in the depths of the earth"; site has "embroidered in the lowest parts of the earth". The site's wording should be used exactly or the quotation marks dropped.

18. **minor. 33:5 inconsistency.** ἄνθρωπον is "the human being" in 33:4 and "humankind" in 33:5-6 (site Genesis 1:27 has "the human" for the same noun with the article). Quote-matching may justify 33:5's first line (site's Genesis 1:26 "humankind"), but "God made humankind; male and female he made them" should follow the Greek article ("God made the human being") or the note should explain.

19. **minor. Gloss at 37:3 is anachronistic and contradicts note 37:3.** "generals, colonels, captains and sergeants" for ἔπαρχοι, χιλίαρχοι, ἑκατόνταρχοι, πεντηκόνταρχοι; the note says commanders of fifty are "not a Roman rank". Modern military ranks also change the structure (ἔπαρχος is a prefect, not a general). Replace with a plain gloss ("army officers of every size of unit").

20. **minor. Missing `settings` for quoted speeches.** The brief asks for a setting for each quoted speech. Present: Psalm 34, the unknown Scripture, Genesis 1, Psalm 50, Psalms 2 and 110, Eliphaz. Missing: 26:2-3 (the psalmist; Job), 27:7 (Psalm 19), 28:3 (Psalm 139), 29:2-3 (Deuteronomy 32 and the unknown nation text), 30:4-5 (Zophar, Job 11), 34:6 (the heavenly court), 34:8 (1 Corinthians 2:9 / Isaiah 64:4). The 24-26 and 27-28 range settings cover the argument, not the speakers.

21. **minor. Note 39:5 ("ea"): "let alone" is unsourced, and Lightfoot's reading is missing.** Lightfoot (p1v2 line 14970) glosses ἔα δέ as "how much more" and cites a Septuagint manuscript (A) that has ἔα δὲ τοὺς κατοικοῦντας. Lake's "Away then" and Lightfoot's "how much more" differ from the "Ah," the Plain English Translation chose. Cite Lightfoot for "let alone"/"how much more" and say which sense the translation follows.

22. **minor. Phoenix topic core (`content/proposals/works/topics/phoenix.json`) is thin.** One section only; no Herodotus 2.73 (the earliest witness, with his own doubt), no mention of Pliny as Lake names him, no Heliopolis, no Clement's own features (spice tomb, worm, priests' records), no statement that the bird is a legend and that Clement presents it as fact (his section in `topics.json` has this, so the core should link or carry it). The Tertullian quotation is an English translation of the Ante-Nicene Fathers series (checked: "The righteous shall flourish like the phœnix"); name the translation or paraphrase. The `aka` list ("bird of Heliopolis") is fine. The lead line says "it lives five hundred years ... said to" correctly. I found no factual error in what the core states (Psalm 92:12 Greek, Lake's note, site wording "sprout like the date palm" all check).

23. **minor. Minor Greek-text slips of Lake's digital text not logged.** `lake-slips.md` lists four. Also: 37:5 prints `σώμᾳτι`; one of the two scans (TheApostolicFathersV1) shows `σώμᾳτι` too, so this may be a printing slip by Lake rather than an OCR slip (the other scan's line is garbled); report to the lead for `tools/lake-fixes.json`/a note, not as a variant. Stray full stops where the sense wants commas, read in the digital text: 28:3 (`τῆς γῆς. ἐκεῖ`), 35:8 (`κλέπτην. συνέτρεχες`), 35:11 (`θεοῦ. μήποτε`); I did not check them against the scans.
