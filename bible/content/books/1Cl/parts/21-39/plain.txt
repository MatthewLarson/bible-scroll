## Live Worthy of God
21:1 Watch out, beloved, that his many kindnesses do not turn into a judgment on us. That is what will happen if we do not live as citizens worthy of him, doing in harmony what is good and pleasing in his sight.
21:2 For it says somewhere, "The Spirit of the Lord is a lamp that searches the storerooms of the belly."
21:3 Let us see how near he is, and that none of our thoughts escapes him, and none of the reasonings we carry on.
21:4 So it is right for us not to desert our post and leave his will.
21:5 Let us offend foolish, senseless people who lift themselves up and boast in their arrogant talk, rather than offend God.
21:6 Let us show reverence for the Lord Jesus Christ, whose blood was given for us. Let us respect our leaders. Let us honor those who are older. Let us train the young in the training that teaches the fear of God. Let us guide our wives toward what is good.
21:7 Let them display a pure character that is worth loving. Let them show that their gentle will is sincere through and through. Let them make the forbearance of their tongue plain by their silence. Let them give their love not by favoring one side, but equally, in holiness, to all who fear God.
21:8 Let our children share in the training that is in Christ. Let them learn how strong humility is with God, what pure love can do with God, and how good and great the fear of him is. Let them learn that it saves all who live in it in holiness, with a pure mind.
21:9 For he searches out thoughts and musings. His breath is in us, and when he wants, he will take it away.
## The Fear of the Lord
22:1 The faith that is in Christ confirms all these things. For he himself, through the Holy Spirit, calls us like this: "Come here, children, and hear me out; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.
22:2 Is there anyone who wants life, who longs for days of seeing good?
22:3 Keep your tongue away from evil and your lips from words of deceit.
22:4 Turn aside from evil and do what is good.
22:5 Look for peace and run after it.
22:6 The Lord's eyes are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer. But the Lord's face is against those who do evil, to wipe their memory from the earth.
22:7 The righteous man cried out, and the Lord heard him and rescued him out of all his troubles.
22:8 Many are the whippings of the sinner, but mercy will surround those who put their hope in the Lord."
## Do Not Be Double-Minded
23:1 The Father, who is merciful in every way and loves to do good, has a tender heart toward those who fear him. Gently and kindly he pays out his gifts to those who come to him with an undivided mind.
23:2 So let us not be double-minded, and let our soul not dream up fancies about his overflowing and glorious gifts.
23:3 May this Scripture stay far from us, where it says, "Miserable are the double-minded, those who waver in their soul and say, 'We heard these things back in our fathers' days, too. And look, we have grown old, and none of it has happened to us.'
23:4 You fools! Compare yourselves to a tree. Take a vine. First it sheds its leaves, then a shoot comes, then a leaf, then a flower, and after that a sour grape, and then a ripe cluster." You see that in a short time the fruit of the tree reaches ripeness.
23:5 It is true: his will is going to be done quickly and suddenly. The Scripture also bears witness to this: "He will come quickly and will not delay," and, "The Lord will come suddenly to his temple, and the Holy One whom you are waiting for."
## The Resurrection to Come
24:1 Let us consider, beloved, how the Master keeps showing us that there will be a resurrection to come. He made the Lord Jesus Christ its firstfruits when he raised him from the dead.
24:2 Let us look, beloved, at the resurrection that happens with the seasons.
24:3 Day and night make a resurrection plain to us. The night goes to sleep, and the day rises. The day goes away, and night comes on.
24:4 Let us take the crops. How does sowing happen, and in what way?
24:5 The sower went out and threw each of the seeds into the ground. They fall on the ground dry and bare, and they break down. Then, out of their breaking down, the majesty of the Master's providence raises them up. From the one seed it makes many grow and bring out fruit.
## The Phoenix
25:1 Let us look at the strange sign that takes place in the lands of the east, that is, in the parts around Arabia.
25:2 For there is a bird named the phoenix. It is the only one of its kind, and it lives five hundred years. When it is near its end and about to die, it makes itself a tomb out of frankincense, myrrh and the other spices. When its time is complete, it goes into the tomb and dies.
25:3 As its flesh rots, a kind of worm is born. It feeds on the juices of the dead creature and grows wings. Then, once it is full-grown and strong, it picks up that tomb, where the bones of the bird before it lie. Carrying them, it makes the journey from the land of Arabia all the way to Egypt, to the city called Heliopolis.
25:4 There, in broad daylight, with everyone watching, it flies down onto the altar of the sun, sets the bones there, and then starts back the way it came.
25:5 Then the priests look up the records of the years, and they find that it has come when the five-hundredth year was complete.
## God Will Raise His Servants
26:1 Do we think it is something great and amazing, then, if the Creator of all things will bring about the resurrection of those who have served him in holiness, with the confidence of a good faith? He shows us the greatness of his promise even through a bird!
26:2 For it says somewhere, "And you will raise me up, and I will give you thanks," and, "I lay down and fell asleep; I woke up, for you are with me."
26:3 And again Job says, "And you will raise up this flesh of mine, which has endured all these things."
## Nothing Escapes God
27:1 With this hope, then, let our souls be bound to the one who is faithful in his promises and righteous in his judgments.
27:2 The one who commanded us not to lie will much less lie himself. For nothing is impossible with God except lying.
27:3 So let our faith in him be kindled again in us, and let us understand that all things are near to him.
27:4 By the word of his majesty he set all things in place, and by a word he can overturn them.
27:5 "Who will say to him, 'What have you done?' Or who will stand against the might of his strength?" When he wants, and as he wants, he will do all things, and not one of the things he has decreed will pass away.
27:6 All things are in his sight, and nothing has escaped his counsel,
27:7 since "the heavens keep recounting God's glory, and the sky's dome reports what his hands have made. One day gushes out talk to the next, and one night passes knowledge to the next. And there are no words and no speeches whose voices are not heard."
28:1 Since all things are seen and heard, then, let us fear him. Let us leave behind the filthy cravings that lead to worthless deeds, so that his mercy may shelter us from the judgments to come.
28:2 For where can any of us run away from his mighty hand? And what world will take in anyone who deserts him?
28:3 For the Writing says somewhere, "Where will I go, and where will I hide from your face? If I go up into heaven, you are there. If I go away to the farthest ends of the earth, your right hand is there. If I make my bed in the depths, your spirit is there."
28:4 So where could anyone go, or where could anyone run off to, away from the one who holds all things in his embrace?
## God's Chosen Portion
29:1 So let us come to him in holiness of soul, lifting up pure and spotless hands to him. Let us love our forbearing and tender-hearted Father, who made us a chosen portion for himself.
29:2 For this is how it is written: "When the Most High divided up the nations, when he scattered the children of Adam, he set the borders of the nations to match the number of the angels of God. His people Jacob became the Lord's portion, and Israel the share of his inheritance, measured out with a cord."
29:3 And in another place it says, "Look, the Lord is taking for himself a nation from among the nations, the way a man takes the firstfruits of his threshing floor. And out of that nation will come the most holy things."
## Holiness and Humility
30:1 Since we are the portion of the Holy One, then, let us do everything that belongs to holiness. Let us run from slander, from filthy and impure embraces, from drunkenness and uprisings and disgusting cravings, from loathsome adultery, and from disgusting pride.
30:2 "God takes his stand against the proud," it says, "but to the humble he gives grace."
30:3 So let us join ourselves to those to whom grace has been given by God. Let us put on harmony, being humble and self-controlled. Let us keep ourselves far away from all gossip and slander, and be justified by works, not by words.
30:4 For it says, "The one who says a lot will hear a lot in reply. Or does the smooth talker think that he is righteous?
30:5 Blessed is the one born of a woman who lives a short life. Do not be a person of many words."
30:6 Let our praise be with God, and not come from ourselves. For God hates those who praise themselves.
30:7 Let the testimony to our good conduct be given by others, as it was given to our fathers, the righteous.
30:8 Brashness, self-will and recklessness belong to those cursed by God. Forbearance, humility and gentleness are found with those blessed by God.
## The Paths of Blessing
31:1 So let us hold tight to his blessing, and let us see what the paths of blessing are. Let us unroll the record of what happened from the beginning.
31:2 Why was our father Abraham blessed? Was it not because he did righteousness and truth through faith?
31:3 Isaac knew with confidence what was going to happen, and he was gladly led up to be a sacrifice.
31:4 Jacob, in humility, left his land because of his brother. He went to Laban and served him as a slave, and he was given the scepter of the twelve tribes of Israel.
## Not Through Ourselves
32:1 If anyone thinks these things through honestly, one by one, he will recognize how great the gifts are that he gave.
32:2 For from him come the priests and all the Levites who serve at the altar of God. From him, in his human descent, comes the Lord Jesus. From him come kings and rulers and leaders in the line of Judah. And his other scepters are held in no small glory, since God promised, "Your offspring will be like the stars of the sky."
32:3 So all of them were given glory and greatness, not through themselves or their works or the right things they did, but through his will.
32:4 And so we too, who were called in Christ Jesus through his will, are not justified through ourselves. We are not justified through our own wisdom or understanding or godliness, or through works we have done in holiness of heart, but through faith. It is through faith that God Almighty has justified all those from the beginning of time. To him be the glory for ever and ever. Amen.
## Do Not Stop Doing Good
33:1 So what are we to do, brothers? Are we to sit idle, stop doing good, and leave love behind? May the Master never let that happen, at least not to us! No, let us hurry, with energy and eagerness, to finish every good work.
33:2 For the Creator and Master of all things himself takes joy in his works.
33:3 By his supremely great might he set the heavens firmly in place, and by his understanding, which no one can grasp, he put them in order. He separated the earth from the water that surrounds it and settled it on the firm foundation of his own will. He commanded the animals that roam on it to exist by his own decree. He prepared the sea and the creatures in it, and then shut it in by his own power.
33:4 Above all, he shaped the human being with his holy and faultless hands, as the stamp of his own image. The human being is the most excellent of his creatures and, in mind, the greatest of all.
33:5 For this is what God says: "Let us make humankind in our image and likeness." And God made the human being; male and female he made them.
33:6 So when he had finished all these things, he praised them and blessed them and said, "Grow and multiply."
33:7 Let us see that all the righteous have been adorned with good works. And the Lord himself adorned himself with good works and was glad.
33:8 Since we have this model to trace, let us come to his will without holding back. Let us work the work of righteousness with all our strength.
## The Good Worker
34:1 The good worker takes the bread he has earned with his head held high. The lazy, careless worker cannot meet his employer's eyes.
34:2 So we must be eager to do good, for all things come from him.
34:3 For he tells us beforehand, "Look, here is the Lord, and his reward goes ahead of him, to pay each person back by his work."
34:4 So he urges us, if we believe in him with our whole heart, not to be lazy or careless in any good work.
34:5 Let our boasting and our confidence be in him. Let us submit to his will. Let us think about the whole crowd of his angels, how they stand ready and serve his will.
34:6 For the Scripture says, "Tens of thousands of tens of thousands stood beside him, and thousands upon thousands served him, and they cried out, 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Armies; all creation is full of his glory.'"
34:7 So we too, gathered together in one place in harmony, with one conscience, must cry out to him earnestly, as if from one mouth, so that we may come to share in his great and glorious promises.
34:8 For it says, "No eye has seen, and no ear has heard, and it has not come up into a human heart, all that the Lord has prepared for those who wait for him."
## The Gifts of God
35:1 How blessed and amazing God's gifts are, beloved!
35:2 Life in immortality, brightness in righteousness, truth with freedom of speech, faith with confidence, self-control with holiness: all these lie within our understanding.
35:3 What, then, are the things being prepared for those who wait? The Creator and Father of the ages, the all-holy one, he alone knows how many they are and how beautiful.
35:4 So let us strive to be found among the number of those who wait, so that we may have a share in the promised gifts.
35:5 But how will this happen, beloved? It will happen if our mind is set firmly on God in faith. It will happen if we seek out what is pleasing and acceptable to him, if we carry out what fits his faultless will, and if we follow the way of truth. We must throw away from ourselves all unrighteousness and wickedness, greed, fights, spite and deceit, gossip and slander, hatred of God, pride and arrogance, empty glory-seeking and lack of hospitality.
35:6 For those who do these things are hateful to God, and not only those who do them, but also those who applaud them.
35:7 For the Scripture says, "But to the sinner God said, 'Why do you go listing my rules and taking my covenant into your mouth?
35:8 You hated being taught a lesson, and you threw my words behind you. If you saw a thief, you ran along with him, and you threw in your share with adulterers. Your mouth piled up evil, and your tongue wove deceit. You sat and talked against your brother, and you set a trap for your own mother's son.
35:9 You did these things, and I kept silent. You imagined, you lawless man, that I would be like you.
35:10 I will prove you wrong and set you in front of your own face.
35:11 Understand this, you who forget God, or he may snatch you away like a lion, and there will be no one to rescue you.
35:12 A sacrifice of praise will glorify me, and there is the way by which I will show him the salvation of God.'"
## Jesus Christ, Our High Priest
36:1 This is the way, beloved, in which we found our salvation, Jesus Christ, the high priest of our offerings, the protector and helper of our weakness.
36:2 Through him we look steadily into the heights of heaven. Through him we see, as in a mirror, God's faultless and most exalted face. Through him the eyes of our heart were opened. Through him our senseless and darkened mind blossoms again toward the light. Through him the Master willed us to taste the knowledge that never dies. He is the brightness that shines out from God's majesty, and he is greater than the angels by as much as the name he has inherited is more excellent than theirs.
36:3 For it is written this way: "He makes his angels into winds, and his attendants into a flame of fire."
36:4 But about his Son the Master said this: "You are my son. This day I have fathered you. Ask it of me, and I will hand you the nations to inherit, and the far ends of the earth to hold as your own."
36:5 And again he says to him, "Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies down as a footstool for your feet."
36:6 Who, then, are the enemies? The wicked, and those who set themselves against his will.
## The Army and the Body
37:1 So, brothers, let us serve as soldiers with all our energy under his faultless orders.
37:2 Let us think about the men who serve as soldiers under our commanders: how orderly, how readily, how obediently they carry out what they are told to do.
37:3 Not all of them are prefects, or commanders of a thousand, or of a hundred, or of fifty, and so on down the line. But each one, in his own rank, carries out what the emperor and the commanders order.
37:4 The great cannot exist without the small, nor the small without the great. There is a kind of blending among them all, and in this lies their usefulness.
37:5 Let us take our body. The head is nothing without the feet, and in the same way the feet are nothing without the head. Even the smallest parts of our body are necessary and useful to the whole body. All of them breathe together and share one submission, so that the whole body may be kept safe.
38:1 So let our whole body be kept safe in Christ Jesus. And let each one submit to his neighbor, in keeping with the gift he has been placed in.
38:2 Let the strong look after the weak, and let the weak respect the strong. Let the rich supply what the poor need, and let the poor give thanks to God that he gave him someone through whom his need may be filled. Let the wise show his wisdom not in words but in good works. Let the humble not testify about himself, but leave it to someone else to testify about him. Let the one who is pure in the flesh not brag about it, since he knows that it is someone else who supplies him with his self-control.
38:3 So let us think it over, brothers. What material were we made from? What sort of beings, and who, were we when we came into the world? From what sort of tomb and darkness did the one who formed and created us bring us into his world? He prepared his kindnesses for us before we were born.
38:4 Since we have all these things from him, we ought to give him thanks in every way. To him be the glory for ever and ever. Amen.
## Against the Proud
39:1 Senseless, foolish, stupid and untaught people jeer at us and sneer at us, because they want to lift themselves up in their own minds.
39:2 For what can a mortal do? Or what strength does a child of the earth have?
39:3 For it is written, "There was no shape before my eyes, but I heard a breeze and a voice:
39:4 'What? Will a mortal be pure before the Lord? Or will a man be blameless in his deeds, when he does not trust his servants, and has noticed something crooked in his angels?
39:5 Not even heaven is pure in his sight. How much less those who live in houses of clay, the same clay that we ourselves are made from! He struck them like a moth, and from morning to evening they are gone. Because they could not help themselves, they perished.
39:6 He breathed on them, and they died, because they had no wisdom.'
39:7 But call out, and see if anyone will answer you, or if you will see any of the holy angels. For anger destroys the senseless, and jealousy puts to death the one who has gone astray.
39:8 I have seen senseless people putting down roots, but at once their home was eaten up.
39:9 May their sons be far from safety. May they be mocked at the doors of lesser men, with no one to rescue them. For what was prepared for them, the righteous will eat, but they themselves will not be rescued from evils."
