## Mourning in every city
4:1 Wherever this order arrived, the Gentiles held a public feast with shouting and joy. The hatred that had long been hardened in their minds now came out into the open.
4:2 But for the Jews there was grief beyond words, and a cry of mourning on every side, with tears. Their hearts were on fire with groaning everywhere, as they wailed over the ruin that had suddenly been decreed for them, which no one had expected.
4:3 What district or city, what pitiful place at all, what lanes were not filled with the beating of breasts and wailing for them?
4:4 The governors of each city sent them away all together, with bitterness and hearts that had no pity. They did it so harshly that some of their enemies too wept at their miserable banishment. Seeing such strange punishments, these enemies kept before their eyes the pity all people share, and they thought how uncertain the end of a life can be.
4:5 The old man was led away, his head thick with gray hair. His feet were bent and slow with age, but a violent shove drove him on, with no respect at all, to march fast.
4:6 Brides, young women who had only just gone into the bridal room to share their lives with a husband, took up wailing instead of delight. Their hair, still wet with perfume, was fouled with dust. They were led off with their heads uncovered, and all together they raised a lament instead of wedding songs, like women torn by the cubs of a foreign breed.
4:7 In chains, in front of everyone, they were dragged by force all the way to where they were put on board the ship.
4:8 Their husbands, in the full bloom of youth, had ropes wound around their necks instead of wedding garlands. Instead of feasting and the easy fun of young men, they spent what was left of their wedding days in laments, seeing Hades already lying at their feet.
4:9 They were brought down like animals, driven in iron chains. Some had their necks nailed to the benches of the ships. Others had their feet locked fast in shackles that could not be broken.
4:10 Besides this, a thick deck of planks lay over them, so that their eyes were in darkness on every side, and for the whole voyage down they were handled like traitors.
## Penned in the hippodrome
4:11 When they had been brought to Schedia, as the place is called, and their voyage was over, the king gave the order he had decided on: they were to be penned in the hippodrome in front of the city. It had an enormous circuit, and it suited his purpose well, to make an example of them before everyone who came down into the city and everyone who went out from it into the countryside to travel. They were to have nothing to do with his troops, and they were not to be thought worthy of a place inside the city wall at all.
4:12 When this was done, the king heard that their own people from the city were often slipping out in secret to mourn their brothers' shameful misery.
4:13 He flew into a rage and ordered that these too be treated with care, exactly as those had been, and that they should fall short of those others' punishment in no way at all.
## The registration fails
4:14 The whole race was to be registered name by name. This was not for the forced labor mentioned a little earlier. It was so that they could be tortured with the torments he had ordered, and at last be wiped out within a single day.
4:15 So the registration of them went on with bitter haste and eager, steady watching, from the rising of the sun to its setting. For forty days it went on, and still it could not be finished.
4:16 The king was filled with great joy that did not let up. He held drinking parties in front of all the idols. With a mind full of things far from the truth, and with an unholy mouth, he praised things that are mute and cannot speak to them or help them, and he said things against God that were not fit to be said.
4:17 When the time already mentioned was over, the scribes brought the king word that they no longer had the means to keep registering the Jews, because the Jews were too many to count.
4:18 Even so, most of them were still in the countryside. Some still lived in their houses, and others were here and there in the land. The work was too much for all the governors in Egypt.
4:19 The king threatened the scribes harshly, as men who had been bribed to plot the Jews' escape. But he came to be fully convinced of the truth about it,
4:20 for they showed him proof that even the papyrus and the reed pens they wrote with had already run out.
4:21 It was the unconquerable providence of him who helped the Jews from heaven, at work.
## Wine and incense for the elephants
5:1 Then the king called for Hermon, the officer in charge of the elephants. The king was full of heavy anger and fury, and nothing at all would change his mind.
5:2 He ordered that on the coming day the elephants, all five hundred of them, were to be given plenty of handfuls of frankincense and a great deal of unmixed wine to drink. When the lavish supply of drink had made them wild, Hermon was to drive them in to bring death on the Jews.
5:3 After he gave these orders, he went back to his feasting. He gathered the Friends and the men of the army who were most hostile to the Jews.
5:4 Hermon, the master of the elephants, carried out the order to the letter.
5:5 The servants under him went out in the evening and tied the hands of the wretched people. They took every other step to keep them under guard through the night, for they thought the whole race would meet its end in ruin all at once.
5:6 To the Gentiles the Jews seemed to have no protection at all, because of the chains and the force that hemmed them in on every side.
5:7 But they all cried out with tears, in a cry that would not stop, to the Lord Almighty, who rules with sovereign power over every power, their merciful God and Father. They begged him
5:8 to turn back the unholy plan against them, and to rescue them by a splendid appearance from the death that lay ready at their feet.
5:9 So their prayer went up to heaven with all their strength.
## The king's long sleep
5:10 Hermon gave the pitiless elephants their drink until they were full of the large supply of wine and stuffed with frankincense. Early in the morning he came to the palace to report this to the king.
5:11 Then he who freely gives to all he chooses sent a portion of sleep to the king. Sleep is a good thing, made from the beginning of time and laid on people night and day.
5:12 By the Master's working the king was held fast in a sweet, deep sleep. So his lawless purpose failed completely, and the plan he would not change was badly cheated.
5:13 The Jews escaped the hour that had been set, and they praised their holy God. Again they begged the one who is quick to make peace to show the arrogant Gentiles the strength of his own mighty hand.
5:14 It was already nearly the middle of the tenth hour when the man assigned to the invitations saw the guests all gathered. He went up and nudged the king.
5:15 With trouble he woke him and pointed out that the hour for the drinking party was already passing, and he told him about the guests.
5:16 The king thought it over and turned to his drinking. He ordered the guests who had come to the banquet to recline at table across from him.
5:17 When they had done so, he urged them to give themselves to feasting, and to make this part of the banquet a time of joy, celebrating it to the full.
5:18 The party dragged on, and then the king called for Hermon. With bitter threats he asked him why the Jews had been allowed to live through the day that was now past.
5:19 Hermon pointed out that he had carried out the order during the night, and the Friends backed him up.
## The king forgets
5:20 The king, whose cruelty was worse than that of Phalaris, said they could thank today's sleep for it. "Get the elephants ready the same way for the coming day, and no delay, to wipe out the lawless Jews."
5:21 When the king had said this, all who were there gladly agreed with joy, and each one went home.
5:22 They did not use the night for sleep so much as for thinking up every kind of cruel sport against those they took for wretches.
5:23 At dawn the rooster had only just crowed, and Hermon, who had armed the beasts, was moving them about in the great colonnade.
5:24 Crowds from all over the city had gathered to see this most pitiful sight, and they waited eagerly for morning.
5:25 The Jews, breathing their last in that fleeting moment, stretched out their hands toward heaven. With many tears, and songs of mourning, they begged the Most Great God to help them again, and quickly.
5:26 The sun had not yet scattered its rays, and the king was waiting for his Friends, when Hermon came up beside him and called him to come out. He showed him that what he wanted was ready.
5:27 The king heard him and was struck with amazement at being called out for such a lawless thing. Gripped by complete ignorance, he asked what this business was that had been carried out for him with such haste.
5:28 God, Master over all, was at work in this. He had put into the king's mind a forgetting of what he had plotted before.
5:29 Hermon and all the Friends pointed to the beasts and the troops. "They are ready, Your Majesty, just as you so firmly intended."
5:30 At these words the king was filled with heavy rage, because by God's providence his every thought about the matter had been scattered. He stared hard at Hermon and said with a threat,
5:31 "If your parents were here, or your children's children, they would have furnished this rich feast for the wild beasts in place of the Jews. The Jews have given me no cause for blame. They have shown me and my ancestors full and firm loyalty, beyond all others.
5:32 In fact, if it were not for the affection I have for you, who were raised with me, and for your service, you would have lost your life in their place."
5:33 So Hermon faced a dangerous threat that he had not expected, and his eyes and his face fell.
5:34 One by one the Friends slipped away with gloomy faces, and they sent the gathered crowd away, each one to his own business.
5:35 When the Jews heard the king's words, they praised the Lord, the God who shows himself, King of kings, for they had received this help from him too.
## The third order
5:36 The king set up the whole banquet again in the same way, and he urged the guests to turn to merriment.
5:37 He called for Hermon and said with a threat, "How many times must I give you orders about these very things, you most wretched man?
5:38 Even now, arm the elephants for tomorrow, to wipe out the Jews."
5:39 But his Kinsmen, at table with him, were amazed at his unsteady mind, and they spoke up:
5:40 "Your Majesty, how long will you keep testing us like this? Three times now you have ordered them wiped out. Then, when it comes to the deed, you change your mind and undo what you decided.
5:41 Because of this the city is in turmoil from waiting. It is filling up with mobs, and it is often close to being plundered."
5:42 At this the king, a Phalaris in every way, filled with senselessness, thought nothing of the changes of heart that had come over him to protect the Jews. He swore a firm oath that could never be carried out: he would send them to Hades at once, mangled by the beasts' knees and feet.
5:43 He would march against Judea and quickly level it to the ground with fire and spear. Their temple, the one "we may not enter," he would quickly bring down in fire and leave empty, for all time, of those who offer sacrifice there.
5:44 Then the Friends and Kinsmen left overjoyed and confident, and they posted the troops at the most suitable places in the city to keep watch.
5:45 The elephant master had driven the beasts, one might say, into a state of madness with the sweetest-smelling drinks, wine laced with frankincense, and had decked them out with fearsome gear.
5:46 About dawn, when the city was already packed with countless crowds all over the hippodrome, he went into the palace and urged the king on to the business at hand.
5:47 The king, his impious mind filled with heavy rage, rushed out in full force with the beasts. He wanted to watch with an unmoved heart, with his own eyes, the painful and miserable end of the people we have been speaking of.
5:48 The Jews saw the dust from the elephants going out toward the gate, and from the armed force following them, and from the marching crowd, and they heard the deep roar of the noise.
5:49 They thought this was the last moment of their lives, the end of their most wretched waiting. They turned to pity and wailing and kissed one another. They hugged their relatives and fell on their necks, parents on their children, mothers on their young daughters. Other women held newborn babies at their breasts, drawing their last milk.
5:50 Yet they also called to mind the help that had come to them from heaven before. All together they threw themselves face down, and they took the babies from the breast.
5:51 They cried out with a very loud voice and begged the Sovereign of all to show himself and have pity on them, now that they stood at the gates of Hades.
## Eleazar's prayer
6:1 Now there was a man named Eleazar, well known among the Jews of the countryside. He was already well on in years, and every virtue adorned his whole life. He hushed the elders around him who were calling on the holy God, and he prayed this:
6:2 "King, great in might, Most High, God Almighty, you steer all creation with tender mercy.
6:3 Look on the offspring of Abraham, on the children of Jacob whom you made holy, the people of your own holy portion, who are dying unjustly as foreigners in a foreign land.
6:4 Father, there was Pharaoh, the ruler of this Egypt long ago. He had chariots in great numbers, and lawless daring and a tongue full of big talk lifted him up. You drowned him in the sea and destroyed him and his proud army, and you made your mercy shine as a light on Israel's race.
6:5 There was Sennacherib, the cruel king of the Assyrians, proud of his forces beyond counting. With the spear he had already brought the whole earth under his hand, and he rose up against your holy city, his speech heavy with boasting and daring. You, Master, broke him to pieces and showed your might plainly to many nations.
6:6 There were the three friends in Babylonia, who willingly handed their lives over to the fire rather than serve empty things. You made the red-hot furnace wet with dew, and you rescued them, unharmed to the last hair, and you sent the flame against all their enemies.
6:7 There was Daniel, thrown down to the ground to the lions as food for wild beasts because of jealous slander. You brought him up into the light unhurt.
6:8 And Jonah was wasting away in the deep-sea monster's belly. You looked on him, Father, and showed him unharmed to all his own people.
6:9 So now, you who hate arrogance, rich in mercy, Judge of all, show yourself quickly to the holy ones of Israel's race, abused by hateful, lawless Gentiles.
6:10 If our life in this land far from home has become caught up in ungodly acts, then rescue us from the hand of our enemies, and destroy us yourself, Master, by whatever death you choose.
6:11 Do not let the empty-minded bless their empty gods over the death of the ones you love, saying, 'Not even their God rescued them.'
6:12 But you, Eternal One, who have all strength and all sovereign power, look on us now. Have mercy on us. By the senseless arrogance of lawless men we are being taken out of life as if we were traitors.
6:13 Let the Gentiles cower today before your unconquerable power, Honored One. You have the power to save the race of Jacob.
6:14 The whole crowd of little children and their parents beg you with tears.
6:15 Show all the Gentiles that you are with us, Lord, and have not turned your face from us. As you said, 'Even when they were in their enemies' land, I did not overlook them,' so make it come true, Lord."
## Two angels from heaven
6:16 Eleazar was just ending his prayer when the king came up to the hippodrome with the beasts and the whole snorting pride of his forces.
6:17 When the Jews saw this, they raised a great cry to heaven. The valleys nearby echoed it, and it struck the whole army with a panic no one could stop.
6:18 Then the God of great glory, the Almighty and true God, let his holy face be seen and opened the gates of heaven. From them two angels came down, glorious and terrible to look at, who could be seen by everyone except the Jews.
6:19 They stood against the enemy's forces and filled them with turmoil and terror, and they bound them with fetters that held them motionless.
6:20 Even the king's body began to shudder, and he forgot his sullen daring.
6:21 The beasts turned back on the armed forces following them and began trampling them and destroying them.
## The king turns
6:22 The king's anger was changed into pity and tears over what he had plotted before.
6:23 He heard the shouting and saw them all lying face down to die. He wept, and in anger he threatened his Friends, saying,
6:24 "You are ruling as if you were king, and you have gone beyond tyrants in cruelty. You are even trying to remove me, your benefactor, from my rule and from life itself, by plotting in secret what does the kingdom no good.
6:25 Who took each of these men from his home and gathered them here so senselessly? They have faithfully held the strongholds of our country for us.
6:26 Who has so lawlessly loaded with torments the people who, from the start, have been far above all nations in goodwill toward us in every way, and who have many times taken on the worst dangers people face?
6:27 Loose them! Undo these unjust chains! Send them home in peace, and take back the orders that were given.
6:28 Set free the sons of the Almighty, heavenly, living God. From our ancestors' days until now he has given our affairs a steadiness that nothing could block, and glory with it."
6:29 That is what he said. In a moment they were set free, and they blessed their holy God and Savior, for they had just escaped death.
## A feast of rescue
6:30 Then the king called in the officer in charge of the revenues to the city, and he ordered that the Jews be supplied with wines and everything else needed for a feast, for seven days. He decided that in the very place where they had thought they would meet their ruin, they should celebrate their rescue with every kind of joy.
6:31 So the people who before were scorned and near to Hades, or rather already walking in it, set up a cup of rescue instead of a bitter and miserable death. The very place that had been made ready for their fall and their grave they divided up into places at table, full of gladness.
6:32 They stopped the mournful song of their laments and took up the song of their ancestors, praising God, the Savior of Israel and maker of wonders. They pushed away all wailing and shrieking and formed dances as a token of peaceful gladness.
6:33 In the same way the king also held a great banquet over these events. Without stopping he gave thanks to heaven, in grand style, for the amazing rescue that had come to them.
6:34 Those who before had counted on their ruin, and on their being food for the birds, and who had gladly registered them, now groaned, covered with shame. Their fire-breathing daring was put out completely.
6:35 And the Jews, as we have said already, formed the dance we spoke of, and they spent the time feasting, with glad thanksgiving and psalms.
6:36 They made a rule about these things for all of them in common, for their whole community of resident aliens, for all generations: they set the days we have mentioned to be kept as days of joy. This was not for the sake of drinking and gluttony, but because of the rescue God had brought about for them.
6:37 Then they made a request to the king, asking to be sent home.
6:38 They were registered from the twenty-fifth of Pachon to the fourth of Epiphi, for forty days. Their destruction was set for the fifth to the seventh of Epiphi, three days.
6:39 On those days the Sovereign of all showed his mercy in glory and rescued them all together, unharmed.
6:40 They feasted, with everything supplied by the king, until the fourteenth, the day on which they also made their request to be sent home.
6:41 The king agreed and wrote for them the letter given below to the governors in each city, a letter full of generous goodwill:
## The king's letter of protection
7:1 "King Ptolemy Philopator to the governors in Egypt and all who are in charge of affairs: greetings and good health.
7:2 We ourselves are well, and our children too, for the great God guides our decrees as we choose.
7:3 Some of our Friends, out of malice, kept at our side and pressed us, and they persuaded us to gather the Jews of our kingdom together in groups and punish them with outlandish penalties, as rebels.
7:4 They argued that our affairs would never be stable because of the ill will these people have toward the other nations, until this was done.
7:5 They brought them down in chains, roughly, like slaves, or rather like traitors. Without any hearing or inquiry they tried to kill them, puffed up with a cruelty wilder than the custom of the Scythians.
7:6 But we threatened those men severely for this, because of the fairness we have toward all people, and we barely spared their lives. We have come to know for certain that the God of heaven shields the Jews and always fights on their side, as a father for his sons.
7:7 We also took into account the firm goodwill of a friend that they have toward us and our ancestors. So we have rightly cleared them of every charge of any kind.
7:8 We have ordered every one of them to return to his own home. No one is to harm them in any way at all, or to taunt them without reason about what has happened.
7:9 For you should know that if we plot any evil against them, or cause them any grief at all, we will have as our enemy, not a human being, but the Most High God, who rules over all power. He will avenge what is done in every way, and there will be no escaping him, ever. Farewell."
## The renegades punished
7:10 When they received this letter, they were not eager to leave at once. Instead they asked the king that those of the Jewish race who had willfully broken faith with the holy God by breaking God's law might receive at their hands the punishment they deserved.
7:11 They argued that people who had broken the commands of God for the sake of their stomachs would never be loyal to the king's commands either.
7:12 The king accepted that they were telling the truth, and he urged them on. He gave them full freedom to destroy those who had broken the law of God, everywhere in his kingdom, openly, without needing any royal authority or review.
7:13 Then their priests and the whole crowd cheered him, as was fitting, and they left with joy, shouting "Hallelujah!"
7:14 On the way, whenever they came upon any of their own people who had been defiled, they killed him, and they put them to death as public examples.
7:15 On that day they killed more than three hundred men, and they kept it as a day of joy and gladness, for they had overpowered the unholy.
## The journey home
7:16 But the people who had kept hold of God even to death, and who had been given full rescue and release, set out from the city crowned with all kinds of the sweetest-smelling flowers. With joy and shouting, with praises and songs of every tune, they thanked their fathers' God, the holy Savior of Israel.
7:17 They came to Ptolemais, which is called "Rose-Bearing" because of what the place is like. There, as they had all decided together, the fleet waited seven days for them.
7:18 There they held a drinking feast for their rescue, for the king had generously supplied every one of them with everything for the journey, until each reached his own house.
7:19 When they had landed in peace, with fitting thanksgiving, they decided there too to keep these days as days of joy for all their time as resident aliens.
7:20 They declared the days holy and set them up on a pillar, and they founded a place of prayer at the spot where they had feasted. Then they went away unharmed, free and overjoyed, kept safe on land and sea and river under the king's order, each to his own home.
7:21 They had very great authority in the sight of their enemies, with glory and awe, and no one at all shook them down for any of their goods.
7:22 All of them got back all their property, as it was listed in the register, so that anyone holding any of it gave it back to them with a very large payment. The great God had done great things to the full for their rescue.
7:23 Blessed be the Rescuer of Israel for all time to come. Amen.
