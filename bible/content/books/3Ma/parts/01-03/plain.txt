## Victory at Raphia
1:1 Philopator learned from men who had come back that Antiochus had taken from him the lands he ruled. So he called out all his forces, foot soldiers and horsemen, took his sister Arsinoe along, and set off for the country around Raphia, where Antiochus and his men had pitched camp.
1:2 A certain Theodotus resolved to put a scheme into action. He gathered the finest Ptolemaic soldiers who had once been placed under him and slipped across at night to Ptolemy's tent, planning to kill him alone and so finish the war.
1:3 But Dositheus, who was called Drimylus's son, had taken the king away. Dositheus was born a Jew, but later he abandoned the law and became a stranger to the teachings of his fathers. He had laid some nobody down to sleep in the tent, and that man ended up receiving the king's punishment.
1:4 A hard battle followed, and things were going rather well for Antiochus. Then Arsinoe went up and down the lines again and again. With her hair let down, weeping and wailing, she begged the troops to fight boldly to save themselves, their children and their wives. She promised that every man would get two minas of gold for a victory.
1:5 The result was that the enemy were destroyed in hand-to-hand fighting, and many were captured in battle too.
1:6 Now that he had the better of the plot, the king chose to go round the cities nearby and put heart into them.
1:7 He did so, and he handed out gifts to their shrines, and this gave his subjects confidence.
## The King at the Temple
1:8 The Jews sent him some men from the council of elders and from the elders. They came to welcome him, to bring guest gifts and to share his joy over what had happened. Their visit made him even keener to go to them quickly.
1:9 When he had come over to Jerusalem, he sacrificed to the faithful God, gave thanks, and did some of the things that suit the place. Then he entered the place itself, and he was struck by the earnest care and devotion he saw.
1:10 He was impressed, too, by how well run the temple was, and he took it into his head to plan to go into the sanctuary.
1:11 They told him that this was not right. No Gentile may go in, they said, and not all the priests either: only the high priest, who leads them all, and he only one time each year. But he would not be persuaded at all.
1:12 The law was even read out to him, yet he still would not stop insisting that he must go in. "They may be barred from this honor," he said, "but I should not be."
1:13 He asked why no one present had kept him out when he went into any shrine at all.
1:14 Someone answered, without thinking, that it was wrong to make a wonder of that very thing.
1:15 "Even so," he said, "what reason is there that I should not enter all of them, with their consent or without it?"
## Jerusalem Cries Out
1:16 The priests threw themselves down in all their robes. They begged the Most Great God to help them in what was happening and to turn aside the drive of the man who was setting out to do evil. Shouts mixed with weeping filled the temple.
1:17 The people left behind in the city were thrown into confusion and rushed out, not knowing what was going on.
1:18 Unmarried girls, who were kept shut in their rooms, ran out together with their mothers, their hair covered with dust. They even made the streets full of groaning.
1:19 Brides newly dressed for their weddings deserted the wedding rooms prepared for them. They cast off the modesty that suited them and ran in disorder all over the city.
1:20 The mothers and nurses in charge of newborn infants left them anywhere at all, some indoors, some in the lanes. They never turned around as they flocked to the temple, highest of all.
1:21 The prayers of those gathered there rose in every form, against the unholy thing he was setting out to do.
1:22 With them were the boldest of the citizens, who could not stand it any longer as he pressed on and meant to carry out his plan.
1:23 They called for a rush to arms and to die bravely by their fathers' law. They caused great turmoil in the place. It was hard for the old men and the elders to turn them back, but at last they returned to stand in prayer with the rest.
1:24 All the while the people went on praying, as they had been.
1:25 The senior men at the king's side made many attempts to talk his haughty mind out of the scheme he had planned.
1:26 But he grew bold and brushed them all aside. He was already going forward, thinking he would finish what he had said he would do.
1:27 Seeing this, even those around him turned and joined our people. They called on the one who holds every power to defend them in this hour and not to let this lawless, arrogant act pass.
1:28 The crowds kept up a cry so constant and so full of anguish that it became a roar no one could describe.
1:29 The people were not the only ones crying out, it seemed: the walls and the whole floor echoed too. For at that moment everyone would rather have died than see the place made unholy.
## Simon's Prayer
2:1 Then Simon the high priest knelt down facing the sanctuary. He stretched out his hands in the proper way and prayed like this:
2:2 "Lord, Lord, King of the heavens and Master of all creation, Holy One among the holy, the only Ruler, the Almighty: pay attention to us. We are worn down by an unholy, profane man, who snorts with boldness and strength.
2:3 For you created all things and rule over the whole world. You are a just Sovereign, and you sit in judgment on any who do things in pride and insolence.
2:4 You destroyed those who did wrong long ago. Giants were among them, sure of their own strength and daring, and you brought water without measure on them.
2:5 The Sodomites practiced arrogance, and their wicked ways were plain for all to see. You burned them to ashes with fire and sulfur and set them up as a warning to later generations.
2:6 Bold Pharaoh made your holy people Israel his slaves. You tested him with many kinds of punishment and made your power known. By them you made your great might known.
2:7 He hunted them down with chariots and a huge army, and you drowned him under the deep sea. But the people who put their trust in you, the Sovereign of all creation, you carried across unharmed.
2:8 They saw what your hand had done, and they praised you, the Almighty.
2:9 You, King, made the earth without limit or measure, and you chose this city. You set this place apart as holy for your name, though you lack nothing. You made it glorious with a splendid appearance and established it to bring glory to your great and honored name.
2:10 You loved the house of Israel, and out of that love you gave a promise: if we turn away and trouble catches up with us, and then we come to our place and pray, you will hear our prayer.
2:11 Faithful and true you are, indeed.
2:12 Again and again, when our fathers were hard pressed, you helped them in their low state and pulled them out of great evils.
2:13 But look, holy King: now, because of our many great sins, we are worn down. We have been put under our enemies, and we are weak and helpless.
2:14 And now that we have fallen, this bold, profane man is trying to insult the holy place on earth that was marked out for the name of your glory.
2:15 The heaven of heaven, where you live, is out of human reach.
2:16 But because you were pleased to have your glory among your people Israel, you made this place holy.
2:17 Do not punish us through these men's uncleanness, and do not judge us through this profaning. Do not let the lawless boast in their fury or exult with arrogant tongues, saying,
2:18 'We have trampled the house of holiness, as the houses of loathsome idols are trampled.'
2:19 Wipe out our sins, scatter our wrongs, and let your mercy shine out at this very hour.
2:20 Let your compassion come quickly to meet us. Give songs of praise to the lips of the fallen, of those whose spirits are crushed, and make peace for us."
## The King Struck Down
2:21 Then God, who watches over all things, the holy Forefather, holy among the holy ones, heard the prayer that was rightly offered. He flogged the man who was so lifted up with insolence and boldness.
2:22 He shook him this way and that, like a reed in the wind, until he lay powerless on the floor, his limbs paralyzed. He could not even speak, for a just judgment had struck him.
2:23 His Friends and bodyguards saw the sharp punishment that had seized him. They were afraid his life might even slip away, so they hauled him out in a hurry, stunned by terror beyond measure.
2:24 After a while he came to himself. Yet even after this rebuke he felt no regret at all. He left making bitter threats.
## The Pillar in Alexandria
2:25 He crossed back to Egypt and went further in his wickedness, with the drinking partners and companions already mentioned, men cut off from all that is right.
2:26 His countless acts of excess were not enough for him. He went so far in his boldness that he made slanders in those places, and many of his Friends, keeping a close eye on what the king intended, went along with his wishes.
2:27 He meant to shame the nation openly. On the tower beside the palace courtyard he put up a stone pillar and had words carved on it:
2:28 "No one who does not sacrifice may enter their temples. All the Jews are to be put on the census rolls and reduced to the condition of household slaves. Any who object are to be dragged off by force and removed from among the living.
2:29 Everyone enrolled is to have the mark of Dionysus, an ivy leaf, burned into his body. They are to be listed on the rolls under the reduced rights already set for them."
2:30 He did not want to look like an enemy of every one of them, so he added underneath: "If any of them prefer to live with people who are initiates of the mystery rites, they are to stand with the Alexandrians as equal citizens."
2:31 Some gave themselves up readily and lightly, because they hated the steps that the city's godliness required. They expected to share in some great glory from their closeness to the king.
2:32 But most of them held strong with noble spirit and did not desert their godliness. They traded money for their lives and, without fear, tried to rescue themselves from being enrolled.
2:33 They were full of hope that they would find help. They loathed those of their own who went over, judged them enemies of the nation, and shut them out of the life they shared and every kind of help.
## Slander against the Jews
3:1 The impious king heard of it, and his anger boiled over. He raged at the Jews of Alexandria, and even more fiercely he set himself against the Jews out in the country. He ordered them all to be rounded up quickly into one place and given the worst of deaths.
3:2 As these plans were going forward, people who had joined together to do the race harm put out an ugly rumor against it. They used as their opening the claim that the Jews kept them from their customs.
3:3 The Jews, though, stayed well disposed toward the kings and kept faith with them without wavering.
3:4 They worshiped God and lived by his law, so they kept themselves apart in the matter of food. For this reason some of them came to be seen as hateful.
3:5 But they adorned their dealings with others by doing what was right, and so everyone thought well of them.
3:6 Everyone was talking about the good conduct of the race, yet the foreigners took no account of it at all.
3:7 Instead they kept talking about how the Jews stood apart in their worship and their food. They claimed that these men would not share the drink offerings of alliance with the king or his forces. They called them enemies, standing hard against the state. In this way they pinned no small fear on them.
3:8 The Greeks in the city had suffered no wrong from them. They saw the uproar no one had expected around these men, and the mobs that kept rushing together without warning. They had no power to help, for the government was a tyranny. But they comforted them and were upset, and they thought things would turn around.
3:9 For a community that large, guilty of nothing, would not be left uncared for like this.
3:10 Already some neighbors, friends and business partners were secretly drawing a few of them aside. They gave their word to stand shield to shield with them and to do everything in their power to help.
## The King's Letter
3:11 But the king was puffed up by his present success and did not see the power of the Most Great God. Thinking he would keep to the same plan forever, he wrote the following letter aimed at them:
3:12 "King Ptolemy Philopator, to the governors and soldiers in Egypt and in every place: greeting, and may you be well.
3:13 I am well myself, and so are our affairs.
3:14 You know yourselves about our campaign into Asia. With the gods fighting at our side, never failing us, it reached the end we had reckoned on.
3:15 We then judged it best to nurse along the nations living in Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, not with the might of the spear but with fairness and much kindness, and we were glad to be good to them.
3:16 We granted the temples city by city the richest incomes. We were also led up to Jerusalem, to pay honor to the temple of those villains, whose folly never ends.
3:17 In word they welcomed our coming, but in deed they were false. When we were eager to enter their sanctuary and to honor it with the finest, most splendid gifts,
3:18 they were swept along by their long-standing pride and barred our way in. They escaped our force only because of the kindness we show all people.
3:19 They have shown their hostility to us openly. Alone of all the nations, they lift their necks proudly against kings, their own benefactors included, and they will not bear anything true.
3:20 We, for our part, put up with their folly. We came back victorious, and in Egypt we met all the peoples with kindness and behaved as suited us.
3:21 In doing so we made it known to everyone that we held no grudge against their kin here. Because of their service as allies, and the countless matters entrusted to them in good faith from the beginning, we chose to change their status. We wished to grant them the citizenship of the Alexandrians and to make them partners with our priests for all time.
3:22 But they took it the opposite way. With their inborn bad character they pushed away what was good and kept leaning toward what is low.
3:23 Not only did they turn down the priceless citizenship, but by word and by silence they also loathe the few among them who are truly loyal to us. At every turn they watch with suspicion, expecting that their most disgraceful way of life will soon make us undo our measures.
3:24 The evidence has fully persuaded us that these people mean us harm in every way. So we are guarding against a danger: should some sudden uprising come against us later, these impious people must not be standing behind us, traitors and barbarian enemies.
3:25 We have therefore ordered this: the moment this letter reaches you, that same hour, send us those who are settled in your districts, along with wives and children. Treat them with insult and roughness, and bind them in iron chains on all sides. They are to die a death without remedy and without honor, the kind enemies deserve.
3:26 Once all of them are punished at one stroke, we have concluded, our affairs will stand steady and in the best order for the rest of time.
3:27 Anyone who hides a Jew, whether an old man, a little child or even a baby still nursing, will be beaten to death with the most loathsome tortures, and his whole household with him.
3:28 Anyone who wants to may report them. The informer will get the goods of the person found guilty, plus two thousand drachmas out of the royal treasury, and he will be crowned with freedom.
3:29 If a Jew is found hidden anywhere at all, that place is to be put off-limits and set on fire. No mortal being will ever be able to use it again, in any way, forever."
3:30 This was the wording of the letter.
