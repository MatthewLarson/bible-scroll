## The call and the flight
1:1 The word of the LORD came to Jonah son of Amittai, and it said:
1:2 "Get up and go to the great city, Nineveh. Call out against it, for their wickedness has come up in front of me."
1:3 But Jonah got up to run away from the LORD's presence, toward Tarshish. He went down to Joppa and found a ship about to sail for Tarshish. He paid its fare, then went down inside to go along with them, away from the LORD's presence.
## The storm at sea
1:4 Then the LORD hurled a great wind onto the sea, and a great storm came on the sea. The ship seemed about to break apart.
1:5 The sailors were afraid. Each man cried for help to his own god, and they hurled the ship's cargo into the sea to make the load lighter for themselves. Jonah, meanwhile, had gone down to the deepest part of the ship. He had lain down and sunk into a deep sleep.
1:6 The chief of the sailors came to him and said, "What is wrong with you? You are sleeping! Get up and call to your god. Perhaps the god will turn his mind to us, and we will not perish."
1:7 Each man said to his neighbor, "Come, let us cast lots, so we can find out on whose account this disaster has come to us." They cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.
1:8 They said to him, "Tell us, please, on whose account this disaster has come to us. What is your work, and where have you come from? Which country is yours, and which people do you belong to?"
1:9 "I am a Hebrew," he told them. "The LORD is the one I fear, the God of the heavens, who made the sea and the dry land."
1:10 The men were filled with great fear. "What is this you have done?" they asked him. They knew he was running away from the LORD's presence, since he had already told them.
1:11 They asked him, "What should we do with you, so that the sea will go calm and leave us alone?" For the sea kept getting stormier and stormier.
1:12 He told them, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea, and the sea will go calm for you. I know that this great storm is on you because of me."
1:13 The men dug their oars in to return to the dry land, but they were not able to, because the sea kept getting stormier and stormier against them.
1:14 So they called out to the LORD: "We beg you, LORD, do not let us perish for the life of this man. Do not lay innocent blood on us. You, LORD, have done exactly what you wanted."
1:15 Then they lifted Jonah and hurled him into the sea, and the raging of the sea came to a stop.
1:16 The men were filled with great fear of the LORD. They offered the LORD a sacrifice and swore vows to him.
1:17 The LORD appointed a huge fish to swallow Jonah. For three days and three nights Jonah was inside the fish.
## Jonah's prayer in the fish
2:1 Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from inside the fish.
2:2 He said: / "I called to the LORD out of my trouble, / and he answered me. / From the belly of Sheol I cried for help; / you heard my voice.
2:3 You threw me into the deep, / into the heart of the seas, / and a river surrounded me. / All your breakers and your waves / passed over me.
2:4 I said, 'I have been driven away / from before your eyes.' / Even so, I will look again / toward your holy temple.
2:5 Water closed in on me up to my throat. / The deep surrounded me. / My head was bound with seaweed.
2:6 I went down to where the mountains end. / The earth with its bars was shut behind me forever. / But out of the Pit you brought my life up, / LORD my God.
2:7 As my life ebbed away inside me, / I called the LORD to mind. / My prayer came to you, / to your holy temple.
2:8 Those who devote themselves to empty nothings / abandon their loyal love.
2:9 But as for me, with a voice of thanksgiving / I will sacrifice to you. / The vows I made, I will fulfill. / Rescue is the LORD's."
2:10 The LORD spoke to the fish. It vomited Jonah onto the dry land.
## The second call
3:1 The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time, and it said:
3:2 "Get up and go to the great city, Nineveh, and call out to it the announcement I am telling you."
3:3 So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the word of the LORD said. Now Nineveh was a city great in God's eyes, a walk of three days.
3:4 Jonah set out into the city, going one day's walk, and he called out, "Forty days more, and Nineveh will be overturned!"
## Nineveh turns back
3:5 The people of Nineveh believed in God. They called a fast, and all of them, from their great ones to their small ones, put on sackcloth.
3:6 When word reached the king of Nineveh, he rose from his throne and laid his robe aside. He wrapped himself in sackcloth and sat down on the ashes.
3:7 He had a proclamation made in Nineveh: "By order of the king and his great men: No human being or animal, no herd or flock, may taste anything. They may not graze, and they may not drink water.
3:8 Let human beings and animals be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out to God with strength. Let each one turn back from his evil way, and from the violence in his hands.
3:9 Who knows? God may turn back and change his mind, and turn back from his burning anger, so that we do not perish."
3:10 God saw their deeds, that they had turned back from their evil way. So God changed his mind about the harm he had spoken of doing to them. He did not do it.
## Jonah's anger
4:1 But it was a great evil to Jonah, and he burned with anger.
4:2 He prayed to the LORD and said, "I beg you, LORD, was this not what I said while I was still in my own country? That is why I hurried to flee to Tarshish. I knew that you are a God gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and great in loyal love, one who changes his mind about the harm.
4:3 So now, LORD, please take my life from me, for my death is better than my life."
4:4 The LORD said, "Are you right to burn with anger?"
## The plant, the worm and the wind
4:5 Then Jonah left the city and sat down on its east side. He made himself a booth there and sat in its shade, waiting to see what would happen in the city.
4:6 The LORD God appointed a leafy plant, and it climbed up above Jonah to give shade for his head and rescue him from his misery. Jonah rejoiced over the plant with great joy.
4:7 God appointed a worm the next day, as the dawn came up, and it struck the plant, and the plant dried up.
4:8 As the sun came up, God appointed a hot east wind, and the sun struck Jonah's head. He grew faint and begged to die. "My death is better than my life," he said.
4:9 God said to Jonah, "Are you right to burn with anger over the plant?" "I am right to burn with anger," he said, "even to death."
## The question left open
4:10 The LORD said, "You had pity on the plant, which you did not work for and did not make grow, which sprang up in one night and perished in one night.
4:11 And should I not have pity on Nineveh, the great city, where there are more than 120,000 people who do not know the difference between their right hand and their left, and many animals too?"
