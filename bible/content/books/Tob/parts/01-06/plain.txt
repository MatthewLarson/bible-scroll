## Tobit of Naphtali
1:1 This is the book of the story of Tobit son of Tobiel, son of Ananiel, son of Aduel, son of Gabael, son of Raphael, son of Raguel, of the family of Asiel, from the tribe of Naphtali.
1:2 In the days of Shalmaneser king of the Assyrians, Tobit was taken captive from Thisbe. Thisbe is in upper Galilee, on the south side of Kedesh of Naphtali and above Asser, toward the west, with Phogor to its south.
## Faithful when others were not
1:3 All the days of my life I, Tobit, have walked in the ways of truth and done what is right. I gave many alms to my kinsmen and my people, who went with me as captives to Nineveh, in the land of the Assyrians.
1:4 When I was still in my own country, the land of Israel, and was young, the whole tribe of my ancestor Naphtali broke away from the house of my ancestor David and from Jerusalem. Jerusalem was the one city, out of all the tribes of Israel, where all Israel's tribes were to offer sacrifice. There the temple where God dwells had been made holy, built in that city for every generation to come.
1:5 All my kinsmen and the house of my ancestor Naphtali offered sacrifices on all the mountains of Galilee to the calf that Jeroboam king of Israel had made in Dan.
1:6 I was the only one who went to Jerusalem, and I went often, for the festivals, as the everlasting law written for all Israel commands. I would set off quickly for Jerusalem, carrying the first fruits and the first crops, a tenth of my livestock, and the first wool shorn from my sheep.
1:7 I gave these to the priests, the sons of Aaron, at the altar. I gave a tenth of the grain, the wine, the olives, the pomegranates and the other fruits to the sons of Levi who served in Jerusalem. For six years out of seven I set aside the second tenth in money, and every year I went and spent it in Jerusalem.
1:8 And I gave it to the orphans and the widows and to the converts who had joined the Israelites. In the third year I brought it and gave it to them, and we ate it, as the command about it in the law of Moses says. I also did as Deborah, the mother of our forefather Ananiel, had taught me, because my father died and left me an orphan.
1:9 When I became a man, I married a woman from the descendants of our own family line. She bore me a son, and I named him Tobias.
1:10 After I was carried off as a captive to Assyria, I came to Nineveh. All my kinsmen and the people of my own family ate the food of the Gentiles,
1:11 but I took care not to eat the food of the Gentiles.
1:12 Because I remembered my God with all my soul,
1:13 the Most High gave me favor and good standing with Shalmaneser, and I became his buyer, purchasing everything he needed.
1:14 I used to travel to Media and buy for him there, until he died. While I was in the land of Media, I left bags of silver, ten talents in all, in the keeping of my kinsman Gabael son of Gabri.
1:15 When Shalmaneser died, his son Sennacherib became king in his place. The roads into Media were cut off, and I could no longer travel to Media.
## Burying the dead
1:16 In the days of Shalmaneser I gave many alms to my kinsmen, the people of my own family.
1:17 I gave my bread to people who were hungry and clothing to people who had none. If I saw that one of my own people had died and been thrown out behind the city wall of Nineveh, I buried him.
1:18 I also buried anyone Sennacherib killed when he fled from Judea, in the days when the King of heaven carried out judgment on him for the blasphemies he had spoken. In his anger he killed many Israelites. I would steal their bodies and bury them, and when Sennacherib looked for them, he could not find them.
1:19 But a man from Nineveh went and told the king about me, that I was the one burying them, and I went into hiding. When I learned that the king knew about me and that I was being hunted to be put to death, I was afraid and ran away.
1:20 Everything I had was seized. They left me nothing; it all went into the king's treasury, except my wife Anna and my son Tobias.
1:21 Less than forty days later his two sons killed him and escaped to the mountains of Ararat, and his son Esarhaddon became king after him. Esarhaddon put Ahiqar, my brother Anael's son, in charge of all the accounts of his kingdom, and Ahiqar had power over the whole government.
1:22 Then Ahiqar spoke up for me, and I came back down to Nineveh. Under Sennacherib king of the Assyrians, Ahiqar had been chief cupbearer, keeper of the signet ring, governor and treasurer, and Esarhaddon appointed him again. He was my nephew, one of my own family.
## A dinner at Pentecost
2:1 So in the reign of Esarhaddon I came back down to my house, and my wife Anna and my son Tobias were given back to me. At our festival of Pentecost, the holy Feast of Weeks, a fine dinner was made ready for me, and I lay down at the table to eat.
2:2 The table was set before me with many kinds of food. I said to my son Tobias, "Son, go out and find a poor man among our kinsmen, one among the captives at Nineveh who remembers God with his whole heart. Bring him here to share my meal. I will wait for you, son, until you come back."
2:3 Tobias went to look for a poor man among our kinsmen. He came back and said, "Father!" I said to him, "Here I am, son." He answered, "Father, listen! One of our people has been murdered and thrown out in the marketplace. He has just been strangled, there where he lies."
2:4 I leapt to my feet and left the meal without tasting it. I lifted him out of the street and put him in one of the small rooms to wait until the sun went down, when I would bury him.
2:5 Then I came back, washed, and ate my bread in mourning.
2:6 I remembered the word that the prophet Amos spoke against Bethel: "Your festivals will be turned into mourning, and all your ways into a song of grief."
2:7 And I wept. When the sun had set, I went and dug a grave and buried him.
2:8 My neighbors laughed at me. "Isn't he afraid anymore?" they said. "He was already hunted to be killed for this very thing, and he ran away. And look, here he is burying the dead again!"
## Tobit loses his sight
2:9 That same night I washed and went into my courtyard. I lay down to sleep beside the courtyard wall, with my face uncovered because of the heat.
2:10 I did not know that there were sparrows in the wall above me. Their droppings landed, still warm, in my eyes, and white films grew over them. I went to doctors to be cured, but the more they rubbed their ointments on me, the more the white films blinded my eyes, until I could not see at all. For four years I had no use of my eyes. All my kinsmen grieved for me, and Ahiqar took care of me for two years, until he went away to Elymais.
2:11 At that time my wife Anna earned wages by doing women's work.
2:12 She would send the work to the people who ordered it, and they would pay her. On the seventh day of the month of Dystrus she cut a finished piece of cloth from the loom and sent it to them. They paid her the whole wage, and they also gave her a young goat from their flock for a meal at home.
2:13 When she came in to me, the goat began to bleat. I called her and said, "Where did this young goat come from? Has it been stolen? Give it back to its owners. We are not allowed to eat anything stolen."
2:14 She told me, "It was given to me as a gift, on top of my wages." But I did not believe her. I kept telling her to give it back to its owners, and I turned red with anger at her over it. Then she answered me, "Where are your alms now? Where are your right deeds? Look, all this about you is known!"
## Tobit's prayer
3:1 Then my soul was full of grief. I groaned and wept, and with groans I began to pray:
3:2 "Lord, you are righteous, and everything you do is right. Every path you take is mercy and truth. You judge the world.
3:3 And now, Lord, remember me and look on me. Do not punish me for my sins and my unknowing wrongs, or for those of my ancestors. I sinned against you
3:4 and refused to listen to your commands. So you handed us over to plunder, captivity and death. You made us a proverb, a thing people gossip about and mock, among all the nations where you scattered us.
3:5 And now all your many judgments are true, when you deal with me for my sins, because we have not kept your commands and have not walked before you in truth.
3:6 So now treat me as you see fit. Give the order to take my spirit from me, so that I may be released from the face of the earth and turn back into earth. Death would do me more good than life, for I have heard lying insults, and great grief is with me. Lord, give the order to set me free from this misery. Release me to the everlasting place, and do not turn your face away from me, Lord. Death would do me more good than seeing so much misery in my life and hearing insults."
## Sarah in Ecbatana
3:7 That same day it happened that Sarah, daughter of Raguel of Ecbatana in Media, also heard insults, from one of her father's maids.
3:8 She had been given in marriage to seven men, but Asmodeus, the evil demon, killed every one of them before he could sleep with her as husbands do. The maid said to her, "You are the one who kills your husbands! Look, you have been given to seven husbands already, and you have not carried the name of even one of them.
3:9 Why are you beating us because your husbands died? Go and join them! May we never see a son or daughter of yours, ever!"
3:10 That day her soul was full of grief, and she cried. She went up to the room upstairs in her father's house, wanting to hang herself. But she thought it over again and said, "They might shame my father and say to him, 'You had only one daughter, a dear one, and she hanged herself because of her troubles.' Then I would bring my father, old as he is, down to Hades in sorrow. It is better for me not to hang myself. I will beg the Lord to let me die, so that I will not hear insults anymore as long as I live."
## Sarah's prayer
3:11 At that moment she spread out her hands toward the window and prayed: "Blessed are you, merciful God, and blessed is your name forever. Let all your works bless you forever.
3:12 And now I have lifted my face and my eyes to you.
3:13 Command that I be released from the earth, so that I no longer hear insults.
3:14 You know, Master, that I am clean of any impurity with a man.
3:15 I have not stained my name or my father's name in the land where I am a captive. I am my father's only child. He has no other child to be his heir, and he has no close kinsman or relative for whom I should keep myself as a wife. Seven husbands of mine have died already. Why should I go on living? But if it does not please you to kill me, Lord, then listen now to the insults I suffer."
## God hears them both
3:16 At that very moment the prayers of both of them were heard before the glory of God.
3:17 Raphael was sent to heal the two of them. He was to peel the white films from Tobit's eyes, so that he could see God's light with his own eyes. He was to give Sarah daughter of Raguel as a wife to Tobias son of Tobit, and to set her free from Asmodeus, the evil demon. For it fell to Tobias, before all others who wanted her, to receive her as his inheritance. At that moment Tobit came back from the courtyard into his house, and Sarah daughter of Raguel came down from the upstairs room.
## Tobit's charge to his son
4:1 That day Tobit remembered the money he had left in Gabael's keeping at Rages in Media.
4:2 He said to himself, "Look, I have asked for death. Why not call my son Tobias and tell him about this money before I die?"
4:3 He called his son Tobias, and Tobias came to him. Tobit said to him, "See that I am buried well. Honor your mother, and never leave her alone for as long as she lives. Do what makes her glad, and never cause her spirit pain in any matter.
4:4 Remember her, son, because she faced many dangers for you while you were in her womb. When she dies, bury her beside me in the same grave.
4:5 All your days, son, keep the Lord in mind. Do not choose to sin or to break his commands. Do what is right every day of your life, and keep off the roads of injustice.
4:6 For those who act in truth will succeed in what they do, and so will all who do what is right.
## Give alms
4:7 Give alms from what you own, and do not give them with a grudging eye. Never turn your face from someone who is poor; then God will never turn his face from you.
4:8 If you have plenty, give alms in keeping with your plenty. If you have only a little, do not be afraid to give alms in keeping with that little.
4:9 By doing this you put away a good deposit for yourself, ready for the day of hard need.
4:10 For alms rescue from death and do not let a person go into the darkness.
4:11 To all who give alms, their gift is a fine offering before the Most High.
## Marry within the family
4:12 Guard yourself, son, from all sexual immorality. Above all, choose your wife from your own forefathers' family line. Do not marry an outsider, a woman who is not of your father's tribe, because we are the sons of the prophets. Noah, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were our forefathers from the beginning. Remember, son, that all of them married women from among their own kin. They were blessed in their children, and their descendants will inherit the land.
4:13 So now, son, love your kinsmen. Do not look down in your heart on your kinsmen, the sons and daughters of your people, and refuse to take a wife from among them. For pride brings ruin and much unrest, and idleness brings loss and great need, because idleness is the mother of hunger.
## Rules for living
4:14 When someone works for you, do not let his pay stay with you overnight; give it to him right away. If you serve God, you will be paid back. Watch yourself, son, in everything you do, and show good training in all your conduct.
4:15 Do not do to anyone what you hate. Do not drink wine until you are drunk, and do not let drunkenness travel with you on your way.
4:16 Share your bread with the hungry and your clothes with those who have none. Whatever you have left over, give as alms, and do not give alms with a grudging eye.
4:17 Pour out your bread on the grave of the righteous, but do not give it to sinners.
4:18 Ask every sensible person for advice, and never scorn advice that can help you.
4:19 At all times bless the Lord God, and ask him to make your ways straight and to let all your paths and plans succeed. For no nation has counsel, but the Lord himself gives all good things. Whomever the Lord wishes he brings low, down to the depths of Hades. So now, son, remember these commands, and do not let them be wiped from your heart.
## The money in Media
4:20 And now, son, I am telling you that I left ten talents of silver in the keeping of Gabael son of Gabri, at Rages in Media.
4:21 Son, do not be afraid that we have become poor. Many good things are yours if you fear God, keep far from every sin, and do what is good before the Lord your God."
5:1 Then Tobias answered his father Tobit, "Father, I will do everything you have told me.
5:2 But how can I collect it from him, when he and I have never met? What sign can I give him so that he will know who I am, trust me and give me the money? And I do not know the roads to Media, to get there."
5:3 Then Tobit answered his son Tobias, "He gave me a document in his own hand, and I gave him one in mine. I cut it in two, and each of us took one part, and I put one with the money. It is twenty years now since I left this money in his keeping. So now, son, find a man you can trust to travel with you. We will pay him wages until you come back. Go and get this money from him."
## A stranger at the door
5:4 Tobias went out to look for a man who knew the way and would travel with him to Media. Outside, he found Raphael the angel standing in front of him. Tobias had no idea that this was an angel of God.
5:5 Tobias asked him, "Where are you from, young man?" He answered, "I am one of the Israelites, your kinsmen, and I have come here to find work." Tobias said to him, "Do you know the way to Media?"
5:6 He said, "Yes. I have been there many times, and I know all the roads well. I have often traveled to Media and stayed with our kinsman Gabael, who lives in Rages in Media. From Ecbatana to Rages is a journey of two regular days, for Rages lies in the hill country and Ecbatana in the middle of the plain."
5:7 Tobias said to him, "Wait for me, young man, while I go in and tell my father. I need you to come with me, and I will pay you your wages."
5:8 He said, "All right, I will wait here. Only do not be long."
5:9 Tobias went in and told his father Tobit, "Look, I have found a man from among our kinsmen, the Israelites." Tobit said, "Call the man in to me, so that I can find out about his family and his tribe, and whether he can be trusted to travel with you, son." So Tobias went out and called him. "Young man," he said, "my father is calling for you."
## Tobit questions the guide
5:10 He went in, and Tobit greeted him first. The young man said to him, "I wish you much joy!" Tobit answered, "What joy do I still have? My eyes are useless. I cannot see the light of the sky. I lie in the dark like the dead, who never see the light anymore. I am alive, yet I live among the dead. I hear human voices, but I cannot see the people." The young man said, "Take heart. God is close to healing you. Take heart." Tobit said to him, "My son Tobias wants to go to Media. Can you go along with him and guide him? I will pay you your wages, brother." He answered, "I can travel with him. I know all the roads. I have often gone to Media and crossed all its plains, and I know its mountains and all its roads."
5:11 Tobit said to him, "Brother, what family are you from, and what tribe? Tell me, brother."
5:12 He said, "Why do you need to know my tribe?" Tobit answered, "I want to know the truth, brother: whose son you are and what your name is."
5:13 He said, "I am Azariah, son of the great Hananiah, one of your kinsmen."
5:14 Tobit said to him, "Welcome, and may you be safe and well, brother! Do not be hurt, brother, that I wanted to know the truth about your family. It turns out that you are a kinsman, from a good and noble family. I knew Hananiah and Nathan, the two sons of the great Semelias. They used to go with me to Jerusalem and worship there with me, and they did not go astray. Your kinsmen are good men. You come from a good root. Welcome, and joy to you!"
5:15 Then Tobit said, "I will pay you a drachma a day as wages, and give you what you need, just as I do for my son. Go with my son,
5:16 and I will add something more to your wages." He answered, "I will go with him. Do not be afraid. We will leave in good health and come back to you in good health, because the road is safe."
5:17 Tobit said to him, "God bless you, brother." Then he called his son and said, "Son, get ready what you need for the journey, and set out with your kinsman. May God in heaven bring you there safely and bring you back to me in good health. May his angel go with you and keep you safe, son." Tobias set out on his journey. He kissed his father and his mother, and Tobit said to him, "Go in good health."
## Anna weeps
5:18 Then his mother wept and said to Tobit, "Why did you send my child away? Isn't he the staff we lean on, the one who goes in and out in front of us?
5:19 Do not pile up money on money. Let it count as rubbish next to our child.
5:20 What the Lord has given us to live on is enough for us."
5:21 Tobit said to her, "Do not worry. Our child will go safely and come back to us safely. Your eyes will see him on the day he comes back to you in good health. Do not worry, and do not be afraid for them, my sister.
5:22 For a good angel will go with him. His journey will go well, and he will come back in good health."
6:1 And she stopped weeping and was quiet.
## The fish in the Tigris
6:2 The young man set out, and the angel went with him. The dog came out with him too and went along with them. The two of them traveled on until night came on them for the first time, and they camped by the Tigris River.
6:3 The boy went down to the Tigris River to wash his feet. A big fish jumped out of the water and tried to gulp down the boy's foot, and he shouted.
6:4 The angel said to the young man, "Grab the fish and hold on to it!" The young man got a grip on the fish and dragged it up onto dry land.
6:5 The angel said to him, "Split the fish open. Take out the gall, the heart and the liver, and put them aside to keep, and throw out the guts. This fish's gall, heart and liver make good medicine."
6:6 The young man split the fish open and saved the gall, the heart and the liver. He roasted part of the fish and ate it, and he salted the rest and kept it. Then the two of them traveled on together until they came near Media.
6:7 Then the young man asked the angel, "Brother Azariah, what medicine is there in the fish's heart and liver, and in its gall?"
6:8 He said to him, "Make smoke from the fish's heart and liver in front of any man or woman who is attacked by a demon or an evil spirit. Every attack will run from that person, and the spirits will never stay with him again.
6:9 And the gall is to rub on the eyes of a person who has white films over them. Blow on the films, and the eyes will become well."
## Raphael speaks of Sarah
6:10 When they had entered Media and were already coming near Ecbatana,
6:11 Raphael said to the young man, "Brother Tobias!" He answered, "Here I am." Raphael said to him, "Tonight we must stay at Raguel's house. The man is your relative, and he has a daughter named Sarah.
6:12 He has no son or other daughter, only Sarah. You are her closest kinsman, and of all men you have the first right to take her as your inheritance. It is also your right to inherit what her father owns. The girl is sensible, brave and very beautiful, and her father is a good man."
6:13 He went on, "You have the right to take her. Listen to me, brother. Tonight I will speak to her father about the girl, so that we may take her as your bride. When we come back from Rages, we will hold her wedding. I know that Raguel can by no means keep her from you or promise her to another man. He would deserve death under the ruling of the book of Moses, since he knows that the right to take his daughter as an inheritance belongs to you before any other man. So now listen to me, brother. Let us speak about the girl tonight and promise her to you in marriage. When we come back from Rages, we will take her and bring her home with us to your house."
## Tobias is afraid
6:14 Then Tobias answered Raphael, "Brother Azariah, I have heard that she has already been given to seven husbands, and they died in their bridal rooms. Each one died on the night he went in to her. I have also heard people say that a demon kills them.
6:15 So now I am afraid. The demon does not harm her, but he kills anyone who wants to come near her. I am my father's only son. If I die, I will bring my father and mother down to their grave, grieving over me. They have no other son to bury them."
6:16 Raphael said to him, "Have you forgotten what your father commanded? He told you to marry a woman from your father's house. Listen to me now, brother. Pay no attention to this demon, and marry her. I know that tonight she will be given to you as your wife.
6:17 When you go into the bridal room, take some of the fish's liver and the heart and put them on the ashes of the incense. The smell will spread,
6:18 and the demon will smell it and run away, and he will never be seen near her again. When you are about to lie with her, the two of you must first get up and pray. Beg the Lord of heaven to show you mercy and keep you safe. Do not be afraid, for she was allotted to you before the ages. You will save her, and she will go with you. And I take it you will have children by her, who will be like brothers to you. Do not worry." When Tobias heard Raphael's words and learned that Sarah was his kinswoman, from the family line of his father's house, he loved her deeply, and his heart clung to her.
