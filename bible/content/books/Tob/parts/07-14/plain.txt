## A welcome in Ecbatana
7:1 As they entered Ecbatana, Tobias said to him, "Brother Azariah, lead me directly to Raguel, our brother." He led him to Raguel's house, and there was Raguel, sitting at the door of his courtyard. They greeted him before he could greet them, and he answered, "A warm welcome, brothers! I am glad you have come safe and well." Then he brought them into his house.
7:2 He said to his wife Edna, "This young man is the image of my brother Tobit!"
7:3 Edna asked them, "Where are you from, brothers?" They told her, "We are of the people of Naphtali, the ones carried off as captives to Nineveh."
7:4 She said to them, "Do you know our brother Tobit?" They told her, "We know him." She said to them, "Is he well?"
7:5 They told her, "He is well, and he is alive." And Tobias said, "He is my father."
7:6 Raguel jumped up, kissed him and wept.
7:7 He said to him, "A blessing on you, my child, son of a fine and good father! What a cruel misfortune, that a righteous man who gave alms has gone blind!" He threw his arms around the neck of his kinsman Tobias and wept.
7:8 His wife Edna wept for him, and their daughter Sarah wept too.
## Sarah is given to Tobias
7:9 Raguel killed a ram from his flock and gave them a hearty welcome. After they had bathed and washed and lain down at the table for dinner, Tobias said to Raphael, "Brother Azariah, speak to Raguel, and have him give me my sister Sarah."
7:10 Raguel heard what he said, and he said to the young man, "Eat, drink and have a good time tonight. There is no man but you, brother, whom it is right for my daughter Sarah to marry. And I, for my part, am not free to give her to anyone else, since you are my closest kin. But I will be completely honest with you, my child.
7:11 I have given her to seven men from our own kin, and each one died the night he went in to her. Still, my child, eat and drink now; the Lord will see to you both." Tobias said, "I will not eat or drink a thing here until you settle my matter." Raguel answered, "I am doing it. She is yours by the ruling written in the book of Moses, and Heaven has decided that you should have her. Take your sister. From this moment you are her brother and she is your sister. She is given to you from today for all time. May the Lord of heaven grant you both a safe night, my child, and give you mercy and peace."
7:12 Raguel sent for his daughter Sarah, and she came to him. Taking her by the hand, he handed her over to Tobias and said, "Receive her as your wife, by the law and by the ruling written in the book of Moses. Have her, and bring her home to your father in good health. And may the God of heaven make your way smooth and give you peace."
7:13 Then he called her mother and had her bring a sheet for writing. On it he drew up a marriage contract: he was giving Sarah to Tobias as his wife, under the ruling of Moses' law.
7:14 After that they began to eat and drink.
## A mother's comfort
7:15 Raguel called his wife Edna. "Sister," he said, "prepare the second bedroom and bring her in there."
7:16 She did as he said: she spread the bed in the room and led Sarah in. She cried over her, then dried her tears and said to her,
7:17 "Take heart, daughter. The Lord of heaven give you gladness instead of your grief! Take heart, daughter." And she left the room.
## The demon flees
8:1 When the eating and drinking were over, they wanted to go to sleep. They took the young man away and brought him into the bedroom.
8:2 Tobias recalled what Raphael had told him. He pulled the fish's liver and heart out of his bag and laid them on the ashes of the incense.
8:3 The smell of the fish stopped the demon, and it fled up into the regions of Egypt. Raphael went after it, and there he shackled its feet and bound it fast without delay.
## The wedding-night prayer
8:4 They left and closed the door of the room behind them. Tobias rose from the bed and said to her, "Sister, stand up. Let us pray and plead with our Lord to show us mercy and keep us safe."
8:5 She stood up, and the two of them began to pray and beg to be kept safe. This is how he began: / "Blessed are you, God of our fathers, / and blessed is your name for all the ages to come. / May the heavens and everything you have made bless you for all ages.
8:6 You made Adam, / and for him you made Eve his wife, a helper to lean on. / From both of them came the offspring of humankind. / You said, 'It is not good for the man to be by himself. / Let us make him a helper like him.'
8:7 Now I am taking this sister of mine, not for sexual immorality, but in truth. / Command mercy for me and for her, / and let us grow old side by side."
8:8 Together they said, "Amen."
## A grave dug in the night
8:9 Then they slept the night through. Raguel, though, got up and called his servants, who went out with him and dug a grave.
8:10 For he said, "What if he dies? Then people will laugh at us and mock us."
8:11 Once the grave was dug, Raguel went back into the house and called his wife.
8:12 He said, "Have one of the maids go in and find out whether he is alive. If he has died, we will bury him so that nobody hears of it."
8:13 They sent the maid in. They lit the lamp and opened the door, and she went in and found the pair of them sleeping peacefully, side by side.
8:14 The maid came back out and let them know that he was alive and all was well.
## Raguel blesses God
8:15 They blessed the God of heaven and said: / "Blessed are you, God, with every pure blessing. / Let them bless you forever.
8:16 Blessed are you, because you have made me glad. / What I feared has not come about; / instead, in your great mercy, you have acted for us.
8:17 Blessed are you, because you had mercy on two only children. / Master, show them mercy and keep them safe, / and bring their lives to a close in gladness and mercy."
## Fourteen days of feasting
8:18 Then he told his servants to fill in the grave before morning came.
8:19 He told his wife to bake plenty of bread. He went out to the herd, brought back two oxen and four rams, and gave orders to have them made ready, and the cooking began.
8:20 Then he called Tobias and said to him, "For fourteen days you are not to stir from here. Stay right here with me, eating and drinking, and bring gladness back to my daughter, who has suffered so much grief.
8:21 Take half of all I have right now, and go back to your father in good health. The rest is yours and hers when my wife and I have died. Take heart, my child! I am your father now, and Edna is your mother. We are yours and your sister's from this day on, forever. Take heart, my child!"
## Raphael goes to Rages
9:1 Then Tobias called Raphael and said to him,
9:2 "Brother Azariah, take along four servants and two camels, and make the trip to Rages. When you reach Gabael, hand him the handwritten document and collect the money. Then bring Gabael himself back with you to the wedding.
9:3 You see the oath Raguel has sworn; I cannot go against it.
9:4 You know my father will count every day, and if I am one day late, it will hurt him deeply."
9:5 Raphael set out with the four servants and the two camels for Rages in Media, and they spent the night at Gabael's. Raphael handed him his handwritten document and told him the news about Tobias son of Tobit: he had taken a wife and was inviting Gabael to the wedding. Gabael got up and counted out the money bags in front of him, their seals unbroken, and they packed them together.
9:6 At dawn the two of them got up and went off together to the wedding. When they came into Raguel's home, Tobias was lying at the table. He sprang to his feet and greeted Gabael, who burst into tears, blessed him and said, "Fine, good young man, son of a fine, good man, righteous and a giver of alms! The Lord give heaven's blessing to you and to your wife, to your father, and to your wife's mother. Blessed be God, for I have seen my kinsman Tobias, the picture of his father!"
## Tobit and Anna wait
10:1 Meanwhile, day after day, Tobit kept reckoning the days: how many it would take Tobias to get there, and how many to come back. When the days were over and his son had not come,
10:2 he said, "Could he have been held up there? Or could Gabael have died, and now nobody will pay him the money?"
10:3 And he began to grieve.
10:4 His wife Anna said, "My child is lost! He is no longer alive." She began to weep and to wail over her son. She said,
10:5 "Woe is me, my child! I let you go, you, the light of my eyes."
10:6 But Tobit kept telling her, "Hush, sister, and do not fret. He is well. They must have run into some business there that kept them. The man traveling with him can be trusted, and he is one of our own relatives. Stop grieving for him, sister; any moment now he will be here."
10:7 She said to him, "Leave me alone, and do not deceive me. My child is lost." Day after day she ran out and kept scanning the road her son had gone by, and she listened to no one. At sunset she came indoors, and all night long she wailed and cried and got no sleep. When the fourteen days of the wedding feast that Raguel had sworn to hold for his daughter were over, Tobias went to him and said, "Send me on my way. I am sure my father and mother no longer believe they will see me again. Please, father, let me leave and go home to my father. I have told you already how things were when I left him."
## Raguel lets them go
10:8 Raguel said to Tobias, "Stay, my child. Stay with me. I am sending messengers off to your father Tobit, and they will let him know how you are."
10:9 But he said to him, "No. Please let me go from here to my father."
10:10 Then Raguel stood up and gave Tobias his wife Sarah, along with half of all his wealth: male and female servants, oxen and sheep, donkeys and camels, clothing, money and household goods.
10:11 He sent them off in good health. He embraced Tobias and said to him, "Be well, my child, and go in good health! The Lord of heaven make the road easy for you and your wife Sarah. I hope I may see your children before I die."
10:12 He said to his daughter Sarah, "Go to your father-in-law's house. From now on he and his wife are your parents, as truly as the ones who brought you into the world. Go in peace, daughter. While I live, may I hear only good things about you." He said goodbye to them and let them go. And Edna said to Tobias, "Child, my dear brother, the Lord bring you home again! Before I die, may I see children born to you and to my daughter Sarah. Before the Lord I hand my daughter over to you for safekeeping; never make her sad, as long as you live. Peace go with you, my child. I am your mother from now on, and Sarah is your sister. May every one of us prosper together for the rest of our days." Then she kissed both of them and sent them on their way in good health.
10:13 So Tobias went away from Raguel, well and glad, blessing the Lord of heaven and earth, the King of all, for giving him a good journey. He said to him, "May you succeed in honoring them all the days of their lives."
## The journey home
11:1 When they were getting close to Kaserin, across from Nineveh,
11:2 Raphael said, "You know the state your father was in when we left him.
11:3 Let the two of us hurry on before your wife and make the house ready while they are on their way."
11:4 So the two of them walked on together, and Raphael told him, "Take the gall in your hands." And the dog came along, following them.
11:5 Anna was sitting there, searching the road for any sign of her son.
11:6 She spotted him on his way and told his father, "Here comes your son, and the man who went with him!"
11:7 Before Tobias reached his father, Raphael said to him, "I am sure his eyes will be opened.
11:8 Rub the fish's gall into his eyes. The remedy will draw the white films together and flake them off his eyes. Your father will look up and see the light."
## Tobit sees again
11:9 Anna ran to her son and hugged him tightly. "I have seen you, my child," she said to him. "From now on I can die." And she wept.
11:10 Tobit rose and went out the courtyard door, tripping over his own feet. Tobias walked over to him,
11:11 holding the fish's gall. He blew into his eyes, held on to him, and said, "Take heart, father!" Then he put the remedy on him, and it began to work.
11:12 His eyes smarted, and he rubbed them.
11:13 Using both hands, he flaked the white films off from the corners of his eyes. Then Tobit hugged his son around the neck
11:14 and wept. He said to him, "I have seen you, my child, light of my eyes!" Then he said, / "Blessed be God, / blessed be his great name, / blessed be all his holy angels. / May his great name rest on us, / and may all the angels be blessed for all ages.
11:15 He struck me, / and look, I see my son Tobias!" Tobias went inside rejoicing and blessing God with his whole body. He told his father all about it: his journey had gone well, he had brought the money, he had married Sarah, Raguel's daughter, and she was on her way and close to the gate of Nineveh.
## Sarah welcomed in Nineveh
11:16 Glad and blessing God, Tobit went out to the gate of Nineveh to meet his son's bride. The people of Nineveh watched him walk by, striding along with all his strength and with nobody guiding him by the hand, and they were astonished.
11:17 Tobit gave thanks in front of them all that God had been merciful to him and had opened his eyes. He went up to Sarah, his son Tobias's wife, and blessed her: "May you come in safe and well, daughter! Blessed is your God, who brought you to us, daughter. Blessed is your father, blessed is Tobias my son, and blessed are you, daughter. Come into your own house in health, with blessing and gladness. Come in, daughter." On that day all the Jews in Nineveh were filled with joy.
11:18 Ahiqar and Nadab, his nephews, came to Tobit, full of joy.
11:19 Tobias's wedding was celebrated with gladness for seven days.
## The wages
12:1 When the wedding feast was over, Tobit called his son Tobias and said to him, "My child, make sure the man who traveled with you gets his pay, and add something extra to it."
12:2 He said to him, "Father, how much should I pay him? I lose nothing by giving him half of the goods he brought back with me.
12:3 He got me home safe and sound; he cured my wife; he came back with me, bringing the money; and he cured you. What more can I pay him?"
12:4 Tobit said to him, "It is only right, my child, that he should get half of everything he came back with."
12:5 So Tobias called him and said, "Take half of all you came back with as your pay, and go in good health."
## The angel's teaching
12:6 Then Raphael took the two of them aside where no one could hear and said to them, "Bless God. Before everyone alive, thank him for the good he has done you, and so bless and sing praise to his name. Tell all people about God's doings, and give them due honor. Never be slow to thank him.
12:7 A king's secret is best kept hidden, but God's works should be thanked for and brought into the open. Thank him as he deserves. Do what is good, and evil will not find you.
12:8 Prayer joined with truth is good, and so are alms joined with doing what is right; they are better than wealth joined with wrongdoing. Giving alms is better than storing up gold.
12:9 Alms rescue from death, and they cleanse away every sin. Those who give alms will be filled with life.
12:10 But those who commit sin and wrongdoing are at war with their own lives.
## I am Raphael
12:11 "I am going to tell you the whole truth and keep back nothing from you. I told you already: 'A king's secret is best kept hidden, but God's works are revealed with glory.'
12:12 Now listen: when you prayed, and Sarah too, I was the one who carried the memorial of your prayer before the Lord's glory. I did the same each time you buried the dead.
12:13 And when you did not hold back from rising and leaving your meal, but went off and wrapped the dead man for burial,
12:14 it was then that I was sent to put you to the test. God also sent me, at that same time, to heal you and Sarah, your son's wife.
12:15 I am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand in attendance and go in before the glory of the Lord."
12:16 Both of them were thrown into confusion; they fell on their faces in fear.
12:17 He said to them, "Do not be afraid. Peace be with you. Bless God forever.
12:18 When I was with you, it was not by any kindness of mine that I was with you; it was by God's will. Bless him all your days, and sing hymns to him.
12:19 Now you see that I never ate anything; you were seeing a vision.
12:20 So now bless the Lord on earth and thank God. I am going up to the one who sent me. Put in writing everything that has happened to you." And he went up.
12:21 They rose, but they were not able to see him anymore.
12:22 They went on blessing God and singing to him, and thanking him for these great works: an angel of God had appeared to them.
## Tobit's song of praise
13:1 Then Tobit said: / "Blessed be God, who lives forever, / and blessed be his kingdom.
13:2 For he strikes, and he shows mercy. / He brings down to Hades, to the lowest depths of the earth, / and he brings up from the great destruction. / There is nothing that slips out of his hand.
13:3 Give thanks to him, you Israelites, in front of the nations, / for he has scattered you among them,
13:4 and there he has shown you his greatness. / Lift him high before every living being, / because he is our Lord, / he is our God, he is our Father, / he is God forever and ever.
13:5 He will strike you for your wrongs, / and he will have mercy on you all, out of all the nations / where you have been scattered among them.
13:6 When you turn back to him with all your heart and all your being / and live truthfully before him, / then he will turn back to you / and never again hide his face from you. / So look at what he has done for you, / and thank him with your whole mouth. / Bless the Lord of righteousness; / lift high the King of the ages. / Here in the land where I am a captive I thank him, / and I make his strength and greatness known to a nation of sinners: / sinners, turn around and live justly before him; / who knows whether he will want you and show you mercy?
13:7 I lift high my God, / and my soul lifts high the King of heaven / and rejoices in his greatness.
13:8 Let everyone speak, / and let them give thanks to him in Jerusalem.
## Jerusalem rebuilt
13:9 Jerusalem, city of the Holy One, / he will strike you for the deeds of your children, / but he will again have mercy on the children of the righteous.
13:10 Give thanks to the Lord as he deserves, and bless the King of the ages, / and your tent will be built again in you with joy. / May he gladden every captive in you / and love every sufferer in you / and all the generations of all time.
13:11 A brilliant light will shine out to every corner of the earth. / Many nations will come from far off, / people from the ends of the earth, to your holy name. / They will hold gifts in their hands for the King of heaven. / Generations upon generations will shout for joy in you, / and the name of the chosen city, for all the ages.
13:12 A curse on all who speak harsh words! / A curse on all who tear you down and pull down your walls, / who knock over your towers and burn your homes! / But all who fear you will be blessed for all time.
13:13 Then set out, and shout for joy over the children of the righteous, / because all of them will be gathered in / and will bless the Lord of the ages.
13:14 Happy are those who love you, / and happy those who will be glad at your peace. / Happy are all people who grieve over you, / over all the blows you suffered, / for they will rejoice in you / and see all your joy forever.
13:15 My soul blesses the Lord, the great King,
13:16 for Jerusalem will be built, / the city that is his house, for all the ages. / I will count myself happy if some of my offspring survive / to see your glory and thank the King of heaven. / Jerusalem's gates will be made of sapphire and emerald, / and every wall of yours of precious stone. / Jerusalem's towers will be made of gold, / their battlements of pure gold.
13:17 Jerusalem's streets will be set like a mosaic / with red gems and with stone of Ophir.
13:18 Jerusalem's gates will sing out songs of gladness, / and every house in her will cry, / 'Hallelujah! Blessed be the God of Israel!' / And those who are blessed will bless the holy name for ever and beyond."
## Tobit's last words
14:1 Here Tobit's words of thanksgiving were finished. He died in peace, 112 years old, and was given an honorable burial in Nineveh.
14:2 His eyes had failed him at sixty-two. Once his sight came back, he lived in good times and gave alms, and he kept on blessing God and thanking him for his greatness.
14:3 When he was dying, he called his son Tobias and gave him these orders: "My child, take your children
14:4 and get away quickly to Media. For I trust God's word against Nineveh, the things Nahum spoke: all of it will come true, and it will fall on Assyria and Nineveh. Everything that the prophets of Israel, whom God sent, have spoken will happen. Not one of all their words will fall short, and everything will take place at its proper time. There will be more safety in Media than in Assyria and in Babylon. Therefore I know and believe that everything God has said will be fulfilled and will happen, and not one word of what he said will fail. Our kinsmen who live in the land of Israel will all be counted and taken captive from the good land. The whole land of Israel will be a wasteland. Samaria and Jerusalem will be a wasteland, and the house of God will be in mourning and will be burned for a time.
14:5 Yet God will show them mercy once more and lead them back to the land of Israel. They will build the house again, though it will not equal the first, until the appointed times run their full course. After that they will all come back from captivity and build Jerusalem again with honor, and God's house will be built in it, as Israel's prophets foretold.
14:6 All the nations in the whole world will turn and truly fear God. They will all give up their idols, which led them astray with their lies,
14:7 and they will bless the eternal God in righteousness. In those days every Israelite who is saved and keeps God faithfully in mind will be gathered in. They will come to Jerusalem and live securely for all time in Abraham's land, and it will be given into their hands. Those who love God in truth will be glad, while those who do sin and wrong will disappear from the whole earth.
14:8 And you, my child, get out of Nineveh; do not stay on here.
14:9 Now, children, here is my command to you: be God's servants in truth, and do what he finds pleasing. Teach your children too that they must do what is right and give alms, keep God in mind, and bless his name at every time, truthfully and with all their strength.
14:10 The day you bury your mother next to me, do not stay the night anywhere within its borders. I can see that it is full of wrongdoing and that deceit is everywhere in it, and no one there is ashamed. Think, my child, about how Nadab treated Ahiqar, the man who brought him up. Was Ahiqar not put down into the earth while he was still alive? But God threw the disgrace back in Nadab's face. Ahiqar came out into the light, while Nadab went down into darkness forever, because he had tried to murder Ahiqar. Because Ahiqar gave alms, he escaped from the deadly snare Nadab had laid for him; but Nadab fell into that snare himself, and it was the end of him.
14:11 So look, my children, at what alms do, and at what wrongdoing does: it kills. And now my life is ebbing away." They laid him on his bed, and he died. And he was buried with honor.
## Tobias in Ecbatana
14:12 After his mother died, Tobias laid her to rest with his father. He and his wife then left for Media, and he made his home in Ecbatana with Raguel, his father-in-law.
14:13 He cared for them with honor in their old age, and he buried them in Ecbatana in Media. He inherited the household of Raguel and that of his father Tobit.
14:14 He died with honor at the age of 117.
14:15 Before he died, he saw and heard of the destruction of Nineveh. He saw its captives being led away to Media, the captives that Achiacharus king of Media had taken. He blessed God for everything he had done to the people of Nineveh and Assyria. Before his death he rejoiced over Nineveh, and he blessed the Lord God for ever and ever. Amen.
