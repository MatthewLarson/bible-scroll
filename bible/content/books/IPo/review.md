# Hostile review: Ignatius to Polycarp (IPo), flat, 1:0 + 21 verses

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-13. Checked: plain.txt against `texts/orig/IPo.json` and Lake's printed page (local OCR, `.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers01lake.txt`); notes, variants, chapters and book.json against Lightfoot's apparatus and notes (`.cache/dl/lightfoot-ign/p2v2.txt`, OCR; its sigla are garbled in places, so each variant point below is stated as "Lightfoot's apparatus appears to read" where the OCR is unclear); every Bible, Ignatius, Polycarp and Martyrdom quotation against the site's own plain.txt; the Catholic Encyclopedia (newadvent.org) and Eusebius (newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm) by web fetch. `node tools/build.js --check IPo`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

A careful, honest book. The Plain English Translation is faithful to Lake's Greek verse by verse (no mistranslation, no softening that changes meaning, key terms agree with the published IEp, IRo, ISm and with `proposals/works/terms.json`); every Lightfoot and Lake quotation I opened is accurate; the Bible quotations match the site's wording. The ὑπηρέται "assistants" choice is right (see finding 14). No invented source, reading or fragment found, so nothing critical. The real problems are (1) the variants file leaves out several sense-changing readings that Lightfoot's apparatus records, (2) the 4:3 slavery note and the 5:2 marriage notes are one-sided or make claims no opened source supports, (3) a factual slip about Polycarp's age that is also in the published topic, and (4) canon rows for Catholic, Orthodox and Lutheran churches that rest on encyclopedia or district pages rather than the churches' own documents.

## Findings

### Major

1. **Variants file omits readings Lightfoot flags that change the sense.** `variants.json` (8 entries). Lightfoot's apparatus (OCR) appears to give, with Σ the Syriac abridgment and A the Armenian:
   - 6:2: "τὸ βάπτισμα ... περικεφαλαία": Σ and A read "faith ... as arms, and hope as a helmet" (baptism, love and the spear are not there). The most notable difference in the letter, and the Syriac abridgment is a named witness in the book's own reliability section.
   - 1:3 ὁμοήθειαν θεοῦ: G reads βοήθειαν ("help"), L "consuetudinem", Σ/A "will"; Lightfoot discusses it at length (the note is already quoted in notes.json 10, which does not mention the variant).
   - 6:1 "μετ᾽ αὐτῶν ... ἐν θεῷ": g, Σ, A read "παρὰ θεῷ" ("with God"); Lightfoot judges παρὰ θεῷ "the correct reading".
   - 1:2 τόπον (Σ/A "things becoming"; Lightfoot discusses it, and it bears on note 7) and παρακαλεῖν (Σ "pray for all"); 2:3 θέμα (g θέλημα; Σ/A "promise"); 5:2 πλέον (Σ "except", g πλήν, which is why Lake's English has "except to the bishop"); 2:1 λοιμοτέρους (Σ/A "bad"), ἐμβροχαῖς (Σ/A "gentleness"); 7:3 σύντονον (g σύντομον); 8:1 ἐπεί GA / ἐπεὶ οὖν Lg.
   - brief-ipo.md names 2:2 (ὁ om. G) and 8:1 (ἐπεί / ἐπεὶ οὖν) as entries; neither is a variant entry (2:2 is only in a note).
   Fix: add entries for at least 6:2, 1:3, 6:1, 1:2 (τόπον), 2:3 (θέμα), 5:2 (πλέον/πλήν), 8:1 and 2:2, each with witnesses as Lightfoot gives them and a one-line reason for following Lake. Re-read the page of Lightfoot's apparatus for each; do not rely on this OCR's sigla.

2. **4:3 slavery note and deep note (notes.json 22; chapters.json ch. 4 `deep`; topic `slavery`) are one-sided and under-sourced.**
   - They say "Paul too says 'if you are able to become free, make use of it' (1 Corinthians 7:21)" as settled. The site's own 1 Corinthians 7:21 note ("'Rather use': use what?") says the Greek has no object and can mean "use your slavery". Lightfoot, the writer's only source here, pairs Ignatius's πλέον δουλευέτωσαν with 1 Timothy 6:2 and with 1 Corinthians 7:21 "according to one, though not the most probable, interpretation", i.e. he hears the "stay a slave" sense. The note gives only the reading that helps its argument.
   - "Zahn, he reports, took it as freedom granted 'by the community'": Lightfoot reports it and then rejects it with a reason (with ἐλευθεροῦσθαι one would expect ὑπὸ τοῦ κοινοῦ, not ἀπὸ). The reason is omitted; the reader is left to think it is an open choice.
   - "The verse shows that churches did sometimes buy slaves their freedom", repeated in chapter 4 `views.H` ("shows a church fund that could buy slaves their freedom") and in the `slavery` topic, is Lightfoot's inference from the one reading he prefers. Say so.
   - "The verse has been read in more than one way: as a sign that the early church accepted slavery as it found it, and as evidence that churches were already spending money..." credits two views to nobody. The brief required "how the churches and scholars read it, each from a source opened"; only Lightfoot (and Zahn at second hand) is opened. Name a second source that was actually read, or cut the sentence and say the site gives Lightfoot's reading only.
   - Note 22 says only "Lake's margin gives 1 Timothy 6:2" without saying what is echoed (Lightfoot: Paul's "serve them better still" to slaves with believing masters, which Ignatius turns to all slaves).

3. **5:2 views notes (notes.json 25, 26; chapters.json ch. 5 `readings` and `deep`) assert things no opened source supports.** The decision not to write a card is acceptable under the brief (church documents not opened; a `views` note was the allowed alternative). But the notes then do what the brief says not to do without documents:
   - Note 26 `p`: "The first known Christian rule tying marriage to the church's leader". "First known" is credited to no opened source (Lightfoot refers to Probst and Bingham on "the sanction given by the Church ... in the early ages" and does not call this the first).
   - Chapter 5 `deep` last paragraph: "Churches today differ on how far the church must approve or witness a marriage." No church document is cited for any church. Either remove it, or say plainly that the site has not opened the churches' own rules on this and does not state them.
   - Chapter 5 `readings` ("Read as pastoral counsel" / "Read as an early rule of church order"): "On this reading ..." with no holder named. Name who reads it each way from an opened source (Lightfoot's "approval" and the Catholic Encyclopedia's "religious character of matrimony" fit the two) or label them as the site's own framing.
   - The only Catholic voice is the 1910 Catholic Encyclopedia, a reference work, not a church document; it only cites "Polycarp 5" in a list (verified at newadvent.org/cathen/07644a.htm: the quoted phrases are the article's own summaries). Say it is that.

4. **Polycarp "burned ... at eighty-six" is wrong as stated (notes.json 1, `p`/`b`; also the published `polycarp` topic lead).** Martyrdom of Polycarp 9:3 (the site's text): "For eighty-six years I have been his slave": the years he served Christ, not his age. The Martyrdom (as the site's MPo has it) also has him killed after the fire failed to consume him, so "burned" is incomplete. The Catholic Encyclopedia Ignatius article does not give either claim (checked). Fix IPo note 1 now ("put to death at Smyrna, most probably in AD 155; at his trial he said he had served Christ eighty-six years"); and flag the same wording in `content/topics/polycarp.json` (lead) to the lead. Also "most probably AD 155": Lightfoot says "A.D. 155 or 156" (smyr note on Alce, p2v2.txt line 34445) and the topic's LCMS paragraph gives 156; match the source.

5. **Canon rows sourced to something other than the church's own document (book.json `canon.accepted_by`).**
   - Catholic row: the Trent decree (Catholic's own), the USCCB calendar, and the Catholic Encyclopedia for what "the Catholic Church" says of chapter 5. The encyclopedia is not a church document.
   - Eastern Orthodox row: the statement that the Orthodox churches "have a New Testament identical with the Catholic" is Reid in the Catholic Encyclopedia (1908). An Orthodox canon claim sourced to a Catholic reference work is the pattern the Apostolic Fathers lessons forbid. The OCA calendar entry supports only the feast and Polycarp's letter, not the canon.
   - LCMS row: `lcms-southern-ignatius` is a district page ("a district page, not a synod document", per its own source note; the build.md items list says the Lutheran Service Book calendar was not found).
   These are inherited from ISm and the other published letters, so fix together at alignment, but each row here must say what its source is (a reference work, a district page) or be sourced to the church's own document.

### Minor

6. **"Polycarp's own letter shows he took up the task" / "shows the plan went ahead" (chapters.json ch. 7 `views.H`; notes.json 35; book.json section 4 "shows the request reached Philippi").** Polycarp to the Philippians 13:1 (site text) says only: send your letter to Syria if anyone goes; "I will do this if I get a good opportunity, either myself or someone I send as an envoy." It shows Polycarp expected to send someone, not that the council met or the envoy went. Lightfoot says Polycarp "refers to this intended delegate". Reword to "Polycarp's letter shows he expected to send an envoy".

7. **Anti-docetic reading of 3:2 stated as fact (notes.json 19; chapters.json ch. 3 `deep`).** "The chain answers the same teachers as Ignatius to the Smyrnaeans 1-3" and "answer those who said Christ suffered only in appearance". Lightfoot (p2v2.txt ~34610) says there is only "a passing allusion expressed in general terms (§ 3)" to the heresy of Smyrnaeans, and cites Melito, Irenaeus and Gregory for the pairs of opposites as a stock contrast. The reading is widely held, but label it as the usual reading (and name a source opened) rather than the text's own claim.

8. **Quotation not exact: "our hands have touched" (1 John 1:1)** in notes.json 19 and chapters.json ch. 3 `deep`. The site's 1 John 1:1 reads "our hands touched". Quote it as the site has it.

9. **Margin references.** (a) `texts/src/IPo.app.json` puts 1 Timothy 6:2 "at 4:2"; the note at 4:3 is right (Lightfoot ties the echo to πλέον δουλευέτωσαν in 4:3). The margin position cannot be read from the OCR text; check the scan page and add a fix to `tools/lake-fixes.json` if it sits at 4:3. (b) The 5:1 margin: the digital file has "Eph. 3, 15. 20"; the scan OCR reads "Eph.5 25. 30". Note 24 is titled "Ephesians 5:25 and 5:29" and says "Lake's margin points to Ephesians 5"; Lightfoot's reminiscence is Eph 5:29. Say the margin gives 5:25 and 5:30 (if the scan confirms) and Lightfoot says 5:29, or fix `lake-fixes.json`.

10. **USCCB row carried from ISm without a fresh look (book.json Catholic row; source `usccb-calendar-2026-ignatius`).** Flagged as the writer admitted. The source note says ISm's writer opened the PDF on 2026-11-12, so the row is not unsupported; but I could not open it (the PDF URL I tried returned 404) and so could not confirm the exact title in quotation marks. Independent check: web search and the Universalis US calendar both give 17 October, Saturday of week 28, Memorial, for Ignatius of Antioch, Bishop and Martyr. The `c.107` in the Common Worship row I could confirm only through secondary Church of England pages. Keep the dates; either confirm the quoted titles against the PDF/CW pages in this session or drop the quotation marks.

11. **Chapter `settings` and `readers` thin (chapters.json).** Only chapters 1, 6 and 7 have `settings`; 1:1-5:2 is one block. The brief asked for `readers` by evidence naming the widows, slaves, married people and the continent (4-5) and the households greeted (8:2-3); none appears. The same Lightfoot point ("not private", meant for the church) is used in two `readers` fields (1:0 and 1:1-5:2). Add `settings` for 4:1-3 and 5:1-2 and for the greetings of 8:2-3.

12. **Unmarked supplied words in the Plain English Translation.** 8:2 "the wife of the procurator": the Greek is τὴν τοῦ Ἐπιτρόπου with no noun; Lightfoot prefers "widow". 8:1 "as the will of God requires": the Greek has only τὸ θέλημα. Both are explained in notes (41, 39), so this is not an error, but the gloss for "the procurator" should say "wife supplied", and the verse lines could mark the supplied word as the site does elsewhere.

13. **6:1 συγκοιμᾶσθε, συνεγείρεσθε ("rest together, rise together").** Lightfoot says these refer to the trainer's set hours for bed and rising and that any "death and resurrection" sense "seems altogether out of place" (p2v2.txt ~37530). The note (28) and gloss leave the reader free to hear the resurrection. Add Lightfoot's one sentence.

14. **ὑπηρέται "assistants" vs the brief's "servants" (asked specifically): the writer is right.** The brief's own key-terms line says "assistants and servants" but also that ὑπηρέτης stays apart from δοῦλος (lead decision 10); `proposals/works/terms.json` records "assistants" as the site's 1 Corinthians 4:1 (verified: "assistants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries"; Acts 13:5 also "assistant"). Lake has "servants", which the note could mention. Small points: note 29 says "the site's 'assistants' for hypēretai", but the site's Luke 1:2 has "servants of the message"; and πάρεδροι "those who sit at his side" has no gloss (Lightfoot "assessors").

15. **Small wording and drift items.** 2:1 "bring ... into line" for ὑπότασσε softens "bring into subjection" (Lake "bring to subjection"); consider "bring them under control". `book.json` audience says Polycarp "received Ignatius during his long stay there": Lightfoot says "his stay"; "long" is not in the source.

16. **Source coverage.** `book.json` rejected_why says Eusebius's survey of books at 3.25 does not mention Ignatius. True (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm: the accepted, disputed and rejected lists do not name him), but `eusebius-greek-lake-3` records only 3.36 and 4.23 as read. Add 3.25 to the source note, with the date it was read.

17. **Style nit.** notes.json 31 contains one em dash inside Lightfoot's quotation ("Ephes. vi. 13—17"); use a hyphen or "13-17" in the quotation.
