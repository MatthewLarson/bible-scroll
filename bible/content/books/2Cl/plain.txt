## Think of Christ as of God
1:1 Brothers, we must think of Jesus Christ as we think of God, as the Judge of the living and the dead. And we must not make little of our salvation.
1:2 For if we think little of him, we also hope to receive little. Those who listen as if these were little things are sinning, and we are sinning too, when we do not know where we were called from, by whom, and to what place, and how much Jesus Christ endured to suffer for our sake.
1:3 So what will we give him in return? What fruit could be worthy of what he has given us? And how much do we owe him in holy living?
1:4 For he granted us the light. Like a father, he addressed us as sons. We were perishing, and he saved us.
1:5 So what sort of praise will we give him, or what payment in return for what we have received?
1:6 Our minds were maimed. We bowed down to stones and wood, gold, silver and bronze, things people had made, and our whole life was nothing but death. We were wrapped in darkness and our eyes were full of mist. But we have received our sight again: by his will we have thrown off the cloud that was wrapped around us.
1:7 For he took pity on us. His heart went out to us, and he saved us, when he saw how far we had wandered, how ruined we were, and that we had no hope of salvation except the hope that comes from him.
1:8 For he called us before we existed. He willed us to come to be out of nothing.
## The barren woman and her children
2:1 "Be glad, barren woman, you who bear no children! Break out and cry aloud, you who have no labor pains! For the children of the desolate woman are many, more than those of the woman who has a husband." When he said, "Be glad, barren woman, you who bear no children," he meant us. For our church was barren before children were given to her.
2:2 And when he said, "Cry aloud, you who have no labor pains," he means this: we should lift up our prayers to God with a single heart, and not lose heart like women in labor.
2:3 And when he said, "For the children of the desolate woman are many, more than those of the woman who has a husband," he meant that our people seemed desolate, abandoned by God. But now that we have believed, we have become more than those who seemed to have God.
2:4 And another Scripture also says, "I did not come to call righteous people. I came to call sinners."
2:5 He means that those who are perishing must be saved.
2:6 For the great and wonderful thing is not to hold up what is standing, but what is falling.
2:7 In the same way, Christ also willed to save what was perishing. And he saved many, when he came and called us, who were already perishing.
## Confess him by what you do
3:1 He has shown us such great mercy. First of all, we who are living do not sacrifice to dead gods or bow down to them. Instead, through him we have come to know the Father of truth. What is knowing him, then, if not this: not denying the one through whom we came to know him?
3:2 He himself says too, "Whoever confesses me in front of people, I will confess him in front of my Father."
3:3 So this is our reward: that we confess the one through whom we were saved.
3:4 But how do we confess him? By doing what he says and not ignoring his commandments, and by honoring him not only with our lips, but with our whole heart and our whole mind.
3:5 He also says in Isaiah, "This people gives me honor with their lips, but their heart is far away from me."
4:1 So let us not just call him Lord, for that will not save us.
4:2 For he says, "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will be saved, but the one who does what is right."
4:3 So then, brothers, let us confess him by what we do: by loving one another, by not committing adultery, not speaking against one another, not being jealous, but being self-controlled, merciful and good. We ought to feel one another's pain, and not love money. These are the deeds by which we should confess him, not their opposites.
4:4 And we must not fear people more than God.
4:5 That is why, if you do such things, the Lord said, "Even if you are gathered with me in my embrace, if you do not do my commandments, I will throw you out and say to you, 'Go away from me. I do not know you or where you are from, you workers of lawlessness.'"
## Lambs among wolves
5:1 So, brothers, let us leave behind our stay as resident aliens in this world, and do the will of the one who called us. Let us not be afraid to leave this world.
5:2 For the Lord says, "You will be like lambs surrounded by wolves."
5:3 Peter answered him, "But what if the wolves tear the lambs to pieces?"
5:4 Jesus said to Peter, "The lambs should not fear the wolves once they are dead. And you, do not fear those who kill you and can do nothing more to you. Fear the one who, after you die, has authority over soul and body, to throw them into the fire of Gehenna."
5:5 Know this, brothers: our stay in this world, in this flesh, is small and brief. But the promise of Christ is great and wonderful: rest in the kingdom to come, and eternal life.
5:6 What must we do to gain these things, then? Only this: live holy and righteous lives, treat the things of this world as belonging to someone else, and not want them.
5:7 For when we want to get these things, we fall away from the right road.
## Two ages, two masters
6:1 And the Lord says, "No household slave can be a slave to two masters." If we want to be slaves both to God and to wealth, it does us no good.
6:2 For "what is the gain, if someone wins the whole world but loses his soul?"
6:3 This age and the age to come are two enemies.
6:4 This age calls for adultery, corruption, love of money and deceit. The other one says goodbye to them.
6:5 So we cannot be friends of both. We must say goodbye to this one and keep company with the other.
6:6 We think it is better to hate the things here, because they are small, brief and perishable, and to love the things there, the good things that do not perish.
6:7 For if we do Christ's will, we will find rest. If not, nothing will rescue us from eternal punishment, if we ignore his commandments.
6:8 And the Scripture also says in Ezekiel, "Even if Noah and Job and Daniel stand up, they will not rescue their children in the captivity."
6:9 If even such righteous men cannot rescue their children by their own righteous deeds, what confidence will we have of entering God's palace, if we do not keep our baptism pure and unstained? Or who will be our advocate, if we are not found to have holy and righteous works?
## The contest and the crown
7:1 So then, my brothers, let us compete. We know that the contest is close at hand, and that many sail in for contests that perish. But not all are crowned, only those who have trained hard and competed well.
7:2 So let us compete, that all of us may be crowned.
7:3 So let us run the straight course, the contest that does not perish. Let many of us sail to it and compete, so that we too may be crowned. And if we cannot all be crowned, let us at least come close to the crown.
7:4 We must know this: anyone who competes in a contest that perishes and is caught cheating is whipped, taken away and thrown out of the stadium.
7:5 What do you think? What will happen to someone who cheats in the contest of incorruption?
7:6 For of those who have not kept the seal, he says, "Their worm will not die, and their fire will not be put out, and they will be a spectacle to all flesh."
## Repent while there is time
8:1 So while we are on earth, let us repent.
8:2 For we are clay in the craftsman's hand. Think of a potter. If he is making a pot and it gets bent or cracked in his hands, he shapes it again. But once he has put it into the fiery kiln, he can no longer do anything for it. It is the same with us. While we are in this world, let us repent with our whole heart of the evil things we have done in the flesh, so that we may be saved by the Lord while we have time for repentance.
8:3 For after we have left the world, we can no longer confess our sins or repent there.
8:4 So, brothers, if we do the Father's will, keep the flesh pure and obey the Lord's commandments, we will receive eternal life.
8:5 For the Lord says in the gospel, "If you did not keep what is small, who will give you what is great? For I tell you, someone who is faithful in the smallest things is faithful in big things as well."
8:6 So this is what he means: keep the flesh pure and the seal spotless, so that we may receive eternal life.
## This flesh will rise
9:1 And none of you should say that this flesh is not judged and does not rise.
9:2 Think about it. In what were you saved? In what did you receive your sight, if not while you were in this flesh?
9:3 So we must guard the flesh as a temple of God.
9:4 For just as you were called in the flesh, you will also come in the flesh.
9:5 If Christ, the Lord who saved us, was spirit at first and became flesh, and called us in that way, then we too will receive our reward in this flesh.
9:6 So let us love one another, so that we may all come into God's kingdom.
9:7 While we have time to be healed, let us give ourselves over to God, our healer, and pay him back.
9:8 Pay him how? By repenting from a sincere heart.
9:9 For he knows all things before they happen, and he knows what is in our hearts.
9:10 So let us give him praise, not only from our mouths but from our hearts, so that he may welcome us as sons.
9:11 For the Lord said, "My brothers are those who do the will of my Father."
## Run after goodness
10:1 So, my brothers, let us do the will of the Father who called us, so that we may live. Let us rather run after goodness, and leave behind wickedness, which leads the way to our sins. And let us run away from godlessness, so that evil does not overtake us.
10:2 For if we work hard at doing good, peace will run after us.
10:3 This is why a person cannot find peace: people bring in human fears, and choose the pleasure that is here over the promise that is coming.
10:4 For they do not know how much torment the pleasure here brings, or what delight the promise to come holds.
10:5 If they did these things only by themselves, it could be put up with. But as it is, they keep on teaching evil to innocent souls. They do not know that they will face a double judgment, they and those who listen to them.
## Do not be double-minded
11:1 So let us serve God with a clean heart, and we will be righteous. But if we do not serve him, because we do not believe God's promise, we will be miserable.
11:2 For the prophetic word also says, "Miserable are the double-minded, those who waver in their heart and say, 'We heard these things long ago, back in our fathers' days, too. But we have waited day after day and have seen none of them.'
11:3 You fools! Compare yourselves to a tree. Take a vine. First it sheds its leaves, then a shoot comes, and after that a sour grape, and then a ripe cluster.
11:4 In the same way my people have had turmoil and trouble. After that they will receive the good things."
11:5 So, my brothers, let us not be double-minded. Let us hope and hold out, so that we may also receive the reward.
11:6 For the one who made the promise is trustworthy: he will pay each person back for his works.
11:7 So if we do what is right before God, we will enter his kingdom and receive the promises "that no ear has heard and no eye has seen, and that have not come up into a human heart."
## When the two are one
12:1 So let us watch for God's kingdom every hour, with love and righteousness, because we do not know the day God will appear.
12:2 For when someone asked the Lord himself when his kingdom would come, he said, "When the two are one, and the outside is like the inside, and the male with the female is neither male nor female."
12:3 Now the two are one when we speak the truth to one another, and one soul lives in two bodies with no pretending.
12:4 And "the outside like the inside" means this: by "the inside" he means the soul, and by "the outside" the body. So just as your body can be seen, let your soul also be plain to see in your good works.
12:5 And "the male with the female, neither male nor female" means this: a brother who sees a sister should have no thought of her as female, and she should have no thought of him as male.
12:6 "When you do these things," he says, "my Father's kingdom will come."
## The name insulted among the Gentiles
13:1 So, brothers, let us repent now at last. Let us keep a clear head for what is good, for we are full of much foolishness and wickedness. Let us wipe our past sins off ourselves and be saved by repenting from the soul. Let us not become people-pleasers. And let us not want to please only ourselves, but also the people outside, by our righteousness, so that the name is not insulted because of us.
13:2 For the Lord says, "My name is constantly insulted among all the Gentiles," and again, "Woe to the one because of whom my name is insulted." How is it insulted? When you do not do what I want.
13:3 For when the Gentiles hear God's sayings from our mouths, they marvel at them as beautiful and great. Then they learn that our deeds are not worthy of the words we speak, and from there they turn to insults, saying it is some myth and delusion.
13:4 For when they hear from us that God says, "It is no credit to you if you love those who love you. It is a credit to you if you love your enemies and those who hate you," when they hear this, they marvel at such overflowing goodness. But when they see that we do not love those who hate us, and do not even love those who love us, they laugh at us, and the name is insulted.
## The church before the sun and moon
14:1 So, brothers, if we do the will of God our Father, we will belong to the first church, the spiritual one, which was created before the sun and moon. But if we do not do the Lord's will, we will come under the Scripture that says, "My house has become a cave for bandits." So let us choose to belong to the church of life, so that we may be saved.
14:2 I do not think you are unaware that the living church is the body of Christ. For the Scripture says, "God made the human being male and female." The male is Christ, the female is the church. And the Books and the apostles also say that the church does not belong to the present, but has been there from the beginning. For she was spiritual, as our Jesus also was, but he was revealed in the last days to save us.
14:3 The church, being spiritual, was revealed in the flesh of Christ. This shows us that if any of us keeps her in the flesh and does not corrupt her, he will receive her back in the Holy Spirit. For this flesh is the copy of the spirit, and no one who has corrupted the copy will share in the original. So this is what he means, brothers: keep the flesh, so that you may share in the spirit.
14:4 Now if we say that the flesh is the church and the spirit is Christ, then whoever has abused the flesh has abused the church. Such a person, then, will not share in the spirit, which is Christ.
14:5 So great is the life and incorruption that this flesh can share in, when the Holy Spirit is joined to it. No one can speak it out or tell of "what the Lord has prepared" for his chosen.
## Self-control and bold prayer
15:1 I do not think the advice I have given about self-control is a small thing. Whoever follows it will not regret it, but will save both himself and me, the one who gave the advice. For it is no small reward to turn back a soul that is wandering and perishing, so that it is saved.
15:2 For this is the payment we can give back to God who created us: that the one who speaks and the one who listens both speak and listen with faith and love.
15:3 So let us stand firm, righteous and holy, in what we have believed, so that we may ask boldly of God, who says, "While you are still speaking, I will say, 'Look, I am here.'"
15:4 For this saying is a sign of a great promise. The Lord says he is more ready to give than the one who asks is to ask.
15:5 Since we share in such great kindness, let us not begrudge ourselves the gaining of such good things. For these words bring as much joy to those who have done them as they bring condemnation to those who have ignored them.
## The day burning like an oven
16:1 So, brothers, since we have been given no small opportunity to repent, let us turn back to God who called us while we have time, while we still have one who is ready to welcome us.
16:2 For if we say goodbye to these pleasures, and conquer our own soul by not doing its evil desires, we will share in the mercy of Jesus.
16:3 You know that the day of judgment is already coming, burning like an oven. Some of the heavens will melt, and the whole earth will be like lead melting in the fire. And then the secret and open deeds of people will be brought to light.
16:4 So almsgiving is good, as repentance for sin is. Fasting is better than prayer, but almsgiving is better than both. And "love lays a cover over a great number of sins," while prayer from a good conscience rescues from death. Blessed is everyone who is found full of these things, for almsgiving lightens the weight of sin.
## Keep the elders' warnings at home
17:1 So let us repent with our whole heart, so that none of us is lost along the way. For if we have commandments to do this too, to pull people away from idols and teach them, how much more must a soul that already knows God not be lost!
17:2 So let us help one another to lead the weak upward toward what is good, so that we may all be saved. Let us turn one another around and warn one another.
17:3 And let us not seem to believe and pay attention only now, while the elders are warning us. When we have gone home, too, let us remember the Lord's commands. Let us not be dragged off course by worldly desires, but let us come here more often and try to make progress in the Lord's commandments, so that we may all think the same way and be gathered together for life.
17:4 For the Lord said, "I am coming to gather all the nations, tribes and languages." By this he means the day of his appearing, when he will come and redeem us, each one according to his works.
17:5 The unbelievers will see his glory and his power, and they will be astonished when they see the kingship of the world in Jesus' hands. They will say, "Woe to us! It was you, and we did not know it. We did not believe, and we did not obey the elders who told us about our salvation." And "their worm will not die, and their fire will not be put out, and they will be a spectacle to all flesh."
17:6 He means that day of judgment, when they will see those among us who lived godless lives and twisted the commandments of Jesus Christ.
17:7 But the righteous, who have done good, endured torments and hated the soul's pleasures, will see how those who went astray and denied Jesus by their words or their deeds are punished with terrible torments in a fire that is never put out. Then they will give glory to their God, saying, "There will be hope for the one who has served God with his whole heart."
## The preacher's own confession
18:1 So let us also be among those who give thanks, who have served God, and not among the godless who are judged.
18:2 For I myself am a sinner through and through. I have not yet escaped temptation, but am still surrounded by the devil's tools. Yet I am doing my best to pursue righteousness, so that I may be strong enough at least to come near it, because I fear the judgment to come.
## I am reading you an appeal
19:1 So, brothers and sisters, after the God of truth, I am reading you an appeal to pay attention to what is written, so that you may save both yourselves and the one who reads among you. For the reward I ask of you is that you repent with your whole heart, giving yourselves salvation and life. If we do this, we will set a goal for all the young people who want to work hard at godliness and at God's kindness.
19:2 And let us not be annoyed and angry, foolish as we are, when someone warns us and turns us from wrong to righteousness. For sometimes we do evil without knowing it, because of the double-mindedness and unbelief inside us, and our understanding has gone dark from empty desires.
19:3 So let us do what is right, so that we may be saved in the end. Blessed are those who obey these instructions. Even if they suffer hardship for a little while in this world, they will gather in the immortal fruit of the resurrection.
19:4 So the godly person should not grieve if he is miserable in these present times. A blessed time is waiting for him. He will live again above with the fathers and rejoice in an age that has no grief.
## The contest of the living God
20:1 And do not let your mind be troubled by this either: we see the wicked getting rich, while God's slaves are in hard straits.
20:2 So let us have faith, brothers and sisters. We are competing in the trial of the living God, and we are being trained by the life we have now, so that we may be crowned in the life to come.
20:3 None of the righteous has received his fruit quickly. He waits for it.
20:4 For if God paid the righteous their reward right away, we would soon be training ourselves in business and not in reverence for God. We would seem to be righteous, but we would be chasing profit and not godliness. That is why God's judgment strikes a spirit that is not righteous and loads it with chains.
20:5 To the only God, who cannot be seen, the Father of truth, who sent us the Savior and Founder of incorruption, through whom he also revealed to us the truth and the life of heaven: to him be the glory forever and ever. Amen. The second letter of Clement to the Corinthians.
