## Two kings at war
1:1 It was the twelfth year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, who ruled over the Assyrians in Nineveh, the great city. In those days Arphaxad ruled over the Medes in Ecbatana.
1:2 Around Ecbatana he built walls of cut stones, each three cubits wide and six cubits long. He made the wall seventy cubits high and fifty cubits thick.
1:3 At its gates he set up towers a hundred cubits high, and he laid its foundations sixty cubits wide.
1:4 He made its gates rise to a height of seventy cubits, and they were forty cubits wide, so that his mighty army could march out and his foot soldiers could form their ranks.
1:5 In those days King Nebuchadnezzar made war on King Arphaxad in the great plain, that is, in the territory of Ragau.
1:6 All who lived in the hill country came to meet him, and all who lived along the Euphrates, the Tigris and the Hydaspes, and in the plain, Arioch king of the Elymeans. Many nations gathered to join the battle line of the sons of Cheleoul.
## The west refuses the summons
1:7 Then Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians sent word to all who lived in Persia and to all who lived in the west: to those who lived in Cilicia and Damascus, Lebanon and Antilebanon, and all who lived along the coast,
1:8 and to the peoples of Carmel and Gilead, upper Galilee and the great plain of Esdraelon,
1:9 and to all who were in Samaria and its towns, and beyond the Jordan as far as Jerusalem, Betane, Chelous and Kadesh, and the river of Egypt, and Tahpanhes and Rameses and all the land of Goshen,
1:10 as far as beyond Tanis and Memphis, and to all who lived in Egypt, as far as the borders of Ethiopia.
1:11 But all who lived in the whole land thought little of the word of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians. They did not join him in the war, because they were not afraid of him. To them he was only a man like any other. They sent his messengers away empty-handed and in disgrace.
1:12 Nebuchadnezzar was furious with this whole land. He swore by his throne and his kingdom that he would take revenge on all the territory of Cilicia, Damascus and Syria. He would kill them with his sword, and all who lived in Moab, and the Ammonites, and all Idumea, and everyone in Egypt, as far as the borders of the two seas.
## Arphaxad falls
1:13 In the seventeenth year he lined up his army against King Arphaxad. He won the war and routed Arphaxad's whole army, all his cavalry and all his chariots.
1:14 He took control of his cities and came to Ecbatana. He seized its towers, plundered its streets, and turned its beauty into its shame.
1:15 He caught Arphaxad in the mountains of Ragau and ran him through with his hunting spears, and he destroyed him once and for all that day.
1:16 Then he went back with them, he and all his mixed host, a very great crowd of warriors. There he lounged and feasted, he and his army, for a hundred and twenty days.
## The king's revenge
2:1 In the eighteenth year, on the twenty-second day of the first month, there was talk in the palace of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians of taking revenge on the whole earth, as he had said.
2:2 He called together all his servants and all his nobles and shared with them his secret plan. With his own mouth he spelled out all the harm he meant for the earth.
2:3 They decided to destroy all flesh that had not obeyed the word of his mouth.
2:4 When he had finished laying out his plan, Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians called for Holofernes, the commander in chief of his army, second only to himself, and said to him,
2:5 "This is what the great king, the lord of the whole earth, says: Go out from my presence and take with you men who trust in their own strength: a hundred and twenty thousand foot soldiers, and horses with their riders, twelve thousand.
2:6 Go out to confront the whole land to the west, because they disobeyed the word of my mouth.
2:7 Tell them to get earth and water ready, for I will come out against them in my anger. I will cover the whole face of the earth with the feet of my army, and I will hand them over to my troops to plunder.
2:8 Their slain will fill their ravines and their streams, and a river will overflow, full of their dead.
2:9 I will lead them away captive to the ends of the whole earth.
2:10 As for you, go out and take all their territory for me ahead of time. If they surrender to you, hold them for me until the day I punish them.
2:11 But as for those who refuse to obey, do not let your eye spare them. Hand them over to slaughter and plunder throughout the land you rule.
2:12 For as I live, and by the might of my kingdom, I have spoken, and I will do these things by my own hand.
2:13 And you, do not disobey a single one of your lord's commands. Carry them out to the full, just as I have ordered you, and do not put off doing them."
## Holofernes marches west
2:14 So Holofernes went out from his lord's presence. He called together all the high officers, the generals and the officers of the army of Assyria.
2:15 He counted out picked men for battle, as his lord had ordered him: a hundred and twenty thousand, and twelve thousand mounted archers.
2:16 He arranged them the way a great army is drawn up for war.
2:17 He took camels, donkeys and mules for their baggage, a huge number, and sheep, cattle and goats beyond counting for their food.
2:18 He took plenty of rations for every man, and a great deal of gold and silver from the king's palace.
2:19 Then he set out with his whole army, to march ahead of King Nebuchadnezzar and to cover the whole face of the earth to the west with their chariots, horsemen and picked foot soldiers.
2:20 A large mixed crowd went out with them, like locusts, like the sand of the earth, for there was no counting how many they were.
2:21 They marched out of Nineveh three days' journey toward the plain of Bectileth. They camped near Bectileth, by the mountain to the north of upper Cilicia.
2:22 From there he took his whole army, his foot soldiers, horsemen and chariots, and went off into the hill country.
2:23 He cut his way through Put and Lud, and plundered all the people of Rassis and the Ishmaelites who lived along the edge of the desert, south of the land of the Chaldeans.
2:24 He crossed the Euphrates and went through Mesopotamia. He razed all the high towns along the wadi Abron, all the way to the sea.
2:25 He seized the territory of Cilicia and cut down everyone who stood against him. He came to the borders of Japheth, which lie to the south, facing Arabia.
2:26 He surrounded all the Midianites, set their tents on fire, and plundered their sheepfolds.
2:27 He went down into the plain of Damascus at the time of the wheat harvest. He set all their fields on fire and wiped out their flocks and herds. He looted their towns, winnowed their plains bare, and struck down all their young men with the edge of the sword.
2:28 Fear and trembling of him fell on those who lived along the coast, in Sidon and Tyre, and on those who lived in Sur and Ocina, and all who lived in Jamnia. Those who lived in Ashdod and Ashkelon were terrified of him.
## The coast surrenders
3:1 So they sent messengers to him with words of peace. They said,
3:2 "We, the servants of Nebuchadnezzar the great king, lie before you. Treat us however you see fit.
3:3 Our farmsteads, all our wheat fields, our flocks and herds, and all the sheepfolds of our camps lie before you. Do with them whatever you please.
3:4 Our towns, too, and the people who live in them are your slaves. Come and deal with them as seems good in your eyes."
3:5 The men came to Holofernes and gave him this message.
3:6 Then he came down to the coast with his army. He put garrisons in the high towns and took picked men from them as allies.
3:7 The people there and all the countryside around welcomed him with garlands, dancing and tambourines.
3:8 Yet he laid waste all their territory and cut down their sacred groves. It had been given to him to destroy all the gods of the land, so that all the nations would worship Nebuchadnezzar alone, and every language and tribe would call on him as god.
3:9 He came opposite Esdraelon, near Dothan, which faces the great ridge of Judea.
3:10 They camped between Geba and Scythopolis, and he stayed there a whole month to gather all the baggage of his army.
## Judea gets ready
4:1 The Israelites who lived in Judea heard about all that Holofernes, the commander in chief of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians, had done to the nations. They heard how he had looted all their temples and destroyed them.
4:2 They were very, very afraid of him, and they were alarmed for Jerusalem and for the temple of the Lord their God.
4:3 For they had only lately come up from captivity. Not long before, all the people of Judea had been gathered together, and the vessels, the altar and the house had been made holy again after they were defiled.
4:4 So they sent word to every district of Samaria, and to Kona, Beth-horon, Belmain and Jericho, to Choba and Aesora, and to the valley of Salem.
4:5 They took over all the tops of the high mountains before the enemy could, and they walled the villages on them. They stored up food to get ready for war, since their fields had just been harvested.
4:6 Joakim, the high priest who was then in Jerusalem, wrote to the people of Bethulia and Betomasthaim, which faces Esdraelon, across from the plain near Dothan.
4:7 He told them to hold the mountain passes, because they were the way into Judea. It would be easy to stop anyone coming up, since the way up was narrow, room for two men at most.
4:8 The Israelites did as Joakim the high priest and the council of elders of all the people of Israel, who sat in Jerusalem, had ordered them.
## A nation in sackcloth
4:9 Every man of Israel cried out to God with great earnestness, and they humbled themselves with great earnestness.
4:10 They, their wives, their little children and their cattle, and every foreigner living with them, every hired worker and every slave bought with money, put sackcloth around their waists.
4:11 Every man of Israel, and the women and children who lived in Jerusalem, lay face down in front of the temple. They put ashes on their heads and spread out their sackcloth before the Lord.
4:12 They wrapped the altar in sackcloth. With one heart they cried out earnestly to the God of Israel. They begged him not to let their little children be carried off, or their wives taken as spoil, or the towns of their inheritance destroyed, or the holy place defiled and mocked, for the nations to gloat over.
4:13 The Lord heard their cry and saw their distress. Throughout Judea and in Jerusalem the people fasted for many days before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty.
4:14 Joakim the high priest, and all the priests who stood before the Lord, and those who served the Lord, with sackcloth around their waists, offered the regular burnt offering, and the vows and freewill gifts of the people.
4:15 There were ashes on their turbans, and they cried out to the Lord with all their strength to look on the whole house of Israel for good.
## Who are these people?
5:1 Holofernes, the commander in chief of the army of Assyria, was told that the Israelites had made ready for war. They had shut the passes through the hill country, walled every high mountaintop, and set obstacles in the plains.
5:2 He flew into a great rage. He called together all the rulers of Moab, the generals of Ammon and all the satraps of the coast,
5:3 and said to them, "Tell me, you sons of Canaan: who is this people that lives in the hill country? What towns do they live in? How large is their army? What does their power and strength rest on? Who has risen up over them as king, to lead their army?
5:4 And why have they turned their backs on me and refused to come and meet me, unlike everyone else who lives in the west?"
## Achior tells Israel's story
5:5 Achior, the leader of all the Ammonites, said to him, "Let my lord hear a word from the mouth of your servant. I will tell you the truth about this people who live in this hill country near where you are staying. No lie will come out of your servant's mouth.
5:6 This people are descendants of the Chaldeans.
5:7 They once lived as foreigners in Mesopotamia, because they did not want to follow the gods of their ancestors who were in the land of the Chaldeans.
5:8 They left the way of their forefathers and worshiped the God of heaven, the God they had come to know. So they were thrown out from before their gods. They fled to Mesopotamia and lived there as foreigners for a long time.
5:9 Then their God told them to leave the place where they were living and go to the land of Canaan. They settled there and grew rich in gold and silver and in a great many cattle.
5:10 They went down to Egypt, because a famine had covered the face of the land of Canaan. They lived there as foreigners for as long as they were fed there. There they became a great multitude, so many that their people could not be counted.
5:11 But the king of Egypt rose up against them. They outwitted them and set them to hard labor and brick-making. They beat them down and made them slaves.
5:12 They cried out to their God, and he struck the land of Egypt with plagues that had no cure. So the Egyptians drove them out of their sight.
5:13 God dried up the Red Sea before them,
5:14 and he led them on the road to Sinai and Kadesh-barnea. They drove out everyone who lived in the desert,
5:15 and they settled in the land of the Amorites. By their strength they destroyed all the people of Heshbon, and they crossed the Jordan and took possession of the whole hill country.
5:16 They drove out before them the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, the people of Shechem and all the Girgashites, and they lived there a long time.
5:17 As long as they did not sin before their God, good things were theirs, for the God who is with them hates wrongdoing.
5:18 But when they turned away from the way he had laid down for them, they were utterly destroyed in many wars and taken captive to a land not their own. The temple of their God was razed to the ground, and their towns were taken by their enemies.
5:19 But now they have turned back to their God. They have come up from the Diaspora, where they had been scattered, and taken possession of Jerusalem, where their sanctuary is. They have settled in the hill country, because it was empty.
5:20 So now, my master and lord, if there is any error in this people, and they are sinning against their God, we will see that this is their stumbling block. Then we will go up and defeat them in war.
5:21 But if there is no lawlessness in their nation, let my lord pass them by. Otherwise their Lord and their God will hold a shield over them, and we will be a disgrace before the whole earth."
## The officers want Achior dead
5:22 When Achior finished saying these things, all the people standing in a circle around the tent began to grumble. Holofernes' nobles, and all who lived on the coast and in Moab, said he should be cut to pieces.
5:23 "We will not be afraid of the Israelites! Look, they are a people with no power and no strength for a hard battle.
5:24 So let us go up, master Holofernes, and they will be a meal for your whole army."
## Who is god but Nebuchadnezzar?
6:1 When the uproar of the men around the council died down, Holofernes, the commander in chief of the army of Assyria, spoke to Achior in front of the whole crowd of foreigners, and to all the Moabites:
6:2 "Who are you, Achior, and you hirelings of Ephraim, to play the prophet among us as you have today? You tell us not to make war on the people of Israel, because their God will hold a shield over them. Who is god except Nebuchadnezzar? He will send out his might and wipe them off the face of the earth, and their God will not rescue them.
6:3 We his servants will strike them down like one man. They will not stand up to the power of our horses.
6:4 We will burn them up with them. Their land will be drunk with their blood, and their plains will be filled with their dead. Not even the soles of their feet will hold their ground before us. They will perish and be lost, says King Nebuchadnezzar, lord of the whole earth. For he has spoken: the words he has said will not be empty.
6:5 As for you, Achior, you hireling of Ammon, who said these words on this day of your wrongdoing: you will not see my face again from this day until I take revenge on this people that came out of Egypt.
6:6 Then the iron of my army and the crowd of my servants will pierce your sides, and you will fall among their slain when they come back.
6:7 My servants will take you back to the hill country and leave you in one of the towns on the passes.
6:8 You will not die until you are destroyed along with them.
6:9 And if you really hope in your heart that they will not be captured, do not let your face fall. I have spoken, and not one of my words will fail."
## Achior handed over to Israel
6:10 Holofernes ordered his servants who waited on him in his tent to seize Achior, take him back to Bethulia, and hand him over to the Israelites.
6:11 So his servants seized him and took him out of the camp into the plain. From the middle of the plain they moved up into the hill country and came to the springs below Bethulia.
6:12 When the men of the town on the hilltop saw them, they took up their weapons and went out of the town to the top of the hill. All the slingers kept them from coming up by hurling stones at them.
6:13 So they slipped in under the hill, tied Achior up, and left him lying at the foot of the hill. Then they went back to their lord.
6:14 The Israelites came down from their town and found him. They untied him, brought him into Bethulia, and set him before the rulers of their town.
6:15 In those days these were Uzziah son of Micah, of the tribe of Simeon, Chabris son of Othniel, and Charmis son of Malchiel.
6:16 They called together all the elders of the town, and all their young men and the women ran to the assembly. They stood Achior in the middle of all their people, and Uzziah asked him what had happened.
6:17 He answered and told them what had been said in Holofernes' council. He told all that he himself had said in front of the rulers of the Assyrians, and all that Holofernes had boasted against the house of Israel.
6:18 Then the people fell down and worshiped God, and they cried out,
6:19 "Lord God of heaven, look down on their arrogance! Have pity on the low state of our people, and look today on the faces of those who are set apart for you."
6:20 Then they reassured Achior and praised him highly.
6:21 Uzziah took him from the assembly to his house and gave a banquet for the elders. All that night they called on the God of Israel for help.
## The siege of Bethulia
7:1 The next day Holofernes ordered his whole army, and all the people who had come to fight alongside him, to break camp and move against Bethulia. They were to seize the passes up into the hill country first and make war on the Israelites.
7:2 That day all their fighting men broke camp. Their army of warriors was a hundred and seventy thousand foot soldiers and twelve thousand horsemen, not counting the baggage of the men on foot among them, a huge crowd.
7:3 They camped in the valley near Bethulia, by the spring. They spread out in width toward Dothan as far as Belbaim, and in length from Bethulia to Cyamon, which faces Esdraelon.
7:4 When the Israelites saw how many there were, they were badly shaken. They said to one another, "Now these men will lick clean the face of the whole land. Neither the high mountains nor the ravines nor the hills will bear their weight."
7:5 Each man took up his weapons. They lit fires on their towers and stayed on guard all that night.
7:6 On the second day Holofernes led out all his cavalry in full view of the Israelites who were in Bethulia.
7:7 He looked over the ways up to their town and scouted out the springs of water. He seized them and set camps of soldiers over them. Then he went back to his people.
## Take the water
7:8 All the rulers of the descendants of Esau, all the leaders of the people of Moab, and the generals of the coast came to him and said,
7:9 "Let our master hear a word, so that your army may not suffer a blow.
7:10 This people, the Israelites, do not trust in their spears but in the heights of the mountains where they live. It is not easy to climb to the tops of their mountains.
7:11 So now, master, do not fight them in a pitched battle, and not one man of your army will fall.
7:12 Stay in your camp and keep every man of your army safe. Let your servants take over the spring of water that flows out at the foot of the mountain,
7:13 because that is where all the people of Bethulia get their water. Thirst will kill them, and they will give up their town. We and our people will go up to the nearby mountaintops and camp on them as an outpost, so that not one man gets out of the town.
7:14 They will waste away with hunger, they and their wives and their children. Before the sword reaches them, they will lie strewn in the streets where they live.
7:15 So you will pay them back with an evil reward, because they rebelled and did not come to meet you in peace."
7:16 Their words pleased Holofernes and all his servants, and he gave orders to do as they had said.
7:17 The camp of the Ammonites moved out, and with them five thousand Assyrians. They camped in the valley and seized the water supply and the springs of the Israelites.
7:18 The descendants of Esau and the Ammonites went up and camped in the hill country opposite Dothan. They sent some of their men south and east, opposite Egrebel, which is near Chus on the wadi Mochmur. The rest of the Assyrian army camped in the plain and covered the whole face of the land. Their tents and their baggage trains were pitched in a great throng, a huge crowd.
7:19 The Israelites cried out to the Lord their God, for their spirit sank. All their enemies had surrounded them, and there was no way to slip out from among them.
7:20 The whole Assyrian camp, its foot soldiers, chariots and horsemen, stayed around them for thirty-four days. All the water jars of everyone living in Bethulia ran dry.
7:21 The cisterns were going empty. They did not have enough water to drink their fill for a single day, because their water was given to them by measure.
7:22 Their little children lost heart, and the women and young men fainted from thirst. They collapsed in the streets of the town and in the gateways, and there was no strength left in them.
## The people demand surrender
7:23 Then all the people, the young men, the women and the children, crowded around Uzziah and the rulers of the town. They cried out with a loud voice and said in front of the elders,
7:24 "May God judge between you and us! You have done us a great wrong by not making peace with the Assyrians.
7:25 Now there is no helper for us. Instead, God has sold us into their hands, to be laid low before them by thirst and terrible ruin.
7:26 So now, call them in and hand the whole town over to be plundered by Holofernes' people and all his army.
7:27 It is better for us to become their plunder. We will be slaves, but we will stay alive. We will not have to watch our babies die before our eyes, and our wives and our children give up their lives.
7:28 We call heaven and earth to witness against you, and our God, the Lord of our ancestors, who punishes us for our sins and for the sins of our ancestors. May he not do to us today what we have described!"
7:29 Then a great wailing went up from the whole assembly together, and they cried out to the Lord God with a loud voice.
## Five more days
7:30 Uzziah said to them, "Take courage, brothers! Let us endure five more days. In that time the Lord our God will turn his mercy toward us, for he will not abandon us for good.
7:31 But if these days pass and no help reaches us, I will act on your words."
7:32 Then he sent the people away, each to his own post. They went up onto the walls and towers of their town, and he sent the women and children to their homes. And there was great distress in the town.
