# Hostile review: Baruch 1-5 (part 01-05)

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-31. Checked against `texts/orig/Bar.json` (Swete, Vaticanus, all 141 verses), `texts/src/Bar.app.json`, `texts/src/Bar.lat.json`, `texts/en/Bar.json` (KJV), the site's Plain English Translation of Daniel, Jeremiah, Deuteronomy, Isaiah and others, `content/terms.json` and `content/proposals/works/terms.json`. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Bar/01-05` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The translation is careful and mostly faithful, the Daniel 9 parallel is handled well, the apparatus readings match Swete's footnotes, and the patristic and liturgical citations for 3:36-38 are real (I opened Cyprian, Hippolytus, Apostolic Constitutions, the Jewish Encyclopedia, the Catholic Encyclopedia, USCCB and OCA pages). There is no invented source. The weak points are all on the one verse the brief marked as the crux, 3:38, and on the use of named scholars. (1) The decision to have no 3:38 card rests on "no church document addresses it", which is wrong: the OCA (Orthodox) says Baruch "clearly foresaw the Incarnation", while the USCCB's NABRE note and the Catholic Encyclopedia read the verse as Wisdom/Law. (2) The Plain English Translation renders 3:38 "she" and the notes call that "the grammar", but the Greek verb has no gender and the nearest subject in 3:37 is God. (3) The topic page `jesus` puts the Plain English Translation's "she" into the mouths of the Fathers and gives an editorial verdict. (4) A view is credited to Gigot that his article does not state. (5) The critical side rests on a 1902 encyclopedia article only. (6) Two translation slips (1:20 "laid on Moses", 2:21 imperative made a promise). Everything else is minor. Count: 0 critical, 7 major, 18 minor.

## Findings

### Major

**1. Major. No 3:38 card, decided on a false premise.** Location: chapters.json ch. 3 `deep` ("No church teaching document read for this page addresses the question directly... the difference is between ways of reading, not between churches' teachings"); progress.md; `.scratch/bar-01-05/sources-checked.md` ("none found"; the scratch list shows only Gigot was searched for 3:38).
Evidence I opened now:
- OCA, "Prophet Baruch" (oca.org/saints/lives/2020/09/28/102745-prophet-baruch): "In his prophetic book, Baruch clearly foresaw the Incarnation of the Lord Jesus Christ"; the verses are "read on the eve of the Nativity of Christ at the Third Hour, and are ascribed to the Prophet Jeremiah"; it adds that Chrysostom held Baruch was the first to foretell that God would become man. (That Chrysostom says so is the OCA's claim; I did not check Chrysostom.)
- USCCB NABRE, bible.usccb.org/bible/baruch/3, note on 3:37-38: "As in Sir 24:8, Wisdom is given to Israel but also is said to live with all human beings." The verse is printed "Thus she has appeared on earth."
- Catholic Encyclopedia (Gigot, newadvent.org/cathen/02319c.htm): Wisdom "has appeared on the earth" only "in the guise of the Law."
- Challoner/Haydock notes in the Douay tradition read it of the incarnation (found by search at ccel.org and haydockcommentary.com; I did not open the full notes).
So an Orthodox church publication and a Catholic bishops' Bible disagree in print about what the verse is, and that is the very comparison the site's "Where Christians differ" cards exist for. Both churches read it in worship, but the card question is how each church understands it. The decision may still be defensible (the readings do not divide the churches' doctrines, and the Orthodox liturgy and the Catholic lectionary do not say how to read it), but the page must not claim that no church document addresses it.
Fix: either add the card (top `wisdom` or `incarnation` if it exists), with Orthodox (OCA: prophecy of the Incarnation; Vespers/Hours reading), Catholic (NABRE note: Wisdom; Douay notes and Fathers: incarnation; Easter Vigil reading), Protestant/Anglican (not Scripture; Article VI) and Jewish (Torah) answers; or keep views notes and delete the sentence "No church teaching document read for this page addresses the question", replacing it with what these pages actually say. Add the OCA and USCCB note to sources.

**2. Major. "The grammar points this way": overstated, one-sided argument for "she" at 3:38.** Location: notes.json 3:38 (views note), chapters.json ch. 3 deep ("The grammar points this way... 'he gave <i>her</i>'"), plain.txt 3:37-38.
Greek: 3:37 `ἐξεῦρεν πᾶσαν ὁδὸν ἐπιστήμης, καὶ ἔδωκεν αὐτὴν Ἰακὼβ`; 3:38 `μετὰ τοῦτο ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ὤφθη, καὶ ἐν τοῖς ἀνθρώποις συνανεστράφη`. The feminine `αὐτήν` is the object and agrees with `ὁδόν` ("the way"), not with a personified Wisdom; the Plain English Translation itself writes "it" there. `ὤφθη` and `συνανεστράφη` carry no gender. The nearest third-person subject running through 3:36-37 is God (`ἐξεῦρεν... ἔδωκεν`), which is exactly why the Fathers, the Noetians (Hippolytus, Against Noetus 2) and the Vulgate/KJV read "he". The case for Wisdom is 4:1 (`Αὕτη ἡ βίβλος`) and the context, which is a good case, but it is not "grammar". The Plain English Translation then commits to "she" with no neutral wording, and the note hides that the subject-continuity argument favors "he".
Fix: say in the note and deep section that the verb is ungendered, that the continuing subject is God, and that the case for Wisdom is the context of 4:1 and Sirach 24; drop "the grammar points this way". Consider keeping "she" but adding it as a gloss-level choice, or a variant-style entry (Wisdom/God) at 3:38 so the reader can switch.

**3. Major. Topic `jesus` mixes the Plain English Translation's "she" into the Fathers' quotation and gives an editorial verdict.** Location: topics.json id `jesus`.
The section says "Early Christians read Baruch 3:36-38 as a prophecy of Christ: 'This is our God; no other will be reckoned next to him ... After this she was seen on earth and went about among human beings.'" The Fathers I opened read "he" (Cyprian, Testimonies 2.6: "After this He was seen upon earth, and He conversed with men"; Apostolic Constitutions 5.20: "Afterwards He was seen upon earth"). Quoting the Plain English Translation's "she" as what they read is false. It ends: "The Greek, read with Baruch 4:1, more naturally speaks of wisdom given as the law", which is the site's voice taking a side on the contested verse (see finding 2). `word-of-god` has the same drift ("wisdom 'seen on earth'").
Fix: quote the Fathers in their own wording (or "he") and attribute it; remove or label the closing verdict; link to the 3:38 note.

**4. Major. Gigot is credited with a view his article does not state.** Location: chapters.json ch. 1 `settings` 1:1-14 `views` ("Written by Baruch in Babylon about 581 BC and sent to Jerusalem (Gigot, defending the traditional view)") and `readers` ("Gigot, defending the traditional view, took it as written in Babylon five years after the fall"); ch. 1 `views.C` ("Gigot defended Baruch's authorship while allowing that the book was compiled").
Checked at newadvent.org/cathen/02319c.htm: Gigot reports that the Jerome tradition of Baruch's death in Egypt "conflicts with the data found in the opening chapter", presents three grounds for Catholic authorship and the non-Catholic objections separately, says Catholic scholars who accept compilation hold that Baruch "must be numbered among" the compilers, and gives no personal verdict that the book was written in Babylon in 581. His own note-level points (1:14 means public Temple reading, hence a Hebrew original; Trent defined it canonical) are fine and are correctly used at 1:14 and ch. 1 C.
Fix: attribute only what Gigot says ("Catholic scholars... compiled work", "Hebrew original because of 1:14"); do not give him the 581 BC date or the label "defending the traditional view".

**5. Major. The "critical" voice is one 1902 encyclopedia article, and its consensus claims are unsourced.** Location: chapters.json ch. 1 `readings` ("Most critical scholars read 1:1-14 as a story written to introduce older prayers"), ch. 4 `views.H` ("Critics read 4:5-5:9 as a third composition... perhaps by a different hand"), the dating `readers` fields, notes 1:11, 2:26, 4:3, 4:32. All dating proposals come from Toy (1902) and "Kneucker and others of Toy's day"; the post-AD 70 date is presented as a live option on the same footing as Maccabean, with no statement of where current scholarship stands.
STYLE rule 3 requires consensus labeled as consensus with who dissents. The writer's sources-checked lists no work after 1912 (apart from Bullard-Hatton on one verse). I could not open a current scholarly source this session, so I cannot say what the present majority is; the page cannot either. The sentences beginning "Most critical scholars" are claims about modern scholarship backed only by a 1902 article.
Fix: open a current commentary or survey (the Source Library MCP or a publisher page) and either cite it for the consensus, or reword every "most/critics" sentence to "Toy (1902) judged..." and add one honest sentence: "The sources read for this page are from 1902 and 1907."

**6. Major. 1:20 "the curse that the Lord laid on Moses his servant".** Location: plain.txt 1:20; echoed in topics.json `blessings-and-curses` (quoted).
Greek: `ἡ ἀρὰ ἣν συνέταξεν Κύριος τῷ Μωυσῇ παιδὶ αὐτοῦ ἐν ἡμέρᾳ ᾗ ἐξήγαγεν...`. `συντάσσω` + dative is "ordain, give orders to"; the curse was commanded to Moses, not placed on him. "Laid on Moses" reads as a burden carried by Moses. Daniel 9:11's Plain English Translation says "written in the law of Moses, the servant of God".
Fix: "the curse that the Lord commanded Moses his servant to announce" or "the curse that the Lord appointed through Moses his servant"; fix the quotation in the topic.

**7. Major. 2:21 turns an imperative into a promise.** Location: plain.txt 2:21.
Greek: `Κλίνατε τὸν ὦμον ὑμῶν καὶ ἐργάσασθε τῷ βασιλεῖ Βαβυλῶνος, καὶ καθίσατε ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν ἣν δέδωκα τοῖς πατράσιν ὑμῶν`: three imperatives (bow, work, settle/sit). The Plain English Translation gives "and you will go on living in the land", which is the shape of Jeremiah 27:11 ("I will let stay... live there") and the site's Jeremiah Plain English Translation, not of Baruch's Greek. Also `ἐργάσασθε` "work" is rendered "work for the king" consistently, good.
Fix: "and stay in the land I have given your ancestors" (imperative), and let the note at 2:21 say it matches Jeremiah's promise in sense.

### Minor

**8. Minor. 3:3 adds "on your throne".** Greek `σὺ καθήμενος τὸν αἰῶνα` is "you sitting/enthroned for all time" (no `θρόνος`). The Latin has `sedes`. Lamentations 5:19 supplies "throne", but the Plain English Translation adds it without a note. Fix: "You are seated for all time" or note that "enthroned" is the sense.

**9. Minor. Plain English Translation departs from the site's Daniel wording where the Greek is the same, undisclosed.** 2:2 `ὑποκάτω παντὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ` = Daniel 9:12 Theodotion's phrase; Plain English Translation Baruch "under the whole sky", Plain English Translation Daniel "under the heavens". 2:17 `ἴδε` (same word as Daniel 9:18 `ἰδέ`): Baruch "see", Daniel "look". The brief says keep the same English where the Greek matches and say in a note where not. Nothing is said. (Daniel's Hebrew is plural "heavens", so "sky" can be defended; say so.)

**10. Minor. Possible drift toward the NRSV at 4:5.** Greek `μνημόσυνον Ἰσραήλ` ("memorial of Israel"). The Plain English Translation "you who keep Israel's name alive" adds "name" and a verb of preserving; the NRSV (read at bible.oremus.org) has "who perpetuate Israel's name". The Greek has no "name". Other verses checked (3:26 "expert in war" vs Plain English Translation "trained for war"; 3:37-38; 4:1) differ enough. Fix: redo 4:5, e.g. "you who are Israel's memorial" or "what is left of Israel".

**11. Minor. Gloss 4:31 "Miserable" = "Pity".** Greek `δείλαιοι` is "wretched, miserable" of the persecutors, a woe. "Pity" reads as an imperative to pity them. Fix: "Doomed" or "Poor them".

**12. Minor. Gloss 2:11 "a raised arm" = "a fist raised to strike".** The Greek `βραχίων ὑψηλός` is the idiom of power (the note says "an arm lifted to strike" too); "to strike" is not in the text. Fix: "mighty power".
