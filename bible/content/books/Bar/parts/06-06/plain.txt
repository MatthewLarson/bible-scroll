## A letter to the captives
6:1 This is a copy of the letter Jeremiah sent to the people who were about to be led off as captives to Babylon by the king of the Babylonians. He wrote to tell them what he had been ordered to say by God. You sinned against God, and because of those sins Nebuchadnezzar king of the Babylonians will lead you off to Babylon as captives.
6:2 When you get to Babylon, you will be there for years and years, a long stretch of time, as long as seven generations. Then I will lead you out of there in peace.
6:3 There in Babylon you will see silver gods, gold gods and wooden gods, carried high on shoulders, putting fear into the nations.
6:4 So be on your guard. Do not let yourselves grow like the foreigners until you are just like them, and do not let fear of these gods grab you
6:5 when you see people crowding ahead of them and behind them, bowing down to them. Say instead, in your own mind, "You are the one we must worship, Master."
6:6 For my angel is with you, and he himself is looking after your lives.
## Gold, crowns and stolen silver
6:7 A woodworker planed their tongues smooth. They are gold-plated and silver-plated. Yet they are a lie, and they cannot speak.
6:8 Just as people do for a girl who loves pretty things, they take gold
6:9 and make crowns for the heads of their gods. There are also times when the priests slip gold and silver away from their gods and run through it on themselves.
6:10 Some of it they will even pass along to the prostitutes up on the roof. And they dress them in clothes as if they were people: silver gods, gold gods and wooden ones.
6:11 Yet these gods cannot keep themselves safe from tarnish and from being eaten away, even with purple cloth thrown around them.
6:12 The dust of the house settles on them so heavily that someone has to wipe their faces.
6:13 Like a man who judges a land, one of them holds a scepter. Yet he cannot kill the one who wrongs him.
6:14 Another holds a dagger in his right hand, and an ax too, yet he cannot rescue himself from war or from robbers. This makes it plain that they are not gods. So have no fear of them.
## Dust, smoke and bats
6:15 Like a man's pot that breaks and is no use any more,
6:16 so are their gods, set up in their houses. Dust from the feet of everyone coming in fills their eyes.
6:17 The courtyards are walled in, as for a man who has wronged a king or someone being taken away to be executed. Just so, the priests fortify the gods' houses with doors, bolts and bars, to keep robbers from stripping them.
6:18 They keep lamps burning, more than for their own use, and the gods cannot see even one of them.
6:19 They are no different from one of the house's beams. Their hearts, so people say, are licked hollow. Creeping things from the ground gnaw at them and at their clothing, and they feel nothing.
6:20 The smoke from the house has turned their faces black.
6:21 Bats and swallows flit onto their bodies and their heads, and birds too, and the cats as well.
6:22 This is how you will know that they are not gods. So do not keep on fearing them.
## Bought, carried and sold
6:23 As for the gold they have on to make them handsome, it stays dull until someone rubs the tarnish off. They did not feel a thing even while they were being poured in the mold.
6:24 Every one of them was bought at a price, and not one has breath in it.
6:25 Having no feet, they ride on people's shoulders, and so they show the whole world their own dishonor. The ones who wait on them are shamed as well,
6:26 because whenever one tumbles to the ground, they are the ones who must lift it. Stand it up straight and it will not move on its own; let it lean and it will never right itself. Offerings are laid out for them the way they are laid out for the dead.
6:27 The priests sell off the sacrifices brought to them and spend the money freely. Their wives, too, pickle some of it in salt and pass none of it on to anyone poor or weak.
6:28 A woman in her monthly bleeding or a woman fresh from childbirth may handle their sacrifices. Now that these things have shown you they are not gods, have no fear of them.
## What they cannot do
6:29 What would make anyone call them gods? Women lay out food before silver gods, gold gods and wooden gods.
6:30 Inside the houses the priests sit on their seats with ripped tunics, shaved heads, shaved beards and bare heads.
6:31 Before their gods they wail and cry out, the way some do at the meal held after a death.
6:32 The priests strip clothes off the gods and will put them on their own wives and little ones.
6:33 Whether someone does them harm or does them a kindness, they are unable to pay it back. Neither crowning a king nor dethroning one is in their power.
6:34 They are just as unable to give riches, or even small change. A man may make them a vow and never pay it, and they will never come after him for it.
6:35 No one will be saved from death by them. They will not snatch the weaker from the stronger.
6:36 They will never make a blind man see again. They will never get a person out of a tight spot.
6:37 A widow gets no mercy from them, and an orphan gets no kindness.
6:38 The wooden ones, the gold-plated ones and the silver-plated ones are no better than rocks off a hillside. Those who wait on them will be disgraced.
6:39 So how can they be thought of as gods, or called gods?
## The Chaldeans themselves
6:40 Besides, even the Chaldeans themselves dishonor them. When they see a man who is mute and cannot talk, they bring Bel out and plead for the man to speak, as though Bel were able to notice.
6:41 Yet they cannot take the hint and leave those gods behind, for they have no sense.
6:42 The women put cords around themselves and sit in the streets, burning bran like incense.
6:43 Whenever a man going by draws one of them away and she lies with him, she mocks the woman next to her: that one was not judged as worthy as she was, and her cord was never broken.
6:44 Everything done for them is a lie. So how can they be thought of as gods, or called gods?
## Made by men who die
6:45 Woodworkers and goldsmiths built them. They will turn out as nothing other than what the artisans choose to make them.
6:46 The very men who make them will never live to a great age,
6:47 so what future do the things they made have? For they have left behind lies and disgrace for the generations after them.
6:48 Whenever war breaks out against them, or trouble, the priests put their heads together over where to hide along with them.
6:49 Then how is it possible not to notice that they are not gods, when they keep themselves safe neither from war nor from trouble?
6:50 Since they are wood plated with gold and silver, later on everyone will find out that they are a lie. To every nation and to the kings it will be clear that they are not gods but something human hands have made, and no work of God is in them.
6:51 Who is there, then, who still has to learn that they are not gods?
## No help in fire or war
6:52 They will never raise anyone up to be king of a land, nor send rain down on people.
6:53 They cannot try a case for themselves, nor save anyone from injustice, since they are helpless.
6:54 They are like crows between the sky and the earth. And when fire falls on a house of gods, whether wooden or plated with gold or silver, their priests will run off and come out alive, while the gods burn up like the beams in the middle.
6:55 Kings and enemies they will never resist.
6:56 So how can they be taken for gods, or thought of as gods? These gods, wooden, silver-plated and gold-plated, will never get away safe from thieves or from robbers.
6:57 Anyone with strength will peel off their gold and their silver, and will go off holding the clothes that were draped on them. And they will never help even themselves.
6:58 So it would be better to be a king who puts his bravery on display, or a handy pot in a home that its owner gets good use from, than the false gods. Better a door in a home, keeping safe whatever is inside, than the false gods; better a wooden post in a palace than the false gods.
## Sun, moon and clouds obey
6:59 For the sun and the moon and the stars shine brightly, and when they are sent out on a task they do as they are told.
6:60 Lightning too is plain to see when it appears, and in the same way the wind blows across every country.
6:61 The clouds, once God orders them to move across the whole inhabited world, finish what they were told.
6:62 Fire sent down from above to eat up mountains and woods carries out its orders. These gods are not their equal, either in looks or in powers.
6:63 So they are not to be thought of as gods, or called gods, since they are unable to give a ruling or to do people any good.
6:64 Now that you know they are not gods, have no fear of them.
## A scarecrow in a cucumber field
6:65 Kings get no curse from them, and no blessing.
6:66 They will never put signs in the sky for the nations to see. They will not blaze like the sun or give light the way the moon does.
6:67 Wild beasts are better than they are: a beast can escape into cover and look after itself.
6:68 It is not clear to us in any way at all that they are gods. So have no fear of them.
6:69 Their wooden, gold-plated, silver-plated gods are like a scarecrow in a cucumber field that guards nothing.
6:70 They are like a thornbush in a garden where every bird comes to sit, and like a dead body tossed out into the darkness: so are their wooden, gold-plated, silver-plated gods.
6:71 The purple cloth and the marble rotting on them will make it known that they are not gods. Afterward they will be eaten away themselves and become a disgrace in the country.
6:72 So the righteous person who keeps no idols is better off, because he will be far from disgrace.
