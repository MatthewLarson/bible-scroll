## Greeting
1:1 From James, a slave of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the Dispersion: greetings.
## Joy in trials, and wisdom from God
1:2 My brothers, when you fall into all sorts of trials, treat it as complete joy.
1:3 You know that when your faith is proved, it produces endurance.
1:4 Let endurance carry its work through to the end, so that you may be complete and whole, short of nothing.
1:5 If any of you is short of wisdom, let him ask God for it. God gives to everyone with an open hand and without scolding, and it will be given to him.
1:6 But let him ask in faith and not waver at all, for a person who wavers is like an ocean wave that the wind whips up and throws about.
1:7 A man like that should not imagine he will get anything at all from the Lord.
1:8 He is a double-minded man, never steady on any road he takes.
## The low and the rich
1:9 Let the lowly brother boast that he is raised high,
1:10 and the rich one that he is brought low, since like a wildflower he will pass away.
1:11 The sun comes up with its burning heat and dries out the grass, its flower falls off, and the beauty of how it looked is lost. In the same way the rich man will wither while he is still on his rounds.
## Tested, not tempted
1:12 Blessed is the man who endures through trial. Once he has been proved genuine, he will get the crown of life that he promised to those who love him.
1:13 When someone is being tempted, he must never say, "It is God who is tempting me." God is beyond the reach of evil's temptation, and he tempts no one himself.
1:14 Each person is tempted by his own desire, as it drags him off and lures him with bait.
1:15 Then desire becomes pregnant and gives birth to sin, and sin, once it is grown, bears death.
1:16 Don't let yourselves be led astray, my beloved brothers.
1:17 All good giving and every complete gift comes from above. It comes down from the Father of the lights, with whom there is no shifting, no shadow cast by turning.
1:18 It was his will to bring us to birth by the word of truth, so that we would be a kind of firstfruits among the things he made.
## Hearing and doing
1:19 You know this, my beloved brothers. But let every person be ready to listen, slow to talk, slow to anger.
1:20 For a man's anger does not bring about God's righteousness.
1:21 So take off all dirtiness and the evil that spills over everywhere. With gentleness accept the word that has been rooted in you, the word that has power to save your souls.
1:22 Become doers of the word, not just hearers, or you fool yourselves.
1:23 Anyone who is a hearer of the word and not a doer is like a man studying the face he was born with in a mirror.
1:24 He takes a look at himself, goes off, and right away forgets what kind of man he was.
1:25 But the one who bends close to look into the complete law, the law of freedom, and stays with it, who does not hear and forget but does the work, will be blessed as he does it.
1:26 Suppose someone imagines he is a religious man, yet he does not bridle his tongue and he deceives his own heart. That man's religion is empty.
1:27 In the eyes of God the Father, clean and unspoiled religion is this: to visit orphans and widows in their trouble, and to keep yourself free of the world's stains.
## No favorites
2:1 My brothers, do not mix favoritism with the faith of Jesus Christ, our Lord of glory.
2:2 Say a man with gold on his fingers and bright clothes walks into your synagogue, and a poor man in dirty clothes walks in as well.
2:3 You look with favor on the one wearing the bright clothes and say, "Please, sit here in a good place." But to the poor man you say, "You, stand," or, "Sit down there below my footstool."
2:4 Haven't you drawn lines among yourselves, and turned into judges whose reasoning is wicked?
2:5 Hear me, my beloved brothers. Isn't it the poor, poor as the world counts it, whom God picked out to be rich in faith and to inherit the kingdom he promised to those who love him?
2:6 Yet you treated the poor man with contempt. Aren't the rich the people who lord it over you? Aren't they the ones hauling you before the judges?
2:7 Don't they insult the good name that was pronounced over you?
2:8 If you truly carry out the royal law, as the Scripture puts it, "You are to love your neighbor as yourself," you do well.
2:9 But if you play favorites, what you do is sin, and the law shows you up as transgressors.
2:10 Someone might keep the entire law and trip over only one command, but he has become answerable for all of it.
2:11 The One who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder." So if you do not commit adultery, yet you murder, you have still become a transgressor of the law.
2:12 So talk, and so live, as people who will be judged through a law of freedom.
2:13 The judgment will have no mercy for the one who showed none. Mercy boasts over judgment.
## Faith that works
2:14 My brothers, what use is it if a person claims to have faith but has no works? That kind of faith cannot save him, can it?
2:15 Say a brother or a sister has no proper clothes and goes without food for the day,
2:16 and one of you tells them, "Go in peace. Stay warm and get enough to eat," yet you do not give them what their bodies need. What use is that?
2:17 Faith is the same. If it has no works, it is dead in itself.
2:18 But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith apart from the works, and I will use my works to show you the faith.
2:19 You believe that God is one. Fine. The demons believe it as well, and they shiver with fear.
2:20 You empty man, would you like to learn that faith without the works is idle?
2:21 Take Abraham our father. When he laid his son Isaac on the altar as an offering, was he not justified by works?
2:22 You can see that his faith was at work along with his works, and that the works made his faith complete.
2:23 So the Scripture came true that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness," and he was called the friend of God.
2:24 So you see that it is by works that a person is justified, and not by faith alone.
2:25 Rahab the prostitute was the same. She took in the messengers and sent them away by a different road. Was she not justified by works?
2:26 For the body without spirit is a dead body, and in the same way faith without works is a dead faith.
## The tongue
3:1 My brothers, do not let many of you become teachers. You know that we will receive a heavier judgment.
3:2 All of us trip up in many ways. Whoever never trips in what he says is a complete man, able to bridle the whole body as well.
3:3 We put bits in horses' mouths so that they will do what we want, and with that we turn their whole body.
3:4 Or look at ships. They are huge, and fierce winds drive them, yet a tiny rudder steers them wherever the helmsman's urge decides.
3:5 In the same way the tongue is a small member, but it brags of big things. See how little a flame it takes to set a huge forest alight!
3:6 And the tongue is a fire. It is a world of injustice, placed among our body's members. It stains the whole body, it sets fire to the wheel of existence, and Gehenna sets it on fire.
3:7 Human beings have tamed, and still tame, every species: wild animals and birds, creatures that creep and creatures of the sea.
3:8 The tongue, though, no one among people is able to tame: an unsettled evil, loaded with poison that kills.
3:9 We use it to bless the Lord and Father, and we use it to curse human beings, who came into being after God's likeness.
3:10 From one and the same mouth pour blessing and curse. It is not right, my brothers, for things to go this way.
3:11 A spring does not gush both sweet and bitter water from one opening, does it?
3:12 My brothers, a fig tree cannot grow olives, can it, or a vine grow figs? And salty water cannot yield sweet water either.
## Two kinds of wisdom
3:13 Is anyone among you wise and understanding? Then let his good life show his works, done with wisdom's gentleness.
3:14 But if your hearts hold bitter jealousy and rivalry, stop bragging and lying against the truth.
3:15 That kind of wisdom does not come down from above. It belongs to the earth, to the merely natural self, to demons.
3:16 Wherever jealousy and rivalry live, you find chaos and every vile practice.
3:17 The wisdom from above, though, is first of all clean. Then it is peaceable, fair-minded, open to persuasion, full of mercy and good fruits, unwavering, without hypocrisy.
3:18 Righteousness is a fruit sown in peace by people who make peace.
## Friends of the world, or of God
4:1 Where do the wars among you come from, and where do the fights come from? Is it not from here, from your cravings for pleasure that wage war in your body's members?
4:2 You want and do not have. You murder and you envy, and still you cannot lay hold of it. You fight and you make war. The reason you do not have is that you do not ask.
4:3 And when you ask, you get nothing, because you ask in a bad way, to pour it out on your pleasures.
4:4 You adulteresses! Haven't you realized that being the world's friend means being God's enemy? So anyone who wants to be a friend of the world is setting himself up as an enemy of God.
4:5 Or do you think the Scripture is speaking for no reason? With jealousy he yearns for the spirit he placed to live in us.
4:6 Yet the grace he gives is greater. So it says, "God takes his stand against the proud, but to the humble he gives grace."
4:7 Put yourselves under God, then. Stand up against the devil, and he will run away from you.
4:8 Come close to God, and he will come close to you. Clean your hands, sinners. Make your hearts pure, you who are double-minded.
4:9 Feel your misery. Grieve and cry. Let your laughing be turned into grief, and your joy into a downcast face.
4:10 Make yourselves low before the Lord, and he will raise you high.
## Do not judge
4:11 Brothers, stop running one another down. Anyone who runs down a brother, or passes judgment on his brother, runs down the law and passes judgment on the law. And if you judge the law, you are no longer a doer of the law but its judge.
4:12 Only one is Lawgiver and Judge: the one who has power to save and to destroy. You, then, who are you, judging your neighbor?
## If the Lord wills
4:13 Now then, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will travel to such-and-such a city. We will stay a year there, buy and sell, and turn a profit."
4:14 You do not know what your life will be like tomorrow. You are a puff of steam, seen for a moment and then gone.
4:15 What you should say instead is, "If the Lord wants it, we will live, and we will do this thing or that."
4:16 But now you brag in your swagger. Every boast like that is wicked.
4:17 So if someone knows how to do good and does not do it, that is sin for him.
## Woe to the rich
5:1 Now then, you who are rich: cry, and wail aloud over the miseries that are on their way to you.
5:2 Your riches have gone rotten. Your clothes are full of moth holes.
5:3 Your gold and silver are eaten through with rust, and that rust will be a witness against you. It will devour your flesh like fire. You piled up treasure in the last days.
5:4 Listen! The pay of the laborers who cut your fields, the pay you cheated them out of, cries out. The shouts of the reapers have gone into the ears of the Lord of Armies.
5:5 Here on earth you lived soft and spent freely on your pleasures. You fattened your hearts on a day of slaughter.
5:6 You condemned the righteous man, and you killed him. He does not stand against you.
## Patience until the Lord comes
5:7 So, brothers, hold out patiently until the coming of the Lord. Think of the farmer. He waits for the land's precious crop, patient over it until it receives the early and the late rains.
5:8 You also must hold out patiently. Make your hearts firm, because the coming of the Lord has drawn close.
5:9 Brothers, stop groaning about one another, or you will be judged. See, the Judge stands right outside the doors.
5:10 Brothers, for a model of enduring hardship and of patience, look at the prophets, who spoke in the Lord's name.
5:11 Look, we call those who endured blessed. You have heard of Job's endurance, and you saw the outcome the Lord gave, for the Lord is very tender-hearted and compassionate.
5:12 More than anything, my brothers, do not take oaths, whether by heaven or by earth or with any other oath. Let your "Yes" mean yes and your "No" mean no, so that you do not come under judgment.
## Prayer, healing and the wanderer
5:13 Is one of you going through hard things? He should pray. Is someone in good spirits? He should sing praise.
5:14 Is one of you ill? Let him send for the elders of the church. Let them pray over him, when they have anointed him with oil in the name of the Lord.
5:15 The prayer that comes from faith will save the one who is worn down by sickness, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has done sins, they will be forgiven him.
5:16 So then, confess your sins to each other and pray for each other, so that you will be healed. The request of a righteous person, when it is put to work, is very strong.
5:17 Elijah was a man who felt what we feel. He prayed earnestly for no rain, and for three years and six months no rain fell on the land.
5:18 He prayed once more, and the sky sent down rain, and the land brought out its fruit.
5:19 My brothers, suppose one of you strays away from the truth, and someone turns him back.
5:20 That person should know this: whoever turns a sinner back from the wrong road he wandered onto will save his soul from death, and will lay a cover over a great number of sins.
