## Vision 5: The Shepherd comes
25:0 Vision 5.
25:1 I had prayed at home and was sitting on my couch, when a man came in. He was glorious to look at and dressed like a shepherd. He had a white goatskin wrapped around him, a bag over his shoulders and a staff in his hand. He greeted me, and I greeted him back.
25:2 Right away he sat down beside me and said to me, "I have been sent by the most revered angel to live with you for all the remaining days of your life."
25:3 I thought he had come to test me, and I said to him, "Who are you, then? Because I know the one I was handed over to." He said to me, "You do not recognize me?" "No," I said. "I am the shepherd you were handed over to," he said.
25:4 While he was still speaking, his form changed, and I recognized him: he was the one I had been handed over to. At once I was thrown into confusion and seized with fear. I was completely crushed with grief that I had answered him so rudely and so foolishly.
25:5 He answered me and said, "Do not be confused. Be made strong in my commandments, the ones I am about to command you. For I was sent," he said, "to show you again all the things you saw before, the main points, which are useful to you. First of all, write down my commandments and the parables. The other things you will write as I show them to you. That is why," he said, "I am commanding you to write the commandments and parables first: so that you can read them at any time and be able to keep them."
25:6 So I wrote down the commandments and parables, as he commanded me.
25:7 So if you hear them and keep them, and walk in them, and do them with a pure heart, you will receive from the Lord all that he has promised you. But if you hear them and do not repent, and go on adding to your sins, you will receive from the Lord the opposite. The Shepherd, the angel of repentance, commanded me to write all this down just this way.
## Mandate 1: Believe in one God
26:1 First of all, believe that God is one. He created all things and put them in order. He made all things out of what did not exist, so that they came to be. He holds all things, but he alone cannot be held.
26:2 So believe him and fear him, and because you fear him, be self-controlled. Keep these things, and you will throw off all wickedness and put on every virtue of righteousness, and you will live to God, if you keep this commandment.
## Mandate 2: Simplicity and giving
27:1 He said to me, "Hold on to simplicity and be innocent, and you will be like little children, who do not know the wickedness that destroys people's lives.
27:2 First, do not slander anyone, and do not enjoy listening to someone who slanders. Otherwise you, the listener, will also be guilty of the slanderer's sin, if you believe the slander you hear. For if you believe it, you too will have something against your brother. So you will be guilty of the slanderer's sin.
27:3 Slander is evil. It is a restless demon, never at peace, always living among quarrels. So keep away from it, and you will always be on good terms with everyone.
27:4 Put on reverence, which holds no evil stumbling block, but everything about it is smooth and cheerful. Do what is good. From the earnings of your work, which God gives you, give to all who are in need, simply, without hesitating over whom to give to and whom not to. Give to all, for God wants his own gifts given to all.
27:5 So those who receive will give an account to God of why they received and for what. Those who receive because they are in trouble will not be condemned, but those who receive under false pretenses will pay the penalty.
27:6 So the one who gives is innocent. He received from the Lord a task of service to carry out, and he carried it out simply, without sorting out whom to give to and whom not. So this service, carried out simply, became glorious in God's sight. The one who serves God simply in this way will live.
27:7 So keep this commandment, as I have told it to you, so that your repentance and the repentance of your household may be found in simplicity, and your innocence may be pure and unstained."
## Mandate 3: Love the truth
28:1 Again he said to me, "Love the truth, and let only truth come out of your mouth. Then the spirit God made to live in this flesh of yours will be found true in the sight of all people, and so the Lord who lives in you will be glorified, because the Lord is true in every word, and in him there is no lie.
28:2 So those who lie reject the Lord and become people who rob the Lord. They do not hand back to him the deposit they received. For they received from him a spirit that does not lie. If they give it back as a liar, they have defiled the Lord's commandment and become robbers."
28:3 When I heard this, I cried very hard. Seeing me crying, he said, "Why are you crying?" "Because, sir," I said, "I do not know if I can be saved." "Why?" he said. "Because, sir," I said, "never in my life have I spoken a true word. I have always spoken craftily with everyone, and I have passed off my lies as truth to everyone. No one ever contradicted me. They believed what I said. So how can I live, sir," I said, "after doing these things?"
28:4 "Your thinking is good and true," he said. "As a slave of God, you ought to have walked in truth. You should not have let an evil conscience live with the spirit of truth, or brought grief on the spirit that is reverent and true." "Sir," I said, "I have never understood words like these so clearly."
28:5 "Well, now you are hearing them," he said. "Keep them. Then even the lies you told before in your business dealings may become believable, once these words of yours are found true. For those can become believable too. If you keep these things and from now on speak nothing but the truth, you will be able to win life for yourself. And whoever hears this commandment and keeps away from that most evil thing, lying, will live to God."
## Mandate 4: Purity, divorce and the one repentance
29:1 "I command you," he said, "to keep yourself pure. Do not let thoughts come into your heart about another man's wife, or about any sexual sin, or about anything evil of that kind. For if you let such thoughts rise, you are committing a great sin. But if you always remember your own wife, you will never go wrong.
29:2 For if this thought comes into your heart, you will go wrong, and if other evil thoughts like it come, you are committing sin. For this thought is a great sin for a slave of God. And if anyone does this evil deed, he brings about his own death.
29:3 So watch yourself. Keep away from this thought. For where reverence lives, lawlessness should not come into the heart of a righteous man."
29:4 I said to him, "Sir, let me ask you a few things." "Speak," he said. "Sir," I said, "suppose a man has a wife who is faithful in the Lord, and he finds her in some act of adultery. Does the husband sin if he goes on living with her?"
29:5 "As long as he does not know," he said, "he does not sin. But if the husband knows about her sin, and the wife does not repent but keeps on in her sexual sin, and the husband goes on living with her, he becomes guilty of her sin and a partner in her adultery."
29:6 "Then what, sir," I said, "is the husband to do, if the wife keeps on in this passion?" "He must divorce her," he said, "and the husband must stay by himself. But if, after divorcing his wife, he marries another woman, he too commits adultery."
29:7 "Then, sir," I said, "if after the wife is divorced she repents and wants to return to her own husband, will she not be taken back?"
29:8 "Yes, indeed," he said. "If the husband does not take her back, he sins and brings a great sin on himself. The one who has sinned and repents must be taken back, but not over and over. For God's slaves there is one repentance. So because of the repentance, the husband ought not to marry. This rule holds for wife and husband alike.
29:9 It is not only adultery," he said, "if a man defiles his flesh. Whoever does things like the Gentiles do also commits adultery. So if someone keeps on in deeds like these and does not repent, keep away from him and do not live with him. Otherwise you also share in his sin.
29:10 That is why you were commanded to stay by yourselves, whether husband or wife: because in cases like these, repentance is possible.
29:11 So I am not giving an excuse," he said, "for this business to end this way. I am saying it so that the one who has sinned will sin no more. As for his earlier sin, there is One who is able to give healing. For he is the one who has authority over all."
## Repentance is understanding
30:1 I asked him again, "Since the Lord has counted me worthy to have you live with me always, bear with a few more words from me. For I understand nothing, and my heart has been hardened by the things I used to do. Give me understanding, because I am very foolish and grasp nothing at all."
30:2 He answered me, "I am in charge of repentance," he said, "and I give understanding to all who repent. Or do you not think," he said, "that repenting is itself understanding? Repenting," he said, "is great understanding. For the sinner understands that he has done evil before the Lord. The thing he did comes up in his heart, and he repents. He no longer does evil, but does good generously. He humbles his own soul and punishes it, because he sinned. So you see that repentance is great understanding."
30:3 "That is why, sir," I said, "I ask you about everything so carefully. First, because I am a sinner: I need to know what works to do so that I may live, because my sins are many and of every kind."
30:4 "You will live," he said, "if you keep my commandments and walk in them. And whoever hears these commandments and keeps them will live to God."
## Is there a repentance after baptism?
31:1 "Sir," I said, "I will go on and ask one more thing." "Speak," he said. "Sir," I said, "I have heard from some teachers that there is no other repentance except that one, when we went down into the water and received forgiveness for the sins we had committed before."
31:2 He said to me, "You heard rightly, for that is how it is. Someone who has received forgiveness of sins was meant to sin no more, and to make his home in purity.
31:3 But since you ask so carefully about everything, I will explain this to you as well, without giving an excuse to those who will believe in the future or to those who have just now believed in the Lord. For those who have just now believed or will believe do not have repentance for sins. They have forgiveness of their earlier sins.
31:4 So for those who were called before these days, the Lord has set a repentance. For the Lord knows hearts and knows all things before they happen. He knew how weak people are, and how many-sided the devil's schemes are: that he would do some harm to God's slaves and act wickedly against them.
31:5 So the Lord, being full of compassion, had compassion on what he had made and set this repentance, and the authority over this repentance was given to me.
31:6 But I tell you," he said, "after that great and solemn calling, if someone is tempted by the devil and sins, he has one repentance. But if he sins again and again and repents, it does him no good. He will hardly live."
31:7 I said to him, "I have come to life again by hearing these things from you so clearly. For I know that if I no longer add to my sins, I will be saved." "You will be saved," he said, "and so will all who do these things."
## Marrying again after a death
32:1 I asked him again, "Sir, since you bear with me this once, explain this to me too." "Speak," he said. "Sir," I said, "if a wife, or a husband, dies, and the one left behind marries, does the one who marries sin?"
32:2 "He does not sin," he said. "But if he stays by himself, he gains for himself more honor and great glory with the Lord. But even if he marries, he does not sin.
32:3 So guard your purity and reverence, and you will live to God. Keep these things I tell you, and the things I am going to tell you, from now on, from the day you were handed over to me, and I will live in your house.
32:4 Your earlier wrongs will be forgiven if you keep my commandments. And all will be forgiven if they keep these commandments of mine and walk in this purity."
## Mandate 5: Patience and bad temper
33:1 "Be patient," he said, "and understanding, and you will master all evil deeds and do all that is right.
33:2 For if you are patient, the Holy Spirit who lives in you will be pure. He will not be darkened by another, evil spirit. He will live in a roomy place and will rejoice and be glad, together with the vessel he lives in. He will serve God with great cheerfulness, having well-being within himself.
33:3 But if any bad temper comes in, at once the Holy Spirit, who is delicate, feels cramped. He does not have a clean place, and he tries to leave it. For he is choked by the evil spirit and has no room to serve the Lord as he wishes, because he is defiled by the bad temper. For the Lord lives in patience, but the devil in bad temper.
33:4 So when both spirits live in the same place, it is harmful and evil for the person they live in.
33:5 Suppose you take a very little wormwood and pour it into a jar of honey. Is not the whole honey spoiled? So much honey is ruined by the tiniest bit of wormwood. It ruins the sweetness of the honey, and the honey no longer pleases its owner, because it has turned bitter and lost its use. But if the wormwood is not put into the honey, the honey is found sweet and becomes useful to its owner.
33:6 You see that patience is sweeter than honey and useful to the Lord, and he lives in it. But bad temper is bitter and useless. So if bad temper is mixed with patience, the patience is defiled, and its prayer is no longer useful to God."
33:7 "Sir," I said, "I would like to know how bad temper works, so that I can guard myself against it." "Yes, indeed," he said. "If you and your household do not guard yourselves against it, you have lost all your hope. But guard yourself against it, for I am with you. And all who repent with their whole heart will keep away from it. For I will be with them and will keep them safe, for all of them were justified by the most revered angel.
34:1 Now hear," he said, "how bad temper works, how evil it is, how it overturns my slaves by the way it works, and how it leads them away from righteousness. It does not lead astray those who are full of faith, and it cannot work on them, because my power is with them. It leads astray the empty and double-minded.
34:2 When it sees people like that standing steady, it pushes its way into that person's heart, and over nothing at all the man or woman becomes bitter: over everyday matters, or food, or some small thing, or some friend, or something given or received, or other foolish matters like these. All these things are foolish and empty and senseless and of no use to God's slaves.
34:3 But patience is great and strong. It has power, it is sturdy, and it thrives in a great open space. It is cheerful and glad and free of worry, giving glory to the Lord at all times. It has nothing bitter in it and stays gentle and quiet through everything. This patience lives with those whose faith is whole.
34:4 But bad temper is first of all foolish, flighty and senseless. Then from foolishness comes bitterness, from bitterness rage, from rage anger, and from anger lasting fury. Then this fury, built up out of so many evils, becomes a great sin that cannot be healed.
34:5 For when these spirits live in one vessel, where the Holy Spirit also lives, that vessel cannot hold them all. It overflows.
34:6 So the delicate Spirit, who is not used to living with an evil spirit or with harshness, leaves such a person and looks for a home with gentleness and quiet.
34:7 Then when he leaves the person he lived in, that person becomes empty of the righteous Spirit, and from then on he is filled with the evil spirits. He is unsettled in everything he does, pulled this way and that by the evil spirits, and his mind is completely blinded to good thinking. That is what happens to all the bad-tempered.
34:8 So keep away from bad temper, that most evil spirit. Put on patience, and stand against bad temper and bitterness, and you will be found in the company of the reverence the Lord loves. So see that you never brush this commandment aside. For if you master this commandment, you will also be able to keep the other commandments I am about to give you. Be strong in them and take power, and let all who want to walk in them take power."
## Mandate 6: The two ways and the two angels
35:1 "In the first commandment," he said, "I commanded you to keep faith, fear and self-control." "Yes, sir," I said. "But now," he said, "I want to show you their powers too, so that you understand what power and working each one has. For their workings go two ways. They have to do with what is righteous and what is unrighteous.
35:2 So trust what is righteous, and do not trust what is unrighteous. For what is righteous has a straight road, but what is unrighteous a crooked one. Walk on the straight and level road, and leave the crooked one alone.
35:3 For the crooked road has no paths, only trackless ground and many stumbling blocks. It is rough and thorny. So it is harmful to those who walk on it.
35:4 But those who walk on the straight road walk smoothly and do not stumble, for it is neither rough nor thorny. So you see that it is better to walk on this road."
35:5 "Sir," I said, "I would like to walk on this road." "You will walk on it," he said, "and so will anyone who turns to the Lord with his whole heart."
36:1 "Now hear about faith," he said. "Two angels are with each human being. One is of righteousness, and one is of wickedness."
36:2 "Then how, sir," I said, "will I know how they work, since both angels live with me?"
36:3 "Listen," he said, "and you will understand them. The angel of righteousness is delicate and modest, gentle and quiet. So when he comes into your heart, at once he talks with you about righteousness, about purity, about reverence, about being content, and about every righteous deed and every glorious virtue. When all these come into your heart, know that the angel of righteousness is with you. These, then, are the works of the angel of righteousness. So trust him and his works.
36:4 Now look at the works of the angel of wickedness as well. First of all, he is bad-tempered and bitter and senseless, and his works are evil and overturn God's slaves. So when he comes into your heart, know him by his works."
36:5 "Sir," I said, "I do not know how I will recognize him." "Listen," he said. "When some bad temper or bitterness falls on you, know that he is in you. Then there is the desire for a lot of business, and for lavish meals of many kinds, and many drinking bouts and binges, and all sorts of luxuries no one needs, and desires for women, and grabbing for more, and a great deal of arrogance and boasting, and everything close to these and like them. So when these come into your heart, know that the angel of wickedness is in you.
36:6 Now that you recognize his works, stand clear of him and do not trust him in anything, because his works are evil and of no use to God's slaves. So you have the workings of both angels. Understand them, and trust the angel of righteousness.
36:7 But stand clear of the angel of wickedness, because his teaching is evil in every deed. For if a man is faithful and the thought of this angel comes into his heart, that man, or woman, is bound to commit some sin.
36:8 But again, if a man or woman is very wicked and the works of the angel of righteousness come into his heart, he is bound to do something good.
36:9 So you see," he said, "that it is good to follow the angel of righteousness, and to say goodbye to the angel of wickedness.
36:10 This commandment shows what concerns faith: that you should trust the works of the angel of righteousness, and do them, and live to God. But believe that the works of the angel of wickedness are harsh. So if you do not do them, you will live to God."
## Mandate 7: Fear the Lord, not the devil
37:1 "Fear the Lord," he said, "and keep his commandments. If you keep God's commandments, you will be strong in everything you do, and what you do will be beyond compare. For if you fear the Lord, you will do everything well. This is the fear you should have, and so be saved.
37:2 But do not fear the devil. For if you fear the Lord, you will master the devil, because there is no power in him. Where there is no power, there is no fear either. But where there is glorious power, there is fear too. For everyone who has power is feared, but the one who has no power is looked down on by all.
37:3 But fear the works of the devil, because they are evil. So if you fear the Lord, you will not do them, but will keep away from them.
37:4 So fears are of two kinds. If you want to do evil, fear the Lord, and you will not do it. But if you want to do good, fear the Lord, and you will do it. So the fear of the Lord is strong and great and glorious. So fear the Lord, and you will live to him. And all who fear him and keep his commandments will live to God."
37:5 "Sir," I said, "why did you say of those who keep his commandments, 'They will live to God'?" "Because," he said, "all creation fears the Lord, but it does not keep his commandments. So those who fear him and keep his commandments have life with God. But as for those who do not keep his commandments, there is no life in them either."
## Mandate 8: What to hold back from, and what not
38:1 "I told you," he said, "that the things God created go two ways. Self-control goes two ways too. With some things you must hold back, and with some you must not."
38:2 "Sir," I said, "tell me which things I must hold back from, and which not." "Listen," he said. "Hold back from evil and do not do it. But do not hold back from good: do it. For if you hold back from doing good, you commit a great sin. But if you hold back from doing evil, you do great righteousness. So hold back from every kind of wickedness, and do what is good."
38:3 "Sir," I said, "what kinds of wickedness must we hold back from?" "Listen," he said: "from adultery and sexual sin, from lawless drunkenness, from evil luxury, from lavish meals and showy wealth, from bragging, high-mindedness and arrogance, from lying, slander and hypocrisy, from holding grudges, and from all abuse.
38:4 These deeds are the most evil of all in human life. So God's slave must hold back from these deeds. For the one who does not hold back from these cannot live to God. So hear also the things that go with them."
38:5 "Sir," I said, "are there still more evil deeds?" "Yes, many," he said, "that God's slave must hold back from: stealing, lying, cheating, false witness, greed, evil desire, deceit, vanity, boasting, and everything like these.
38:6 Do you not think these are evil?" "Very evil," I said, "for God's slaves." "The one who serves God as his slave must hold back from all of these. So hold back from all of them, so that you may live to God and be enrolled with those who hold back from them. These, then, are the things you must hold back from.
38:7 Now hear," he said, "the things you must not hold back from, but must do. Do not hold back from what is good, but do it."
38:8 "Sir," I said, "show me the power of the good things too, so that I may walk in them and serve them, and by doing them be able to be saved." "Listen," he said, "to the works of the good things too, which you must do and not hold back from.
38:9 First of all, faith, the fear of the Lord, love, harmony, words of righteousness, truth and endurance. Nothing in human life is better than these. If anyone keeps these and does not hold back from them, he is blessed in his life.
38:10 Then hear what goes with them: serving widows, looking after orphans and people in need, buying God's slaves out of their troubles, being hospitable (for in hospitality, now and then, good is done), opposing no one, being quiet, making do with less than everyone else, respecting the old, practicing righteousness, keeping the brotherhood together, putting up with insults, being patient, not holding grudges, encouraging those who are weary in spirit, not throwing away those who have stumbled from the faith but turning them back and putting heart into them, warning those who sin, not squeezing debtors and people in need, and anything else like these.
38:11 Do you think these things are good?" he said. "Sir," I said, "what is better than these?" "Then walk in them," he said, "and do not hold back from them, and you will live to God.
38:12 So keep this commandment. If you do good and do not hold back from it, you will live to God, and all who act this way will live to God. And again, if you do not do evil, and hold back from it, you will live to God, and all who keep these commandments and walk in them will live to God."
## Mandate 9: Do not be double-minded
39:1 He said to me, "Remove double-mindedness from yourself, and do not be double-minded at all about asking God for something, saying to yourself, 'How can I ask the Lord for something and receive it, when I have sinned against him so much?'
39:2 Do not reason this way. Turn to the Lord with your whole heart and ask him without hesitating, and you will know his great compassion: he will never abandon you, but will fulfill what your soul asks for.
39:3 For God is not like people who hold grudges. He holds no grudge, and he has compassion on what he has made.
39:4 So clean your heart of all the empty things of this age, and of the words I told you before, and ask the Lord. You will receive everything, and you will not go without any of the things you ask for, if you ask the Lord without hesitating.
39:5 But if you hesitate in your heart, you will receive none of the things you ask for. For those who hesitate toward God are the double-minded, and they get none of what they ask for at all.
39:6 But those who are complete in faith ask for everything trusting in the Lord, and they receive, because they ask without hesitating, not double-minded at all. For every double-minded man, unless he repents, will hardly be saved.
39:7 So clean your heart of double-mindedness. Put on faith, because it is strong, and trust God that you will receive all the things you ask for. And if you ever ask the Lord for something and receive it slowly, do not be double-minded because you did not get what your soul asked for quickly. For surely it is because of some testing or some wrong you do not know about that you are receiving what you asked for slowly.
39:8 So do not stop asking for what your soul asks for, and you will receive it. But if you grow weary and are double-minded as you ask, blame yourself and not the One who gives to you.
39:9 Look at this double-mindedness. It is evil and senseless, and it pulls many up by the roots from the faith, even people who are very faithful and strong. For this double-mindedness is a daughter of the devil, and it does great evil to God's slaves.
39:10 So look down on double-mindedness and master it in every matter, clothed with faith, which is strong and powerful. For faith promises everything and finishes everything. But double-mindedness, which does not trust itself, fails in every work it does.
39:11 So you see," he said, "that faith comes from above, from the Lord, and has great power. But double-mindedness is an earthly spirit from the devil, with no power.
39:12 So serve faith, which has power, and keep away from double-mindedness, which has no power, and you will live to God, and all who think this way will live to God."
## Mandate 10: Grief and the Holy Spirit
40:1 "Remove grief from yourself," he said, "for it too is a sister of double-mindedness and of bad temper."
40:2 "Sir," I said, "how is it their sister? For bad temper seems to me to be one thing, double-mindedness another, and grief another." "You are a senseless man," he said, "and you do not see that grief is more evil than all the spirits and most terrible to God's slaves. More than all the spirits it ruins a person and wears out the Holy Spirit, and then again it saves."
40:3 "Sir," I said, "I am senseless, and I do not understand these parables. For how it can wear out and then again save, I do not see."
40:4 "Listen," he said. "There are those who never searched for the truth or looked into the divine nature. They simply believed, but they are tangled up in business and wealth and friendships with Gentiles and many other affairs of this age. All who are devoted to these things do not understand the parables about the divine nature. For they are darkened by these activities. They are ruined and become dry and barren.
40:5 Good vineyards, when they are neglected, go wild with thorns and weeds of every kind. In the same way, people who have believed and then fall into these many activities I have spoken of wander away from their good sense and understand nothing at all about righteousness. Even when they hear about the divine nature and about the truth, their mind is taken up with their business, and they understand nothing at all.
40:6 But those who have the fear of God and search into the divine nature and the truth, and have their heart toward the Lord, understand everything said to them more quickly and grasp it, because they have the fear of the Lord in them. For where the Lord lives, there is great understanding too. So hold fast to the Lord, and you will understand and grasp everything.
41:1 So listen, foolish one," he said, "to how grief wears out the Holy Spirit and then again saves.
41:2 When the double-minded person takes on some task and fails at it because of his double-mindedness, this grief goes into the person and grieves the Holy Spirit and wears him out.
41:3 Then again, when bad temper takes hold of a person over some matter, and he becomes very bitter, again grief goes into the heart of the person who lost his temper. He is grieved over what he did, and he repents, because he did evil.
41:4 So this grief seems to bring salvation, because he did evil and repented. So both actions grieve the Spirit: double-mindedness, because it did not succeed at its task, and bad temper grieves the Spirit because it did evil. So both are grievous to the Holy Spirit, double-mindedness and bad temper.
41:5 So remove grief from yourself, and do not crush the Holy Spirit who lives in you, or else he may plead with God and leave you.
41:6 For the Spirit of God who was given to this flesh does not put up with grief or with being cramped.
42:1 So put on cheerfulness, which always finds favor with God and is welcome to him, and take your delight in it. For every cheerful man does good, and thinks good, and looks down on grief.
42:2 But the grieving man always does evil. First, he does evil because he grieves the Holy Spirit, who was given to people cheerful. Second, by grieving the Holy Spirit he commits lawlessness, by not praying to the Lord or confessing to him. For the prayer of a grieving man never has the power to go up to God's altar."
42:3 "Why," I said, "does the prayer of the one who grieves not go up to the altar?" "Because," he said, "grief has settled in his heart. So grief, mixed with his prayer, does not let the prayer go up pure to the altar. For just as vinegar and wine mixed together do not give the same enjoyment, so grief mixed with the Holy Spirit does not give the same prayer.
42:4 So clean yourself of this evil grief, and you will live to God. And all who throw grief away from themselves and put on every kind of cheerfulness will live to God."
## Mandate 11: The true prophet and the false
43:1 He showed me people sitting on a bench, and another man sitting on a chair. He said to me, "Do you see the people sitting on the bench?" "I see them, sir," I said. "These are faithful," he said, "and the one sitting on the chair is a false prophet, who destroys the understanding of God's slaves. But it is the understanding of the double-minded he destroys, not of the faithful.
43:2 So these double-minded people come to him as to a fortune-teller and ask him what will happen to them. And that false prophet, who has no power of a divine spirit in him, talks with them according to their questions and their wicked desires, and fills their souls with what they want to hear.
43:3 For he is empty himself, and he gives empty answers to empty people. Whatever he is asked, he answers to match the person's emptiness. But he also says some true things, for the devil fills him with his own spirit, to see if he can break any of the righteous.
43:4 So those who are strong in the faith of the Lord, clothed with the truth, do not attach themselves to spirits like that, but keep away from them. But those who are double-minded and are always repenting go to fortune-tellers, as the Gentiles also do, and bring a greater sin on themselves by worshiping idols. For whoever asks a false prophet about some matter is an idol worshiper, empty of the truth and senseless.
43:5 For no spirit given by God has to be questioned. It has the power of the divine nature and speaks everything by itself, because it is from above, from the power of the divine Spirit.
43:6 But the spirit that is questioned and speaks according to people's desires is earthly and flighty, without power. And it says nothing at all unless it is questioned."
43:7 "Then how, sir," I said, "will a person know which of them is a prophet and which a false prophet?" "Hear about both kinds of prophet," he said. "As I am about to tell you, so you will test the prophet and the false prophet. Test the person who has the divine Spirit by his life.
43:8 First, the one who has the Spirit from above is gentle and quiet and humble-minded. He keeps away from all wickedness and from the empty desires of this age. He makes himself poorer than all people. He gives no answers to anyone who questions him. He does not speak in private. And the Holy Spirit does not speak when a person wants to speak. He speaks when God wants him to speak.
43:9 So when the person who has the divine Spirit comes into a gathering of righteous men who have faith in the divine Spirit, and that gathering of men prays to God, then the angel of the prophetic spirit who is assigned to him fills the person. Filled with the Holy Spirit, the person speaks to the crowd as the Lord wishes.
43:10 This is how the Spirit of the divine nature will be made known. Such, then, is the power of the Lord concerning the Spirit of the divine nature.
43:11 Now hear," he said, "about the spirit that is earthly and empty and has no power, but is foolish.
43:12 First, that person who seems to have a spirit lifts himself up and wants to have the seat of honor. He is quickly bold, shameless and full of talk. He lives among many luxuries and many other deceits, and he takes pay for his prophecy. If he does not get it, he does not prophesy. Can a divine spirit take pay and prophesy? A prophet of God cannot do this. The spirit of prophets like that is earthly.
43:13 Then too, he never comes near a gathering of righteous men, but runs from them. He attaches himself to the double-minded and empty, prophesies to them in a corner, and deceives them, saying everything emptily to suit their desires. For he gives his answers to empty people. An empty vessel set together with empty ones does not break: they suit one another.
43:14 But when he comes into a gathering full of righteous men who have a Spirit of the divine nature, and they pray, that person is emptied. The earthly spirit runs from him in fear, and that person is struck dumb and completely shattered, unable to say anything.
43:15 For if you stack wine or oil in a storeroom and put an empty jar among them, and then you want to clear the storeroom, you will find that jar you put there empty still empty. So too the empty prophets: when they come among the spirits of righteous people, they are found just as they came.
43:16 You have the life of both kinds of prophet. So test the person who says he carries the Spirit by his works and his life.
43:17 But you, trust the Spirit who comes from God and has power. Do not trust the earthly and empty spirit at all, because there is no power in it. For it comes from the devil.
43:18 So hear the parable I am about to tell you. Take a stone and throw it up at the sky. See if you can touch it. Or again, take a squirt of water and squirt it up at the sky. See if you can make a hole in the sky."
43:19 "Sir," I said, "how can these things happen? Both things you have named are impossible." "Just as these things are impossible," he said, "so the earthly spirits are powerless and weak.
43:20 Now take the power that comes from above. Hail is a very tiny grain, yet when it falls on a person's head, how it hurts! Or again, take a drop that falls from the roof tile to the ground and makes a hole in the stone.
43:21 So you see that the smallest things coming from above have great power when they fall on the earth. So too the divine Spirit, coming from above, is powerful. So trust this Spirit, and keep away from the other."
## Mandate 12: Evil desire and good desire
44:1 He said to me, "Remove every evil desire from yourself, and put on the desire that is good and reverent. For if you are clothed with this desire, you will hate evil desire and will rein it in as you wish.
44:2 For evil desire is wild and hard to tame. It is frightening, and by its wildness it eats people up badly. Above all, if a slave of God falls into it and has no understanding, he is eaten up by it terribly. It eats up people like that, who do not have the garment of good desire but are tangled up in this age. These it hands over to death."
44:3 "Sir," I said, "what are the works of evil desire that hand people over to death? Tell me, so that I may keep away from them." "Listen," he said, "to the works by which evil desire puts God's slaves to death.
45:1 Above all, desire for another man's wife, or another woman's husband, and for showy wealth, many empty foods, drinking bouts and many other foolish luxuries. For every luxury is foolish and empty for God's slaves.
45:2 These, then, are the evil desires that put God's slaves to death. For this evil desire is the devil's daughter. So you must keep away from the evil desires, so that by keeping away you may live to God.
45:3 But all who are mastered by them and do not stand against them will die in the end. For these desires are deadly.
45:4 But you, put on the desire for righteousness, arm yourself with the fear of the Lord, and stand against them. For the fear of God lives in good desire. If evil desire sees you armed with the fear of God and standing against it, it will run far away from you and will never be seen by you again, because it fears your weapons.
45:5 So when you have won and been crowned for your victory over it, come to the desire for righteousness. Hand over to it the victory prize you received, and serve it as it wishes. If you serve good desire and put yourself under it, you will be able to master evil desire and put it under you as you wish."
46:1 "Sir," I said, "I would like to know in what ways I must serve good desire." "Listen," he said. "Do righteousness and virtue, truth and the fear of the Lord, faith and gentleness, and all the good things like these. If you do these, you will be a slave of God who pleases him, and you will live to him. And everyone who serves good desire will live to God."
## Can the commandments be kept?
46:2 So he finished the twelve commandments and said to me, "You have these commandments. Walk in them, and urge those who hear them that their repentance may be pure for the rest of the days of their life.
46:3 Carry out with care this task of service I am giving you, and you will get much done. For you will find favor with those who are going to repent, and they will be persuaded by your words. For I will be with you and will make them be persuaded by you."
46:4 I said to him, "Sir, these commandments are great and good and glorious, and able to gladden the heart of a person who is able to keep them. But I do not know if these commandments can be kept by a human being, because they are very hard."
46:5 He answered me, "If you set it before yourself that they can be kept, you will keep them easily, and they will not be hard. But if it has already come into your heart that a human being cannot keep them, you will not keep them.
46:6 But now I tell you: if you do not keep them, but brush them aside, you will not have salvation, and neither will your children or your household, since you have already decided for yourself that these commandments cannot be kept by a human being."
47:1 He said these things to me very angrily, so that I was confused and very afraid of him. For his form changed, so that no human being could bear his anger.
47:2 But seeing me all shaken and confused, he began to speak to me more gently and more cheerfully. He said, "You foolish, senseless, double-minded man, do you not see the glory of God, how great it is and strong and wonderful? He created the world for humankind's sake, he put all his creation under humankind, and he gave him all authority to master everything under heaven.
47:3 So if," he said, "a human being is lord of all God's creatures and masters them all, can he not master these commandments too? A person who has the Lord in his heart," he said, "can master everything and all these commandments.
47:4 But those who have the Lord on their lips, while their heart is hardened and they are far from the Lord: for them these commandments are hard and rough going.
47:5 So you who are empty and flighty in the faith, put your Lord into your heart, and you will know that nothing is easier than these commandments, or sweeter, or gentler.
47:6 Turn back, you who walk in the devil's commandments, which are hard and bitter and wild and shameless. And do not fear the devil, because he has no power against you.
47:7 For I will be with you, the angel of repentance who masters him. The devil only causes fear, but his fear has no strength. So do not fear him, and he will run away from you."
48:1 I said to him, "Sir, listen to a few words from me." "Say what you wish," he said. "Sir," I said, "people are eager to keep God's commandments. There is no one who does not ask the Lord to make him strong in his commandments and to put him under them. But the devil is hard, and he overpowers them."
48:2 "He cannot," he said, "overpower God's slaves who hope in him with their whole heart. The devil can wrestle with them, but he cannot throw them. So if you stand against him, he will be beaten and run away from you in shame. But those who are empty," he said, "fear the devil as if he had power.
48:3 When a person fills a great many jars with good wine, and a few of those jars are only partly full, he comes to the jars and does not inspect the full ones, for he knows that they are full. But he inspects the partly empty ones, afraid that they have gone sour. For partly empty jars go sour quickly, and the wine loses its pleasure.
48:4 So too the devil comes to all God's slaves to test them. Those who are full in the faith stand against him strongly, and he goes away from them, because he finds no place to get in. So then he comes to the partly empty, and finding a place, he goes into them and does in them whatever he wants, and they become his slaves.
## The angel of repentance: do not fear the devil
49:1 But I, the angel of repentance, say to you: do not fear the devil. For I was sent," he said, "to be with you who repent with your whole heart, and to make you strong in the faith.
49:2 So trust God, you who have given up on your life because of your sins, who keep adding to your sins and weighing your life down. For if you turn to the Lord with your whole heart, and do what is right for the rest of the days of your life, and serve him rightly as he wills, he will bring healing to your earlier sins, and you will have power to master the works of the devil. Do not fear the devil's threat at all, for it is as limp as a dead man's sinews.
49:3 So listen to me, and fear the One who can do everything, save and destroy, and keep these commandments, and you will live to God."
49:4 I said to him, "Sir, now I have been made strong in all the Lord's rulings, because you are with me. And I know that you will smash all the devil's power, and we will master him and overcome all his works. And I hope, sir, with the Lord making me strong, to be able to keep these commandments you have given me."
49:5 "You will keep them," he said, "if your heart becomes pure toward the Lord. And all will keep them who clean their hearts of the empty desires of this age, and they will live to God."
