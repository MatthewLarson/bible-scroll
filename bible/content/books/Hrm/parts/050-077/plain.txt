## Similitude 1: Two cities
50:1 He said to me, "You know that you, the slaves of God, live in a foreign land. For your city is far from this city. So if you know your city, the one where you are going to live, why are you getting fields ready here, and costly estates, buildings and empty houses?
50:2 Whoever gets these things ready for this city cannot return to his own city.
50:3 You foolish, double-minded, miserable man! Don't you see that all these things belong to someone else and are under another's authority? For the lord of this city will say, 'I do not want you living in my city. Get out of this city, because you do not live by my laws.'
50:4 So you have fields and houses and many other possessions. When he throws you out, what will you do with your field and your house and everything else you got ready for yourself? For the lord of this country says to you, and he has the right, 'Either live by my laws or leave my country.'
50:5 Then what are you going to do? You have a law in your own city. Will you deny your law completely for the sake of your fields and your other property, and walk by the law of this city? Watch out. Denying your law may not be to your good. For if you want to return to your city, you will never be let in, because you denied the law of your city. You will be shut out of it.
50:6 So watch out. Since you live in a foreign land, get nothing more ready for yourself than what is enough for you to live on. Be ready, so that when the master of this city wants to throw you out for opposing his law, you can leave his city and go to your own city. There you will live by your own law, with no one to insult you, full of joy.
50:7 So watch out, you who serve the Lord as slaves and have him in your heart. Do the works of God. Remember his commandments and the promises he made, and believe him, that he will keep them if his commandments are kept.
50:8 So instead of fields, buy souls in distress, as each of you is able. Visit widows and orphans and do not overlook them. Spend your wealth and all your estates on fields and houses like these, which you received from God.
50:9 This is why the Master made you rich: so that you would carry out these services for him. It is much better to buy fields and property and houses like these, which you will find in your city when you come home to it.
50:10 This lavish spending is good and holy. It has no grief and no fear. It has joy. So do not practice the lavish spending of the nations, for it does you no good, you slaves of God.
50:11 Practice your own lavish spending, the kind you can rejoice in. Do not counterfeit, do not touch what belongs to someone else, and do not desire it. For it is evil to desire other people's things. Do your own work, and you will be saved."
## Similitude 2: The elm and the vine
51:1 I was walking in the field, looking at an elm and a vine, and thinking about them and their fruit. Then the Shepherd appeared to me and said, "What are you puzzling over about the elm and the vine?" "Sir," I said, "I am thinking that they suit each other perfectly."
51:2 "These two trees," he said, "are set up as a pattern for the slaves of God." "I would like to know," I said, "the pattern in these trees you are talking about." "Do you see the elm and the vine?" he said. "I see them, sir," I said.
51:3 "This vine," he said, "bears fruit, but the elm is a tree that bears no fruit. Yet unless this vine climbs up onto the elm, it cannot bear much fruit while it lies on the ground. And the fruit it does bear is rotten, because it is not hanging on the elm. So when the vine is trained onto the elm, it bears fruit from itself and from the elm too.
51:4 So you see that the elm also gives much fruit, not less than the vine, but even more." "How does it give more, sir?" I said. "Because," he said, "the vine gives a lot of good fruit when it hangs on the elm, but on the ground it bears little, and that is rotten. So this parable is set up for the slaves of God, for the poor and the rich."
51:5 "How, sir?" I said. "Explain it to me." "Listen," he said. "The rich man has money, but toward the Lord he is poor, because he is distracted by his wealth. His prayers for others and his praise to the Lord are very small. And what he has is weak and small and has no other power. So when the rich man leans on the poor man and supplies what he needs, he believes that what he does for the poor man will find him a reward with God. For the poor man is rich in prayer for others and in praise, and his prayer has great power with God. So the rich man supplies the poor man with everything, without hesitating.
51:6 And the poor man, supplied by the rich man, prays to God for him and thanks God for the one who gives to him. And the rich man keeps on caring for the poor man eagerly, so that the poor man will lack nothing all his life. For he knows that the poor man's prayer is acceptable to the Lord and rich before him.
51:7 So the two of them complete the work together. The poor man works with prayer, which makes him rich, and which he received from the Lord. He pays this back to the Lord who supplies him. And the rich man in the same way gives the poor man, without hesitating, the wealth he received from the Lord. This is a great work, and God accepts it, because the rich man understood what his wealth was for. He worked for the poor man out of the Lord's gifts and carried out his service rightly.
51:8 Now people think the elm bears no fruit. They do not know or see that when a drought comes, the elm holds water and feeds the vine. Then the vine, with water that never fails, gives back a double crop, for itself and for the elm. In the same way the poor pray to the Lord for the rich and make their wealth complete. And the rich, supplying the poor with what they need, make their prayers complete.
51:9 So both become partners in the righteous work. Whoever does these things will not be abandoned by God, but will be written in the books of the living.
51:10 Blessed are those who have wealth and understand that they are made rich by the Lord. For whoever understands this will be able to do some good service."
## Similitude 3: Trees in winter
52:1 He showed me many trees with no leaves. They looked dry to me, for they were all alike. And he said to me, "Do you see these trees?" "I see them, sir," I said. "They are all alike, and dry." He answered me, "These trees that you see are the people who live in this world."
52:2 "Then why, sir," I said, "do they look dry and alike?" "Because," he said, "in this world neither the righteous nor the sinners can be seen for what they are. They are alike. For this world is winter for the righteous, and they cannot be told apart, since they live among the sinners.
52:3 In winter the trees have shed their leaves and look alike, and no one can see which are dry and which are alive. In the same way, in this world neither the righteous nor the sinners can be seen for what they are. All are alike."
## Similitude 4: Trees in summer
53:1 He showed me many trees again. Some were budding and some were dry. And he said to me, "Do you see these trees?" "I see them, sir," I said. "Some are budding and the others are dry."
53:2 "These budding trees," he said, "are the righteous, who are going to live in the age that is coming. For the age that is coming is summer for the righteous, but winter for the sinners. So when the Lord's mercy shines out, then those who serve God will be shown for what they are. Everyone will be shown for what they are.
53:3 In summer the fruit of each tree comes out, and people can tell what kind it is. In the same way the fruit of the righteous will be seen. All of them will be known to be thriving in that age.
53:4 But the nations and the sinners, the dry trees you saw, will be found like that: dry and without fruit in that age. They will be burned up like wood, and they will be seen for what they are, because the way they acted in their life was evil. The sinners will be burned because they sinned and did not repent. The nations will be burned because they did not know the one who created them.
53:5 So bear fruit, so that in that summer your fruit may be known. Stay out of too many dealings, and you will never sin. For people who do many things also sin in many things. They are distracted by their business and do not serve their Lord at all.
53:6 So how," he said, "can such a person ask anything of the Lord and receive it, if he does not serve the Lord? Those who serve him will receive what they ask for. But those who do not serve the Lord will receive nothing.
53:7 But if someone works at one business, he can serve the Lord too. For his mind will not be corrupted away from the Lord. He will serve him with a pure mind.
53:8 So if you do these things, you can bear fruit for the age that is coming. And whoever does these things will bear fruit."
## Similitude 5: The fast that God accepts
54:1 I was fasting and sitting on a mountain, thanking the Lord for all he had done for me. Then I saw the Shepherd sitting down beside me, and he said, "Why have you come here so early in the morning?" "Because, sir," I said, "I am keeping a station."
54:2 "What is a station?" he said. "I am fasting, sir," I said. "And what is this fast you are keeping?" he said. "I am fasting the way I used to, sir," I said.
54:3 "You people do not know how to fast to the Lord," he said. "This useless fast you keep for him is not a fast." "Why do you say this, sir?" I said. "I tell you," he said, "that what you think is fasting is not a fast. But I will teach you what a fast is that is full and acceptable to the Lord. Listen," he said.
54:4 "God does not want an empty fast like this. For if you fast to God this way, you do nothing for righteousness. Instead, keep this kind of fast to God:
54:5 Do nothing evil in your life, but serve the Lord as his slave with a pure heart. Keep his commandments and walk by his instructions, and do not let any evil desire rise up in your heart. Believe God: if you do these things, and fear him, and keep yourself from every evil act, you will live to God. If you do these things, you will keep a great fast, one that God accepts.
## The slave in the vineyard
55:1 Listen to the parable I am going to tell you. It has to do with fasting.
55:2 A man had a field and many slaves, and he planted a vineyard on part of the field. He chose a slave who was faithful, who pleased him and whom he valued. He called him and said, 'Take this vineyard I planted and put stakes in it until I come. Do nothing else to the vineyard. Keep this order of mine, and I will set you free.' Then the slave's master went away on a journey abroad.
55:3 After he left, the slave went and put stakes in the vineyard. When he finished staking the vineyard, he saw that it was full of weeds.
55:4 So he thought it over and said to himself, 'I have carried out my master's order. Next I will dig this vineyard. It will look better dug up. And with no weeds it will give more fruit, because the weeds will not choke it.' So he went and dug the vineyard and pulled out all the weeds in it. And that vineyard became beautiful and thriving, with no weeds to choke it.
55:5 After a time the master of the slave and of the field came back and went into the vineyard. He saw the vineyard neatly staked, and dug too, and all the weeds pulled out, and the vines thriving. He was very glad at what the slave had done.
55:6 So he called his beloved son, who was his heir, and the friends who were his advisers. He told them what he had ordered his slave to do and what he had found done. And they shared the slave's joy at the good word his master had spoken for him.
55:7 He said to them, 'I promised this slave his freedom if he kept the order I gave him. He kept my order, and he added a good work to the vineyard as well, and it pleased me very much. So in return for this work he did, I want to make him an heir together with my son. He thought of something good, and he did not let it go. He carried it out.'
55:8 The master's son agreed with him in this plan, that the slave should become an heir together with the son.
55:9 A few days later the master gave a dinner and sent the slave many dishes of food from it. The slave took the dishes his master had sent him, kept what was enough for himself, and shared the rest with his fellow slaves.
55:10 His fellow slaves were glad to get the food. They began to pray for him, that he would find even more favor with the master, because he had treated them so well.
55:11 His master heard about all these things, and again he was very glad at what the slave had done. The master called his friends and his son together again and told them what the slave had done with the food he had received. And they agreed all the more that the slave should become an heir together with his son.
56:1 I said, "Sir, I do not know what these parables mean, and I cannot understand them unless you explain them to me."
56:2 "I will explain everything to you," he said, "whatever I talk about with you.
56:3 I will show you his commandments. But if you do some good beyond what God commands, you will win more glory for yourself, and you will be held in higher honor with God than you would have been. So if you keep God's commandments and add these services too, you will be glad, if you keep them the way I command."
56:4 I said to him, "Sir, whatever you command me, I will keep it, for I know that you are with me." "I will be with you," he said, "because you are so eager to do good. And I will be with all who are this eager," he said.
56:5 "This fast," he said, "is very good, when the Lord's commandments are kept. So this is how you will keep the fast you are going to keep.
56:6 First of all, guard yourself against every evil word and every evil desire, and clean your heart of all the empty things of this world. If you guard yourself against these things, this fast will be complete for you.
56:7 And this is what you will do. When you have carried out what is written, on the day you fast you will taste nothing but bread and water. Then add up what the food you would have eaten that day would have cost, and give that amount to a widow or an orphan or someone in need. In this way you will be humble, so that the one who has received from your humility may fill his own soul and pray to the Lord for you.
56:8 So if you complete the fast the way I commanded you, your sacrifice will be acceptable to God. This fast will be written down, and service done this way is good and glad, and very acceptable to the Lord.
56:9 You will keep these things this way, you and your children and your whole household. When you have kept them, you will be blessed. And all who hear them and keep them will be blessed, and they will receive whatever they ask of the Lord."
## The parable explained
57:1 I begged him again and again to make clear to me the parable of the field, the master, the vineyard, the slave who staked the vineyard, the stakes, the weeds pulled out of the vineyard, the son, and the friends who were advisers. For I understood that all these things were some kind of parable.
57:2 But he answered me, "You are far too bold with your questions. You ought not to ask anything at all. For if you need to have something made clear, it will be made clear." I said to him, "Sir, whatever you show me and do not make clear, I will have seen for nothing, if I do not understand what it is. In the same way, if you tell me parables and do not explain them to me, I will have heard something from you for nothing."
57:3 He answered me again, "Whoever is a slave of God and has his Lord in his heart asks him for understanding and receives it, and he explains every parable. The Lord's words that are spoken in parables become known to him. But those who are weak and lazy about prayer hesitate to ask anything of the Lord.
57:4 Yet the Lord is full of compassion and gives to all who ask him, without fail. But you have been made strong by the holy angel and have received this kind of prayer from him, and you are not lazy. Why don't you ask the Lord for understanding and receive it from him?"
57:5 I said to him, "Sir, since I have you with me, I have to ask you and question you. For you show me everything and talk with me. But if I saw or heard these things without you, I would ask the Lord to make them clear to me."
58:1 "I told you just now," he said, "that you are sly and bold, asking for the explanations of the parables. But since you keep at it like this, I will explain to you the parable of the field and all the rest that goes with it, so that you can make it known to everyone. Now listen," he said, "and understand it.
58:2 The field is this world. The lord of the field is the one who created all things, put them in order and gave them power. The slave is the Son of God. The vines are this people, whom he himself planted.
58:3 The stakes are the Lord's holy angels, who hold his people together. The weeds pulled out of the vineyard are the lawless acts of God's slaves. The dishes he sent him from the dinner are the commandments he gave his people through his Son. The friends and advisers are the holy angels who were created first. The master's journey abroad is the time that is left until his coming."
58:4 I said to him, "Sir, all this is great and wonderful and glorious. Could I," I said, "have understood these things? No one else could understand them either, even someone very wise. Sir," I said, "make clear to me something more that I am going to ask you."
58:5 "Say what you want," he said. "Sir," I said, "why is the Son of God put in the parable in the form of a slave?"
59:1 "Listen," he said. "The Son of God is not put in the form of a slave. He is put in great authority and lordship." "How, sir?" I said. "I do not understand."
59:2 "Because," he said, "God planted the vineyard. That is, he created the people and handed them over to his Son. And the Son put the angels over them to guard them. And he himself cleansed their sins, working hard and bearing much toil. For no vineyard can be dug without toil or labor.
59:3 So when he had cleansed the people's sins, he showed them the paths of life and gave them the law that he received from his Father.
59:4 Now listen to why the Lord took his Son and the glorious angels as advisers about the slave's inheritance.
59:5 The Holy Spirit, who existed before, who created all creation, God made to live in flesh that he wanted. This flesh, in which the Holy Spirit lived, served the Spirit well as a slave. It walked in reverence and purity and did not defile the Spirit in any way.
59:6 So it lived well and purely, worked hard with the Spirit and worked together with it in everything, and conducted itself with strength and courage. Then he chose it as a partner with the Holy Spirit. For the way this flesh lived pleased him, because while it had the Holy Spirit it was not defiled on earth.
59:7 So he took the Son and the glorious angels as advisers, so that this flesh too, which had served the Spirit as a slave without fault, might have some place to live, and might not seem to have lost the reward for its service as a slave. For all flesh in which the Holy Spirit has lived will receive a reward, if it is found undefiled and spotless.
59:8 Now you have the explanation of this parable too."
60:1 "Sir," I said, "I am glad I heard this explanation." "Now listen," he said. "Keep this flesh of yours pure and undefiled, so that the Spirit that lives in it may testify for it, and your flesh may be justified.
60:2 Watch out, so that it never enters your heart that this flesh of yours is perishable, and you misuse it in some defilement. If you defile your flesh, you will defile the Holy Spirit too. And if you defile the flesh, you will not live."
60:3 "But sir," I said, "if someone was ignorant before these words were heard, how can a person who defiled his flesh be saved?" "As for the earlier sins of ignorance," he said, "only God can give healing, for all authority is his,
60:4 if from now on you do not defile your flesh or the Spirit. For the two belong together, and one cannot be defiled without the other. So keep both pure, and you will live to God."
## Similitude 6: Two shepherds
61:1 I was sitting in my house, praising the Lord for all I had seen, and thinking over the commandments: they are good and strong and glad and glorious, and able to save a person's soul. I said to myself, "I will be blessed if I walk by these commandments. And whoever walks by them will be blessed."
61:2 While I was saying this to myself, I suddenly saw him sitting beside me, and he said, "Why are you double-minded about the commandments I gave you? They are good. Do not be double-minded at all. Put on faith in the Lord, and you will walk in them. For I will make you strong in them.
61:3 These commandments are good for those who are going to repent. For if they do not walk in them, their repentance is for nothing.
61:4 So you who repent, throw off the evils of this world that wear you out. If you put on every virtue of righteousness, you will be able to keep these commandments and add no more to your sins. So walk by these commandments of mine, and you will live to God. All these things I have told you."
61:5 After he had said these things to me, he said, "Let's go into the country, and I will show you the shepherds of the sheep." "Let's go, sir," I said. We came to a plain, and he showed me a young shepherd dressed in a suit of clothes the color of saffron.
61:6 He was grazing a very large flock of sheep. These sheep seemed to be living in luxury and running wild. They were glad, skipping here and there. And the shepherd himself was very glad over his flock. The shepherd's very look was full of gladness, and he ran about among the sheep.
62:1 And he said to me, "Do you see this shepherd?" "I see him, sir," I said. "This," he said, "is the angel of luxury and deceit. He wears out the souls of God's slaves and turns them away from the truth. He deceives them with evil desires, and they are lost in them.
62:2 For they forget the commandments of the living God and walk in deceits and empty luxuries, and they are lost through this angel, some to death and some to ruin."
62:3 I said to him, "Sir, I do not know what 'to death' means and what 'to ruin' means." "Listen," he said. "The sheep you saw glad and skipping are those who have torn themselves away from God for good and given themselves over to the desires of this world. For these there is no repentance that leads to life, because they added to their sins and spoke evil against the name of God. Death belongs to people like these.
62:4 The sheep you saw that were not skipping, but grazing in one place, are those who have given themselves over to luxuries and deceits but have spoken no evil against the Lord. These have been ruined and turned from the truth. For these there is hope of repentance, by which they can live. So ruin has hope of some renewal, but death brings destruction forever."
62:5 Again I went on a little way, and he showed me a big shepherd, wild-looking, wearing a white goatskin. He had a bag on his shoulders and a very hard staff full of knots, and a big whip. His glare was so bitter that I was afraid of him. That was the look he had.
62:6 This shepherd took over sheep from the young shepherd, the ones that were running wild and living in luxury but not skipping. He threw them into a place full of cliffs, thorns and thistles, so that the sheep could not get free of the thorns and thistles but were caught in them.
62:7 So they grazed caught among the thorns and thistles, and they were very miserable from the beatings he gave them. He drove them here and there and gave them no rest. Those sheep could not stand still at all.
63:1 When I saw them whipped and made miserable like this, I grieved for them, because they were tortured like this and had no relief at all.
63:2 I said to the Shepherd who was talking with me, "Sir, who is this shepherd who is so heartless and bitter, and has no pity at all on these sheep?" "This," he said, "is the angel of punishment. He is one of the righteous angels, but he is put in charge of punishment.
63:3 So he takes over those who wander away from God and walk in the desires and deceits of this world. He punishes them as they deserve, with harsh punishments of many kinds."
63:4 "I would like to know, sir," I said, "what these punishments of many kinds are." "Listen," he said, "to the many kinds of torments and punishments. The torments belong to everyday life. Some are punished with losses, some with need, some with sicknesses of every kind, some with all kinds of unrest, some by being insulted by worthless people and suffering in many other affairs.
63:5 For many are unsettled in their plans. They take on many things, and nothing ever goes well for them. They say that they do not succeed in their affairs, and it does not enter their heart that they have done evil works. Instead, they blame the Lord.
63:6 So when they have been afflicted with every kind of suffering, then they are handed over to me for good training. They are made strong in faith in the Lord, and for the rest of their days they serve the Lord with a pure heart. If they repent, then the evil works they did come into their heart, and then they praise God and say that he is a righteous judge and that they suffered justly, each for his own deeds. From then on they serve the Lord with a pure heart, and they succeed in everything they do, receiving from the Lord whatever they ask. Then they praise the Lord that they were handed over to me, and they no longer suffer any of the evils."
64:1 I said to him, "Sir, make one more thing clear to me." "What do you want to know?" he said. "Sir," I said, "are those who live in luxury and are deceived tormented for the same length of time as they lived in luxury and were deceived?" He said to me, "They are tormented for the same length of time."
64:2 "Sir," I said, "that is very little torment. People who live in luxury like this and forget God ought to be tormented seven times as long."
64:3 He said to me, "You are foolish, and you do not understand the power of torment." "Sir," I said, "if I understood it, I would not have asked you to make it clear to me." "Listen," he said, "to the power of both.
64:4 The time of luxury and deceit is one hour. But an hour of torment has the power of thirty days. So if someone lives in luxury and is deceived for one day, and is tormented for one day, the day of torment counts for a whole year. So as many days as someone lives in luxury, that many years he is tormented. So you see," he said, "that the time of luxury and deceit is very short, but the time of punishment and torment is long."
65:1 "Sir," I said, "I still do not understand at all about the time of deceit and luxury and torment. Make it clearer to me."
65:2 He answered me, "Your foolishness hangs on, and you do not want to clean your heart and serve God. Watch out," he said, "or the time will run out and you will be found foolish. Listen then," he said, "as you wish, so that you may understand it.
65:3 Someone who lives in luxury and is deceived for one day, doing whatever he wants, is clothed in great foolishness and does not understand what he is doing. For the next day he forgets what he did the day before. Luxury and deceit leave no memories, because of the foolishness they are clothed in. But when punishment and torment cling to a person for one day, he is punished and tormented as if for a year. For punishment and torment leave long memories.
65:4 So when he is tormented and punished for a whole year, he remembers the luxury and deceit, and he knows that he is suffering these evils because of them. So everyone who lives in luxury and is deceived is tormented this way, because though they had life, they have handed themselves over to death."
65:5 "Sir," I said, "what kinds of luxury are harmful?" "Whatever a person does with pleasure," he said, "is luxury for him. Even a bad-tempered man lives in luxury when he gives his passion what it wants. And the adulterer, the drunkard, the slanderer, the liar, the greedy man, the cheat, and whoever does things like these gives his own sickness what it wants. So he lives in luxury in what he does.
65:6 All these kinds of luxury are harmful to God's slaves. These are the deceits that those who are punished and tormented suffer for.
65:7 But there are also kinds of luxury that save people. For many who do good are carried along by their own pleasure in it and live in luxury. This luxury is good for God's slaves, and it wins life for a person like that. But the harmful luxuries I spoke of before win torments and punishments for them. And if they keep on in them and do not repent, they win death for themselves."
## Similitude 7: Affliction for Hermas's household
66:1 A few days later I saw him in the same plain where I had seen the shepherds. He said to me, "What do you want?" "Sir," I said, "I am here so that you will order the shepherd who punishes to leave my house, for he afflicts me badly." "You must be afflicted," he said. "For this is what the glorious angel ordered about you. He wants you to be tested." "Sir," I said, "what have I done that was so evil that I should be handed over to this angel?"
66:2 "Listen," he said. "Your sins are many, but not so many that you should be handed over to this angel. But your household has done great lawless acts and sins. The glorious angel was made bitter by their works, and that is why he ordered you to be afflicted for a time, so that they too may repent and clean themselves of every desire of this world. So when they repent and are cleansed, then the angel of punishment will leave you."
66:3 I said to him, "Sir, if they did such things that the glorious angel was made bitter, what did I do?" "There is no other way," he said, "for them to be afflicted, unless you, the head of the household, are afflicted. For when you are afflicted, they will be afflicted too, as they must. But while you are doing well, they can have no suffering."
66:4 "But look, sir," I said, "they have repented with their whole heart." "I know too," he said, "that they have repented with their whole heart. So do you think the sins of those who repent are forgiven at once? Not at all. The one who repents must torment his own soul, be firmly humble in everything he does, and be afflicted with all kinds of suffering. And if he bears the sufferings that come on him, then the one who created all things and gave them power will surely have compassion and give him some healing.
66:5 And he will surely do this if he sees that the heart of the one who repents is pure of every evil act. But it is good for you and your household to be afflicted now. Why should I say much to you? You must be afflicted, as that angel of the Lord ordered, the one who handed you over to me. And thank the Lord for this, that he thought you worthy to be shown the suffering ahead of time, so that, knowing it beforehand, you may bear it with strength."
66:6 I said to him, "Sir, stay with me, and I will be able to bear any suffering." "I will be with you," he said. "And I will ask the angel who punishes to afflict you more lightly. You will be afflicted for a short time, and then you will be brought back to your house. Only stay humble and serve the Lord with a heart that is completely pure, you and your children and your household. Walk in my commandments that I give you, and your repentance can be strong and pure.
66:7 If you keep these commandments with your household, every suffering will leave you. And suffering will leave all," he said, "who walk in these commandments of mine."
## Similitude 8: The willow and the sticks
67:1 He showed me a great willow tree that covered plains and mountains. All who have been called by the name of the Lord had come under the willow's shade.
67:2 A glorious angel of the Lord, very tall, was standing beside the willow, holding a large sickle. He was cutting branches from the willow and handing them out to the people sheltered under it. He handed them small sticks, about a cubit long.
67:3 After everyone had received the sticks, the angel put down the sickle, and that tree was whole, just as I had seen it before.
67:4 I wondered to myself and said, "How can the tree be whole when so many branches have been cut off?" The Shepherd said to me, "Do not be surprised that this tree stayed whole when so many branches were cut off. When you have seen everything," he said, "it will be made clear to you what it means."
67:5 The angel who had handed out the sticks to the people asked for them back. They were called to him in the order they had received them, and each one of them gave back the sticks. The angel of the Lord took them and looked them over.
67:6 From some he received sticks that were dry and eaten as if by moths. The angel ordered those who handed in sticks like these to stand apart.
67:7 Others handed in dry sticks that were not eaten by moths. These too he ordered to stand apart.
67:8 Others handed in sticks that were half dry. These stood apart.
67:9 Others handed in their sticks half dry and with cracks. These stood apart.
67:10 Others handed in their sticks green but with cracks. These stood apart.
67:11 Others handed in their sticks half dry and half green. These stood apart.
67:12 Others brought their sticks with two parts of the stick green and the third part dry. These stood apart.
67:13 Others handed in sticks with two parts dry and the third part green. These stood apart.
67:14 Others handed in their sticks almost all green. Only the smallest part of their sticks was dry, just the tip. But they had cracks in them. These stood apart.
67:15 Others' sticks had only a very little green, and the rest of the sticks was dry. These stood apart.
67:16 Others came bringing their sticks green, just as they had received them from the angel. Most of the crowd handed in sticks like this. The angel was very glad about these. These stood apart.
67:17 Others handed in their sticks green and with shoots. These stood apart, and the angel was very glad about them.
67:18 Others handed in their sticks green and with shoots, and their shoots had something like fruit. The people whose sticks were found like this were full of joy. The angel was overjoyed about them, and the Shepherd was very glad about them.
68:1 The angel of the Lord ordered crowns to be brought. And crowns were brought that looked as if they were made of palm leaves. He crowned the men who had handed in the sticks with shoots and some fruit, and sent them off into the tower.
68:2 He sent the others into the tower too, those who had handed in green sticks with shoots whose shoots had no fruit. He gave them seals.
68:3 All who went into the tower had the same clothing, white as snow.
68:4 He also sent off those who had handed in their sticks green, as they had received them. He gave them clothing and seals.
68:5 When the angel had finished these things, he said to the Shepherd, "I am going. You will send these into the walls, as each one deserves to live there. Look over their sticks carefully, and then send them off. Look them over carefully. Watch out that no one gets past you," he said. "If anyone gets past you, I will test them at the altar." When he had said this to the Shepherd, he went away.
68:6 After the angel left, the Shepherd said to me, "Let's take everyone's sticks and plant them, to see if any of them can live." I said to him, "Sir, how can these dry ones live?"
68:7 He answered me, "This tree is a willow, and its kind loves life. So if the sticks are planted and get a little moisture, many of them will live. Then let's try pouring water on them too. If any of them can live, I will share its joy. But if it does not live, no one will find that I was careless."
68:8 The Shepherd told me to call them in the order each of them had stood. They came group by group and handed in their sticks to the Shepherd. The Shepherd took the sticks and planted them in their groups. After he planted them, he poured so much water on them that the sticks could not be seen for the water.
68:9 After he had watered the sticks, he said to me, "Let's go. In a few days let's come back and look at all the sticks. For the one who created this tree wants all who received branches from this tree to live. And I hope too that most of these little sticks will live, now that they have had moisture and been watered."
69:1 I said to him, "Sir, tell me what this tree is. I am puzzled about it, because so many branches were cut off and yet the tree is whole, and nothing seems to have been cut from it. That is what puzzles me."
69:2 "Listen," he said. "This great tree that covers plains and mountains and the whole earth is God's law, which was given to the whole world. And this law is the Son of God, preached to the ends of the earth. The peoples under its shade are those who have heard the preaching and believed in him.
69:3 The great and glorious angel is Michael, who has authority over this people and steers them. For he is the one who puts the law into the hearts of those who believe. So he looks over those he gave it to, to see whether they have kept it.
69:4 And you see the sticks of each one, for the sticks are the law. So you see many sticks made useless, and you will know that these are all the people who did not keep the law. And you will see where each one lives."
69:5 I said to him, "Sir, why did he send some into the tower and leave others to you?" "All who broke the law they received from him," he said, "he left under my authority, for repentance. But all who have already pleased the law and kept it he keeps under his own authority."
69:6 "Then who, sir," I said, "are those who were crowned and went into the tower?" "All who wrestled with the devil and defeated him," he said, "are crowned. These are the ones who suffered for the law.
69:7 The others, who also handed in green sticks with shoots but without fruit, are those who were afflicted for the law, but did not suffer, and did not deny their law either.
69:8 And those who handed in green sticks, just as they had received them, are reverent and righteous people who have walked with a very pure heart and kept the Lord's commandments. You will know the rest when I look over these sticks that have been planted and watered."
70:1 A few days later we came to the place. The Shepherd sat down in the angel's place, and I stood beside him. He said to me, "Tie on a towel of rough linen and serve me." I tied on a clean towel made of sackcloth.
70:2 When he saw me with the towel tied on and ready to serve him, he said, "Call the men whose sticks have been planted, group by group, in the order each one gave in his sticks." So I went off to the plain and called them all. They all stood in their groups.
70:3 He said to them, "Each of you pull up your own sticks and bring them to me."
70:4 The first to hand them in were those who had had the dry and cut sticks. Since these were found dry and cut, he ordered them to stand apart.
70:5 Next those who had the dry sticks that were not cut handed them in. Some of them handed in their sticks green, and some dry and cut as if by moths. He ordered those who handed in green sticks to stand apart, but those who handed in dry and cut ones he ordered to stand with the first.
70:6 Next those who had the half-dry sticks with cracks handed them in. Many of them handed in sticks that were green and had no cracks. Some handed in green sticks with shoots, and fruit on the shoots, like the fruit of those who went into the tower crowned. Some handed in sticks that were dry and eaten, some dry and not eaten, and some just as they had been, half dry and with cracks. He ordered each one to stand apart, some with their own groups and others by themselves.
71:1 Next those whose sticks were green but had cracks handed them in. All of these handed in green sticks and stood in their own group. The Shepherd was glad about them, because they had all been changed and had put away their cracks.
71:2 Those who had sticks half green and half dry handed them in too. Some of them had sticks that were found completely green, some half dry, some dry and eaten, and some green with shoots. All of these were sent off, each to his own group.
71:3 Next those who had two parts green and the third part dry handed them in. Many of them handed in green sticks, many half-dry ones, and others dry and eaten ones. All of these stood in their own group.
71:4 Next those who had two parts dry and the third part green handed them in. Many of them handed in half-dry sticks, some dry and eaten ones, others half dry and with cracks, and a few green ones. All of these stood in their own group.
71:5 Those who had had their sticks green, but with the smallest part dry and with cracks, handed them in. Some of these handed in green sticks, and some green with shoots. These also went off to their group.
71:6 Next those who had only a very little green and the other parts dry handed them in. Most of their sticks were found green, with shoots and fruit on the shoots, and others were all green. The Shepherd was overjoyed at these sticks, that they were found like this. These went off, each to his own group.
72:1 After the Shepherd had looked over everyone's sticks, he said to me, "I told you that this tree loves life. Do you see how many repented and were saved?" "I see, sir," I said. "So that you may see," he said, "how great and glorious the Lord's compassion is. He gave a spirit to those who were worthy of repentance."
72:2 "Then why, sir," I said, "did not all of them repent?" "He gave repentance," he said, "to those whose heart he saw would become pure and serve him with their whole heart. But he did not give repentance to those whose deceit and wickedness he saw, who were going to repent in hypocrisy, so that they would not profane his name again."
72:3 I said to him, "Sir, now make clear to me what kind of people those are who handed in the sticks, each one of them, and where they live. Then those who believed and received the seal, and broke it and did not keep it whole, may hear it, recognize their own works and repent. Then they will receive a seal from you, and praise the Lord that he had compassion on them and sent you to renew their spirits."
72:4 "Listen," he said. "Those whose sticks were found dry and eaten by moths are the deserters and betrayers of the church. In their sins they spoke evil of the Lord, and what is more, they were ashamed of the Lord's name, which was pronounced over them. These are lost to God for good. And you see that not one of them repented, although they heard the words you spoke to them, which I commanded you. Life has left people like these.
72:5 Those who handed in sticks that were dry but not rotted are close to them too. For they were hypocrites who brought in strange teachings and led God's slaves astray, above all those who had sinned. They would not let them repent, but persuaded them with their foolish teachings. These have hope of repenting.
72:6 And you see that many of them have repented since I spoke my commandments to them, and they will still repent. But those who will not repent have lost their life. Those of them who repented became good, and the walls, the first ones, became their home. Some of them even went up into the tower. So you see," he said, "that repenting of sins brings life, but not repenting brings death.
73:1 Now listen about those who handed in half-dry sticks with cracks in them too. Those whose sticks were half dry are double-minded. For they are neither alive nor dead.
73:2 Those who had half-dry sticks with cracks in them are double-minded and slanderers. They are never at peace among themselves but always split into factions. But repentance is open to these too," he said. "You see that some of them have repented. And there is still hope of repentance for them," he said.
73:3 "Those of them who have repented," he said, "will have their home in the tower. Those of them who have repented more slowly will live in the walls. But those who do not repent, and keep on in what they do, will surely die.
73:4 Those who handed in their sticks green but with cracks were always faithful and good. But they had a kind of jealousy among themselves about first places and about some sort of glory. All these are foolish, being jealous of one another about first places.
73:5 But these too, being good, heard my commandments, cleaned themselves and repented quickly. So their home was in the tower. But if anyone turns back to factions again, he will be thrown out of the tower and lose his life.
73:6 Life belongs to all who keep the Lord's commandments. And in the commandments there is nothing about first places or about any kind of glory, but about patience and about a person's humility. So in people like these is the Lord's life, but in those who stir up factions and break the law there is death.
74:1 Those who handed in sticks half green and half dry are those who are tangled up in business and do not hold on to the saints. That is why half of them lives and half is dead.
74:2 So many of them repented when they heard my commandments. And those who repented have their home in the tower. But some of them fell away for good. These have no repentance, for because of their business they spoke evil against the Lord and denied him. So they lost their life because of the wickedness they did.
74:3 But many of them were double-minded. These can still repent if they repent quickly, and their home will be in the tower. If they repent more slowly, they will live in the walls. But if they do not repent, they too have lost their life.
74:4 Those who handed in sticks with two parts green and the third part dry are those who denied in various ways.
74:5 So many of them repented and went off to live in the tower. But many fell away from God for good, and these lost their life for good. And some of them were double-minded and split into factions. For these there is repentance, if they repent quickly and do not keep on in their pleasures. But if they keep on in what they do, these too bring about their own death.
75:1 Those who handed in sticks with two parts dry and the third part green are people who became believers, but then grew rich and became respected among the nations. They put on great pride and became arrogant. They left the truth and did not hold on to the righteous, but lived together with the nations, and this way became more pleasant to them. Yet they did not fall away from God. They stayed in the faith, but did not do the works of faith.
75:2 So many of them repented, and their home was in the tower.
75:3 But others lived together with the nations for good, were ruined by the empty glory of the nations, fell away from God and did what the nations do. These were counted with the nations.
75:4 Others of them were double-minded, not hoping to be saved because of the things they had done. Others were double-minded and made splits among themselves. So for those who were double-minded because of what they had done, there is still repentance. But their repentance must be quick, so that their home may be in the tower. But for those who do not repent and keep on in their pleasures, death is near.
76:1 Those who handed in their sticks green, but with the very tips dry and with cracks, were always good and faithful and honored with God. But they sinned a very little, because of small desires and because they held small things against one another. When they heard my words, most of them repented quickly, and their home was in the tower.
76:2 But some of them were double-minded, and some who were double-minded made a greater split. Yet in these there is hope of repentance, because they were always good. It will be hard for any of them to die.
76:3 Those who handed in their sticks dry, but with a very little green, are those who only believed, but did the works of lawlessness. Yet they never fell away from God. They bore the name gladly and gladly welcomed God's slaves into their homes. So when they heard of this repentance, they repented without hesitating, and they are doing every virtue and righteousness.
76:4 Some of them are also afraid, because they know the things they did. All of these will have their home in the tower."
77:1 When he had finished explaining all the sticks, he said to me, "Go and tell everyone to repent and live to God. For the Lord had compassion and sent me to give repentance to everyone, though some are not worthy because of their works. But the Lord is patient, and he wants the calling that came through his Son to be saved."
77:2 I said to him, "Sir, I hope that all who hear these things will repent. For I am sure that each one will recognize his own works, fear God and repent."
77:3 He answered me, "All who repent with their whole heart," he said, "and clean themselves of the evils I spoke of before, and add nothing more to their sins, will receive healing from the Lord for their former sins, if they are not double-minded about these commandments. And they will live to God. But all who add to their sins," he said, "and walk in the desires of this world will condemn themselves to death.
77:4 But you, walk in my commandments, and you will live to God. And all who walk in them and act rightly will live to God."
77:5 When he had shown me these things and told me everything, he said to me, "I will show you the rest in a few days."
