# Hostile review: Shepherd of Hermas 78-92 (Similitude 9.1-15)

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-11-13. Method: all 111 verses read against Lake's Greek (`texts/orig/Hrm.json`), Lake's apparatus (`texts/src/Hrm.app.json`), Lake's printed volume (local OCR `.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers02lakeuoft.txt`) and Crombie (`texts/en/Hrm.json` `anf`). Chapman checked at newadvent.org/cathen/07268b.htm (opened twice today). Lightfoot checked in the Source Library scan (book `6a3f035097e91e1768f69f31`, scans 340-342 = printed pp. 323-325). Cross-references and quotations of other books spot-checked against their plain.txt. `node tools/build.js --check Hrm/078-092`: the part itself is clean (111 verses, 0 part warnings); the command still exits 1 because of six errors in `Hrm/book.json` `canon.cited_in` (at 1:4, 6:5, 39:6, 29:6, 93:5, 7:4 "is not a verse of Hrm"), which belong to the book-level writer, not this part.

## Verdict

The translation is faithful and the apparatus is the best-made part of the book so far: every Lake footnote I checked (80:3, 81:3, 83:3, 85:7, 86:4, 89:2, 89:4, 90:8, 92:4) is reported accurately, the 20/25/15 numbers are handled openly, the Chapman and Lightfoot quotations are word-exact and Lightfoot's page citation is right. The weaknesses are in the Christology material at chapter 89 (a misattribution, an angel-Christology reading with no holder, an unsourced "no church" claim, and a note and a translation that do not agree on 89:3), in one invented claim at 92:4 ("righteous before Moses"), in a term collision with part 001-024, and in a cluster of apparatus gaps. No fabricated source, quotation or fragment found. 0 critical, 6 major, 11 minor.

## Findings

### Major

1. **92:4 note and chapter 92 `D` (notes.json 92:4; chapters.json n=92): "Hermas reaches back further, to the righteous before Moses."** Nothing in 92:4 or anywhere in Lake's text or footnotes says "before Moses". The Greek has only "first generation" (πρώτη γενεά), "second generation of righteous men", "prophets of God and his servants", "apostles and teachers". Chapter 92 `D` then says the rows "reach back before Christ", also an inference presented as fact. Not verified in any opened source; this is the writer's invention. Fix: delete "before Moses"; say Hermas gives no names or dates, and label any reading (prophets and righteous of Israel) as an inference or credit it to an opened commentary.

2. **89:2 (plain.txt, note "Older than creation", variant 89:2): the syntax of the key Christology verse is hidden.** Greek: `ὥστε σύμβουλον αὐτὸν γενέσθαι τῷ πατρὶ τῆς κτίσεως αὐτοῦ`. Lake's English construes it differently from the site ("so that he was the counsellor of his Creation to the Father", OCR line ~11893), and Crombie differently again ("a fellow-councillor with the Father in His work of creation"). The site gives "the Father's counselor for his creation", which silently picks one construal and renders ὥστε + infinitive (result) as "so that" (reads as purpose). The note says "Lake reads 'his creation' without deciding", which misdescribes Lake: he makes κτίσεως depend on σύμβουλον, not on πατρί. Fix: a note stating the three construals (counselor "of creation" to the Father; counselor to "the Father of his creation"; fellow-counselor in creating), the ὥστε point, and why the site chose its wording.

3. **89:3 translation versus its note: "he was made known".** Greek `φανερὸς ἐγένετο` is "became manifest/visible" (Lake "was manifested"; Crombie "became manifest"); the brief itself says "made manifest". "Made known" turns an appearing into a revealing and moves toward the adoptionist/revelation-only reading, yet the note (89:3) argues from the phrase as "the language of a Son who already existed and then appeared", and Hebrews 9:26 ("has been revealed") is the parallel it gives. The note, the chapter 89 `sum` and the `son-of-god` topic all quote "made known". Also "the last days of the completion" is unclear at 8th-grade level (the site's Matthew 13:40 has "completion of the age"; Lake's footnote says "the consummation... this age"). Fix: "he appeared (became visible) in the last days, at the end of the age" or similar, and make the note say what changed from the Greek; update the quotes in the topics and chapter text.

4. **Chapter 89 `deep` sections: misattribution and holder-less readings.**
   a. "Chapman... pointing to verses like 78:1 and the parable of Similitude 5 (59:5-7)". Chapman's article does not cite any verse and does not mention Similitude 5 or the slave (checked at newadvent: "doesn't mention Similitude V"). The pointing is the writer's. Fix: "Chapman does not cite verses; the verses most often linked are 78:1 and 59:5-7" or drop.
   b. "A Son pictured among the angels" names no holder; the `D` line on the chapter page gives it to "Lake's note at 69:3". Lake's footnote (OCR 9535-9545) says only that Sim. ix makes the glorious angel the Son, and that this, with the identification of the Son with the Spirit in Sim. ix (cf. v. 5), "makes it very hard to reconstruct the Christology". He does not propose that the Son is an angel. The brief requires angel, adoptionist, Spirit and orthodox readings "each named from a source opened". The chapter has no named holder for an angel reading and none for an adoptionist reading (yet `links` carry "Adoptionism"). Fix: say plainly that the readings were not found in an opened source, or open one (Lightfoot's pp. 324-325 is the only full treatment opened) and name it.
   c. The first half of Lake's 69:3 footnote is missing from the OCR (the OCR line begins "? But in Sim. ix. he is the Son of God" and the word before "of the Son with the Spirit" is garbled "ἐλ ἀοίδιμον"). The notes at 78:3 and 89:8 quote the clause "there the glorious angel is Michael, 'But in...'" as if both halves were Lake's note, and the 78:1 and 89 texts supply "this verse"/"78:1" for his garbled word. The reading is plausible but was not seen whole. Check the scan before quoting; mark the supplied words if kept.
   d. "He quotes 89:2-3 in full": Lightfoot's note quotes 89:1-3 (from "Πρῶτον" to "εἰσέλθωσι"). Minor but easily fixed.

5. **Unsourced universal claims about the churches (chapter 89 `deep` "How the churches have read it"; `church` topic; `angels` topic).** "No church holds Hermas's book as Scripture today, and none has made his pictures its own teaching about Christ" and "Hermas is not Scripture for any church" are not backed by any source opened in this part; the brief says to speak per church family only with a source ("No church prints Hermas in its Bible today: say so per church family only with a source"). "Whether Hermas's 'glorious angel' and 'glorious man' are one figure is debated" (angels topic) is credited to no one. Fix: cut to what the opened sources say (Muratorian fragment, Eusebius, Athanasius are in the book-level canon block) or cite, and name who debates.

6. **The Shepherd's own identity is promised but not delivered (note 78:1 first note, note 78:3).** The first 78:1 note ends "Whether the Shepherd is only an angel or stands for more is taken up at 89:8"; the 89:8 note is about the Son as angel and says nothing of the Shepherd. The brief's "The Shepherd / angel of repentance" place ("78:1; 111-114: who he is... whether Hermas means the Son of God, an angel, or both, with who holds each reading") therefore has no treatment in this part. The 78:3 note slides between "the glorious angel" (Michael at 69:3), "the most revered angel" (25:2) and "the glorious man" without saying which Hermas means at 78:3. Fix: write the note at 78:1 or 78:3 (or hand it explicitly to 093-114 and change the cross-reference), naming who holds each reading from an opened source or saying none was opened.

### Minor

7. **89:1 note "One Son, two pictures": "The Greek verb is singular: they are one thing."** `ἡ πέτρα αὕτη καὶ ἡ πύλη ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ ἐστί`: the singular ἐστί agrees with the singular predicate ὁ υἱός (and with the nearer subject), which is ordinary Greek; I did not open a grammar, and the note opens none. It is presented as fact in a `views` note and again as Claude's theory in the `spec` box. Fix: either source it or move it wholly into the labeled `spec` box and soften "they are one thing".

8. **Cross-part term collision (flag for the lead; 001-024 is not reviewed here).** 001-024 renders ἐπιστήμη "Understanding" (16:5, 16:7; terms.json "understanding (16:5, 7, as the 4 Maccabees entry)"). This part renders σύνεσις "Understanding" in the virgins' names (92:2) and "understanding" at 79:6; content/terms.json (Baruch entry, key 625) gives σύνεσις "discernment" and ἐπιστήμη "understanding". So the same book now has one English word for two Greek virtues, and the 92:2 note has to explain that "Epistēmē ... is a different Greek word" while the English is identical. Also 78:2 renders σεμνῶς "holy" (Lake's "holily") where 001-024 renders σεμνότης "Reverence". And ἀσύνετε is "slow-witted" at 89:1 but "without sense and understanding" at 91:4 (ἄφρων εἶ καὶ ἀσύνετος), against the Sirach entry "a person without understanding". Fix: choose σύνεσις "Discernment" (or another word kept apart from ἐπιστήμη) and align both parts; use one rendering of ἀσύνετος.

9. **78:1-2 capitals and pronoun.** The note at 78:1 covers the capital in "that Spirit is the Son of God" (rightly: it follows Lake's English "that Spirit is the Son of God", OCR line 10451). But Lake prints lowercase at 78:2 ("when you were strengthened by the spirit", then capital "through the same Spirit"); the plain has capital "the Spirit had made you strong" with no note, though the brief says every capital that decides a doctrine gets a note. Also "its own strength" for τῇ ἰσχύϊ σου adds "own" (Lake: "in your strength"), and neuter πνεῦμα is "it" ("when it spoke with you") where the same sentence says Spirit = Son. The note's reason for the capital ("a small 'spirit' would make the Church's speaker sound like some lesser spirit") is the writer's argument; say so, and mention 8:1 (the Church "created first of all") as the tension.

10. **87:1 "lime and fine clay".** `ἄσβεστον καὶ ὄστρακον λεπτόν`: ὄστρακον is a potsherd or tile (a lexicon snippet from lsj.gr via search, not fully opened); Lake's English says "a light clay" and Crombie "fine-baked clay". "Fine clay" loses the fired-pottery sense (lime plus crushed pottery is mortar). The note hedges ("fine clay or potsherd"), the plain does not; the gloss "plaster and filler" is wrong (it is mortar). Chapter 87 `R` ("Lime and clay were the ordinary materials of Roman builders") has no source opened. Also κόπρια "dirt" (87:3) softens "dung, refuse".

11. **Variants: gaps and errors.**
   - Lake's apparatus marks "Retranslated from LE/EL, om. A" at 83:2, 86:5 and 90:4, and gives these as notes only, while 81:2, 81:3, 82:5, 83:6 (twice), 84:3 and 92:1 get variant entries: inconsistent.
   - Unreported in variants.json: 87:1 (A reads `ἡρμοσμένων εἰς τὴν οἰκοδομὴν καὶ βεβλημένων`, LE `ἠρημένων ... βεβλημένων`), 79:4 (`ἦσαν PAmh, om. A`), 89:4 (`βεβλημένους om. A`).
   - Note 86:5 and chapter 86 `H` say the retranslation is "from the Latin (L1)"; Lake's footnote (app.json 86:5) reads "LE" (Latin both and Ethiopic).
   - Variant 86:4 `why` says the footnote "is not in the digital apparatus". It is: `texts/src/Hrm.app.json` has `86:4 pa/ntas A (probably, but it is difficult to read), forsitan L...`. My check of the OCR confirms the writer's reading of the printed page (line 11530). The second reading's `plain` ("these stones") drops the word; give "surely" or "in any case".
   - Variant 80:3 says "Every witness reads 'cut'"; Lake's note names A, E, L only: write "A, E and L".
   - Variant 82:5: A's row is blank `(omits)`; A reads on from the first "after a few days" to "we came to the place" (82:6), so give that text, as the variant's own `b` explains.
   - Variant 89:2 lists Lightfoot (a printed book of 1874) under A's row ("quotes the verse in this form"); he is not a manuscript witness and need not have used A. Label him separately.

12. **"Many centuries older than the Athos manuscript" (variant 89:2 `why`; chapter 79 `H`).** Lake's introduction (opened) lists the Amherst papyrus (CXC) with its contents (it does hold Sim. ix 2.1-2 and 4-5, 12.2-3 and 5, so 79:1-2, 79:4-5, 89:2-3, 89:5: correct) but gives no date. The claim is true by my check (a search result gave P. Amherst II 190 as a sixth-century codex fragment; A is fifteenth century by Lake), but no opened source is in sources.json. Add the source or drop the sentence.

13. **91:6 note "the right side of the bench in Vision 3 belongs to those who suffered" (10:1).** The Greek at 10:1 is `τὰ δεξιὰ μέρη τοῦ ἁγιάσματος` ("the right parts of the holy place"); part 001-024's plain says "holy place". The "bench" (συμψέλιον) is at 10:4. Fix the note.

14. **Plain-text points.**
   - 78:3 "just as you were before": the Greek `μηδὲν δειλαινόμενος καὶ ὡς τὸ πρότερον` ("fearing nothing, and as before"); Hermas was afraid in the Visions, so the English reads as a claim about his past. Reword (the "as before" is the vision "as before", Crombie "even as it was before").
   - 80:5 "placed on one another the first ten stones ... one stone at a time": Lake has "put the ten stones ... on one another ... carried them together like a single stone"; the site's "one stone at a time" departs from Lake without a note, and "on one another" is unclear (ἀλλήλαις is a feminine dative, the virgins; I did not open a lexicon to settle it). Add a short note or gloss.
   - 86:2 omits `ἐξ ἀνάγκης` ("of necessity"); 88:8 ὕβριν "harm" softens "insult, outrage"; 80:3 "from a quarry" is added to μὴ λελατομημένοι (acceptable, but a note should say λατομέω is "hew").
   - 89:1 gloss "you're not getting it, are you?" for ἀσύνετε changes the content; 89:6 gloss "the Lord's bodyguard" replaces the wall image (and Lake's own footnote says the Lord is for the moment identified with the tower); 90:7 gloss "God's servants" undercuts the site's term "slave".

15. **Settings and dates.** Chapter 78 and chapter 89 `readers` carry the identical sentence (brief lesson: do not copy one sentence into every chapter's `readers`); chapter 78's `settings` range (78:1-88:9) overlaps chapter 87's (87:5-88:9). "The Christians of Rome in the first half of the 100s" and "a Roman Christian of the early 100s" (chapter 89 deep) state as settled a date the book's own sources dispute (Chapman: 89-99 for the first visions and 135-145 for the final redaction; Lake: Pius "about 148"; "many critics ... at intervals"). Say "probably the middle of the 100s, with the date disputed" and credit.

16. **From-memory or unlabeled inferences.** 78:4 note ("the region Greek and Latin poets made the home of shepherds", "A shepherd showing a vision in shepherd country fits the book's title character") and chapter 78 `R`; 82:6 note ("authentēs ... is rare in early Christian writing"); 88:7 gloss ("an hour or two after sunrise"); 91:6 ("In Hermas's Rome bearing the name could mean suffering"). None has an opened source; the Arcadia closing sentence is the writer's theory and should carry the `spec` label or go.

17. **Brief coverage.** The brief assigns this part a `virgins` topic contribution "(search)"; topics.json has none, and no `virgins` or similar topic exists (searched `content/topics`: `virgin-birth.json` only). The virgins' topic content went into `fruit-of-the-spirit`, which is defensible but should be reported. Several notes carry `top: baptism` (80:3, 89:4) with no `baptism` contribution here (part 093-114 owns it).
