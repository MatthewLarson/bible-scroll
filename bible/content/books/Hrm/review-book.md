# Hostile review: Shepherd of Hermas, book-level files (2026-11-13)

Scope: `content/books/Hrm/book.json` and `sources.json`, the topic core `content/proposals/works/topics/hermas.json`, and the Hermas items in `content/proposals/works/build.md` and `topics.md` (the `apostolic-fathers` and `canon` sections). Checked against `tools/agent-reviewer.md`, `agent-reviewer-works.md`, `brief-hrm.md`, `brief-apfathers.md` (canon block for tier `early`), `content/books/1Cl/review-book.md`, `texts/orig/Hrm.json`, `texts/src/Hrm.extra.json`, `texts/src/Hrm.app.json`, the five parts' plain.txt, and sources opened this session (see the end). The part folders are reviewed separately; I touched them only to check a source key and a verse.

## Verdict

No invented source, reading or fragment. Nearly every patristic quotation checks word for word against an opened source (Eusebius 3.3.6, 3.25.4-7 and 5.8.7 in Greek and NPNF; Irenaeus 4.20.2; Tertullian On Modesty 10 and 20; Origen's Latin in Westcott; Athanasius Festal Letter 39, On the Incarnation 3, On the Decrees 18; Jerome Lives 10 and the Prologus galeatus; Rufinus; Claromontanus's four marked books; Chapman, Reid, Westcott, Lake; the OCA life; the EOTC list; Trullo canon 2; Laodicea 59-60). The ten citation-list mappings I tested all hold, and `--check Hrm` is 0 errors, 0 warnings. The weakness is where the canon-history story is arranged. The Muratorian Fragment carries the date, the authorship and the "too late to be apostolic" reason, yet its own date and place are disputed (the site's works.json says so) and the audience card misstates it. Irenaeus and Origen are credited with opinions that belong to Eusebius and to Rufinus's Latin. The Catholic row drops its source's own caveat. The Lutheran and Oriental Orthodox rows the brief requires are missing, while flat "no church" sentences stand. Two statements of Hermas's own life are firmer than Lake allows. Nothing needs a rewrite; eight major items need fixing before this counts as reviewed.

Findings: 0 critical, 8 major, 10 minor.

## Major

### 1. The Muratorian Fragment: its own date and place are disputed, and the audience card misdescribes it
Location: `audience.to` (last sentence), `authorship` "The Muratorian Fragment: the brother of Pius", `facts` Date, `canon.rejected_why` "Too late to be apostolic", `canon.provenance` "Read and copied" and the `sections` "How the book was read".
- `audience.to`: "The Muratorian Fragment, written in Rome in a later generation by its own account." The Fragment says the opposite of "later generation": `Pastorem vero nuperrime temporibus nostris in urbe Roma Hermas conscripsit` (Westcott p. 199 n. 3 and the Latin on his Appendix C page). Chapman (opened) says the witness is one "who claims to be contemporary". "In Rome" is Westcott's inference ("It comes from the place at which the book was written"); the Fragment's place is not stated in the text.
- The site's own `content/works.json` entry `muratorian-fragment` says "Most scholars date the Greek original to the late 100s, in or near Rome; some (Sundberg, Hahneman) argue for the 300s in the East." Hrm's book.json never mentions this, though its whole date, authorship and "too late" argument rests on the Fragment. Web search this session: Hahneman (1992) argues for about 375 and an Eastern origin, and treats the "very recently" remark about the Shepherd as a fourth-century statement; Ferguson (1982) and Kaestli (1994) are cited for the early date (reviews and summaries only; I did not open those books). Westcott (opened, p. 202 note) also reports that Zahn and Salmon dated the Fragment to the early third century.
- Chapman (opened) adds that the three witnesses to "brother of Pius" (Fragment, Liberian catalogue, Pseudo-Tertullian poem) "are probably citing the same papal catalogue", and that the Fragment's "while Pius sat" "may similarly be an inference" from a list of popes. The book gives the shared-list point but not the consequence: the Fragment is then not an independent contemporary witness, which is how Westcott and Lake use it.
- `canon.rejected_why` "Too late to be apostolic" gives Westcott's "decisive" side only.
Fix: date and place of the Fragment as disputed with named holders (works.json's wording, from sources opened: Westcott for Zahn/Salmon, Sundberg and Hahneman only from a page opened); rewrite the `audience.to` sentence ("by its own account" is false); add Chapman's inference point to the authorship row and to "Too late".

### 2. "Irenaeus quoted it as Scripture" is Eusebius's reading, and Irenaeus names no book
Location: `lead` ¶3, `facts` "Scripture for", `canon.summary`, `canon.provenance` ("Irenaeus in Gaul quoted it"), `topics.md` (both new sections), `build.md` Hermas section.
Irenaeus 4.20.2 (opened, NPNF) has "the Scripture declared, which says, First of all believe that there is one God" and does not mention Hermas or the Shepherd. Westcott (opened, p. 200): Irenaeus "quotes with marked respect a passage which is found in the first of the Commandments, but he does not allude to Hermas by name, nor specify the book from which he derived it", and "it may be reasonably supposed that Hermas here uses words sanctioned by common usage" (n. 2). That Irenaeus quoted the Shepherd as Scripture is what Eusebius says (5.8.7: "not only knows but also receives the writing of the Shepherd"; Greek opened). The book gives Eusebius's remark in one section but states the fact in the site's voice everywhere else, with no mention of Westcott's caution.
Fix: "Eusebius reports that Irenaeus took the first commandment as Scripture"; give Westcott's caution beside it; the same change in `topics.md` and `build.md`.

### 3. "Origen thought it divinely inspired" rests on Rufinus's Latin, and the Greek of Origen and Athanasius was not opened
Location: `authorship` "The Hermas of Romans 16:14?", `sections` "How the book was read", `audience.views` "Readers beyond Rome", `canon.summary`, `hermas.json`, `topics.md`.
- The book says "the site's translation of the Latin of Rufinus quoted by Westcott", which is honest, but the same sentence is then used in the site's voice ("Origen thought it inspired") in four places. The Commentary on Romans survives in Rufinus's Latin; whether `divinitus inspirata` is Origen's word I could not check in an opened source. `sources-checked.md` (writer's scratch) lists "Athanasius' Greek; Clement of Alexandria's Greek" as not verified, and the Origen Greek was not opened either. The works brief (lesson 6 of `agent-reviewer-works.md`) calls "a father's readings claimed without his Greek opened" a finding.
- Chapman (opened) says Origen "repeatedly apologizes, when he has occasion to quote it, on the ground that 'many people despise it'". Omitted, though it bears on how widely it was honored.
Fix: attribute "divinely inspired" to Origen "in Rufinus's Latin" wherever it appears; add Chapman's sentence; say in the sources note which fathers' Greek was not opened.

### 4. Catholic row: Reid's caveat on the Gelasian decree is dropped, and the Damasus attribution is stated as the church's history
Location: `canon.accepted_by` "Catholic Church".
The row says Reid "traces the decision at Rome back to the Roman synod of 382 under Pope Damasus, whose list survives in the Decretum Gelasianum" and quotes "final blow". Reid (opened, reid.txt) writes that the Damasan catalogue is preserved in the Gelasian document, "a compilation partly of the sixth century, but containing much material dating from the two preceding ones". The book omits the sixth-century clause. Web search this session: von Dobschütz (1912) argued the whole decree is a sixth-century pseudonymous work (Burkitt, 1913, largely agreed); the decree quotes Augustine on John (416), so it cannot be 382 as it stands; the book list is the part most tied to 382 and the apocryphal list is attributed to the later compiler; some (Jurgens) still defend a Damasan origin. I did not find where the Shepherd stands in the decree. The only Catholic-church document in the row is Trent, which does not mention Hermas; the rest is two Catholic Encyclopedia articles (1908, 1910) with an imprimatur, which the row labels "Catholic Church".
Fix: give Reid's "partly of the sixth century", state that the dating is disputed with named holders from a source opened (von Dobschütz or Burkitt, opened), say where the Shepherd stands in the decree only from the decree's text opened, and label Reid and Chapman as the Catholic Encyclopedia.

### 5. Required church rows are missing, and flat "no church" claims stand without them
Location: `canon.accepted_by`; `lead` ¶3 ("No church prints the Shepherd in its Bible today"), `facts` "Scripture for" ("No church today"), `canon.summary` ("not in the Bible of any church today"), `how_to_read` ("not in any church's Bible").
- `brief-apfathers.md` (canon block for tier `early`) asks for one row per family: Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Ethiopian, Anglican, Lutheran and Reformed, Baptist and Free churches, "a row with nothing sourced is dropped". Hrm has no Lutheran row, no Coptic/Syriac/Armenian row, and says nothing about which families were searched. `1Cl/review-book.md` item 4 made the same finding and the 1 Clement fixer added rows (Westcott p. 492 for the Lutheran "no recognised definition", Reid for the Coptic canon); the Hrm writer did not carry the lesson over.
- Four sourced rows (Catholic, Anglican, Reformed, Baptist) rest on lists of the 27 books, which is fair; but "No church today" in the site's voice covers families with no row.
- Eastern Orthodox row: the evidence is Laodicea 59-60 and Trullo canon 2 (both opened, both quoted correctly) and Athanasius. The row says Laodicea's list is of "most questionable" genuineness (the NPNF editor's note, opened), then uses its silence on the Shepherd; that list also omits Revelation, and its silence proves little about Hermas. No current Orthodox statement naming Hermas or the New Testament list was found, and the row does not say so (compare the 1 Clement fix, which says exactly that). The OCA life supports only the feast, the see and "according to Tradition".
Fix: add Lutheran and Oriental Orthodox lines from opened sources, or say once which families were searched and yielded nothing; replace the absolute sentences with "none of the churches listed below"; state that no present-day Orthodox statement was found.

### 6. "A freed slave" and "raised as a slave": Lake says the opening sentence is "hardly intelligible"
Location: `subtitle`, `lead` ¶1, `facts` Author, `authorship` "What the book says", `hermas.json` section 2.
The Greek is `Ὁ θρέψας με πέπρακέν με Ῥόδῃ τινὶ εἰς Ῥώμην. μετὰ πολλὰ ἔτη ταύτην ἀνεγνωρισάμην` (1:1). Lake's footnote (opened, vol. 2 OCR near line 647): "As it stands this is hardly intelligible: presumably the meaning is that Hermas was born a slave, and that his owner sold him to Rhoda. It is implied that he then passed out of her possession, and later on made her acquaintance again. The alternative is that ἀνεγνωρισάμην merely means 'came to know her properly.'" The subtitle says "a freed slave", a fact nowhere in Lake, Chapman or the Greek (Chapman says only that Hermas is "a suitable name for a slave"). The book turns Lake's "presumably" into the writer's own statement, and "later met again" is one of Lake's two readings of ἀνεγνωρισάμην.
Fix: "a man who says the person who raised him sold him to a woman named Rhoda in Rome" in the subtitle; Lake's note quoted in the authorship row; drop "freed".

### 7. Audience card: a debated date stated as settled, and a verse that does not say what is cited for it
Location: `audience.to` ("first half of the second century"), `audience.why` ("denied the Lord under persecution (6:2; 72-74)"), `audience.views` "A church under pressure".
- The Date row lists the 90s (Clement of 8:3), about 148 (Lake), Harnack's 135-145 and Westcott's later second century (Montanism). `audience.to` fixes the first hearers in "the first half of the second century", which is one of those views.
- 6:2 (Greek opened): `ἠθέτησαν εἰς τὸν θεὸν καὶ ἐβλασφήμησαν εἰς τὸν κύριον καὶ προέδωκαν τοὺς γονεῖς αὐτῶν`: they rejected God, blasphemed the Lord, betrayed their parents. The verb "deny" (ἀρνέομαι) is at 6:7, 7:4 and 14:5; it is not in 6:2. "Betrayed their parents" as a mark of persecution is an inference no opened source makes (Lake's and Chapman's pages say nothing of it). The audience card builds on it twice ("those who denied the Lord and betrayed their parents (6:2) point to persecution").
Fix: state the date as disputed in the audience card; cite 6:7, 7:4 and 14:5 for denial, and mark the 6:2 reading as the writer's inference or source it.

### 8. `topics.md` canon section: an unsourced superlative that the site's own other books contradict
Location: `topics.md`, proposed `canon` section, first sentence: "No early Christian writing came closer to the New Testament and stayed outside it."
No source says this. Lake (per `brief-apfathers.md`, opened for that brief) says Barnabas "became canonical in some circles" and stands in Sinaiticus "following immediately after the Apocalypse"; Hrm's own sources say 1 Clement stands in the prefixed list of New Testament books in Codex Alexandrinus (per the 1 Clement review). The existing `apostolic-fathers` core says Sinaiticus has Barnabas then the Shepherd. A "closest" ranking across these is the kind of comparative that `agent-writer-works.md` lesson 6 flags ("oldest/first/earliest not credited to an opened source").
Fix: delete the sentence, or say "Among the early Christian writings that stood at the edge of the canon, the Shepherd is one of the best attested" only if a source says it.

## Minor

### 9. Duplicate source key `chapman-cathen-hermas` vs `chapman-hermas-ce`
Both define the same article (Chapman, "Hermas," CE vol. 7, 1910, newadvent.org/cathen/07268b.htm). `chapman-hermas-ce` is in `Hrm/sources.json` and book.json, `hermas.json` and `topics.md`; `chapman-cathen-hermas` is defined in `parts/078-092/sources.json` and cited in that part's notes at 78:1 and 89:8 (3 occurrences in all). The build takes both, so `--check` does not complain; `build.md` says "use one" but nothing is merged. Fix: change the 078-092 notes to `chapman-hermas-ce` and drop its key (a part-folder edit for the part fixer), or define the one key once. The other near-duplicates listed in `build.md` (Eusebius, Jerome, Tertullian, Athanasius, Laodicea, Westcott) are identical-meaning keys with different page ranges and are fine to leave until publish.

### 10. The Athos manuscript: two sources disagree and the book mixes them
Lake (opened): "a MS of the fifteenth century, ... six [leaves] still in the convent of S. Gregory on Mt. Athos, three are at Leipsic, and one, the last, has been lost", brought back by Simonides. Chapman (opened): "a fourteenth-century manuscript on Mount Athos"; Simonides "stole four of the leaves", and "the six leaves which remain on Mount Athos". The book gives Lake's century, six and three, and Chapman's account of the forged text with no note that the two differ on century and on the leaves at Leipzig (three or four). Say so once.

### 11. "The one book he names as written" and the spelling of the name
Hermas 7:4 (Greek, opened): `ὡς γέγραπται ἐν τῷ Ἐλδὰδ καὶ Μωδάτ`; the Plain English Translation has "Eldad and Modat"; book.json, works.json and spec text have "Modad". The Greek has no word for "book" (as `build.md` itself notes). Chapman (opened): the reference "is apparently ironical", which is not given. Use "Modat" where Hermas is quoted, say once that "Modad" is the usual English form, and add Chapman's remark beside Lake's.

### 12. Rufinus and Athanasius: parts of the evidence left out
- Rufinus, Commentary on the Creed 38 (Westcott p. 581, opened): `libellus qui dicitur Pastoris sive Hermas, qui appellatur Duae viae vel Judicium Petri`. The book quotes the clause without saying that Rufinus names the "Two Ways" and "Judgment of Peter" in the same breath. Mention it, or say that the clause is unclear.
- Athanasius (opened): On the Incarnation 3 calls it "the most edifying book of the Shepherd", and On the Decrees 4 has "as the Shepherd has said" with no remark on canon; only On the Decrees 18 says "though it is not of the Canon". The book cites Incarnation 3 and Decrees 18 but not "most edifying" or Decrees 4. It is a one-sided picture of Athanasius.

### 13. Tertullian: Chapman's nuance is omitted
Chapman (opened): Tertullian "in his Catholic days" cited the Shepherd as Scripture; the book had "been excluded at Rome from the ... Instrumentum"; Tertullian "implies that Pope St. Callistus had quoted it as an authority". The book gives Chapman's "exaggerating" about "every council" and not these. The "bishop" of On Modesty is left unnamed, which is safe; Chapman's Callistus is a labelled identification that could be added.

### 14. Dates of Origen's works
`sections` "How the book was read": "Origen (about 230-250) quotes the first commandment ... (On First Principles 1.3.3)". The global key `origen-principles` says "about AD 220-230". Use the key's date for First Principles and Commentary on Romans separately, each from a source opened.

### 15. Cumae: the Latin reading and the premise of spec 2
Lake's note at 1:3 (opened, `Hrm.app.json`): `kw/mas N AE, civitatem Ostiorum L, the editors emend to kou/mas (Cumae)`: the Latin has "city of Ostia". The book gives the editors' Cumae and the "summer holiday in the country" alternative but not the Latin's Ostia. Spec 2 says the Sibyl is the prophetess "whose oracles Romans linked with Cumae": a fact with no source. The spec is labeled and says it depends on Cumae, which is right; give the premise a source or drop it.

### 16. `topics` list, hermas core and `build.md`
- `book.json` `topics` omits `penance` (a new core by part 025-049, `proposals/works/topics/penance.json`, exists) while listing `repentance`.

### 17. `topics.md` apostolic-fathers section: "kept it for reading outside the canon"
"The Muratorian Fragment, Eusebius and Athanasius kept it for reading outside the canon." Eusebius (3.25.4, Greek and NPNF opened) lists it among the `nothoi` and, in 3.3.6, says some disputed it while others thought it most necessary; he does not "keep it for reading". Say "the Fragment and Athanasius kept it for reading; Eusebius put it among the spurious books".
