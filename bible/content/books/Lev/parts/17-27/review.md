# Hostile review: Leviticus 17–27 (part `Lev/17-27`, the Holiness Code)

Reviewer: agent reviewer, 2026-10-23. Scope: chapters 17–27 only (plain.txt, notes.json, variants.json, glosses.json, chapters.json, topics.json, sources.json in `parts/17-27/`), and the ten new topic cores blood, molech, same-sex-relations, love-your-neighbor, capital-punishment, feasts, feast-of-booths, vows, jubilee, exile. `node tools/build.js --check Lev/17-27` reports 0 errors and 0 warnings (369 verses, 201 notes, 10 variants, 106 glosses, 11 chapter pages, 31 topic contributions).

Method. I printed every verse of 17–27 three ways: the literal line (`tools/literal.js`), the Plain English, and the BSB. I checked the Plain line by line against the Hebrew, and against the ESV and NIV. Some of those checks were confirmed today on biblehub, bibliavida and esv.org (marked †); the rest are from memory. I read all 201 notes, all 10 variants, all glosses, all 11 chapter pages, all 31 topic contributions and the 10 new cores. I also checked about 120 rabbinic, patristic, classical and modern citations.

## Verdict

This is a strong part. The literal-line method worked. About 350 of the 369 verses are fresh translations from the Hebrew. They keep idioms that other versions flatten: "lie down in the ways of lying down with a woman," "stand by over your neighbor's blood," "count its fruit as its foreskin," "puts a defect on his fellow citizen." They also keep the past tenses of 18:25, the word order of 26:42, and "crushing cruelty" matching Exodus 1:13–14. Earlier parts drifted on 50–400 verses. Here I found about 15–20 verses that still lean on ESV or NIV wording, most of them in the curse chapter (26) and in chapter 27.

The method was not applied to quotations from other books. Roughly twenty New Testament and prophetic quotations in the notes read as NIV or ESV, some word for word.

On the contested texts:
- **18:22 and 20:13:** the translation is honest and the debate is unusually even-handed. It covers the traditional Jewish reading (with the Noahide point and the Conservative and Reform positions), the traditional Christian reading, Milgrom, Olyan, Brownson and Gagnon. Two things tilt it slightly: the gloss "a thing God hates," and giving Gagnon the last word each time.
- **Molech:** this is the weakest of the fairness areas. The dedication reading (the rabbis, Snaith, Weinfeld) is never set out in its strongest form. The variant misreports the Samaritan Pentateuch.
- **Death penalties:** the rabbinic material quotes only the abolitionist half of Mishnah Makkot 1:10, three times.
- **The rest:** 19:18 and 19:34, the blood ban and Acts 15, the Jubilee, and the curses and exile are fair and well sourced.

Almost all the citations I checked are correct, including every one you asked about. These are: Kiddushin 14b–15a, Arakhin 32b, Megillah 11a and 31b, Sukkah 11b, Mishnah Arakhin 6:3 (loosely applied), Bekhorot 9:7, Makkot 1:10 (accurate but cut short), the Sifra on Akiva and Ben Azzai, and Buber's "dir gleich."

**Findings: 0 critical, 6 major, 12 minor (18 in all).** Item 19 lists checks that passed.

### 3. MAJOR (fairness): Molech. The dedication reading is never given in its strongest form, and the evidence on both sides is not set out

**Where:** note on 18:21 ("Who or what was Molech?"); the 18:21 and 20:2 variants; molech topic contribution and core; notes on 20:2–5.

**What is wrong:** the 18:21 note gives two debates. One is god versus <i>molk</i>-sacrifice (Eissfeldt against Day and Heider). The other is Carthage as child sacrifice versus a children's cemetery. The third great question is whether "making pass" meant killing the child at all, and it appears only as one clause in the core ("and whether the children were killed or dedicated"). The topic contribution calls it "uncommon: Some scholars think... a dedication by fire that did not kill the child." That understates it badly:

- It is the main rabbinic reading. Mishnah Sanhedrin 7:7 says a man is not liable "until he hands [the child] over to Molech and passes it through the fire." The Talmud (Sanhedrin 64b) describes passing the child between bonfires. Rashi and Maimonides (<i>Avodah Zarah</i> 6:3) describe handing the child to the priests and passing it through or over fire. The variant says only that "the Mishnah treats it as a cult act," which hides what kind of act.
- Modern scholars hold it too. Norman Snaith (<i>Vetus Testamentum</i> 16, 1966) and Moshe Weinfeld ("The Worship of Molech and of the Queen of Heaven," <i>Ugarit-Forschungen</i> 4, 1972) argued for dedication or initiation, not burning. Weinfeld's view drew a well-known reply from Morton Smith (1975).
- The opposite case is not set out from the Bible either. The texts that say the children were burned or slaughtered are Deuteronomy 12:31, Jeremiah 7:31, Jeremiah 19:5, Ezekiel 16:20–21, Ezekiel 23:37–39 and Psalm 106:37–38. None of the Ezekiel or Psalm verses is cited in the 18:21 note or the topic. These are the sacrifice view's strongest evidence, and the dedication view has to answer them.
- Targum Pseudo-Jonathan is described wrongly (in the variant at 18:21, and in the topic as "handing a child to idolaters to raise"). It reads 18:21 as having sexual relations with a Gentile woman so that the offspring go to foreign worship. Mishnah Megillah 4:9 silences a translator who renders it as "making an Aramean woman pregnant"; Megillah 25a ties that reading to the school of Rabbi Ishmael.

**Fix:**
- Add a views note, or expand the 18:21 note, with three questions, each in its strongest form: god or offering; killed or dedicated; Israelite or foreign cult.
- For the dedication view, give the rabbis (Mishnah Sanhedrin 7:7; Sanhedrin 64b; Maimonides) and Snaith and Weinfeld.
- For the sacrifice view, give Ezekiel 16:20–21, Ezekiel 23:39, Jeremiah 7:31 and Psalm 106:37–38, with Day, Heider and Smith.
- Move the dedication view out of "uncommon."
- Correct the Pseudo-Jonathan description.
- Tag the 20:2–5 notes with `molech`.
- Add Ezekiel 16:20–21, Ezekiel 20:26, 31, Ezekiel 23:37–39, Psalm 106:37–38, Isaiah 57:5, 9, 2 Kings 3:27, 2 Kings 17:31 and Micah 6:7 to the molech core's refs.

### 4. MAJOR: the 18:21 variant misreports the Samaritan Pentateuch and misses the real variant

**Where:** variants.json 18:21 (and 20:2).

**What is wrong:** the entry lists "Masoretic Text; Samaritan Pentateuch" for <i>leha'avir lammolekh</i>, "to make pass over," and gives the Septuagint only as a vowel difference (<i>melekh</i>, "ruler"). But the Samaritan Pentateuch reads להעביד, <i>leha'avid</i>, "to make serve" (ד for ר). The Septuagint's λατρεύειν, "to serve," agrees with it. So the Greek differs from the Masoretic Text in two ways: in the verb, and in reading <i>mlk</i> as "ruler." This is the variant that matters for the dedication debate in finding 3. One reading says "make pass," the other "make serve," and neither says "burn." (alhatorah.org's "Giving One's Seed to Molekh" records "Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch read להעביד"; the standard apparatus agrees.)

**Fix:** move the Samaritan Pentateuch to the Septuagint side of the verb. Add a reading "to make them serve Molech" or "to serve a ruler," with the orig להעביד and λατρεύειν ἄρχοντι, and explain the ר/ד confusion. Keep "follow 0" if you like, but give the reason.

### 5. MAJOR (fairness): death penalties. The rabbinic material is quoted one-sidedly

**Where:** note on 20:27 ("Did Israel carry out these sentences?"); chapter 20 view J; the capital-punishment topic contribution; and the core, which cites Makkot 1:10 as proof that "The rabbis made executions nearly impossible."

**What is wrong:** Mishnah Makkot 1:10 is quoted three times, and each time it ends with Rabbi Tarfon and Rabbi Akiva ("no one would ever have been put to death"). The Mishnah's last word is Rabban Shimon ben Gamaliel's reply: "They too would have multiplied murderers in Israel." Leaving it out makes the rabbinic tradition sound abolitionist. In fact it debated the question. The pages also leave out the rabbinic teaching that a court could punish beyond the letter of the law in an emergency (Talmud, Sanhedrin 46a, Simeon ben Shetach hanging eighty women in Ashkelon; Mishnah Sanhedrin 6:4). That is a real strand that pulls the other way.

"Under Roman rule the Jewish courts lost the right to execute (John 18:31)" is stated as fact. It is debated: A. N. Sherwin-White argued for it, Jean Juster against, and the stoning of Stephen and of James the brother of Jesus (Josephus, <i>Antiquities</i> 20.200) complicate it.

The capital-punishment topic's "common" line ("do not bind modern states") is fair. The pages do give supporters of the death penalty Genesis 9:6.

**Fix:**
- Quote all of Makkot 1:10 once, in the 20:27 note, and refer to it from the other places (this also fixes the repetition in finding 17).
- Add one sentence on Sanhedrin 46a.
- Hedge John 18:31 ("the Gospel says the council could not execute; historians debate whether this was so").

### 6. MAJOR (fairness): 18:22 and 20:13. The material is balanced overall, but three things tilt it

**Where:** glosses.json 18:22; note "Recent rereadings and replies" (18:22); chapter 18 deep section "The debate over 18:22 and 20:13"; same-sex-relations core.

**What is right:** the translation, "With a male do not lie down in the ways of lying down with a woman. It is abhorrent," is literal and honest. It neither narrows the verse to one act nor widens it. The deep section gives each reading in a fair form, says "The site takes no side," and includes points readers rarely get:
- the rabbinic Noahide reading binding all people (Sanhedrin 54a–58a);
- the Conservative 2006 responsum (Dorff, Nevins and Reisner);
- Reform and Reconstructionist positions;
- the Catechism's §§2357–2359 in full;
- Olyan's purity argument.

Milgrom, Olyan, Brownson and Gagnon are each described accurately.

**What tilts:**
- (a) The gloss turns "It is abhorrent" into "It is a thing God hates." Leviticus 18:22 says only <i>to'evah hi</i>, not <i>to'evat YHWH</i>, "abhorrent to the LORD," the phrase of Deuteronomy and Proverbs. Whose abhorrence is meant is part of the debate: Genesis 43:32 uses the word for Egyptians' disgust, and the writer's own note on <i>to'evah</i> makes this point. The gloss settles in the margin what the note leaves open.
- (b) The traditional side gets the last word in all three places: the note ends with "Gagnon replied at length," the deep section with "Gagnon's response," and the topic with "Gagnon answers." Gagnon's main reply, that the New Testament drops the food and purity laws but repeats the sexual ones, is left unanswered. The obvious counter is not given: Acts 15:20, 29 repeats the Leviticus 17 blood ban for Gentiles, and most churches dropped it. That is the writer's own material from chapter 17. Ezekiel 18:6 also repeats 18:19 (sex during menstruation) as a moral mark.
- (c) The same-sex-relations core's refs omit texts both sides use. They leave out Ezekiel 16:49–50 (Sodom's sin named as pride and neglect of the poor), Matthew 19:4–6 (Jesus on "male and female"), and Deuteronomy 23:17–18 and 1 Kings 14:24; 15:12 (the <i>qadesh</i>, KJV "sodomites"), which lie behind Bailey's and Boswell's cult reading.

**Fix:**
- Change the gloss to "something detestable" or "a disgusting thing," with no subject.
- In the deep section, add a short revisionist answer to Gagnon after his paragraph (the Acts 15 and Ezekiel 18:6 point), or reorder so that neither side always answers last.
- Add the missing refs to the core.

### 7. MINOR: 19:18 and 19:34. "Love your neighbor, like yourself" puts a minority reading into the text

**Where:** plain.txt 19:18, 19:34; and every quotation of these verses across the site.

**What is wrong:** the comma and "like yourself" lean the text toward the Wessely–Buber reading ("he is like you"). The 19:18 note says this keeps the Hebrew open. But STYLE says to translate the most likely meaning and put the alternatives in a note. Most interpreters read "as yourself," and so do the Septuagint (<i>hōs seauton</i>), Jesus, Paul and James. The New Testament pages of this site will quote "as yourself," so the two will not match. The note itself is excellent.

**Fix:** "Love your neighbor as yourself" and "love him as yourself." Keep the note on <i>kamokha</i> and on <i>ve'ahavta le-</i>.

### 8. MINOR: variant 25:33 makes a false claim about English versions; four variants speak in the first person

**Where:** variants.json 25:33, 17:4, 20:23, 26:11.

**What is wrong:**
- The 25:33 entry says the Vulgate's "if they are not redeemed" is "followed in sense by the ESV, NRSV and others." The ESV† reads "And if one of the Levites exercises his right of redemption, then the house... shall be released in the jubilee," with no "not." The NRSV reads "Such property as may be redeemed from the Levites... shall be released" (from memory), also with no "not." The RSV added "does not exercise," and the ESV removed it. The Vulgate does read <i>si redempta non fuerint</i>; there is no need to hedge.
- Four entries say "I have not been able to check..." or "I have not seen...". Honesty is good, but this is the writer's working voice in reader-facing text. The 4QLev-d claim at 17:4 is correct as far as I know: 4Q26 supports the long plus with the Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint, as the Dead Sea Scrolls Bible reports.

**Fix:**
- In 25:33, name the RSV (and NAB if you check it) instead of the ESV and NRSV.
- State the Vulgate plainly.
- Rewrite the hedges in the third person ("the exact wording of each witness differs; this is a summary of the plus"), or check the witness and drop the hedge.

### 9. MINOR: Dead Sea witnesses barely used. 11QpaleoLev-a is listed in sources but cited nowhere

**Where:** variants.json; sources.json (`freedman-mathews-paleolev` is defined but never used).

**What is wrong:** chapters 17–27 are some of the best-attested parts of Leviticus at Qumran:
- 11QpaleoLev-a covers parts of chapters 18–27;
- 4QLev-e (4Q27) and 11QLev-b;
- 4QLXXLev-a (26:2–16), in Greek.

The only Qumran witness actually cited is 4QLev-d at 17:4. With only 10 entries for 369 verses, a reader cannot tell whether the scrolls were checked and agree with the Masoretic Text, or were not checked. I could not verify individual 11QpaleoLev readings today, so I name no specific missing entry. Freedman and Mathews report a number of agreements with the Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint against the Masoretic Text in these chapters.

**Fix:** go through Abegg, Flint and Ulrich (the Dead Sea Scrolls Bible) and Freedman and Mathews for 17–27. Add entries where a reading changes the sense, and add one line in book.json or a chapter page saying that the scrolls were checked and mostly agree with the Masoretic Text. Either cite `freedman-mathews-paleolev` or remove it.

### 10. MINOR: factual slips

- **27:3 note:** "Fifty shekels was also the payment for seducing a virgin (Deuteronomy 22:29)." In Deuteronomy 22:28–29 the man "seizes" (<i>tefasah</i>) her, so most read it as rape; the law of seduction is Exodus 22:16–17. **Fix:** "for a man who seized and lay with an unbetrothed virgin."
- **20:10 note:** "stoning only for a betrothed one (Sanhedrin 7:3; 11:1)." The stoning list is Mishnah Sanhedrin 7:4; 7:3 is about the methods of execution. **Fix:** 7:4; 11:1.
- **18:7 note:** "The Talmud reads two separate offenses and counts sex with one's father as a crime of its own (Sanhedrin 54a)." On 54a the majority reads "your father's nakedness" as the father's wife. Counting the father literally, so that a man is liable twice, is Rabbi Judah's view there. **Fix:** "Rabbi Judah in the Talmud."
- **20:2 note:** "a stone dropped on him; only then did the people throw (Mishnah Sanhedrin 6:4)." The Mishnah has all Israel stone him only if he is still alive after the push and the stone. **Fix:** "and only if he still lived did the people throw."
- **27:21 note:** Mesha "devoted" Nebo to "Ashtar-Kemosh" (line 17 of the stele), not simply "Kemosh."
- **21:7 note:** "the state of Israel... enforces this." The Rabbinate will not register such a marriage, but Israel recognizes such couples' civil marriages made abroad. **Fix:** "the Israeli rabbinate, which controls Jewish marriage there, will not perform such a marriage."
- **19:20 note:** "the STEP glosses also give 'compensation'" cites a word-list database as an authority. Cite Speiser (1960) and Milgrom instead.
- **18:21 variant and molech topic:** Targum Pseudo-Jonathan (see finding 3).

### 11. MINOR: small translation slips

- **26:39:** "in their enemies' lands." The Hebrew is <i>be'artsot oyeveikhem</i>, "your enemies" (the literal line: "in [the] land of enemies your"). **Fix:** "your enemies' lands."
- **18:21 and 21:12:** "...and so treat the name of your God as common," "...and so treat the holy place of his God as common." The Hebrew is a second prohibition, <i>velo techallel</i> and <i>velo yechallel</i>. As written, the English can be read as a result clause, and in 21:12 it can even be read as permitting the act. (In 19:12 the Hebrew <i>vechillalta</i> really is "and so profane," so the rendering is right there.) **Fix:** "Do not... and do not treat the name of your God as common."
- **19:2, 20:7, 20:26:** "You will be holy people" adds "people"; <i>qedoshim tihyu</i> is "you shall be holy." The glossed note works without it.
- **21:3:** "who is close to him because she has no husband" adds a causal "because." The Hebrew is a second relative clause ("who is near to him, who has not belonged to a husband").
- **21:10:** "the sacred clothes": "sacred" is not in the Hebrew (<i>habbegadim</i>).
- **27:32:** "shepherd's" is added; see finding 1.
