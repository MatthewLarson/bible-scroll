# Hostile review: Philippians (Php), flat folder, one part

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-10-29. `node tools/build.js --check Php` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings (run this session). I read plain.txt against `texts/orig/Php.json` (SBL and Byzantine) verse by verse, read all 121 notes, 16 variants, 44 glosses, 4 chapter pages, book.json, topics.json, the four proposal topics and the three `terms.json` entries, and checked claims against sources opened this session (named under each finding).

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly sound book. The translation is faithful and independent of the NIV/ESV, the hymn treatment is good, the notes are honest about options, and most quotations I checked are accurate (Pius XII, Chrysostom, Eusebius, the Baptist Faith and Message, Dositheus, the LXX readings, the Gaudete introit, Thasos). It has no invented sources. The real problems are these. (1) The P46 variant at 3:12 is described as a replacement reading when the apparatus shows an addition with several more witnesses. (2) The 1:16-17 numbering breaks the site's KJV numbering rule and is inconsistent with the book's own handling of 2:7/2:8. (3) The book states two debated things as settled: a Rome-based date ("about ten years") and the identity of the chapter 3 opponents. The "dogs" note does the same and misses Chrysostom's own reading. (4) Several traditions cards are thin or lopsided against the brief (soul sleep missing, only 3 views on church order, only 2 on justification, a non-responsive Catholic answer on perfection). (5) Two Plain English Translation choices add things the Greek lacks (3:9 "I want", 1:28 future prediction), and the 2:7 gloss prejudges the kenosis question that the note leaves open. Note density is high; I list candidate cuts at the end.

Count: critical 0, major 8, minor 27.

## Major findings

### 1. (major) variants.json, 3:12: the P46 reading is misdescribed
Entry "Made perfect or justified?" gives P46 as a replacement: "I have already been justified" in place of "made perfect". Source: only the STEPBible word-list note `δεδικαίωμαι ... in: P46`, which is a word-level machine tag.
Evidence (web search this session, snippet of the NA28 apparatus): the variant is an added clause, "or already justified", attested by P46, D*, (F), G, Latin Irenaeus, Ambrosiaster, i.e. a Western/early addition, not a substitution. The freely-given.org collation table also shows the word as an insertion beside τετελείωμαι. The brief itself called it "a Western addition". The entry's own `b`/`why` admits it does not know whether the word replaces or sits beside. So the entry (a) gives the wrong structure, (b) names only P46 and omits the other witnesses, (c) makes the alternate `plain` "I have already been justified" wrong. It should read "I have already been justified or made perfect".
I could not open the printed NA28 or UBS5 page; I could confirm the shape and the witness names only from the snippet and the collation table.
Fix: recast as an addition: reading 1 = ἢ ἤδη δεδικαίωμαι added before τετελείωμαι, with witnesses named only as far as an opened source supports (P46, D*, G, Irenaeus Latin, Ambrosiaster per the apparatus snippet; confirm in Metzger or Comfort, or name just P46 and "some Western witnesses" with a source). Rewrite `why` (a Western addition that fits the later doctrine debates; not the "correction" the entry guesses). Also drop "(about AD 200)" or add that the date is debated, and correct textual-history topic wording to match.

### 2. (major) plain.txt 1:16-17: numbering breaks the site rule and is inconsistent with 2:7/2:8
STYLE.md rule 1 and PLAN.md key every verse by English (KJV) versification, and brief-nt.md says to find displaced words and "translate them under the KJV number". The book does this at 2:7/2:8 (the Plain English Translation puts "Found in outward shape" in 2:8, as the KJV and all five texts/en versions do; the SBL has it in 2:7). But at 1:16-17 the Plain English Translation follows the SBL order and SBL numbering: 1:16 = love, 1:17 = ambition. In the KJV (and YLT, WEB in `texts/en/Php.json`), 1:16 is the ambition verse. So any KJV-numbered reference to Philippians 1:16 or 1:17 lands on the other verse, and `texts/en` BSB/ASV (critical order) vs KJV/WEB/YLT already disagree. The variant explains it, but the rule is broken without a recorded decision. Cross references from other books, topic refs (Philippians 1:16-17) and "1:16" quotes in settings may now point wrong.
Fix: this needs a lead decision. Either (a) keep the critical order, add a prominent note at 1:16 (not only a variant) and record the exception in STYLE.md/brief-nt.md and the `texts` note, then check every site reference to Php 1:16/1:17; or (b) conform to KJV numbers. Whichever is chosen, say it once at the verse and in STYLE.md. The Plain English Translation's "The second group ... The first group" labels at 1:16-17 are the writer's own reconstruction of the crossing pattern and are not in the Greek; they also become nonsense if the reader flips the variant. Prefer neutral wording ("Some ... Others") in both orders.

### 3. (major) notes.json 3:2 "the dogs", book/chapter text, anti-judaism and judaizers topics: debated identification stated as settled; misuse not documented
- Note "Who are 'the dogs'?" lists pagans, Cynics and libertines as options, then says flatly "Paul is writing about teachers inside the Christian movement". chapters.json ch3 `J` says the same ("Paul's target is teachers inside the Christian movement"), the anti-judaism topic repeats it, and the ch3 `C` view and the "Who were the opponents" deep dive list "local Jews" and mixed groups as live options. The book contradicts itself and takes a side in Jewish voice without saying who holds the other.
- The brief required the misuse against Jews to be "handled honestly". The note says only "has been misused". No example is given. I opened Chrysostom, Homily 10 on Philippians (newadvent.org/fathers/230210.htm): he reads "the dogs" as Jews, specifically Jews who "preach both Christianity and Judaism at the same time", and says the label "dog", once for Gentiles, now belongs to the Jews. The writer cites Chrysostom on 2:6, 4:2 and 4:3 but missed his reading of 3:2, which is the best-sourced Christian example in the sources already on the site. It also bears on fairness: the "dogs" are called Jews by a church father whom the book otherwise quotes as authority.
- "Paul within Judaism" readings are named in J but their actual position on 3:2 (who the "dogs" and "the mutilation" are for them) is not stated.
Fix: (a) state the consensus as consensus (Jewish-Christian or Judaizing teachers is the most common reading) and name who dissents; (b) add Chrysostom Hom. 10 as the example of reading "dogs" as Jews, with a short quote, and say plainly that Paul's own language (3:4-6) does not support using it against Jews; (c) make the ch3 J view, the topic sections and the note agree; (d) say what Paul-within-Judaism readers do with 3:2-3, in a sourced sentence or leave it out.

### 4. (major) chapters.json `traditions` cards: thin, lopsided, and some do not answer their question
The brief planned Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran, Presbyterian, Baptist and congregational views on church order; soul sleep, conscious presence and purgatory; and a card at 4:3 on women's leadership. TRADITIONS in build.js has 21 names, including Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican, Adventist and Pentecostal.
- Ch1, church order (1:1): only Catholic, Reformed, Baptist. Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran are absent although these have distinct, documented views (the Catholic answer is given in CCC 1554, but the Orthodox and Anglican threefold ministry is not). "Baptist" is represented only by the SBC 2000 text, which is fine but should say "Southern Baptist" or add another Baptist/congregational source. The statement that "The church at Philippi is read as an example of this two-office pattern" is the writer's gloss; the BFM page lists Philippians 1:1 among the Article VI proof texts (opened this session) but does not say it is read as this.
- Ch1, intermediate state (1:23): only Catholic and Reformed. Soul sleep/conditional immortality (Adventist and some Anabaptist, Lutheran and Anglican writers) is exactly the conflict on this verse and is missing; Orthodox is missing. The Catholic entry spends its length on purgatory (CCC 1030) rather than on 1:23. The question asks if believers are conscious with Christ right after death: only the Reformed entry answers that.
- Ch3, justification (3:9): two views only (Catholic, Lutheran). Reformed, Methodist and Orthodox (theosis) are missing, and the note itself says the "Reformation reading" is "Luther, Calvin, the confessions".
- Ch3, perfection (3:12): the Catholic entry (CCC 2013) does not answer the question ("a calling to grow in love"); Orthodox, Lutheran (simul justus et peccator) and Pentecostal/Holiness views are missing.
- Ch2, kenosis (2:7): the Orthodox entry says "nothing of the divine nature was lost" for "the Orthodox" and cites Chalcedon "received by the Orthodox Church". Oriental Orthodox churches did not accept Chalcedon (Oriental Orthodox is a separate TRADITIONS name), and the Orthodox kenotic theologian Bulgakov, whom brief-php.md names, is left out. The card presents a mixed tradition as one view.
- Ch4 / 4:3: the planned women-in-ministry card was dropped; the text defers to "the cards at Romans 16". I confirmed Rom parts 12-16 carry a `women-in-ministry` tag, but a verse-level card is still needed here because 4:3 is the one text in Paul that names women as his fellow strugglers.
Fix: add the missing views with an opened source each (or fewer cards), make the Catholic answers at 1:23 and 3:12 address the question, add the Oriental Orthodox caveat and Bulgakov to the kenosis card, restore a 4:3 card, and add Adventist/soul-sleep to the intermediate-state card.

### 5. (major) book.json lead and audience, chapters.json settings: a Rome date and one reading of the opponents presented as fact
- book.json lead: the church he founded "about ten years earlier"; chapters.json ch1 setting 1:1-2 `when`: "about ten years after he founded the church"; ch4 deep dive: "About ten years later". Founding is AD 49 or 50 per the writer's own Wikipedia source; "ten years" holds only for the Rome view (about AD 62). On the Ephesus view (52-55) it is three to six years. The book's own `views` list three dates. Rome is also described as "the traditional view" while the same text says nothing of its best arguments (Acts 28 custody; the long tradition; a developed church with overseers and deacons), whereas the Ephesus view gets its argument stated ("much nearer Philippi"). Rome is the view Wikipedia calls "most likely" and the one the writer's own sources-checked file says is called most likely; the book never says that.
- audience.why, settings 3:1-11 `to`/`why`, chapter 3 `sum`: "teachers who would require circumcision" and "A Gentile church being told it must be circumcised to belong fully explains chapter 3" are stated as settled; the ch3 deep dive says the opponents are uncertain and that Paul says "look out", not "you have been led astray". Setting 4:1-3 `to`: "two women leaders of the church", while note "They fought at my side" says leadership is debated.
Fix: say "some years" or give the range per view; state Rome's arguments and mark it as the most common view only if a source opened this session says so; put the circumcision-teacher reading in `views` with its dissenters; use "co-workers" or "women prominent in the church" in 4:1-3.

### 6. (major) plain.txt 3:9: "I do not want ... I want" adds a verb the Greek lacks and changes the logic
Greek: ἵνα Χριστὸν κερδήσω καὶ εὑρεθῶ ἐν αὐτῷ, μὴ ἔχων ἐμὴν δικαιοσύνην ... ἀλλὰ τὴν διὰ πίστεως Χριστοῦ. The participle μὴ ἔχων means "not having"; it describes the state in which he is found in Christ. The Plain English Translation makes a statement of desire twice: "I do not want a righteousness of my own... I want the righteousness that comes through faith". "Want" is not in the Greek (the sentence has no second verb either; "having" is understood). At the verse that carries the justification doctrine on this site, this turns "found in him, not having my own" into a wish and weakens the Reformation and Catholic readings alike. The note on 3:9 does not flag it.
Fix: "and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own, the kind that comes from the law, but the one that comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness from God that rests on faith", and keep the pistis Christou choice and note.

### 7. (major) glosses.json 2:7 and 2:6 prejudge the kenosis reading the book leaves open
Gloss "emptied himself => gave up his own rights and privileges" chooses one answer to what was laid aside. The book's own note (2:7) says "The emptying is the taking, not a list of things removed", the terms.json entry says the note "says Paul names how ..., not what was given up", and the kenosis card sets Chalcedonian, Lutheran and kenotic answers side by side. The gloss is the first thing a reader sees. Gloss at 2:6 "the very shape of God, what God truly is" likewise picks reading (1) of the four listed in the note.
Fix: make glosses neutral ("took on a slave's form; he did not hold on to his status") or delete both.

### 8. (major) plain.txt 1:28 and 3:18: grammar recast in a way that changes who or what is said
- 1:28 "You will not flinch at anything your opponents do." Greek μὴ πτυρόμενοι is a participle still inside the content of what Paul hopes to hear (ἵνα ... ἀκούω ... ὅτι στήκετε ... μὴ πτυρόμενοι): "not being frightened". The Plain English Translation turns the last element of the wish into a stand-alone prediction. A reader loses that this is what Paul wants to hear about them.
- 3:18 "many are walking a road that ... makes them enemies of Christ's cross." Greek: πολλοὶ γὰρ περιπατοῦσιν ... τοὺς ἐχθροὺς τοῦ σταυροῦ, "many walk, whom I often told you, ... the enemies of the cross". "A road" is added and the identification "they are enemies" becomes a consequence of the road. This softens a charge Paul makes directly and removes the object of "I told you often".
Fix: "that you ... are not frightened in any way by your opponents" (keep it as part of 1:27); at 3:18 keep the Greek structure ("many walk as enemies of the cross of Christ; I told you about them often and now say it with tears").
