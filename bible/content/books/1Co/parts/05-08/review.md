# Hostile review: 1 Corinthians 5-8 (part 05-08)

Reviewer: separate agent. Files read: plain.txt, notes.json (78), variants.json (20), glosses.json (58), chapters.json (4 chapters, 13 settings, 9 traditions), topics.json, sources.json. Checked against `texts/orig/1Co.json` (SBL, Byzantine, edition flags), `texts/en/1Co.json`, the Plain of the other books quoted, `content/terms.json`, and the other three 1Co parts for shared terms. `node tools/build.js --check 1Co` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings. (`--check 1Co/05-08` alone prints two errors from `content/proposals/nt/topics/new-covenant.json`, which is not this part's file; it clears on the whole-book check.)

## Verdict

A careful, honest part. The Plain is built from the Greek, not copied: most close matches with the ESV/NIV are forced by the Greek. Facts are mostly sound; I confirmed Tacitus Annals 12.43 (famine at Rome, AD 51), Dordrecht art. 17 (shunning), the 2002 Church of England, 2009 ELCA and 2024 United Methodist decisions, Trent session 24 canon 10, CCC and canon-law numbers, Westminster 24 and 28, Didache 6:3, Justin Dialogue 35, and Tosefta Shabbat 15:9 (cited by scholars for epispasm; I could not read the text itself, so "as reported" is right). The problems are these. One variant entry misstates what the KJV reads. One note misdescribes Deuteronomy. One papyrus citation is garbled. The Plain at 7:38 and the notes at 7:36 lean toward one reading of a debated passage while claiming to stay neutral, and they leave out the main crux in 7:36. The 6:9 card gives the revisionist side no argument. Two cards (Orthodox on idol meat, and the Catholic/Protestant split on Paul's authority) are inaccurate. The variants file names editions but almost no manuscripts, against the brief. Nothing is invented outright, so I have no criticals.

Counts: critical 0, major 9, minor 24.

## Major

1. **Major. variants.json, 5:13 "Remove the evil person" (entry 5).** Reading 1 is labeled "Byzantine text and Textus Receptus (KJV)": future, "and you will remove." The KJV does not read that. `texts/en/1Co.json` KJV 5:13: "Therefore put away from among yourselves that wicked person" (imperative with *oun*, "therefore"). Only the Byzantine text has *kai exareite*. Evidence: the SBL/Byz strings in `texts/orig`; the KJV text. Fix: split the witnesses. Byzantine (Robinson-Pierpont): future with *kai*. The TR behind the KJV: imperative *exarate oun*, "Therefore." Say that the KJV reader's "Therefore" is a third form. Add `plain` for that reading if kept as a separate reading, and correct the `p` and `why`.

2. **Major. notes.json, 5:13 "the evil person" (note 11).** The note says Deuteronomy uses the refrain "you will remove the evil" for capital crimes "including a man with his father's wife (Deuteronomy 27:20)." Deuteronomy 27:20 (and 22:30) is a curse formula and a prohibition. Neither contains the refrain (the Plain of 27:20 is a curse with "Amen"). The refrain is at 13:5, 17:7, 19:19, 21:21, 22:21, 22, 24, 24:7. Fix: delete the 27:20 clause from the refrain sentence and say instead that 27:20 and 22:30 name the offense; the refrain is Paul's borrowed penalty formula. Also add 13:5 to `r`. The same slip lives in the chapter 5 J lens, so check there too.

3. **Major. notes.json, 8:1 "food sacrificed to idols" (note 63).** The papyrus invitation "to dine at the table of the lord Serapis" is attributed to "Oxyrhynchus Papyrus 1484." The formula "table of the lord Serapis" belongs to P.Oxy. 110 and 523. P.Oxy. 1484 (2nd/3rd century; papyri.info) invites to the Sarapis *couch* at a banquet in a temple of Thoeris, for a coming-of-age celebration. Fix: cite P.Oxy. 110 or 523 for the quoted words, or 1484 for the couch, and drop "as cited in the commentaries" in favor of a named commentary (Blue's article on the Corinthian house church uses the group).

4. **Major. plain.txt 7:36-38, notes.json (notes 59, 60), chapters.json (7:25-38 setting, deep "Four ways").** Three problems with fairness.
   - (a) The Plain keeps "his virgin" in 7:36-37, but 7:38 reads "the one who gives his virgin in marriage," the father-reading sense of *gamizō* ("give in marriage"). Note 60 claims this "leaves open the role of the man." It does not: a fiancé-reading reader cannot hear "he marries his virgin" in it. *Gamizō* is also attested for "marry" in later Greek; the note never says so. Fix: either render the pair neutrally ("the one who marries her ... the one who does not marry her", with "give in marriage" as the alternative) or add a variant-style toggle, and state in the note that the Plain's wording favors the father reading.
   - (b) *Hyperakmos* is the main crux in 7:36 and the note never mentions it. The Plain says "she is past the bloom of youth" (woman's age). Many versions take it as "if his passions are strong" (the man). Both ESV and NIV go that way, so the Plain differs from them, which is fine, but the reader is not told that the word can mean either. Fix: add a word note or fold into note 59, with the evidence for each.
   - (c) The setting card lists three views, the deep dive says four, and the note lists four. Guardian/ward is missing from the card. Note 59 also dates "spiritual marriage" to "scholars from the 1800s (Achelis)"; sources.json gives Achelis, 1902 (see minor 3).

5. **Major. chapters.json, ch. 6 traditions card "soft men... men who lie with males" (card 1).** The question is whether the two words name all same-sex intercourse. Catholic, Orthodox and Baptist answers are given with reasons and sources. Reformed, Lutheran, Anglican and Methodist are reduced to "Divided" plus a list of institutional decisions (ELCA 2009, Lambeth 1998, UMC 2024, PC(USA) 2014-15), with no statement of the reading that the dissenting side holds (Boswell/Martin/Scroggs/Brownson on the words, in the notes only). The strongest form of the revisionist reading appears nowhere on the card. Also the "Yes" answers answer a different question (what the church teaches about same-sex acts), not what the two words mean. Fix: reword the question, or add for each divided tradition a sentence of what each side says about 6:9 itself ("the words name exploitative practices known in Paul's world, so they do not settle committed unions" vs "the pair names the act itself"), sourced to Brownson, Gagnon, Hays. Add a source for the Lutheran and Anglican lines. Check these checks: the 2009 ELCA, 2024 UMC and 1998 Lambeth facts are right.

6. **Major. chapters.json, ch. 8 traditions card "May a Christian eat food that has been offered to idols" (Orthodox).** The Orthodox answer cites Trullo canon 67 for the decree of Acts 15:29, but the canon quotes only blood, things strangled and fornication (Schaff's translation; it omits idols). So the source does not speak to idol meat. The rest of the answer ("treats participation in pagan worship as forbidden") has no source. Fix: give an Orthodox source on idol food (Apostolic Canons 63 and the Council of Gangra deal with blood and with meat; the Didache 6:3 and Apostolic Constitutions are better for idol meat) or say plainly that the Orthodox teach the decree as binding on blood and idol food and have no distinct idol-meat teaching; do not use Trullo 67 as a proof for idol food.

7. **Major. notes.json, 7:12 (note 40), 7:40 (note 62); topics.json `scripture-and-tradition`.** "Catholic and Orthodox readers see an apostle speaking with the church's authority. Protestant readers place this under Scripture's own authority, because Paul is writing inspired Scripture." This sets up a false split. Catholic teaching also holds that Paul's letters are inspired Scripture (Dei Verbum 11), and Protestant churches have had commentary on apostolic authority. No source is given for either side. Also, the topic page repeats it as a settled difference between "the church's authority" and "Scripture speaking." Fix: either say what each tradition actually teaches about distinguishing a command of the Lord from an apostle's own judgment (with a source), or drop the sentence and keep the neutral "some see a saying of Jesus on one question and not on the other."

8. **Major. variants.json, throughout.** brief-nt.md requires that every place where SBL, NA28 and THGNT disagree "names the editions and the manuscripts behind each reading." In 5:4, 5:5, 5:13 (tense), 6:11, 7:15, 7:17 and 8:8, only editions are named, and the entries say "manuscript lists were not checked." The Tischendorf apparatus is in the Source Library MCP (see brief-nt.md) and could have supplied the lists. Names are needed for readers who want to weigh the evidence, and the brief asks for them. The `why` lines for 5:13 (present or future, "differ only in accent") and 7:15 ("us"/"you" sound alike) therefore give no basis to prefer one. Fix: add the main witnesses (papyri, א, A, B, C, D, F, G, Byz, versions, fathers) with dates, as reported by Metzger and the NA28 apparatus, and attribute them. Where a reading cannot be checked, say which edition prints it and leave it at that.

9. **Major. variants.json, 8:3 (entry 16) and 8:2 (entry 15).** The writer flagged P46 here. P46 (about AD 200) does omit *ton theon* in 8:3, but the shorter P46 text also omits *hyp' autou* ("by him"), and the P46 form of 8:2 is shorter too (Letteney, *JBL* 2016, "Toward a New Scribal Tendency: Reciprocal Corruptions and the Text of 1 Corinthians 8:2-3", argues that the shorter text of 8:2-3 is original, which the Plain's note ignores). The entry treats P46 as "a few other early witnesses" and calls the omission easy to explain; the `why` ("it is easy to see why a scribe would drop 'God'") is weak, since the shorter reading is also the one that explains the longer. The reading matters: with the short text, the verse reads "if anyone loves, this one is known," which changes the sense in 8:3 ("known" is what happens to the one who loves). Fix: say P46 is the earliest witness, add that it also lacks "by him," mention that the 8:2 text in P46 differs, cite Metzger and a named later study, and state why the Plain still follows the longer text (wider attestation) in plain words. Also correct `p`: "a few early witnesses" understates P46.

## Minor

1. **Drift toward the NIV/ESV, 5:7-8.** "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" matches the ESV and NIV word for word. In 5:8 "celebrate the festival," "malice and evil" and "unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" match the ESV closely. Much of this is forced (*heortazōmen*, *kakias kai ponērias*, *eilikrineias kai alētheias*), but "lamb" is supplied (the Greek is only "our Passover") and *etuthē* is "slaughtered." Fix: "Christ, our Passover, has been killed" with a note that the Greek says "Passover" for the lamb, or "our Passover lamb has been slaughtered, Christ"; vary 5:8 where the Greek allows ("keep the feast").

2. **Drift and fidelity, other verses.** 5:1 "is the talk of everyone" overstates *holōs akouetai* ("it is actually reported"). 6:19 "your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you? You have that Spirit from God. And you are not your own" keeps the ESV/NIV frame throughout. 7:19 "What counts is keeping God's commands" follows the NIV's supplied "counts." 7:32 "free from concern" matches the NIV. 8:13 "I will never eat meat again" matches the NIV. 6:20 "bring glory to God with your body" renders *en* as "with"; the Greek is "in your body," which is the sense the note and variant R0 give ("glorify God in your body"). Fix each lightly; for 6:20 use "in."

3. **notes.json, 7:36 (note 59).** "Proposed by scholars from the 1800s (Achelis)": sources.json dates Achelis 1902. Fix to "in the early 1900s (Achelis, 1902)." Also, "Cyprian ... Letter 62" is numbered differently in different editions; say "Letter 62 in the Ante-Nicene numbering."

4. **notes.json, 6:19 temple vs sanctuary (note 28) and terms.** The note itself says *naos* is the inner shrine, "not the whole temple area," and terms.json (entry for ναός/ἱερόν) fixes "sanctuary" for *naos* in Matthew, Mark, Luke and Acts. The 1 Corinthians Plain (3:16-17, 6:19), 2 Corinthians 6:16 and Ephesians 2:21 use "temple," so the Pauline letters are consistent with each other and with John but not with the Gospels. "Temple" is natural English for a metaphor and I do not call it wrong, but the note should say that the Plain uses "temple" and why, and the lead should record the Pauline decision in terms.json. The note's claim "can be read as 'your bodies, as a group'" is fair; the Plain's "your body" could keep either, which is good.

5. **plain.txt/variants.json, aphiēmi 7:11-13.** The same verb is "send away" in 7:11 and "divorce" in 7:12-13. The note (38) points out that Paul uses *chōrizō* for the wife and *aphiēmi* for the husband, and gloss 7:11 glosses "send away" as "divorce," which shows the Plain broke its own pairing. Fix: render both as "divorce" (or both as "send away" with the gloss), and make the note describe what the Plain does.

6. **plain.txt, other consistency.** The slogan "all things are permitted" is "Everything is permitted for me" in 6:12 and "Everything is lawful" in 10:23 (part 09-11); note 21 cross-refers to 10:23 as the same slogan. Align in the lead pass. Also *akathartos* in 7:14 and *molynetai* in 8:7 are both "unclean"; the second is "stained/defiled" (the gloss says "stained").

7. **Slogan quotation marks (6:12, 6:13, 7:1, 8:1, 8:4, 8:10).** The Plain puts quotation marks on what many scholars think are Corinthian slogans. The notes at 6:12, 7:1 and 8:1 say so, which is honest. Remaining gaps: (a) 8:4 puts two sets of marks and no note says why; (b) 6:18 is left unquoted although note 27 calls it a likely slogan, so the Plain is not uniform; (c) 7:1 is the most contested use (the Catholic and older reading treats it as Paul's own counsel), and there is no variant-style toggle; (d) STYLE.md says not to add interpretation, and the marks are interpretation. Fix: add a one-line chapter or book note that every mark of this kind is the Plain's editorial decision, add the 8:4 and 6:18 notes, and consider a toggle entry for 7:1 giving the unquoted reading.

8. **notes.json, 5:8 and topics `passover`; chapter 5 T lens and deep dive.** "The verse became a standard Easter reading in both the Roman and Eastern churches" has no source. The Roman rite reads 1 Cor 5:6-8 on Easter Sunday; the Byzantine Pascha epistle is Acts 1:1-8. Say "in the Roman rite" or give the Eastern source. "It is the earliest known text that calls Christ the Passover lamb" is probably true but is an absolute claim; soften to "one of the earliest."

9. **notes.json, 5:11 (note 10).** "Paul's list names six kinds of persons, and the first five are not exotic: the greedy and the swindler, the idol worshiper..., the slanderer, and the drunkard" miscounts: the list has six (sexual sinner, greedy, idol worshiper, slanderer, drunkard, swindler) and the sentence names five after the first. Rewrite.

10. **notes.json, 6:4 (note 15).** The quotation in the last sentence is not closed (`"I say this to make you ashamed.`). Also the claim that "the ancient church often took (2)" and "later bishops' courts appealed to the passage" has no source.

11. **notes.json, 8:9 (note 74).** *Proskomma* is said to be the word of Leviticus 19:14 against tripping the blind. The Greek of Leviticus 19:14 uses *skandalon*; *proskomma* is the word in Isaiah 8:14 (as the note also says). Fix: say the idea is the same as Leviticus 19:14 (LXX *skandalon*).

12. **topics.json, `conscience`.** "uses syneidēsis five times in 8:7-12" is wrong. It occurs three times in 8:7-12 (8:7, 10, 12) and eight times in 8:7-12 together with chapter 10 (note 72 says that correctly). Fix.

13. **chapters.json, ch. 5 settings, "5:9-13."** "or used it as a joke" is not in the text or in any source I know. Remove. In the 5:1-8 card, "probably in the spring before Passover" and in the H lens "with Passover in mind (16:8)" rest only on 16:8 ("until Pentecost") and a guess; note 7 says "some think." The deep dive says 16:8 "shows him keeping the Jewish calendar of feasts in Ephesus"; 16:8 shows only that Pentecost is a date marker. Soften all three.

14. **Settings stated as fact (ch. 7).** 7:10-11 "when": "As he deals with those who wished to leave marriage for the sake of the spirit" is a reconstruction. 7:12-16 "why": "since the believer makes the household holy" states reading (1) of note 42 as Paul's meaning. 7:40 note: "Paul is dealing with some who claim the Spirit's guidance for their strict views" is conjecture. Use "perhaps" or give the views.

15. **notes.json, 7:21 (note 50) and Plain.** The Plain supplies "it" (make use of it = freedom). The note lays out both readings but never says which one the Plain followed or that the object is supplied. The topic page and chapter 7 deep dive do. Add one sentence to the note. The "19th century Americans on both sides" statement has no source. Chrysostom, Bartchy and Harrill are fairly placed.

16. **notes.json, 7:12 (note 41).** Nehemiah 13:23-27 does not tell the men to send their foreign wives away; Nehemiah rebukes them and makes them swear. Ezra 10:3 is the covenant to send them away. Also, "Ezra's divorces reversed?" is a title the body never answers, and the last sentences repeat note 42.

17. **notes.json, 6:16 (note 26).** The Plain of Genesis 2:24 is quoted correctly. "*Kollaomai*, the word for 'sticks close' in the Greek of Genesis": the LXX has *proskollaomai*. Say "the related verb." The "two" is not in the Masoretic text but is in the Samaritan Pentateuch and other versions; say so.

18. **notes.json, 7:26 (note 54).** Tacitus Annals 12.43 reports a famine at Rome in AD 51 (confirmed; Suetonius Claudius 18.2 also). "A grain shortage in the empire" generalizes. Winter's link to Corinth rests on Corinthian evidence (the inscription of Dinippus), not Tacitus. Fix the wording.

19. **notes.json, 8:1 (note 63).** "Paul gives a different answer in chapters 8-10" than Acts 15:29 and Revelation is a contested claim stated as fact (many scholars, Cheung, Gooch, argue 8-10 is consistent with the decree). Say "some read Paul as giving a different answer."

20. **notes.json, 8:5 (note 69).** "(later) an imperial cult" is unclear for Paul's day. Corinth had imperial cult activity under Augustus and Tiberius. Give a source or say "from the first century."

21. **Traditions sources.** Orthodox answers in the divorce, celibacy, infant baptism, idol-meat (see major 6), Shema, perseverance and church-discipline cards carry no source key, against STYLE.md ("sourced"). The Jehovah's Witnesses, Oneness Pentecostal, Lutheran (same-sex), Anglican (celibacy, divorce, same-sex) and Baptist (celibacy) lines are also unsourced. Add Orthodox sources (e.g., the Orthodox Church in America's statements on marriage, the Kallistos Ware or Meyendorff volumes, Rudder/Pedalion canons) or label them as general summaries. Also the Catholic line on 8:6 says the Nicene Creed "takes the words of 8:6 as its own"; it echoes 8:6 (and John 1:3, Colossians 1:16) but does not quote it.

22. **glosses.json.** 5:1 gloss states "living with his stepmother" as fact while note 0 says "most readers." 6:18 "as from a fire" is not in *pheugete*. 7:25 "unmarried young women" fixes *parthenoi* to women (note 53 concedes Rev 14:4). 8:13 "lead my brother or sister into sin" adds "or sister" and reads *skandalizō* as "sin," where the note defines it as "stumble." The 6:11 gloss "declared to be in the right" follows the binding term (terms.json), but the note could point out that the Catholic and Orthodox traditions read the verb as "made righteous."

23. **variants.json, missing entries.** 7:38 (*gamizōn* vs *ekgamizōn*, the Byzantine/TR form; note 60 mentions it but variants.json has no entry, and the choice bears on the father/fiancé debate). 7:34's variant (entry 13) says the Plain keeps "the unmarried woman, the virgin" "in apposition, as the SBLGNT has them," but the Greek has *kai* ("and") between them. The Plain turned "and" into apposition without a note. Also the 5:13 present/future entry gives no reason to prefer present beyond the editors' preference.

24. **Style.** Several note bodies carry sentences over 40 words (notes 12, 17, 59) and "Readers across the churches take this as..." (note 32) is an unsupported generalization. The first sentence of note 58 is repeated in its third. Tighten for an 8th-grade level. No em-dashes found.
