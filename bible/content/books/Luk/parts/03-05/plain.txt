## John prepares the way
3:1 It was the fifteenth year of Tiberius Caesar's rule. Pontius Pilate was governing Judea. Herod ruled Galilee as tetrarch, his brother Philip ruled the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis as tetrarch, and Lysanias ruled Abilene as tetrarch.
3:2 Annas and Caiaphas held the high priesthood. That was when, out in the wilderness, a word from God came to Zechariah's son John.
3:3 He traveled through the whole region around the Jordan, proclaiming a baptism of repentance, for sins to be forgiven.
3:4 This matched what is written in the book of the prophet Isaiah's sayings: "A voice is shouting in the wilderness: / 'Get the Lord's road ready. / Make the paths for him straight.
3:5 Every ravine will be filled up, / and every mountain and hill will be brought low. / What is crooked will become a straight road, / and the rough ways will become smooth roads.
3:6 And all flesh will see God's salvation.'"
3:7 So he used to say to the crowds who were coming out for him to baptize them, "You offspring of vipers! Who warned you to run from the coming anger?
3:8 So produce fruits worthy of repentance. And don't start telling yourselves, 'Our father is Abraham.' Let me tell you: God can raise up children for Abraham from these very stones.
3:9 Yes, the ax already lies against the root of the trees. Any tree that fails to produce good fruit is being cut down and thrown into a fire."
## "What are we to do?"
3:10 So the crowds would ask him, "Then what are we to do?"
3:11 His answer to them was, "If you own two tunics, give one to the person who owns none. If you have food, do the same."
3:12 Some tax collectors came too, to be baptized, and they asked him, "Teacher, what are we to do?"
3:13 He told them, "Do not collect anything beyond the amount you have been assigned."
3:14 Men serving as soldiers asked him as well, "What about us? What are we to do?" He said to them, "Don't squeeze money out of anyone by force or by false charges. Make do with your pay."
## The stronger one is coming
3:15 The people were full of expectation. Everyone was turning it over in their hearts: could John himself be the Messiah?
3:16 John answered them all, "I baptize you in water. But the one stronger than I am is coming. I am not good enough to untie the strap of his sandals. He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire.
3:17 He holds his winnowing fork in his hand, ready to clean out his threshing floor and gather the wheat into his barn. But the chaff he will burn up with a fire no one can put out."
3:18 So, with many other appeals too, he kept telling the people the good news.
3:19 But Herod the tetrarch had been taken to task by John over Herodias, his brother's wife, and over all the wicked things Herod had done.
3:20 On top of all of it he added this too: he shut John away in prison.
## Jesus is baptized
3:21 This happened as all the people were being baptized. After Jesus had been baptized as well, while he prayed, heaven opened,
3:22 and the Holy Spirit came down on him in bodily shape, like a dove. A voice came out of heaven: "You are my dearly loved Son; in you I take delight."

## The devil tests Jesus
4:1 Full of the Holy Spirit, Jesus turned back from the Jordan, and the Spirit kept leading him about in the wilderness
4:2 for forty days, while the devil put him to the test. He ate nothing at all in those days, and when they were over, he was hungry.
4:3 The devil said to him, "If you are God's Son, order this stone to turn into a loaf of bread."
4:4 Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'People will not live by bread alone.'"
4:5 Then the devil led him up and, in a split second, showed him every kingdom of the inhabited world.
4:6 The devil said to him, "All this authority I will give to you, and the glory of these kingdoms, because it has been handed over to me, and I give it to whoever I wish.
4:7 So if you bow down before me, it will all be yours."
4:8 Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'It is the Lord your God you must bow down to, and him alone you must serve.'"
4:9 Then he took him into Jerusalem, set him on the high corner of the temple, and said to him, "If you are God's Son, throw yourself down from here.
4:10 For it is written, 'He will give his angels orders about you, to guard you,'
4:11 and, 'They will hold you up on their hands, so your foot does not hit a stone.'"
4:12 Jesus answered him, "It has been said, 'You must not put the Lord your God to the test.'"
4:13 Having run through every kind of test, the devil left him alone until his next chance.
## Back in Galilee
4:14 In the Spirit's power, Jesus returned to Galilee. Word about him spread out over all the country around.
4:15 He taught in their synagogues, and everyone spoke of him with honor.
## The sermon at Nazareth
4:16 He came to Nazareth, the town where he had grown up. On the Sabbath day he entered the synagogue, as he regularly did, and stood up to read.
4:17 They gave him the book of Isaiah the prophet. He opened out the scroll and found the spot where these words stood written:
4:18 "The Spirit of the Lord is on me, / because he has anointed me / to bring good news to the poor. / He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives / and sight again for the blind, / to send the crushed away free,
4:19 to proclaim a year of the Lord's welcome."
4:20 Folding the scroll closed, he handed it back to the assistant and sat down. All through the synagogue, every eye was locked on him.
4:21 He began by telling them, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled, right in your ears."
4:22 Everyone was speaking in his favor and marveling at the words of grace that poured from his mouth. They kept saying, "This is Joseph's son, isn't it?"
4:23 He said to them, "Of course you will throw this proverb at me, 'Doctor, cure yourself! All the things we heard were done in Capernaum, do them here too, in your hometown.'"
4:24 And he said, "Amen, I tell you, no prophet is welcome in his hometown.
4:25 It is the truth I am telling you: in Elijah's days, when the sky was locked up for three years and six months and a terrible famine struck the whole country, Israel had many widows.
4:26 Elijah was sent to none of them. He was sent only to Zarephath in Sidon's territory, to a woman who was a widow.
4:27 Israel also had many men with skin disease in the prophet Elisha's time, and not one of them was made clean, only Naaman, a Syrian."
4:28 As they heard these words, all of them in the synagogue boiled over with fury.
4:29 They rose up and threw him out of town. They took him to the edge of the hill their town was built on, meaning to hurl him down from there.
4:30 But he passed through the middle of them and kept going.
## A demon in the synagogue
4:31 Down he went to Capernaum, a town of Galilee, and there he would teach them on the Sabbaths.
4:32 People were astounded at his teaching, because his message carried authority.
4:33 A man was in the synagogue with the spirit of an unclean demon in him. He let out a loud shriek,
4:34 "Ha! What is there between us and you, Jesus of Nazareth? Did you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God!"
4:35 Jesus gave it a strict order: "Be muzzled, and come out of him!" The demon threw the man down in the middle of them and came out of him, without hurting him at all.
4:36 Everyone was stunned. They kept saying to one another, "What is this message? With authority and power he orders the unclean spirits, and out they come!"
4:37 The talk about him kept going out to every corner of the countryside.
## Healings at Simon's house
4:38 Leaving the synagogue, he went into Simon's house. Simon's mother-in-law was in the grip of a high fever, and they appealed to him on her behalf.
4:39 He stood over her and gave the fever a strict order, and it let go of her. At once she got up and began serving them.
4:40 When the sun was setting, everyone who had anyone sick with any kind of disease brought them to him. One by one he placed his hands on each of them and healed them.
4:41 Demons also came out of many people, shrieking, "You are God's Son!" But he gave them a strict order and would not let them speak, because they knew him to be the Messiah.
## On to other towns
4:42 When day came he went out and headed for a deserted place. But the crowds went looking for him, caught up with him, and kept trying to hold him back so he would not go away from them.
4:43 He told them, "I must tell the other towns the good news of the kingdom of God too. It was for this that I was sent."
4:44 And he went on preaching in the synagogues of Judea.
## The great catch of fish
5:1 Once, when the crowd was pushing in on him and listening to the word of God, he was standing beside Lake Gennesaret.
5:2 He saw two boats lying at the lake's edge. The fishermen had stepped out of them and were washing the nets.
5:3 Stepping into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, he asked him to pull back a short way from the land. Then, seated in the boat, he went on teaching the crowds.
5:4 When he stopped talking, he told Simon, "Pull out into the deep water, and all of you lower your nets for a catch."
5:5 Simon replied, "Master, we wore ourselves out the whole night and caught nothing. But on the strength of what you say, I will lower the nets."
5:6 They did it, and they trapped a great mass of fish. Their nets were starting to rip apart.
5:7 They waved to their partners in the other boat to come and lend them a hand. The partners came, and they loaded both boats until the boats were starting to go under.
5:8 Simon Peter saw it and fell at Jesus' knees. "Leave me, Lord," he said, "because I am a sinful man."
5:9 Awe had seized him, and everyone with him, at the haul of fish they had brought in.
5:10 The same was true of James and John, Zebedee's sons, who were in business with Simon. Jesus told Simon, "Stop being afraid. From now on it is people you will be catching alive."
5:11 Once they had brought the boats to land, they left it all behind and followed him.
## A man full of skin disease
5:12 Once, while he was in one of the towns, there was a man full of skin disease. Catching sight of Jesus, he fell on his face and pleaded with him, "Lord, if you want to, you are able to make me clean."
5:13 Jesus stretched out his hand, touched him and said, "I want to. Be made clean." At once the skin disease went away from him.
5:14 Jesus ordered him to tell no one. "Instead, go and let the priest see you. Then bring an offering for your cleansing, just as Moses ordered, as evidence to them."
5:15 Yet talk about him traveled farther than ever. Great crowds kept coming together to listen and to be cured of their illnesses.
5:16 He, though, kept slipping away to the wild places and praying there.
## Through the roof
5:17 On one of those days he was teaching. Sitting nearby were Pharisees and teachers of the law, who had come in from every village of Galilee and Judea, and from Jerusalem. The Lord's power was there for him to heal.
5:18 Just then some men arrived, carrying on a cot a man who was paralyzed. They kept trying to bring him inside and set him down in front of Jesus.
5:19 But the crowd left them no way in. So they climbed onto the roof, and through the roof tiles they lowered him, cot and all, down into the middle, in front of Jesus.
5:20 He saw their faith and said, "Man, your sins have been forgiven you."
5:21 The scribes and the Pharisees began reasoning it out: "Who is this man, uttering blasphemies? Who is able to forgive sins except God alone?"
5:22 Jesus, aware of their reasonings, answered them, "Why are you reasoning in your hearts?
5:23 Which is the easier thing to say: 'Your sins have been forgiven you,' or 'Get up and walk around'?
5:24 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority to forgive sins here on earth..." He said to the paralyzed man, "I am telling you: get up, pick up your cot, and go to your house."
5:25 Right away the man rose to his feet before their eyes. He lifted the thing he had been lying on and set off for his house, glorifying God.
5:26 Every one of them was beside themselves. They kept glorifying God, and they were filled with fear. "We have seen things past believing today," they said.
## Levi's banquet
5:27 After this he went out and caught sight of a man named Levi, a tax collector, sitting at the tax office. He told him, "Follow me."
5:28 He left everything, stood up, and began following him.
5:29 Levi threw a big reception for him at his home. A large crowd was there, tax collectors and other people, lying at the table with them.
5:30 This set the Pharisees and their scribes grumbling. They said to his disciples, "Why are you eating and drinking with the tax collectors and sinners?"
5:31 Jesus answered them, "People who are healthy do not need a doctor. Sick people do.
5:32 I have not come to call righteous people. I have come to call sinners to repentance."
## New wine
5:33 They told him, "John's disciples often fast and say their prayers, and the Pharisees' disciples do the same. But yours eat and drink."
5:34 Jesus said to them, "You cannot make the groom's friends fast while the groom is with them, can you?
5:35 But days are coming when the groom will be taken away from them, and then they will fast, in those days."
5:36 He also told them a parable: "Nobody tears a patch from a new coat and puts it on an old coat. If he does, he will tear the new coat, and the patch from the new one will not go with the old.
5:37 And nobody puts new wine in old wineskins. Otherwise the new wine will burst the skins. It will spill out, and the skins will be lost.
5:38 No: fresh skins are what new wine must go into.
5:39 Besides, no one who has been drinking the old wants the new. 'The old is good,' he says."
