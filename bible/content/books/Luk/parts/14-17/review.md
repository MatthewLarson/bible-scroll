# Hostile review: Luke 14–17 (part 14-17)

Reviewer: hostile-reviewer agent (not the writer). Date: 2026-10-26.
Checked: plain.txt verse by verse against `sbl`, `byz` and the edition flags in texts/orig/Luk.json; texts/auto/Luk.json;
all 109 notes, 16 variants, 47 glosses, 4 chapter pages (settings, traditions, deep, spec), 32 topic contributions,
sources.json; the proposals `rule-of-the-congregation` (works.json), μαμωνᾶς and κόλπος (terms.json); parallel wording
against Matthew's published Plain (5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 16, 17, 18, 22, 23, 24, 25) and Mark's Plain (4, 8, 9, 10, 13);
`VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Luk/14-17` = 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is a strong part. The translation is faithful and readable. It follows the SBLGNT except at 15:16, where it
openly and defensibly follows NA28/THGNT. It tracks Matthew's and Mark's Plain closely where the Greek matches and
departs from them exactly where Luke's Greek differs. The notes are dense, mostly accurate and fair to Jewish
readers. Nearly every source the writer flagged as uncertain checks out. I found no invented source, reading or
manuscript, so there are no critical findings. There are three real problems:
1. Three of the four "Claude's theory" boxes say no published work makes the suggestion, but published work does
   (the Antipas–Aretas war at 14:31, the house of Annas at 16:28, the Samaritan and the Temple at 17:14–16).
2. A note makes a false Greek claim at 16:25.
3. The 14:23 traditions card asks a question on which, by its own admission, the churches no longer disagree.

The rest are minor accuracy slips, a few gloss and wording problems, small variant gaps and two places where the
wording drifts toward the ESV.

Findings: **0 critical, 3 major, 21 minor.**

## Findings

### 1. Major: three spec boxes claim no published precedent, but published work exists
**Where:** notes.json 14:31 (spec, "a lost war Luke's readers remembered"); notes.json 16:28 (spec, "a priestly
family in purple and linen?"); chapters.json ch. 17 spec box ("where the Samaritan's priest was").

**What is wrong:** STYLE.md requires a theory to say "where [it] overlaps with published scholarship". Each of these
says the opposite.
- **14:31.** The note says "I have not found this link argued in the commentaries I know". But the Antipas–Aretas war
  as background to the king parable is in older commentaries on Luke 14:31 and in Keener's *IVP Bible Background
  Commentary*. It is also discussed (and doubted) in the recent NTS article "Suing for Peace at Any Cost? Reading the
  Parable of the Two Kings (Luke 14.31–2) in Times of War".
- **16:28.** The note says "I cannot point to a published study that argues it". But the rich man as Caiaphas, with
  the five brothers as Annas' five sons, is an old suggestion. The Wikipedia article "Rich man and Lazarus" credits it
  to Claude-Joseph Drioux and others, and it is widespread in Christadelphian writing.
- **Ch. 17.** The box says "I have not checked whether any commentator argues it". But Dennis Hamm, "What the
  Samaritan Leper Sees: The Narrative Christology of Luke 17:11–19," *CBQ* 56 (1994): 273–287, builds on exactly
  this: the Gerizim/Jerusalem dilemma, and Jesus as the place where God is thanked.

**Fix:** Keep all three as `spec` if the writer wishes. Rewrite each to say that the idea has been proposed before,
name the proposer (add keys: `keener-ivpbbc`, or the NTS article; `drioux` only if a real citation can be found,
otherwise say "an old suggestion, repeated in popular commentary"; `hamm-samaritan-leper`), and say what the box
adds. For 16:28 the honest frame is a known minority suggestion. The `wealth` topic's "uncommon" line already treats
it that way.

### 2. Major: false Greek claim, ἀπέλαβες is not "the word Jesus used" in 6:24
**Where:** notes.json 16:25, "you got your good things in full during your life" ("The great reversal").

**What is wrong:** The note says ἀπέλαβες "means 'you received in full,' the word Jesus used in the woe on the rich,
who have already received their comfort (6:24)". But Luke 6:24 has ἀπέχετε (from ἀπέχω, the receipt formula "paid in
full"), not ἀπολαμβάνω. The two are different verbs with a similar sense.

**Fix:** "The verb ἀπέλαβες means 'you received (in full, or as your due)'. It echoes the woe on the rich in 6:24,
who 'have received' (ἀπέχετε, a different verb used on receipts) their comfort already."

### 3. Major: the 14:23 traditions card is not a live conflict, and leaves out traditions that had a distinct view
**Where:** chapters.json ch. 14 `traditions` (top `church-and-state`).

**What is wrong:** STYLE.md says to use a card "only where the answers really conflict". The card's own question says
"all of those below now reject force". What it records is a historical difference now resolved, and the deep dive
"Compel them to come in" already tells that history well. The card also leaves out two traditions that did have a
distinct position: Orthodox (Byzantine imperial law against heretics; no Augustinian theory of coercion) and Anglican
(an established church with Test Acts until 1828–29). That makes the "now agree" claim look tidier than it is.

**Fix:** Either
(a) drop the card and keep the deep dive and note (simplest), or
(b) reframe it as a question the churches still answer differently, for example "Should the state favor or establish
a church?" Catholic teaching on the confessional state was before 1965 and changed after Dignitatis Humanae. The
Anglican and Lutheran state churches answer one way; Baptists, Anabaptists and American Presbyterians reject
establishment; the Orthodox teach symphonia. Give each its sources.

If (b), also check that `church-and-state` is the right `top` and that the question isn't already carded at Matthew
22:21 ("Caesar", top `church-and-state`). If it is, point there instead of repeating it.

### 9. Minor: the 17:10 merit card leaves out the Orthodox view, and its Lutheran row is cited loosely
**Where:** chapters.json ch. 17 `traditions` (top `justification`).

**What is wrong:**
- The Orthodox have a distinct answer. They reject a treasury of merits and "works of supererogation" as Latin
  categories, and teach synergy: good works are the fruit of cooperation with grace, without a legal reckoning of
  merit. The Confession of Dositheus, decree 13, says works are not mere signs but are rewarded. Leaving the Orthodox
  out makes the card look like a Catholic–Protestant binary.
- The Lutheran row says the Apology "rejects works of supererogation and cites Christ's words". The Luke 17:10
  citation is in Apology IV/V, "Love and the fulfilling of the law" (Book of Concord numbering: Art. V (III)), where
  Melanchthon also answers the opponents' "antistrophe" on the verse. The rejection of supererogation is in the
  articles on satisfactions and monastic vows (XII, XXVII).

**Fix:** Add an Orthodox row with the `dositheus-decrees` key. Make the Lutheran row precise: "Apology IV (V), on
Luke 17:10; and XII and XXVII, against works of supererogation".

### 13. Minor: Sanders' definition of "sinners" stated as fact
**Where:** notes.json 15:1, "Who were the 'sinners'?"

**What is wrong:** The note says "'Sinners' ... is not a name for the common people who were less strict than the
Pharisees". That is one side of a debate: Sanders against Jeremias's view that "sinners" meant the common people, the
am ha'aretz. Dunn ("Pharisees, Sinners and Jesus") argued that "sinners" was also a factional label that stricter
groups used for other Jews. The sentence "That is why ... before they had made restitution or offered sacrifice" is
Sanders' own thesis, and he is a named, debated scholar.

**Fix:** Attribute both sentences to Sanders and add one line for the factional view (Dunn).

### 14. Minor: the size of the bath is given too precisely

Antiquities 8.57, implies the higher figure.

**Fix:** "roughly 22 to 39 liters (6 to 10 gallons)", and set the gloss to "large jars, roughly 6 to 10 gallons
each".

### 15. Minor: quotation of 9:23 doesn't match its Plain; the claim about Simon is too absolute

Cyrene "the only person in the Gospels who literally does what this verse says". In John 19:17 Jesus carries his own
cross, though not "behind" anyone.

**Fix:** "every day", and "the only person who literally carries a cross behind Jesus".

### 20. Minor: glosses
**Where:** glosses.json 15:2, 15:29, 17:6.

**What is wrong:**
- 15:2 glosses "scribes" as "experts in the law of Moses". That is the gloss the νομικός term (proposals/nt/terms.json)
  reserves for "lawyers" (14:3), and the term says the two words are "kept distinct".
- 15:29 glosses "a goat kid" as "even a small party". That glosses the occasion, not the phrase, and reads oddly.
- 17:6 glosses "mustard seed" as "the tiniest seed there is". That is false as botany. Jesus' hyperbole, and the note's
  "the smallest seed a Galilean knew", are better.

**Fix:**
- 15:2: "scholars who copied and taught the Scriptures".
- 15:29: move the gloss onto "to have a party with my friends" ("a small party with my friends"), or drop it.
- 17:6: "a tiny seed, a byword for smallness".

### 21. Minor: Plain wording that adds or blurs
**Where:** plain.txt 16:16, 16:2, 14:31, 15:20.

**What is wrong:**
- 16:16 "The Law and the Prophets went up to John." The Greek has no verb (μέχρι Ἰωάννου). "Went up to" is
  unidiomatic and can read as "went up to meet John". Matthew 11:13's Plain has "prophesied up until John" (a
  different, verbal Greek).
- 16:2 "You can't go on managing for me." "For me" is not in the Greek.
- 14:31 "take advice". βουλεύσεται means "deliberate, consider", with no counselors implied.
- 15:20 "kissed him again and again". κατεφίλησεν is intensive ("kissed him warmly"). Repetition is one possible
  sense, not the only one. It is acceptable if the note says so; currently nothing does.

**Fix:**
- 16:16: "The Law and the Prophets lasted until John." (The chapter summary already says "lasted until John"; keep the
  note's remark that the Greek has no verb.)
- 16:2: "You can't be manager any longer."
- 14:31: "think it over".
- 15:20: "kissed him warmly", or keep the current wording and add a phrase to the 15:20 note.

### 22. Minor: drift toward ESV wording
**Where:** plain.txt 16:8, 14:23, 15:7.

**What is wrong:**
- 16:8 ends "...in dealing with their own generation". This is the ESV's interpretive phrase for εἰς τὴν γενεὰν τὴν
  ἑαυτῶν, and it matches five or more words in order.
- 14:23 "...and make people come in, so that my house is filled" follows the ESV's sentence shape (object "people"
  supplied; "that my house may be filled").
- 15:7 runs close to the ESV ("more joy ... over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous ..."),
  though most of that is forced by the Greek.

**Fix:**
- 16:8: "...are shrewder toward their own kind than the children of light are."
- 14:23: "...and press them to come in. I want my house full."
- 15:7: may stay, since the Greek forces it.

### 23. Minor: small variant gaps
**Where:** variants.json; notes.json 17:11.

**What is wrong:**
- **17:11.** SBL/NA have διὰ μέσον; the Byzantine text and TR have διὰ μέσου (KJV "through the midst of Samaria and
  Galilee"). The Plain "the country between" depends on μέσον. The note's "Literally ... 'through the middle of'"

  note's "literally".
- **15:22.** ταχύ "Hurry" is absent from the Byzantine text and TR (KJV "Bring forth the best robe"). This is optional
  but cheap.
- **14:3.** "or not" (ἢ οὔ) is absent from the Byzantine text and TR (KJV "Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day?").
  Optional.
- **15:21.** Westcott-Hort printed the longer reading in brackets. "Printed by Westcott-Hort" should say "(in
  brackets)" if the fixer can confirm it.

### 24. Minor: φρόνιμος, "shrewd" at 16:8 and "sensible" elsewhere (recommendation)
**Where:** plain.txt 16:8 against Luke 12:42, Matthew 7:24, 24:45 and 25:2 ("sensible"); Matthew 10:16 ("sharp").

**Recommendation:** Keep "shrewdly / shrewder" at 16:8. "Sensible" there would turn the master's praise of a crooked
man into moral approval, and Matthew already varies the word by context (10:16 "sharp"). But Luke deliberately pairs
this passage with 12:42 ("the manager who is faithful and sensible", the same οἰκονόμος and the same φρόνιμος). Add a
short `word` note at 16:8 (anchor "acting so shrewdly", orig φρονίμως / φρόνιμος) that names the link: the manager of
12:42 is faithful and φρόνιμος, this one has only the second quality, and 16:10–12 then demands the first. Record the
context rule (good sense = "sensible"; worldly cleverness = "shrewd") in proposals/nt/terms.json so that the 18-21
part and Matthew stay aligned. The 18-21 part already quotes 16:8 with "shrewder", so no change is needed there.
