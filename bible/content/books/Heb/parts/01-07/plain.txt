## God has spoken in a Son
1:1 In many pieces and in many ways, God spoke long ago to the fathers in the prophets.
1:2 At the end of these days he has spoken to us in a Son. God appointed him heir of all things, and through him he also made the ages.
1:3 He is the brightness that shines out from God's glory and the exact stamp of his being, and he carries all things along by his word of power. Once he had made a cleansing for sins, he took his seat at the right hand of the Majesty in the heights.
1:4 He became better than the angels. The name he received as his inheritance is greater than theirs by just as much.
## The Son above the angels
1:5 For did God ever say to any one of the angels: "You are my son. This day I have fathered you"? And again: "I will be a father to him, and he will be a son to me"?
1:6 Again, when he leads the firstborn into the world, he says: "And let all the angels of God bow down to him."
1:7 Speaking about the angels, he says: "He makes his angels into winds, and his attendants into a flame of fire."
1:8 But to the Son he says: "Your throne, O God, is for ever and ever, and the scepter of fairness is your kingdom's scepter.
1:9 You have loved righteousness and hated lawlessness. For this reason God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of rejoicing above your partners."
1:10 And: "At the start, Lord, you set the earth on its foundations, and the heavens are what your hands have made.
1:11 They will be lost, but you go on. All of them will wear thin like a coat,
1:12 and you will roll them up like a mantle, and like a coat they will be changed. But you are the same one, and your years will never run out."
1:13 And did he ever say to any one of the angels: "Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies down as a footstool for your feet"?
1:14 Aren't they all spirits in holy service, sent on duty for the sake of the people who are going to inherit salvation?
## Do not drift away
2:1 For this reason we have to keep our minds on what we heard all the more, or we may drift off.
2:2 The word spoken through angels held firm, and every transgression and every refusal to listen received the penalty it had earned.
2:3 Then what way out will there be for us if we ignore a salvation this great? It began to be spoken through the Lord, and those who heard him made it sure to us,
2:4 while God joined in their witness with signs and wonders and many different acts of power, and with portions of the Holy Spirit, handed out as he wanted.
## Lower than the angels for a while
2:5 God did not put the world to come under angels, and that is the world we are talking about.
2:6 Instead, someone has declared somewhere: "What is a human being, that you remember him, or a son of man, that you look after him?
2:7 You set him a little below the angels; with glory and honor you crowned him.
2:8 You placed everything down beneath his feet." When he placed everything under him, he left out nothing at all. Now, though, we see that not everything has been put under him yet.
2:9 What we do see is Jesus, who was set a little below the angels, and who, because of the death he suffered, has been crowned with glory and honor. This was so that by God's grace he would taste death on behalf of everyone.
## Made like his brothers
2:10 It suited God, the one all things are for and all things come through, as he led many sons into glory, to make perfect, through sufferings, the Founder of their salvation.
2:11 The one who makes holy and the ones being made holy all come from one. That is the reason he is not ashamed to name them brothers,
2:12 saying: "I will spread the news of your name to my brothers; in the middle of the assembly I will sing your praise."
2:13 And again: "I will keep my trust in him." And again: "Look: I and the children God gave me."
2:14 Since, then, the children have blood and flesh in common, he too took a share in these very things in the same way. He did it so that by his death he could make powerless the one who wields the might of death, that is, the devil,
2:15 and set loose all who lived their whole lives as slaves because they were afraid to die.
2:16 It is clear that he does not take hold of angels. He takes hold of Abraham's offspring.
2:17 This is why he was bound to become like his brothers in everything: to be a high priest who is merciful and faithful in what concerns God, and to make atonement for the people's sins.
2:18 He has suffered through being tested himself, so he can come to the aid of those who are being tested.
## Moses the servant, the Son over the house
3:1 So, holy brothers, partners in a calling from heaven, turn your minds to the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus.
3:2 He was faithful to the one who made him, as Moses also was in his house.
3:3 This one has been judged worthy of greater glory than Moses, in the same way that the one who builds a house gets more honor than the house.
3:4 Each house has someone who builds it, but God is the one who built all things.
3:5 Moses was faithful in the whole of God's house as an attendant, a witness to things that would be spoken later.
3:6 Christ, though, is faithful as a Son set over his house, and that house is us, if we hold on to our boldness and to the pride we take in our hope.
## Today, if you hear his voice
3:7 That is why, as the Holy Spirit says: "Today, if you hear his voice,
3:8 do not make your hearts hard as at the Bitter Quarrel, on the day of the Testing in the wilderness.
3:9 There your fathers tested me with their testing, and they watched my works
3:10 for forty years. So I felt disgust at this generation, and I said, 'They wander in their hearts all the time; they never came to know my ways.'
3:11 As I swore in my anger: 'They will never come into my rest!'"
3:12 Be on guard, brothers, so that not one of you will have a wicked heart of unbelief, the kind that deserts the living God.
3:13 Rather, keep urging each other on day by day, for as long as the word "Today" is still being spoken, so that sin does not trick any of you and harden you.
3:14 We have become partners of Christ, as long as we keep the confidence we began with steady until the end.
3:15 As long as it is being said, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not make your hearts hard as at the Bitter Quarrel,"
3:16 who heard and yet quarreled bitterly with him? Wasn't it everyone who came out of Egypt under Moses' lead?
3:17 Whom did he feel disgust at for forty years? Wasn't it the ones who sinned, whose corpses dropped in the wilderness?
3:18 And who were the people he swore would never come into his rest? Who else but those who refused to obey?
3:19 And we see that unbelief is what kept them from being able to go in.
## The rest that is still open
4:1 The promise that we may come into his rest is still left open. So let us be afraid that one of you might be judged to have missed it.
4:2 We, like them, have had the good news announced to us. But the word they heard was no help to them, since they were not blended by faith with the ones who listened.
4:3 We who have come to believe are going into rest. He said, "As I swore in my anger: 'They will never come into my rest!'" Yet his works had been done ever since the world was founded.
4:4 Somewhere, you see, he spoke about the seventh day like this: "And on the seventh day God rested from all his works."
4:5 And in this place again: "They will never come into my rest!"
4:6 So it is still left for some people to go into it, and the ones who had the good news told to them earlier failed to go in because they disobeyed.
4:7 That is why he marks out a day again, "Today," speaking in David after such a long time, as has been said before: "Today, if you hear his voice, do not make your hearts hard."
4:8 If Joshua had brought them into rest, he would not be talking after that about some other day.
4:9 That means a Sabbath rest is still waiting for God's people.
4:10 The one who has gone into his rest has stopped from his own works too, just as God stopped from the ones that were his.
4:11 So let us be eager to go into that rest, so that nobody falls and becomes another example of the same disobedience.
4:12 The word of God is alive and at work. It cuts deeper than any sword with two edges, going all the way through to where soul and spirit are divided, and joints and marrow. It sifts and judges the musings and plans of the heart.
4:13 Not one created thing is out of his sight. Everything is naked and laid wide open to the eyes of the one we will have to answer to.
## A high priest who feels for us
4:14 We have a great high priest, then, one who has gone right through the heavens: Jesus, the Son of God. So let us keep a strong hold on our confession.
4:15 The high priest we have is not one who is unable to feel for us in our weaknesses. He has been tested in all things, in the same way we are, but apart from sin.
4:16 So let us come near to the throne of grace with boldness, to receive mercy and to find grace that helps us at just the right time.
5:1 Every high priest is taken from among human beings and is put in place for human beings, in what concerns God, to offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.
5:2 He can be patient with people who do wrong without knowing or who wander off course, because he too is clothed in weakness.
5:3 And because of it he is bound to make an offering for sins on his own behalf, just as he does for the people.
5:4 Nobody takes this honor on his own. He is called to it by God, as Aaron was.
5:5 In this way Christ also did not give himself the glory of becoming high priest. The one who gave it was the one who said to him, "You are my son. This day I have fathered you,"
5:6 just as he says somewhere else too: "You are a priest forever, after the pattern of Melchizedek."
5:7 While he lived in the flesh, he offered up requests and pleading to the one who could rescue him out of death. He cried out loudly and wept, and because of his reverence he was heard.
5:8 Even though he was a Son, the things he suffered taught him obedience.
5:9 And once he had been made perfect, he became the cause of eternal salvation to everyone who obeys him,
5:10 for God has hailed him as high priest after the pattern of Melchizedek.
## Milk and solid food
5:11 On this subject we have plenty to say, but it is hard to put into words for you, since your hearing has gone sluggish.
5:12 Given how long it has been, you should be teachers. Instead you need someone to teach you all over again the basic elements, the first lessons, of God's sayings. You have gone back to needing milk instead of solid food.
5:13 Anyone who still lives on milk has no experience with the message of righteousness. He is a baby.
5:14 Solid food belongs to grown-ups, those whose senses have been exercised by habit to tell good and bad apart.
6:1 So let us leave the beginning message about Christ behind and be carried on toward full growth. Let us not be laying the foundation all over again: repentance from dead works and faith in God,
6:2 teaching about washings and the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment.
6:3 And we will do it, if God lets us.
## No way back
6:4 For it is impossible, with those who once received the light, who tasted the gift from heaven and became partners in the Holy Spirit,
6:5 who tasted that God's word is good, and tasted the powers of the age to come,
6:6 and then fell away: impossible to make them new again and bring them to repentance, crucifying the Son of God over again for themselves and exposing him to public shame.
6:7 Think of ground that soaks up the rain that keeps coming down on it and grows plants that are of use to the people who work it. Such ground gets a share of blessing from God.
6:8 But if it puts out thorns and thistles, it fails the test and a curse is not far off. It ends up in the fire.
## Better things for you
6:9 But about you, beloved, we are persuaded of better things, things that go hand in hand with salvation, even if we talk like this.
6:10 God is not so unfair as to forget what you have done, and the love you showed toward his name when you served the saints, and the way you serve them still.
6:11 Our desire is that each one of you show that same eagerness for the full certainty of the hope, right up to the end,
6:12 so you do not turn sluggish, but follow the example of those who, by faith and by staying patient, inherit the promises.
## God's oath, the anchor of hope
6:13 God made a promise to Abraham. He could not swear by anyone greater, so he swore an oath by himself,
6:14 saying: "I will bless you richly, and I will multiply you greatly."
6:15 That is how Abraham, by being patient, came to get what was promised.
6:16 Human beings swear by the one who is greater, and for them the oath puts an end to all their arguing, as a guarantee.
6:17 That is why God, wanting to show even more plainly to those who would inherit the promise that his plan could not be changed, stepped in between with an oath.
6:18 God did this so that through two unchangeable things, where it is impossible that God would lie, we who have run to him for safety would have strong encouragement to grab hold of the hope that lies ahead of us.
6:19 We hold it like an anchor of the soul, sure and steady, an anchor that goes in to the inner side of the veil,
6:20 where Jesus went in for us as the one who runs ahead, now that he has become high priest forever, after the pattern of Melchizedek.
## Melchizedek, king and priest
7:1 Melchizedek was Salem's king and a priest of God Most High. When Abraham was on his way back from striking down the kings, Melchizedek met him and blessed him.
7:2 Abraham also handed him a tenth of everything. Translated, his name first means "king of righteousness." Next, he is king of Salem too, and that means "king of peace."
7:3 He has no father, no mother, no family tree. His days have no start and his life has no end. He has been made like the Son of God, and he remains a priest for all time.
7:4 Look at how great he is! Abraham, the patriarch himself, gave him a tenth taken from the top of the plunder.
7:5 Those of Levi's sons who take up the priesthood have orders, under the law, to take a tenth from the people, which means from their own brothers, though they too came out of Abraham's body.
7:6 But this man, whose family line does not come from them, has taken a tenth from Abraham, and he has blessed the man who held the promises.
7:7 No one can argue with this: the lesser person gets his blessing from the better one.
7:8 Here men who die take the tenths. There, it is one about whom the witness is given that he is alive.
7:9 So to speak, Levi himself, who takes the tenths, has been made to give a tenth through Abraham,
7:10 since he was still in his father's body when Melchizedek met him.
## A different priest, a change of law
7:11 Suppose perfection had come through the Levites' priesthood, the priesthood on which the people's law was based. Then why was it still needed for a different priest to arise, after the pattern of Melchizedek, and not be called after the pattern of Aaron?
7:12 When the priesthood moves to someone else, a change of law has to come with it.
7:13 The man these things are spoken about has his share in a different tribe, and no one from it has ever stood at the altar.
7:14 Everyone knows our Lord sprang up out of Judah, a tribe Moses never mentioned when he spoke about priests.
7:15 The point is plainer still, since a different priest is rising up in the likeness of Melchizedek,
7:16 one who has become a priest not on the basis of a law with a command about the flesh, but by the power of a life that can never be torn down.
7:17 The witness given is this: "You are a priest forever, after the pattern of Melchizedek."
7:18 A command that came before is canceled, then, because it was weak and did no good
7:19 (the law did not make anything perfect), and in its place a better hope is being brought in, and through it we come near to God.
## Sworn in forever
7:20 And all this did not happen apart from an oath being sworn. The others have become priests with no oath sworn,
7:21 but this one with an oath, through the one who said to him: "The Lord has taken an oath, and he will not go back on it: 'You are a priest forever.'"
7:22 To that same degree Jesus has become the one who guarantees a better covenant.
7:23 There have been a great many of those other priests, since death stopped each of them from staying on.
7:24 But because he remains forever, the priesthood he holds never passes to another.
7:25 So he is able to save fully and for all time those who come near to God through him. He lives always, to plead for them.
7:26 This is the high priest that suited us: holy, free of evil, free of stain, separated from sinners, and lifted higher than the heavens.
7:27 Each day the high priests have to bring up sacrifices, first for their own sins, and after that for the people's. He has no such need. He did it once for all, by bringing himself up as the offering.
7:28 The law puts human beings in place as high priests, people who are weak. But the word of the oath, sworn after the law, puts in place a Son made perfect forever.
