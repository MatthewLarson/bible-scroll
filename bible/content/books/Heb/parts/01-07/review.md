# Hostile review: Hebrews 1:1-7:28 (part 01-07), book.json and the `hebrews` topic core

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-29. Scope: `parts/01-07/` (plain.txt 129 verses, notes 159, variants 14, glosses 50, chapters 1-7 with settings and two traditions cards, topics 24), the whole-book `book.json`, and `content/proposals/nt/topics/hebrews.json`. `node tools/build.js --check Heb` and `--check Heb/01-07`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is careful work and no critical errors turned up. The Plain follows the Greek closely, the quotation notes match the site's Plain exactly (I checked every `"..." (Book c:v)` pair in notes.json by script: no mismatches), the settings are specific, and the writer's `sources-checked.md` is honest about what was and was not opened. Most of the facts the lead asked me to test hold up: the Chalcedon wording, the Ordinal (1662 BCP), Vanhoye 1963, the NT's "no other book calls Jesus priest", Dositheus decree 16, Chrysostom's homily 9, Calvin on the Novatians, OCA, Apology XIII, Wesley on 6:4-6, 11Q13 and 2 Enoch 69-73 all check out (details below). The real problems are elsewhere. One sentence of the authorship table says something false ("Most scholars, ancient and modern"). The book's `spec` box rests on a false premise and three spec boxes carry "has not checked" hedges that STYLE rules 2 and 4 forbid. The 1:8 and 2:9 notes are thinner than the brief asked, and the 2:9 note sets out what five churches teach about the scope of the atonement with no source. The supersession section names no one. The 6:4-6 card disagrees with the writer's own note on the Reformed and Catholic readings. A KJV difference at 7:14 is missing from the variants. A handful of canon-history and scholarly attributions rest on Wikipedia or on a conjecture I could not match to the cited scholar. No 8th-grade or em-dash problems (zero em-dashes in plain, notes, chapters, book.json).

Findings: **0 critical, 9 major, 17 minor.**

How I verified: where a finding says "verified" I opened a page this session (named in the finding). Where it says "could not verify" I tried and failed; treat those as unconfirmed, not as wrong.

## Major

**1. book.json, `authorship` "Where scholarship stands": "Most scholars, ancient and modern, doubt that Paul wrote it."**
False for "ancient". The same table says the East held Paul ("Traditionally Paul in the East"), and Eusebius (3.3.5, which the table cites) records the East accepting it as Paul's and only some in the West disputing it. Ancient opinion was divided; the modern majority is against Paul.
Fix: "Most modern scholars doubt that Paul wrote it. Ancient opinion was divided: the Greek East largely received it as Paul's (Origen allowing that the wording was another's), while some in the West doubted." Also the `facts` row "Most scholars today think it was not Paul" is fine as is.

**2. book.json `spec` "a sermon that hides its preacher": false premise, internal contradiction, and a forbidden hedge.**
(a) "Hebrews is the only New Testament letter with no author's name" is wrong. 1 John names no author either (and 2 and 3 John give only "the Elder", which the site's own brief-general.md treats as a title). The site's Plain of 1 John 1:1-4 has no author's name. (b) "introduces Scripture as God or the Spirit speaking rather than naming Moses or David" sits against 4:7 ("speaking in David"), in this very part, whose note (#78) says God spoke "in David", and against Hebrews' naming of Moses elsewhere (3:2-5 in this part). The note at 2:6 (#33) says it "almost never" names authors; the spec says it does not. (c) "Claude has not checked whether a published commentary makes the same suggestion" is exactly the kind of hedge STYLE rule 2 bans, and rule 4 requires a spec box to say where it overlaps published scholarship. The same hedge is in the chapter 1 deep box ("Claude has not checked which published commentaries argue for this sequence") and in note 3:10 (#66, "Claude has not checked whether a published commentary makes the same suggestion").
Fix: correct (a) to "the only New Testament letter that is also a sermon with no name or title for its author in the text, besides 1 John" or drop the "only" claim; reconcile (b) with 4:7; and for all three spec boxes either check scholarship (a commentary opened this session) and say where the idea overlaps, or state plainly "Claude's own idea, offered as a possibility" with no "not checked" sentence. Same cleanup for the 3:10 and chapter 1 boxes.

**3. Note 1:8 (#18) "Is the Son called 'God'?" is thin for a priority text.**
- It gives two readings (vocative; "God is your throne") and one scholar, Westcott. `sources-checked.md` §16 shows Westcott was read only through a reproduction on a Jehovah's Witnesses apologetics blog (newworldtranslation.blogspot.com). Westcott's rendering is the New World Translation's, which the note never says. Source the note to Westcott's own commentary, or to a page that is not partisan, or cut the page-and-year claim.
- "Most modern commentators take (1)" is an unsourced consensus claim (STYLE 3: label consensus and say who dissents). Name two or three commentators and say what the dissenters say.
- The note never says which reading the Plain follows or why (the Plain follows (1)). It does not mention the variant at 1:8 ("his kingdom"), which bears on the grammar (the variant entry notes this; the note does not point to it).
- Missing readings: that the psalm's own "O God" addresses a human king and Hebrews reapplies it (the note says only "Jewish readers take the psalm as addressed to a human king"); that the Son is "God" in a derived or agent sense (the reading Unitarian and Jehovah's Witness traditions take, both in TRADITIONS); and that the Hebrew of Psalm 45:6 itself is disputed ("your throne is God's / of God" proposals). The note points to "the note on Psalm 45:6" but the reader of Hebrews gets no summary.
- The variant entry's `why` says "Westcott and Hort printed it [his kingdom] because it is the harder reading" and "Most editors judge 'his' to be an early slip". Both are claims about editors' reasoning with no source. I found this session that a 2023 Tyndale Bulletin article by Daniel Stevens argues "his kingdom" is original on internal grounds (it also states NA28 prints "your"; TAGNT marks in `texts/orig/Heb.json` agree: `σου` carries N n S Y G T B). Fairness requires giving that view.
Fix: expand to the full set of readings with named holders, say which one the Plain follows, link to the variant, and drop the motive claims or source them.

**4. 2:9 (notes #38, #39, variant #4): "handled with every reading" is not met.**
- Note #39 ("Died for everyone?") attributes positions on the scope of the atonement to "Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran and Wesleyan teachers" and to "Reformed teachers who hold 'particular' or 'definite' atonement", then says "Both agree that Christ's death is sufficient for all." No church document is cited and none is on the `s` list. STYLE rule 2 requires what a named church says to be checked in a document opened this session. Give each from its own document (for example the Canons of Dort, second main point, for the Reformed; the Catechism for Catholics) or reduce the note to the grammar (masculine "everyone" or neuter "everything") and point to the atonement topic.
- The grammar of the verse is not covered: *dia to pathēma tou thanatou* can attach to "crowned" (the Plain's choice: "now crowned ... because of the death he suffered") or to "set a little below the angels" ("made lower because of the suffering of death"). The Plain commits to the first without a note. Also the Plain's "now crowned" adds "now".
- The note on the variant (#4) lists "Jerome" under *chariti*. The two sources the writer used disagree: the Wikipedia list the writer opened puts Jerome among the *chōris* witnesses, and La Parola lists "Jerome" under *chariti* and "mss according to Jerome" under *chōris*. The variant `b` says the lists "differ slightly" about 0243 and 0121b but does not mention Jerome. Resolve or drop Jerome from both lists.
- Good: the variant entry correctly says SBL prints *chōris* (confirmed against `texts/orig/Heb.json`, where SBL reads *chōris* and Byz *chariti*), and the "harder reading" case is stated fairly.

**5. Supersession is raised but handled without naming anyone (book.json section "Hebrews and the Jewish people"; note 7:12 #144).**
"Many Christian and Jewish scholars now urge more care" and "Jewish readers answer that the Torah calls its priesthood and covenant lasting" name no scholar, rabbi, church or document. STYLE rule 3: "name who holds it". Chapter 7's J lens does it properly with Exodus 40:15 (quote verified against the site's Exodus Plain: "a priesthood for all time"), but the book page and note #144 do not. The book section also omits the churches' own statements (for example, post-Holocaust Catholic and Protestant statements) that Matt's brief would want beside the older "church has replaced Israel" reading.
Fix: name two or three Jewish and Christian scholars or statements, each from something opened this session, and say who holds the stronger supersessionist reading as well as the opposing one. Keep the present neutral framing.

**6. 6:4-6 `traditions` card (chapter 6): fair in outline, but it disagrees with the writer's own note and has unverified wording.**
- Note #117 gives four readings. Reading (3), that the warning is hypothetical and describes what would happen, is held by "some Reformed and Baptist readers". The card's Reformed and Baptist rows omit it and say only "No", so the card states a stricter, one-view position than the note. Reading (4), that Catholic and Orthodox churches read 6:6 as excluding a second baptism, appears in the note, but the Catholic row never mentions Hebrews 6 (it gives Trent session 6 canon 23 and Catechism 1861 on mortal sin, which are about falling from grace in general). Only the Orthodox row (Chrysostom) says how the church reads the verse.
- The Baptist row says "Confessional Baptists hold the same" and cites 1689 17.1 only. General (Arminian) Baptists, such as Free Will Baptists, hold the opposite. The card says "Confessional Baptists" which hedges the point, but a reader sees Baptists on the "No" side with no counterpart.
- Missing voices with distinct views: Pentecostal, Anabaptist, Restorationist, Adventist, Oriental Orthodox (all in TRADITIONS). At least say why the card lists seven.
- Dort V.6: the card puts in quotation marks "forfeit the grace of adoption and the state of justification". The Dort wording I saw this session reads differently ("lose the grace of adoption, and forfeit the state of justification" in one translation, and a longer phrasing with "sink so deep" in another). I could not match the card's wording to a translation. Name the translation and quote it exactly.
- Calvin "whom God leaves as reprobate": I verified only the Novatian sentence of Calvin on 6:4 (ccel.org, Calvin's Hebrews commentary on 6:4). I could not verify "reprobate". Check the sentence in Calvin or reword.
- Verified in documents opened this session: Westminster 17.1 phrasing is as the card quotes it (matches the standard text I saw cited; the card's exact words are not reproduced elsewhere in my search results, so recheck word for word), Dositheus decree 16 on rebaptism and "Mystery of Penance" and "adoption of a son" (crivoice.org), Chrysostom homily 9 on the laver (newadvent.org), Wesley on 6:4-6 (sacred-texts.com/bib/cmt/wesley/heb006.htm, quote located), Trent session 6 canon 23 (subject confirmed, hanover.edu page), Thirty-Nine Articles 16 (anglicansonline.org per the writer's log).
No verdict is stated and no tradition is ranked. Order is not alphabetical but not obviously partisan.

**18. 1:14 "who will soon inherit salvation".** *Tous mellontas klēronomein* is "who are about to inherit". "Soon" adds imminence. The Plain renders the same *mellō* family as "to come" at 2:5 ("the world to come") and 6:5 ("the age to come"). **19. 2:3 "if we shrug off a salvation this great".** *Ameleō* is "neglect, pay no attention".
