# Hostile review: 3 John (3Jn)

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-10-29. Checked against `texts/orig/3Jn.json` (SBL, Byzantine, word list with edition marks), `.scratch/nt/3Jn/` (sources-checked.md, drift-audit.md, saved Wikipedia wikitext for 3 John and its variants, saved commentaries), and pages opened this session (Wikipedia "Diotrephes" and "Antilegomena", New Advent Eusebius book 3). `node tools/build.js --check 3Jn` gives the one known error (`1-john` topic) and 0 warnings. 3 John's `sources.json` is `{}`; every source key it uses is defined in 2Jn's sources.json (keys are global, so the check passes, but the book is not self-contained).

## Verdict

The Plain English Translation is faithful and readable; I found no mistranslation of names, numbers or tenses, and the shared lines with 2 John match where the Greek matches. The decision to print the Greek's verse 15 under the KJV's 1:14, with a note, is correct and clearly explained. The NA28-over-SBL choice at 1:4 (no article before "truth") is real: the word list marks `τῇ` as `nSYWG`, so NA28 omits it, and it is the only place in 3 John where NA28 and SBL differ (confirmed against the marks); the variant entry names the editions honestly. The witness lists in variants.json match the saved Wikipedia list (I compared each entry for 1:4, 1:5, 1:8, 1:9, 1:12 and 1:14). The serious problem is attribution. In the Diotrephes material the content credits Margaret Mitchell, C. H. Dodd, John Painter and an NET Bible note with specific views that the Wikipedia pages the writer opened (and the live "Diotrephes" page I opened) do not contain. Those are invented attributions in the sense of STYLE rule 2, and they sit in the deep dive, a settings card, a variant note and the audience card. Besides that, the canon paragraph overstates, the planned church-order and prosperity coverage is thin and rests on Wikipedia alone, and one note describes a word-play the Plain English Translation does not carry. One critical, three major, nine minor.

## Findings

### Critical

**1. Views credited to named scholars and to a Bible note that the opened sources do not contain (chapters.json deep dive "Diotrephes and the Elder" and settings 1:9-10; book.json audience `views`; variants.json 1:9 `b`; book.json `reading` list in 2Jn).**
(a) The deep dive: "Readers explain the conflict in several ways, which Margaret M. Mitchell surveyed in 1998 (as Wikipedia reports): a theological dispute, a clash between rival church authorities, a disagreement about who should pay for the mission, or personal dislike." The settings card repeats it ("the readings Mitchell surveys"). I opened Wikipedia "Diotrephes": it cites Mitchell's article (JBL 117, 1998) in a footnote with "bibliography of the conflict on each possible arena", and nowhere lists these four readings. The saved 3 John page has no Mitchell at all. The four-way list is therefore the writer's, put under a scholar's name.
(b) Audience `views`: "Gaius inside it ... (a suggestion the NET Bible's note, quoted by Wikipedia, mentions and rejects)". Searching the saved 3 John page for any NET Bible note on Gaius finds nothing (the only NET citation in the saved pages is on Athanasius and 2 John).
(c) Variant 1:9, `b`: "a lost letter of recommendation (the view of most, Wikipedia reports, citing Dodd), or 2 John (John Painter)". The saved page says only that Dodd takes 3 John as a recommendation letter for Demetrius; it says nothing of a lost letter of recommendation in 1:9 or of "most". The Painter claim has the same gap as 2 John finding 3a: the page cites Painter 374-375 for a "previous letter ... suppressed by Diotrephes", not for identifying it with 2 John. Plummer (saved) rejects 1 and 2 John for 1:9. The deep dive and the settings card ("Painter, as Wikipedia summarizes him"; "Diotrephes applied the rule of 2 John 1:10 against the Elder's own people") repeat the identification.
Fix: remove the Mitchell list, or reduce it to what the opened page supports (Mitchell wrote on the possible arenas of the conflict, JBL 117 (1998)); delete the NET Bible clause (or open the NET note and quote it); in 1:9 `b` say only what Plummer says (not 1 or 2 John, a lost official letter) and what Wikipedia reports of Dodd; and either open Painter or a page that reports his view, or remove the 2 John identification from every place it appears. Whether "the Elder wrote to the church" was 2 John is a real debate (Plummer says no); present it as a debated question with Plummer's side sourced.

### Major

**2. The canon paragraph rests on Wikipedia and overstates (book.json section "3 John among the books of the Bible").**
"... the Assyrian Church of the East still does not count them, according to its 2020 catechism as Wikipedia reports it. Every other church today includes the letter." Same problems as 2 John finding 2: the hedge is forbidden; the catechism was not opened (the PDF returned HTTP 500 when I tried, so I cannot verify it either); and Wikipedia "Antilegomena" (opened) states that current Lutheran usage questions 2 and 3 John. Also "(as Wikipedia reports, citing Brown and Brooke)" for Origen's remark and Dionysius's "reputed Second or Third Epistle": the saved page does say this, but quoting early fathers through Wikipedia is weak when New Advent (Eusebius 6.25 for Origen; 7.25 for Dionysius) is available and was used for other quotations. Fix as in 2 John: open the catechism and quote it, or cut; replace "every other church"; cite the fathers directly.

**3. The traditions coverage the brief asked for is missing, and the prosperity section is sourced to Wikipedia alone (book.json lenses and chapters.json `views.T`, deep dive "3 John 2 and the prosperity gospel").**
brief-2jn.md asked the T lens for "Diotrephes and church order (Catholic and Orthodox episcopacy, congregational readings)" and the verse-2 treatment "Oral Roberts' use of the verse: verify". The T view gives only the Apostolic Constitutions list and Udall's 1588 dialogue; no church's reading of Diotrephes is given, and the `elders` contribution says only Harnack and "others". The prosperity section cites no church or movement document: its facts (Roberts's Bible falling open in 1947, "seed faith") come from Wikipedia articles via the Guardian obituary, and the closing claim "others read it as a prayer for one friend" names only Plummer. This is a place where Christians really do differ (Word of Faith/Pentecostal-charismatic use, Reformed and Catholic critique), which is exactly what Matt asked to be shown side by side with named sources. The writer decided "no traditions card" without searching church documents (sources-checked.md lists none).
Fix: either find and open two or three primary or church sources (a confession or catechism on church order; a prosperity teacher's own words and a critique by a named theologian) and add a traditions card or a sourced deep dive, or say plainly in the T view that this has not been covered. Do not leave the brief's items silently out.

**4. Same unsourced "churches differ" and missing counter-position as 2 John, as it bears on 3 John (chapters.json `topics`/`views`, topics.json `church-discipline`, `elders`).**
The `church-discipline` contribution says the verb "drives out" is "the same verb used when the man born blind was driven out of the synagogue (John 9:34)" (true and good), but it then sets "the power to shut the door" against 2 John 10 as though that were a documented church comparison; no church document is cited. Treat as part of finding 3.

### Minor

**5. Plain English Translation/note mismatch on the word play at 1:8 (plain.txt 1:8; notes.json 1:8; variants.json 1:8).** The note and the variant `why` say there is a play on words: the brothers "take" (*lambanontes*) nothing from pagans (1:7), so believers ought to "take them up" (*hypolambanein*). The Plain English Translation has "they take nothing" in 1:7 but "bound to support" in 1:8, so the play is not in the English. STYLE: keep word plays where English can carry them; "take them in" or "take up their cause" would. (Also "support" is the ESV/NIV-type rendering; the other reading, KJV "receive", is the Byzantine verb, and both are shown in the variant.)

**6. Plain English Translation adds "everyone" at 1:10 (plain.txt, notes.json 1:10, drift-audit).** The Greek is `ὑπομνήσω αὐτοῦ τὰ ἔργα ἃ ποιεῖ`, "I will bring his works to mind"; no object person is stated. The note repeats "remind everyone". Plummer has "bring up". Suggest "I will bring up the works he is doing" or "call attention to".

**7. Wikipedia-as-authority phrases and report-hedges (many places).** book.json authorship ("C. H. Dodd, as Wikipedia reports"; "Rudolf Bultmann, as Raymond Brown reports"), audience (Dodd "as Wikipedia summarizes"), section "3 John among the books" ("as Wikipedia reports"), "A. E. Brooke, as Wikipedia reports"; notes 1:1 Gaius (Dodd), 1:6, 1:9 (Brown "as Wikipedia quotes him"), 1:12 (Dodd); deep dive (Dodd, Mitchell); settings 1:9-10 (Painter). Replace with the scholar and work; keep the Wikipedia pointer in the source line; fix the Dodd statistics line (30 percent of significant words, 20 percent for 2 John) to say "Dodd counted", since the saved page does give it.

**8. Variants: wording and gaps (variants.json).** (a) 1:12: "Papyrus 74 (first hand, it seems), Alexandrinus (first hand, it seems)" is a hedge; the saved list marks these `vid` ("apparently reads"); write "the first hand appears to read (vid)" once and explain the abbreviation. (b) 1:4 `why` says it is the only place where NA28 and SBL differ: confirmed by the marks, but NA28's own text is inferred from the missing N mark (the word list is the only evidence); say "the word list shows". (c) Missing variants a KJV or Byzantine reader would notice: 1:3 `γάρ` omitted by Sinaiticus, 33, 81 and others (the Plain English Translation's "For I was very glad"); 1:7 `τοῦ ὀνόματος αὐτοῦ` ("his name", Ψ, 614, 630 and others; KJV "his name's sake"); 1:10 `ἐπιδεχομένους` ("those who welcome") in C and others against `βουλομένους` ("those who want to"), which changes the meaning of who Diotrephes blocks; 1:11 `δὲ`; word-order variants at 1:13-14. (d) 1:8 second entry omits minuscule 623 (the saved list has 614 623 630 1505 for `τῆς ἀληθείας`).

**9. "Heraclides, the bearer of this letter" and the health wish (notes.json 1:12, chapters.json `views.H`).** The note quotes P.Oxy. 292 as "Heraclides, the bearer of this letter, is my brother". I could not re-open the papyrus (papyri.info was blocked; the Source Library search returned only Grenfell and Hunt's Part IV headnote, which says 292 is a recommendation letter to the finance minister on behalf of "a brother named Heraclides"). The quotation wording is the writer's from an earlier read and I cannot confirm it. The H view says the papyrus health wish is "the same health wish as 3 John 2": the Greek formulas differ (`πρὸ δὲ πάντων ὑγιαίνειν σε εὔχομαι` against `περὶ πάντων εὔχομαί σε εὐοδοῦσθαι καὶ ὑγιαίνειν`), as the note itself shows; say "a similar".

**10. Reading-level and style slips in the Plain English Translation.** 1:4 "No joy of mine is greater than these: to hear of my own children walking in truth": "than these" followed by an infinitive is stiff for an 8th-grade reader and does not parse in English; "No joy of mine is greater than this: to hear ..." is the natural form, and the Greek `τούτων` plural is already discussed nowhere. 1:11 "does bad", "the bad" is deliberate (note says so) but "does evil/ the bad" reads oddly beside "evil words"; fine to keep, flagged for the lead.

**11. Drift toward NIV/ESV (plain.txt).** I compared from the Greek and my general knowledge of those versions, without writing them out. Several phrases coincide with ESV/NIV wording, nearly all because the Greek is short and literal: 1:6 "send them on their journey in a way worthy of God", 1:7 "for the sake of the Name ... from the Gentiles", 1:8 "support ... fellow workers for the truth", 1:10 "And not satisfied with that ... those who want to ... out of the church", 1:14 "Peace to you. The friends ... Greet ... by name". The drift audit logged all of these as changed. None is a verbatim match of a whole clause; 1:14's "Greet each of the friends by name" adds "each of" (from ESV's "each by name") to `κατ' ὄνομα`, and 1:8 "support" matches the ESV/NIV choice. Suggest "Greet the friends by name" and a different verb at 1:8 (see finding 5).

**12. Numbering note and setting claims (notes.json 1:14; chapters.json).** The numbering note is accurate. One imprecision: chapters.json says Demetrius's setting is "probably as Demetrius brings this letter" and the Gaius card "apparently not a member of Diotrephes' church" while Plummer (saved) says it "does not seem to fit" that Gaius's guests were the bearers of 1:9's letter; these are reasonable, but the first is presented in the audience card as a flat statement ("Demetrius, who probably carried the letter"). Keep "probably" in every place.

**13. Smaller points.** (a) The Gaius note's "a late tradition with no earlier support" repeats a Dodd cite on Wikipedia; fine, cite Dodd. (b) The note on 1:5 "Westcott, as Plummer reports him" is allowed by rule 2 (Plummer read) but add the page. (c) 3Jn `sources.json` is `{}`: add the keys 3 John uses that are not shared (`wiki-3jn-variants` is in 2Jn's file too, so 3 John is not self-contained).

Names, numbers, tenses, counts ("truth" six, "testify/testimony" five, "beloved" four, 221 Greek words in `w` / 219 in SBL; 3 John is the only NT book whose SBL Greek has no `Ἰησ-` or `Χριστ-`, confirmed by script over `texts/orig`); the 3 John 14/15 numbering and the note; Plummer on 1:2 ("model for all friendly wishes"), 1:5 (Vulgate, Wycliffe, Tyndale, Luther), 1:9 (lost letter, "obvious corruption"), 1:10 (*phlyareō*), Diotrephes as the most surprising part ("weigh most heavily"); Harnack 1897, Brown "not a particularly common name", Udall 1588 (all on the live Wikipedia "Diotrephes" page); the shared lines with 2 John 1, 4, 12-14; `--check` 0 warnings. Not re-opened by me: Mishnah Avot 1:5, the Apostolic Constitutions list, Oral Roberts details, the Papyrus Oxyrhynchus text beyond the Source Library headnote.
