# Hostile review: the Prayer of Manasseh (Man)

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-11-12. Checked: plain.txt against texts/orig/Man.json (Swete, Codex Alexandrinus) and Swete's notes (texts/src/Man.app.json); the First1KGreek copy of Swete's Odes (tlg0527.tlg028); notes, variants, glosses, chapters, book.json (canon block, traditions card), sources.json, the proposals in content/proposals/works/ (topics/prayer-of-manasseh.json, terms.json, works.json, topics.md) and the writer's .scratch/Man/sources-checked.md. Opened this session: the Clementine preface at vatican.va (nova-vulgata_appendix_praefatio-lectorem_lt), encyclopedia.com "Manasseh, Prayer of" (Metzger/Bowker), katapi.org.uk (Oesterley), web searches on the Geneva Bible and Nova Vulgata. `node tools/build.js --check Man`: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

A careful, honest piece of work. The translation is faithful to the Greek verse by verse, the writer's variant notes match Swete's footnotes, quotations of the Clementine preface, Article VI, Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:2, 1 Clement 57 and the Apostolic Constitutions check out, and the labeled theories are labeled. The problems are concentrated in four places: (a) a plural "chains" the Greek does not have, which then spreads through the notes, topics and chapter page; (b) a Geneva Bible claim that misstates what the printed book did; (c) the 1:10 "departure from Swete", which is described inconsistently ("once" vs "one of two places") and mixes two readings; (d) the 1:7 longer reading, built from English, with a Syriac witness missing, and a minority (Nestle) view on the prayer's Christian origin left unlabeled as minority. A set of smaller unsourced or overstated claims follows. No invented source found. Totals: 0 critical, 5 major, 14 minor.

## Major

1. **Major. plain.txt 1:10; notes.json 1:10 (history); book.json lead, chapters.json sum; topics.json (manasseh, prayer).** "Many iron chains bend me low." The Greek is singular: `κατακαμπτόμενος πολλῷ δεσμῷ σιδήρου` ("bent down by a great/heavy bond of iron"; the note itself says "much binding of iron"). `πολλῷ` agrees with singular `δεσμῷ`; it is not "many chains". Brenton's "many iron bonds" is not Swete's text. The plural is then used as evidence: the note "speaks of 'many' chains 'of iron'", the manasseh topic ("many iron chains"), and the contrast with the bronze fetters of 2 Chronicles 33:11. Fix: "A heavy iron bond bends me low" or "I am bent under heavy iron fetters" (plural fetters is fair English for one heavy binding, but drop "many"), and rewrite the note and topic sentences that lean on "many".

2. **Major. canon block (accepted_by, Reformed), traditions card (Reformed), chapters.json deep "Where the prayer is found", topics.json `canon`.** "The Geneva Bible (1560) was an exception, printing the prayer among the canonical books (Metzger)." Metzger's article (encyclopedia.com, opened) says that, but other sources I searched say the 1560 Geneva Bible set it right after 2 Chronicles under the label "Apocryphe", with a margin note that it is not in the Hebrew. So it was printed in the Old Testament sequence but marked apocryphal, not counted as canonical. Stating it as a church-by-church fact on one encyclopedia line, in four places and as an "exception" to Reformed practice, is the kind of claim STYLE.md rule 2 forbids resting on one summary. I could not open a facsimile; say so. Fix: either open the 1560 Geneva Bible (table of contents) and describe exactly, or reduce to "Metzger says the Geneva Bible printed it after 2 Chronicles" and drop "among the canonical books" and "exception". Remove from the Reformed card unless verified.

3. **Major. variants.json 1:10 (first entry); book.json "Length" fact and "About this translation" section; reliability section.** The departure from Swete is described three ways: book.json says it departs "once, at 1:10"; the variant's `why` says "one of the two places the Plain English Translation departs from Swete's text" (no second place exists; plain 1:1 to 1:15 otherwise follows Swete); the reliability block calls it "the hard line of 1:10, where the Plain English Translation follows the Zurich Psalter". Substance: Swete's note gives T `σιδηρῳ`, `pr μὴ` before `ἀνανεῦσαι`, `om με`, and `τὴν κεφαλήν` for `ὑπὲρ ἁμαρτιῶν`; so the Greek line is reconstructed as `εἰς τὸ μὴ ἀνανεῦσαι τὴν κεφαλήν μου`. The reconstruction is labeled as such, which is good, but: (a) the entry's `why` says Alexandrinus has "no 'head'" and glosses A as "throw back (the head)", supplying the object from T's reading; A's sense is open (LSJ gloss of `ἀνανεύω` not checked here, say so or check it); (b) a reader is not told that a departure from the base text to a single later manuscript (T, 600s) is the minority path for editors; Brenton agreeing does not make it independent (he may share the same textual tradition). Fix: make "once" vs "two places" consistent; state the second 1:10 variant (below) is not a departure; show A's reading and a literal English for it next to T's; open an edition apparatus (Rahlfs/Göttingen) or LSJ `ἀνανεύω` to say what editors print, or label the choice as the writer's judgment.

4. **Major. variants.json 1:7 (longer verse); book.json "About this translation".** The longer reading is the brief's main variant, and it is built from English only: the entry's `b` admits "The Greek of these lines was not available", and the "plain" wording is composed from Brenton, KJV Apocrypha and the Constitutions' ANF translation. Two defects. (i) Brenton and the KJV Apocrypha are translations, not witnesses; listing them in a "readings" row with the Apostolic Constitutions mixes tradition-level facts. (ii) A witness I found in an opened source is missing: Oesterley (katapi.org.uk) says the Syriac manuscripts, including the Didascalia's, contain the lines that "fell out of the Greek text" and that the Revised Version added them (earliest Syriac Didascalia manuscript 8th or 9th century); the Didascalia itself is the oldest witness to the prayer, so the longer text is not an oddity of the later English. Also Swete's note for 1:7 ("`ὅτι σὺ` ] `σὺ γάρ` T; om `καὶ` 2° T") shows T differs at v7 too and is not mentioned. Fix: recast the readings as (A, Swete) vs (Didascalia, Apostolic Constitutions, Syriac: longer; give the Greek only if the Funk/Lagarde text is opened, otherwise say the Greek was not opened), move Brenton/KJVA into the explanation, add T's v7 difference, and tell readers that Orthodox Greek printings (Apostoliki Diakonia Psalter, Asser's Psalter if opened) may carry the lines.

5. **Major (fairness: consensus labeled). book.json authorship "Christian origin or a Christian text?", audience views, chapters.json settings.views.** Eberhard Nestle's view (the Greek manuscripts' text derives from the Apostolic Constitutions or Didascalia) is the only view on this question given, and "On this view the prayer's first known readers were Christian clergy" is used as one of two audience readings. A source I searched (ISBE, via a search result; not opened in full) says "the common view is that it was extracted by the latter from the Septuagint", and Oesterley (opened) treats Greek composition as the majority position. The writer read only the Jewish Encyclopedia (1901 to 1906) on this. Fix: label Nestle's view as an older minority view, state the majority position (the Didascalia and Constitutions quote an existing Greek prayer), and cite a source opened for each.

## Minor

6. **Minor. book.json facts/lead/canon, topics.json `canon`: "Clementine Vulgate (1592)".** The preface I opened (vatican.va, "Praefatio ad lectorem (trium editionum Clementinarum)") gives no year. 1592 is from memory (not in sources-checked.md). Fix: source the date or drop it ("the Clementine Vulgate").

7. **Minor. book.json canon.rejected_why, "Trent and the Vulgate".** "The second reason was mistaken, since the prayer survives in Greek." In the preface the Greek/Hebrew point ("neque Hebraice, neque Graece quidem exstat") is the first of three reasons; the second is "not found in older manuscripts"; the third "part of no canonical book". Relabel ("The first reason").

8. **Minor. book.json lead, facts, lenses W, chapters.json sum, canon summary: "Reformed, Baptist and most other Protestant churches".** Only Westminster 1.3 (Reformed) is cited. No Baptist confession is cited anywhere. Fix: cite the 1689 Baptist Confession 1.3 after opening it, or drop "Baptist" from the sentence.

9. **Minor. chapters.json sum: "Catholic Bibles once printed it in an appendix".** "Once" is unsupported. The Clementine preface is reprinted in the Nova Vulgata appendix, and I could not confirm whether the current Nova Vulgata still prints the prayer; my search found nothing either way. Fix: say "the Clementine Vulgate printed it in an appendix" and check the Nova Vulgata before saying anything about today. Also unchecked: whether the Catholic liturgy uses it (Liturgia Horarum); a "Catholic" card that omits it should say it was not checked.

10. **Minor. notes.json 1:1 (word).** "the prayer opens with the same words as Judith's prayers." Judith's prayers (ch. 9) do not open with "Lord Almighty"; Jdt 8:13, 4:13 and 16:x use the phrase in narration or address in other places. Soften to "the site renders `παντοκράτωρ` the same way it does in Judith".

11. **Minor. notes.json 1:5.** "Two Greek words found almost nowhere else" (`ἄστεκτος`, `ἀνυπόστατος`) and "Such polished Greek is one of the reasons some scholars think the prayer was written in Greek (JE names Porter and Swete)". The JE (per the writer's log) names Porter and Swete as holding Greek composition, not their reasons; frequency of the two words was not checked in any corpus (and `ἀνυπόστατος` is not rare in later Greek). Fix: drop "almost nowhere else", cite what Porter or Swete say only if opened.

12. **Minor. book.json facts "Setting"; chapters.json settings.when.** "as the heading and 1:10 imply" and "as the heading and the chains of 1:10 suggest": the Greek heading is only "Prayer of Manasseh" (the T heading adds "son of Hezekiah"). Babylon and captivity come from Chronicles, not the heading. Fix: "as 1:10 and 2 Chronicles 33:11 suggest".

13. **Minor. notes.json 1:8 (second paragraph).** Transliteration `etheu`; the Greek `ἔθου` is *ethou*.

14. **Minor. book.json audience.to; chapters.json settings.readers.** "First, Jewish readers of Chronicles ... then Christians" is stated as the audience while the book's own authorship section lists Christian shaping and unknown date and place. Mark as "most likely" and set the debated alternative beside it (the views list does this, but the `to` line reads as settled).

15. **Minor. glosses.json 1:10 "heaped up things you loathe".** The gloss "filled the land with idols you hate" adds "the land" (not in the Greek) and fixes "things you loathe" as idols (`προσοχθίσματα` reading is argued in the note). Fix: "piled up things you hate, such as idols".

16. **Minor. variants.json 1:10 (second entry), reading 2.** T's reading row shows "I did not do your will or keep your commands. I set up abhorrent things", but Swete's note says T's words stand in place of "setting up abominations" (`στήσας ... προσοχθ.] μὴ ποιήσας ... T`), so T does not have both. The `b` text says this; the reading row (and the `p` line "The Zurich Psalter, Brenton and the KJV Apocrypha have a line ... that Alexandrinus lacks") lumps T with the English versions that have both. Split T from Brenton/KJVA.

17. **Minor. book.json canon (pajunen) provenance "A different Manasseh prayer at Qumran".** "It shows that more than one writer tried to give Manasseh's prayer words" is the writer's inference from Pajunen's title (opened only as a bibliography record), stated as fact; no 4Q381 entry exists in works.json. Fix: attribute ("Pajunen argues..."), only if the article's thesis was read; otherwise say only that 4Q381 contains a prayer related to Manasseh's, as listed in the record.

18. **Minor. Orthodox claims: book.json lead, facts, lens O; chapters.json views.O.** "Lenten service of Great Compline": DiPippo's article also says the first part is served on other days; the writer logged "days served" but the content says only Lent. Check DiPippo again and word it exactly. The canon block gives only "Eastern Orthodox churches"; Metzger (opened) lists Armenian, Ethiopic and Syriac manuscripts, and tier `orth` is "in Orthodox Bibles": nothing says whether Oriental Orthodox churches (Armenian, Ethiopian, Coptic, Syriac) read it; add a line "not checked" or sourced entries.

19. **Minor. Source discrepancy not disclosed.** Metzger's article (encyclopedia.com, opened) says the prayer is among the 14 odes of "Codex B (5th century)"; the content says Codex Alexandrinus (A) throughout, which agrees with Swete and the JE. Probably a typo in the source, but the writer should note that the Metzger text says B and was not used for this point.

20. **Minor. book.json reliability "about twenty small places".** The count of T-versus-A differences in Swete's Ode 8 notes was not shown; recount from texts/src/Man.app.json and give the number, or write "many small places".

21. **Minor. proposals/works/topics/prayer-of-manasseh.json; topics.md.** Title "The Prayer of Manasses" departs from works.json (`Prayer of Manasseh`, with "Manasses" as an aka). The proposals note records the reason (alias collision with the manasseh topic) and the merge step, so this is tracked; ensure the merge actually happens before publish and that the `manasseh` topic's `aka` is cleaned then. The `apostolic-constitutions` works entry has no date or place ("fourth century, Syria" would need an opened source); its note is accurate to ANF 7, book 2, section 22.

22. **Minor. topics.json `names-of-god`.** "a title found nowhere in the Bible: 'the God of those who repent'". A universal negative not checked against the 66 books or the other Greek books; reword to "a title not found in the books of the Hebrew Bible that I have checked" only if checked, otherwise drop.

## Checked and found sound

- Verse by verse against the Greek: 1:2 to 1:9 and 1:11 to 1:15 match; "Lord Almighty", "repent/repentance" play (1:7 to 1:13), `ἄνες` double sense, `κατώτατα`, `δύναμις` as "army" all defensible. 1:7 "change your mind about the harm" is a stated interpretive choice with the alternative given.

- Quotations of the Clementine preface, Article VI, Westminster 1.3, Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:2, 1 Clement 57:1, Apostolic Constitutions 2.22 match what was opened.
- No em-dashes in any file; reading level fits; terms agree with terms.json and the Judith rendering of `παντοκράτωρ`.
