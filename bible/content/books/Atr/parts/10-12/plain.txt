## The flood comes
11:1 (About three lines are lost.)
11:2 ... the reed mats.
11:3 For one day the storm ...
11:4 ... were yoked.
11:5 ... with his talons
11:6 he ripped the sky apart.
11:7 ... the land,
11:8 and like a clay pot he smashed its noise.
11:9 ... the flood came out.
11:10 Like a battle, the crushing blow swept over the people.
11:11 A brother could not see his brother;
11:12 no one could be told apart in the disaster.
11:13 The flood roared like a bull;
11:14 like an eagle that screams,
11:15 the winds shrieked.
11:16 The darkness was thick; the sun was gone.
11:17 The offspring were like flies.
## The gods are afraid
11:18 The gods feared the noise of the flood.
11:19 They took cover in the sky;
11:20 they sat down outside.
11:21 Anu feared the noise of the flood;
11:22 the heart of the gods was pounding.
11:23 Anu's understanding was shaken;
11:24 the gods, his sons, huddled together
11:25 in front of him.
## The mother of the gods weeps
11:26 Nintu, the great lady:
11:27 dread covered her lips.
11:28 The Anunnaki, the great gods,
11:29 sat thirsty and starving.
11:30 The goddess saw it and wept,
11:31 the midwife of the gods, the wise Mami:
11:32 "Let the day grow dark;
11:33 let it turn back and be black!
11:34 In the assembly of the gods, I,
11:35 how could I speak,
11:36 along with them, for total destruction?
11:37 Enlil pushed hard and made my mouth speak.
11:38 Like that Tiruru,
11:39 he twisted my words.
11:40 Against my own self and my own body,
11:41 I listened to what was said against their noise.
11:42 The blame is mine: like flies
11:43 the offspring have become.

11:46 I have gone up into the sky;
11:47 it is as if I were living
11:48 in a storehouse.
11:49 Where has Anu gone, the lord of understanding,
11:50 whose word the gods, his sons, obeyed,
