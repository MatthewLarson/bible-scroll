# Hostile review: Atrahasis (Atr), book-level files

Files reviewed: `content/books/Atr/book.json`, `topics.json`, `sources.json`. Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-30. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Atr` was run after all four parts were written and the verses renumbered: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

Method: every date, colophon detail, witness and quotation below was compared this session with SEAL's saved pages (`.cache/dl/seal/node1515, node1517, node39732, node1520`), the downloads in `.scratch/Atr-book/dl/` (Millard's Tyndale Bulletin PDF, Heidel 1949, Clay 1922, Hasel 1974, Heiser 2001), the site's own Plain, and pages opened by web fetch (vatican.va Humani generis, Josephus on Gutenberg, Justin on New Advent, defendinginerrancy.com, egwwritings.org, Andrews University's repository page for Hasel). Where I could not open a source I say so.

## Verdict

A careful, respectful and mostly well-sourced book page: the colophon numbers (416 + 439 + 390 = 1,245), month names, museum numbers, Clay's reading Azag-Aya and his 1966 BC date, Millard's quotations, the Hasel thesis, the Humani generis paraphrase, Justin's sentence and the Chicago Statement articles all check out against what I opened. Its weaknesses are of three kinds. (1) A few facts are wrong or overstated: the Namtar "shame Enlil" claim, a Heidel paraphrase, "like flies" and "smelled" presented as text when SEAL brackets them, and a reliability line that says the Bible never mentions a heavenly rebellion. (2) The traditions card is thin and mislabeled: the "Adventist" row is one scholar, the "Evangelical" row is a parachurch statement plus one scholar, and there is no Jewish, Orthodox, Lutheran or Reformed voice, although the brief asks for Jewish and Christian answers to the borrowing question. (3) Several "holders" are anonymous ("others", "some read", "critical scholars"). The `matches` verdicts are mostly well calibrated and fair; two need adjusting. No finding is critical: I found no invented source, witness or reading.

Count: 0 critical, 7 major, 15 minor.

## Major

### 1. Namtar: the offerings shame Namtar, not Enlil, and "bribed" is wrong (book.json `canon.matches`, Namtar row)
The row says: "Namtar is bribed into stopping by gifts meant to shame Enlil."
Evidence: SEAL's translation of Tablet I 376-384 (node1515): Enki tells Atrahasis to have the heralds say "Do not revere your gods! ... Seek the gate of Namtar! Bring a baked loaf ... Let a flour offering reach him, so that he shall feel obliged (lit.: ashamed) and pass his hand over the gift". The one made to feel "obliged (lit. ashamed)" is Namtar (the pronoun is "he", the god whose gate is sought). Enlil is not shamed. Namtar is also Enlil's own agent (I 361: "Let Namtar cut off quickly their noise"), which the row leaves out. Millard (opened) describes it as persuading the plague-god "to lift his hand".
Fix: "Namtar, Enlil's own plague-god, is won over by offerings that put him in the people's debt (the Akkadian word is literally 'ashamed') and lifts his hand." Drop "bribed" and "meant to shame Enlil". Also note that Enki's instruction ("Do not revere your gods") is a striking point for the S lens.

### 2. The traditions card on Genesis 1-11 and Mesopotamian stories is mislabeled and incomplete (book.json `traditions[0]`)
- **Adventist row.** It is a single scholar, not the church. Hasel's article is real and says what the row says: its own abstract says Genesis 1 "far from being dependent on" Babylonian cosmogonies "designedly polemicizes against them", and p. 89-90 says the "relieve the gods from laboring" picture "is contradicted in Gen. 1" (hasel.txt, opened). But the article is about Genesis 1's cosmology. Many non-Adventists hold the same polemic thesis, and the church itself has no statement on how Genesis relates to Mesopotamian stories that I found. Under the tradition name "Adventist" a reader will read it as the church's position. "Adventist" is also inferred from his employer (Andrews University); no source opened says he was Adventist.
  - adventist.org was blocked for the writer (429), but an openable official text exists: Fundamental Belief 6 (Creation) in the 28 Fundamental Beliefs at m.egwwritings.org/en/book/14102.20 (I opened it; it calls the Genesis creation account an authentic historical account and speaks of six literal days). It does not speak to Mesopotamia either, so the honest fix is to say so: "The church's Fundamental Belief 6 affirms Genesis as history; it does not address Mesopotamian parallels. The Adventist scholar Gerhard Hasel argued ...".
- **Evangelical row.** "Evangelical" is a family, and the statement cited, the Chicago Statement (ICBI, Summit II, 1982; Articles XIII and XXII verified at defendinginerrancy.com), is a parachurch statement that does not mention Mesopotamian texts. "Evangelical scholars who hold this explain the likenesses as shared memory" rests on one scholar (Millard); his evangelical identity is not sourced. The site's own Genesis page cites evangelical Walton's reading (Adam and Eve as archetypes) and Wenham, and the sources `walton-gen` and `wenham-gen` already exist; evangelicals who see Genesis as inspired reuse of the ancient Near Eastern setting are a distinct answer from Millard's, and they are missing.
- **Catholic row.** Humani generis 38-39 verified (vatican.va): Genesis 1-11 "pertain to history in a true sense"; "simple and metaphorical language adapted to the mentality of a people but little cultured"; if the writers "have taken anything from popular narrations (and this may be conceded)" inspiration guarded the choice; such narrations "must in no way be considered on a par with myths". The paraphrase is accurate. It is a real church position. But it is a 1950 encyclical alone: the Catechism, para. 390, says the account of the fall "uses figurative language" while affirming a primeval event (opened at vatican.va, first words only), and Divino afflante Spiritu and Dei Verbum on literary forms are standard Catholic background that I could not open this session. Add 390 at least.
- **Missing traditions.** Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist, Anglican all have distinct positions on Genesis 1-11 as history, but I could not find an openable statement that addresses the Mesopotamian question (the Westminster Confession page I tried returned 404). If no church text addresses the question, the card should be reframed as "Is Genesis 1-11 history, and how does that bear on the Mesopotamian stories?" (where Catholic, Evangelical/Baptist (Baptist Faith and Message), Reformed (Westminster), Lutheran (LCMS) and Orthodox have texts), or reduced to the rows that can be sourced and say so. It must not read as a complete survey.
- **No Jewish view** (see finding 5).

### 3. Reliability section: internal contradiction and an overstated "Agrees" (`canon.reliability.sections[3]`)
- "Goes beyond: it tells of a time before humans when gods worked, of a strike in heaven, and of a slain god, none of which the Bible mentions." The same book.json cites Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4 (angels who left their place), Genesis 6:1-4, Isaiah 14 and 1 Enoch's Watchers as the Bible-side and Jewish-side comparison for the Igigi (`matches` row 4; lens S). A rebellion in heaven is something the Bible does mention, and the brief itself points to Isaiah 14 and Revelation 12. Say: "a strike of the lesser gods; the Bible knows rebellion among heavenly beings (Jude 6; 2 Peter 2:4), but not a strike over labor".
- "Agrees: humans are made from the earth with something from above in them (Genesis 2:7)." Atrahasis puts a slain god's flesh and blood into the clay; Genesis 2:7 has God's breath. The We-ila row of the same file says "The Bible never makes humans out of a divine being's body". The same overstatement appears in `christian_view[0]` ("made from earth with something divine added"). Reword: "both use clay and both add something from the divine side, but the two things are very different".

### 4. "Like flies" and "smelled the scent" are SEAL restorations, presented as text (lead, `cited_in` Genesis 8:20-21, spec box 3, side-by-side section)
The lead quotes "like flies" as the poem's words, and `cited_in` calls the link "a strong and widely noted parallel". The Akkadian of 12:7-8 (III v 34''-35'') is `[iṣinu il]u erēša / [kīma zubb]i elu nīqi paḫru`; only `erēša` ("scent") and `elu nīqi paḫru` ("gathered over the offering") survive, and SEAL translates "[The gods sniffed] the scent, they grouped [like flies] over the offering" (node1517, with brackets). Only one copy (C1) has the passage (checked in `texts/src/Atr.score.json`). The restoration is plausible, because the "big flies" Anu hung up (12:19) survive two lines later, and Gilgamesh XI has the same picture, but the page never says it is a restoration. The lead is the most-read text in the book.
Also, the Ark Tablet's "two by two" (`cited_in` Genesis 6:19-20): SEAL's translation is "wild anima[ls from the s]teppe ..., two by two, [I] did [make enter] into the boat"; the verb "enter" is restored.
Fix: in the lead and the cited_in text say "(a restored reading; the Akkadian is broken here)" or "the gods gathered ... over the offering (the word 'flies' is restored by SEAL)".

### 5. Fairness: no Jewish voice, and an unsourced claim about what "Jews and Christians" think
- `rejected_why[2]`: "Jews and Christians read Genesis as the true account, and so read this one as a distorted telling of the same events." No Jewish source is cited anywhere in the book-level files. Rabbinic literature does not discuss Atrahasis; modern Jewish scholars (the cited Millard footnote itself lists U. Cassuto, *A Commentary on the Book of Genesis* II, 1964) have views on Genesis and the Mesopotamian flood stories. I could not open Cassuto, so I do not say what he holds; the writer should open him (or Sarna or Kaufmann) or remove the sentence. As written it is an unsourced generalization about two religions, and it is exactly where the brief wants "Jewish and Christian, traditional and critical".
- `christian_view[2]` ("every answer"): groups von Rad under "Genesis reworked Mesopotamian stories", but Millard (opened) quotes von Rad as saying the two versions are "independent arrangements of a still older tradition", which is a common-source view and is what Heidel's third option is. Regroup.
- Clay's western-origin view is listed as a live fourth answer with no word on its standing today. Clay's evidence was Amorite words such as *ḫubūru* (Clay, opened), and SEAL (and the site's Plain) treat *ḫubūru* as an ordinary Akkadian word for "uproar". Say who, if anyone, still holds it, or that the writer found no modern holder.

### 6. The Ark Tablet is mislabeled in `cited_in` (Genesis 6:19-20)
Text: "the Ark Tablet, another early copy of the flood speech". SEAL's page (node1520) is titled "Atraḫasis MB Flood (The Ark Tablet)", is filed under Middle Babylonian/Assyrian, gives no provenance ("Unknown"), no museum number (Douglas Simmonds Collection, edited by Finkel 2014 and Wasserman 2020a), and no date. Problems: (a) "early" contradicts SEAL's MB label, since the Sippar copy is Old Babylonian and older; (b) it is not "a copy of the flood speech": it holds Enki's address to the reed wall, then a first-person account by the hero of the boat's size, bitumen and loading; (c) the book's own "The copies" section correctly says "SEAL files the Ark Tablet ... with [the Middle Babylonian] copies", so the two passages disagree; (d) a search result summarizing a 2017 review in Zeitschrift für Assyriologie says Finkel dates the tablet to the early or middle Old Babylonian period, which would conflict with SEAL's MB heading; I could not open the review (503), so this is unverified. If the date is disputed that is itself worth a line.
Fix: "the Ark Tablet, whose date is uncertain (SEAL files it with the Middle Babylonian copies; its first editor Irving Finkel dates it earlier), has the hero say that wild animals went in 'two by two'". Do not say "early copy of the flood speech".

### 7. Two Heidel claims are not what Heidel says (provenance, `christian_view[1]`)
- "Ammi-saduqa was the next-to-last king of the First Dynasty of Babylon (Heidel)." I searched Heidel (opened) for this: he calls Samsuditana "the last king of the First Babylonian Dynasty" and gives Ammizaduga as "First Babylonian Dynasty", but does not say "next-to-last". It is historically a consequence of the succession but the citation is not supported by what was opened. Remove "(Heidel)" or drop the clause.
- "Heidel noted that the moral reason for the Flood is almost wholly absent from the Babylonian stories." Heidel's section "The Reason for the Flood" says that in Gilgamesh the reason "is not nearly so apparent as it is in the Book of Genesis", that Ea nevertheless says "the flood was sent because of the sin of man" ("On the sinner lay his sin", XI 180), and that in Atrahasis it was noise. "Almost wholly absent" is the writer's wording, not Heidel's. Also the `matches` Enlil row says "Heidel's judgment on Enlil's scheme was that there was little justice in it"; Heidel's words are "thoughtless and unjustifiable destruction", which is stronger and should be quoted that way if kept.

### 16. Igigi row is out of step with the notes (`matches` row 4)

### 22. Hasel citation: the volume number
The key `hasel-polemic` (Gen 01-11 sources.json) says *Evangelical Quarterly* 46 (1974). The PDF the writer used (hasel.txt, supplied by Theology on the Web) has a header "45.2 (April-June 1974): 81-102".
