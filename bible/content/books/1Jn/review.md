# Hostile review: 1 John (1Jn), flat folder, one part, plus the cores 1-john, light-and-darkness, johannine-comma

Reviewer: separate agent, 2026-10-29. `node tools/build.js --check 1Jn` (VERBOSE): 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Verdict

This is a careful, well-sourced book. I found no invented source, fragment or quotation. Every patristic and church-document quotation I could open matched: Irenaeus AH 1.26.1, Augustine Hom. 7.5 and 7.8, Clement Strom. 2.15 ("larger Epistle"), Tertullian Praxeas 15, CCC 604-606, Wesley (Sermon 40 II.6, II.19, II.20; Notes on 2:2, 2:20, 3:2, 3:9, 4:8, 5:16), Calvin 2:2 and 1:8, Wachtel/INTF's four NA28 changes, Papyrus 9 and the Comma witness table. The variant witness lists match the STEP VarApp data in the writer's logs. The Plain is not NIV/ESV drift except in the two forced uses of "atoning sacrifice" (finding 3). The weak points are these. One verse (2:19) is rendered in a way that can reverse its sense and has no note. Three of the traditions cards leave out churches that have a distinct view. A few statements about the opponents are stated as settled. A handful of small factual slips (counts, one variant explanation) and several second-hand or unlogged details need fixing. Nothing is critical.

Counts: critical 0, major 5, minor 17.

## Major findings

1. **MAJOR, plain.txt 2:19 (and notes.json 2:19).** "...it would be made visible that not all of them are from us." The Greek is `ἀλλ' ἵνα φανερωθῶσιν ὅτι οὐκ εἰσὶν πάντες ἐξ ἡμῶν`. The word order puts the negative before "all". Translators and handbooks take it as "none of them is from us" (the UBS handbook on 1 John says "none of them" fits the sentence better and both readings are possible; I opened a search summary of it, not the handbook itself). The Plain's "not all of them" can be heard as "some of them are from us", the reverse of the point of the verse ("they were never really from us"). The one note on this verse does not mention the issue. Fix: render "that none of them is from us", or keep the literal wording and add a `word` note (kind `deb`) giving both readings.

2. **MAJOR, traditions cards, three of them.** STYLE rule 3 and the brief ask for the churches that really have a view.
   - 2:2 (atonement): there is no Orthodox entry. The brief names Orthodox among the unlimited-atonement churches, and the writer's log admits the Orthodox page was "left out because not verified". The reader sees no Orthodox answer and no explanation. "Baptist" is represented by Particular Baptists only (1689), though General Baptists are Baptists too. The "Reformed" entry also carries the Remonstrants, who are a distinct (Arminian) tradition and the one the card's own Methodist entry sits beside. The Amyraldian middle, which the brief asked for, is absent. No Anglican entry either.
   - 3:6-9 (perfection): no Orthodox, Baptist or Anglican entry. The Catholic entry (Trent 6.11, canon 23) does not discuss 1 John 3:9 at all; it states the church's teaching on venial sin, so it answers a neighbouring question. Say so, or find the Catholic use of the verse.
   - 5:16-17 (mortal sin): only Catholic, Reformed and Methodist. Lutheran and Orthodox are missing, and the writer opened Augsburg, the Apology and Smalcald.
   Fix: add the missing churches from a document opened this session, or add one line on each card saying which traditions were not documented and why. Do not leave silent gaps.

3. **MAJOR, plain.txt 2:2 and 4:10 ("the atoning sacrifice for our sins"; "He is himself the atoning sacrifice ... not just for ours, but for the whole world as well").** The phrase "atoning sacrifice for our sins" and the clause shape "and not just for ours" follow the NIV's English closely at both verses. The term is fixed by brief-general.md, so this is not the writer's fault, but the drift-audit marks 2:2 "changed" on the strength of "no 'sins of'" and "just ... as well" only. The Greek `ἱλασμός` is not "sacrifice" (the note itself says expiation or propitiation). Fix: the lead should decide whether to keep the term (then note in the drift audit that it is a forced match) or move to a wording that is not NIV's, for example the gloss's own "the means by which sins are covered". The glosses.json entry is already there.

4. **MAJOR, johannine-comma core lead and the `textual-history` contribution.** Both say the added words entered "the printed Greek New Testament in 1522". The book's own variants.json 5:7 lists the Complutensian Polyglot (Greek and Latin) before Erasmus 1522 as a printed witness. The Complutensian Greek New Testament was printed in 1514 and only published in 1520-21 (Wikipedia "Complutensian Polyglot", opened). Wikipedia's Comma article gives the Polyglot two different dates, which the writer saw and did not resolve. Fix: say "Erasmus's Greek text of 1522" and name the Polyglot (printed earlier, issued 1520-21) in the deep dive and the cores, or drop the first-in-print claim. Also the chapter-5 deep dive never mentions the Polyglot at all, though it is the Catholic-side printing that fed Erasmus's critics; one sentence would do.

5. **MAJOR, book.json `audience.to`, chapters.json `readings` and `settings` (1:1-4, 1:5-2:2).** The opponents are described as settled: "claiming to know God and to be beyond sin, and denying that Jesus is the Messiah who came in the flesh". The "if we say" lines as "what those people claimed" are a view (the notes say "some read them", then the chapter reading says it flatly). Docetists, Cerinthians and a Jewish denial are three different views, and the audience card combines them into one description. The three views are also given with no named scholar (brief: "views, named scholars", Brown). Fix: put "some of whom, on one reading," in `audience.to`, and name at least the scholar behind each reconstruction from a source opened this session (Brown's is in the NET note on 2:20 only as a quotation, so check the commentary or a page quoting it). Also `audience.to` states "believers living among temples and idols (5:21)" as a fact; the 5:21 note itself lists three readings of "idols".

## Minor findings

6. **variants.json 2:13, `why`.** "The Byzantine text ... gives three and three in a different place." Wrong. In `texts/orig/1Jn.json` the Byzantine text has four "I am writing" (2:12 and three in 2:13, ending with children) and two "I have written" (fathers, young men): four and two. The critical text is three and three. Fix the sentence.

7. **notes.json 2:6, "That one".** Title/preview say `ekeinos` is used of Jesus "five times"; the body lists six verses (2:6; 3:3, 5, 7, 16; 4:17). The word list shows seven `ἐκεῖνος` forms in the letter, the seventh being 5:16 (`ἐκείνης`, the sin, not Jesus). Say six.

8. **notes.json 2:1, "Little children".** "1 John uses it seven times" counts 3:7, but the Plain and the note's own text follow NA28's `παιδία` at 3:7, so `τεκνία` occurs six times. Also the note cites `paidia` "2:14" (SBL numbering); the Plain's verse is 2:13.

9. **3:7 vs the stated base text.** book.json says the site follows SBLGNT, weighing NA28/ECM where they differ. SBLGNT has `Τεκνία` at 3:7; the Plain follows NA28 `παιδία` ("Children") with no variants.json entry. Same type of gap: 1:7 `δέ` (NA28/ECM split line, om./δέ; the Plain follows `δέ`), 5:6 "Christ is the truth" (61, Vulgate) against "the Spirit is the truth". Add short variants entries or a note for 3:7 at least; the writer's log says the 3:7 witnesses were "NOT FOUND", so say honestly that only the editions are known.

10. **plain.txt small departures from the Greek.** (a) 2:29 "has been born from him **as well**": there is no `καὶ`. (b) 2:29 "**Since** you know": `ἐὰν εἰδῆτε` is "if you know". (c) 2:26 "because of those who": `περὶ τῶν πλανώντων` is "about, concerning". (d) 5:18 "keeps himself **safe**": `τηρεῖ`, "keeps, guards"; "safe" adds. (e) 5:19 "lies in the evil one's **hands**": `ἐν τῷ πονηρῷ`; the note does give the literal, so this is acknowledged, but the anchor shows the addition. (f) 2:15 "has no place in him" for `οὐκ ἔστιν ... ἐν αὐτῷ`. (g) 1:1 supplies "this is what we speak of" for a sentence with no main verb; the note on 1:1 does not say the verb is supplied. (h) 5:1 "the one who gave the birth" is awkward English. None is serious; (a)-(c) should be fixed or noted.

11. **glosses.json decide among views the notes keep open.** 3:9 "seed" = "the life God plants in a person" (the note lists four readings and the brief wanted every view open); 3:4 "lives as if God's law did not exist" (the note offers two); 2:16 "the craving of the flesh" = "giving in to bodily appetites" (the 2:16 note says `sarx` is human nature, "not the body as evil"). Make the glosses neutral.

12. **notes.json 4:9.** "1 John 4:9 says the same thing in the same Greek words" as John 3:16. Not so: John 3:16 has `ἔδωκεν` (gave), 1 John 4:9 `ἀπέσταλκεν` (has sent). Same title `μονογενής`, different verb. Say "the same word for 'only'".

13. **variants.json small accuracy points.**
   - 5:18 `b`: "A few Latin witnesses (followed by the Vulgate) read 'the birth from God keeps him'." STEP lists the Vulgate among the witnesses of `ἡ γέννησις`, with Greek minuscules 1505, 1852, 2138 and the Bohairic; the Vulgate does not "follow" them.
   - 4:3 reading 2: "Polycarp's wording (Philippians 7) is close" is not a witness statement; remove it from the `w` list or put it in `b`.
   - 3:1: "Papyrus 74 (600s, apparently)": "apparently" is a hedge in content (STYLE rule 2). The source gives the date as 7th century; drop the word.
   - 1:7 and 4:3: the Vulgate witnesses (Stuttgart, Clementine, Wordsworth-White) are listed unevenly; at 1:7 the Wordsworth-White Vulgate (`vgww`) goes with "Jesus Christ", not with the first reading.

14. **sources.json `net-bible-1jn`.** It says the NET notes consulted include 1:7 and 2:7. The writer's own log (sources-checked.md) lists NET notes opened on 1:4, 2:20, 2:23, 3:1, 4:3, 5:1, 5:7, 5:18, 5:21 only. Either show the 1:7 and 2:7 notes were opened or remove them from the description.

15. **Second-hand or secondary items, honestly labelled but weak.** (a) `remonstrance-1610`: the Remonstrants' article is "as quoted in Wikipedia". (b) notes 5:8 puts "(Unity of the Church 6, as quoted on Wikipedia)" into the visible body; the deep dive quotes Zahn and Cyprian from the same page. The writer's log admits Cyprian was not opened. Open Cyprian on New Advent and cite him directly, or drop "Unity of the Church 6". (c) The date "probably between AD 95 and 110" is "Wikipedia's summary" in book.json `facts`. (d) De Jonge was read in a third-party transcription. These are permitted by rule 2 but they are the weakest links in the Erasmus section, which is the section the lead cares about most. De Jonge's article and the Cyprian text should be opened first-hand.

16. **Unsourced claims about what churches or groups say.** notes 4:6 "a common Protestant reading ... a common Catholic and Orthodox reading"; notes 2:27 "Groups that stress the Spirit's direct guidance have often quoted the verse"; chapters.json ch.2 J "the 'old command' is Leviticus 19:18" (stated flat; the note says the old command is "from the beginning" of their life as believers and *also* the Torah's, so the chapter-page line is stronger than the note); ch.2 J "The Advocate and the atoning sacrifice draw on the Day of Atonement" (a view, not a fact); ch.3 J "Purity before seeing God (3:3) recalls preparation for the feasts" (no source). Either source these or phrase them as readings.

17. **Wesley quotations I could not match exactly.** Notes 4:18 quotes Wesley "No slavish fear can be where love reigns." The page I opened (Bible Hub transcription of the Notes) reads "perfect, adult love casteth out slavish fear". Notes 3:6 quotes "sinneth not - While he so abideth"; my fetch summarised the note and I could not confirm the dash wording. I could not open the full text of either, so I cannot say they are wrong, but the writer's log shows only the page title, not the sentence. Check against the Notes and quote what is there.

18. **book.json `sections` "1 John and the Gospel of John".** "The letter speaks of Christ's 'coming' (parousia, 2:28) and of 'the last hour' (2:18), which the Gospel does not." The Gospel does speak of Jesus coming again (John 14:3; 21:22-23) and of "the last day"; only the *word* `παρουσία` and "last hour" are absent. Reword.

19. **book.json `spec`.** "Nearly every distinctive word of 1 John comes from the Last Supper discourse" overclaims: light and darkness, antichrist, anointing, `ἱλασμός`, "seed", "lawlessness" are not in John 13-17. And "That the letter is close to John 13-17 is widely noticed" has no source. It is labelled as Claude's theory, which is correct, but narrow the first sentence ("many of its key words") and either source or drop "widely".

20. **trinity and johannine-comma texts: "witnesses to the shorter text".** The deep dive, the `trinity` contribution and variants.json 5:7 say Athanasius, Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus "are witnesses to the shorter text". The STEP apparatus lists them there, but what that means is that they never quote the clause, an argument from silence. Say that. The same variant also lists Tertullian among the short-text witnesses while the deep dive leaves Cyprian's and Tertullian's trinitarian "three are one" language unsaid for the latter; mention Tertullian (Praxeas 25) with Cyprian or drop him from the short list.

21. **Gloss/preview wording.** 4:2 gloss "in the flesh" = "as a real human being" takes the anti-docetic reading; fine for the Plain's choice, but the note on 4:2 describes docetism and Cerinthus as the opponents' views, and the gloss should not settle the identity of the error. Minor.

22. **Fairness note on 5:20.** The note presents "this one" as Jesus (nearest antecedent) or the Father, and cites John 20:28, Titus 2:13, Romans 9:5, but gives no father, church or scholar on either side. Add one named source each way, or say none was opened.
