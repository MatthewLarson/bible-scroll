## Treaties with Rome and Sparta
12:1 Jonathan saw that the time was working in his favor. So he chose men and sent them to Rome to confirm and renew the friendship with the Romans.
12:2 He also sent letters to the Spartans and to other places, along the same lines.
12:3 The men went to Rome and entered the Senate house. They said, "Jonathan the high priest and the Jewish nation have sent us to renew their friendship and alliance with you, as it was before."
12:4 The Romans gave them letters to the people of each place, asking them to see the envoys safely on their way to the land of Judah.
12:5 This is a copy of the letter Jonathan wrote to the Spartans:
12:6 "Jonathan, high priest of the nation, the council of elders, the priests and the rest of the Jewish people, to the Spartans, their brothers: greetings.
12:7 Long ago a letter was sent to Onias the high priest from Areus, who was king among you, saying that you are our brothers, as the copy below shows.
12:8 Onias received the man who was sent with honor, and he accepted the letter, which spoke plainly of alliance and friendship.
12:9 So although we need none of this, since the holy books in our hands give us encouragement,
12:10 we have tried to send word to renew our brotherhood and friendship with you, so that we do not become strangers to you. For a long time has gone by since you sent to us.
12:11 We remember you at every opportunity, without fail, at our feasts and on the other fitting days, in the sacrifices we offer and in our prayers, as it is right and proper to remember brothers.
12:12 And we are glad of your glory.
12:13 But many troubles and many wars have surrounded us, and the kings around us have fought against us.
12:14 We did not want to trouble you or our other allies and friends in these wars.
12:15 For we have the help from Heaven to help us. We have been rescued from our enemies, and our enemies have been humbled.
12:16 So we have chosen Numenius son of Antiochus and Antipater son of Jason, and we have sent them to the Romans to renew our former friendship and alliance with them.
12:17 We have ordered them to go to you as well, to greet you and give you our letter about renewing our brotherhood.
12:18 And now please send us a reply about this."
12:19 This is a copy of the letter they sent to Onias:
12:20 "Areus king of the Spartans, to Onias the high priest: greetings.
12:21 It has turned up in a document about the Spartans and the Jews that they are brothers, and that they come from Abraham's family.
12:22 Now that we have learned this, please write to us about your welfare.
12:23 We for our part write back to you: your herds and your possessions are ours, and ours are yours. So we are ordering our envoys to report to you to this effect."
## Jonathan's campaigns
12:24 Jonathan heard that Demetrius's commanders had come back with a larger army than before to fight against him.
12:25 He set out from Jerusalem and met them in the region of Hamath, for he gave them no chance to set foot in his own country.
12:26 He sent spies into their camp. They came back and told him that the enemy were getting into position to attack them that night.
12:27 When the sun went down, Jonathan ordered his men to stay awake and keep their weapons at hand, ready for battle all night long. He posted guards out in front all around the camp.
12:28 The enemy heard that Jonathan and his men were ready for battle. They were afraid, and their hearts shrank, and they lit fires in their camp.
12:29 But Jonathan and his men did not know it until morning, for they could see the fires burning.
12:30 Jonathan chased after them but did not catch them, because they had crossed the Eleutherus River.
12:31 So Jonathan turned aside against the Arabs called Zabadeans. He struck them down and took their plunder.
12:32 Then he broke camp, came to Damascus and marched through the whole region.
12:33 Simon also went out and marched as far as Ashkelon and the strongholds near it. He turned aside to Joppa and seized it first,
12:34 for he had heard that its people meant to hand the stronghold over to Demetrius's men. He stationed a garrison there to guard it.
## Building up Jerusalem
12:35 Jonathan came back and called the elders of the people together. He made plans with them to build strongholds in Judea,
12:36 to make the walls of Jerusalem higher, and to raise a great rampart between the Citadel and the city. It would cut the Citadel off from the city and leave it standing alone, so that its men could neither buy nor sell.
12:37 They gathered to build. Part of the wall along the ravine on the east side had fallen, and he repaired the section called Chaphenatha.
12:38 Simon built Adida in the Shephelah and fortified it, with gates and bars.
## Jonathan is trapped
12:39 Tryphon wanted to become king of Asia, to put on the crown and to lay hands on King Antiochus.
12:40 He was afraid that Jonathan would not allow it and would fight against him. So he looked for a way to seize Jonathan and kill him. He set out and came to Beth-shan.
12:41 Jonathan went out to meet him with forty thousand men picked for battle, and he came to Beth-shan.
12:42 Tryphon saw that Jonathan had come with a large army, and he was afraid to lay hands on him.
12:43 So he welcomed him with honor and introduced him to all his Friends. He gave him gifts and ordered his Friends and his troops to obey him too.
12:44 He said to Jonathan, "Why have you worn out all these people, when there is no war between us?
12:45 Send them home now. Choose a few men for yourself to stay with you, and come with me to Ptolemais. I will hand it over to you, along with the other strongholds, the many troops and all the officials. Then I will turn around and go back. That is why I am here."
12:46 Jonathan trusted him and did as he said. He sent his troops away, and they went back to the land of Judah.
12:47 He kept three thousand men with him. He left about two thousand of them in Galilee, and one thousand went along with him.
12:48 But as soon as Jonathan entered Ptolemais, the people of Ptolemais shut the gates and seized him. They killed with the sword all who had come in with him.
12:49 Tryphon sent troops and cavalry into Galilee, to the Great Plain, to destroy all Jonathan's men.
12:50 But they learned that Jonathan had been seized and was dead, along with the men who were with him. So they encouraged one another and marched in close order, ready for battle.
12:51 When their pursuers saw that the men would fight for their lives, they turned back.
12:52 All of them reached the land of Judah safely. They mourned for Jonathan and the men with him, and they were very much afraid. Israel mourned with great mourning.
12:53 All the Gentiles around them tried to wipe them out. For they said, "They have no ruler and no one to help them. Now let us go to war against them and wipe out their memory from among men."
## Simon takes the lead
13:1 Simon heard that Tryphon had gathered a large army to come into the land of Judah and wipe it out.
13:2 He saw that the people were trembling with fear. So he went up to Jerusalem and gathered the people.
13:3 He encouraged them and said to them, "You know for yourselves all that I, my brothers and my father's family have done for the laws and the sanctuary, and you know the wars and the hard times.
13:4 Because of this all my brothers have died for Israel's sake, and I alone am left.
13:5 So now, I will never spare my own life in any time of trouble, for I am no better than my brothers.
13:6 Instead, I will take vengeance for my nation and the sanctuary, and for our wives and children, because all the Gentiles have gathered to wipe us out, because they hate us."
13:7 When the people heard these words, their spirit came alive again.
13:8 They answered with a loud voice, "You are our leader in place of Judas and your brother Jonathan.
13:9 Fight our war, and we will do everything you tell us."
13:10 So he gathered all the fighting men. He hurried to finish the walls of Jerusalem and fortified it all around.
13:11 He sent Jonathan son of Absalom to Joppa with a sizable force. Jonathan drove out the people who were in it and stayed there.
13:12 Tryphon set out from Ptolemais with a large army to enter the land of Judah, and he had Jonathan with him as a prisoner.
13:13 Simon camped at Adida, facing the plain.
13:14 Tryphon learned that Simon had risen up in place of his brother Jonathan and was about to meet him in battle. So he sent envoys to him, saying,
13:15 "We are holding your brother Jonathan because of money he owed the royal treasury for the offices he held.
13:16 Now send a hundred talents of silver and two of his sons as hostages, so that when he is released he will not rebel against us. Then we will release him."
13:17 Simon knew they were speaking to him with deceit. Still, he sent the money and the boys, so that he would not stir up great hatred among the people,
13:18 who would say, 'It was because I did not send him the money and the boys that Jonathan died.'
13:19 So he sent the boys and the hundred talents. But Tryphon broke his word and did not release Jonathan.
13:20 After this Tryphon came to invade the city and wipe it out. He went around by the road to Adora. But Simon and his army kept moving alongside him wherever he went.
13:21 The men in the Citadel kept sending envoys to him, urging him to come to them through the wilderness and send them food.
13:22 Tryphon got all his cavalry ready to go. But that night there was a very heavy snow, and he did not come because of the snow. He set out and went into Gilead.
13:23 When he came near Baskama, he killed Jonathan, and Jonathan was buried there.
13:24 Then Tryphon turned back and went off to his own country.
## The tomb at Modein
13:25 Simon sent and took the bones of his brother Jonathan and buried him in Modein, the town of his ancestors.
13:26 All Israel lamented for him with great mourning and mourned him for many days.
13:27 Simon built on top of the tomb of his father and his brothers and raised it high for all to see, faced with smoothed stone at the back and at the front.
13:28 He set up seven pyramids facing one another, for his father, his mother and his four brothers.
13:29 Around these he made works of skill, setting great columns about them, and on the columns he made suits of armor as an everlasting memorial. Beside the armor he had ships carved, to be seen by all who sail the sea.
13:30 This is the tomb he built in Modein. It is there to this day.
## The yoke is lifted
13:31 Tryphon dealt with deceit toward the young King Antiochus and killed him.
13:32 He became king in his place and put on the crown of Asia, and he brought great disaster on the land.
13:33 Simon built up the stronghold of Judea. He walled it around with high towers, great walls, towers, gates and bars, and he stored food in the strongholds.
13:34 Simon chose men and sent them to King Demetrius, asking him to grant the country a release, because all that Tryphon did was plunder.
13:35 King Demetrius sent him an answer along these lines. He replied and wrote him a letter like this:
13:36 "King Demetrius, to Simon the high priest and Friend of Kings, and to the elders and the Jewish nation: greetings.
13:37 We have received the gold crown and the palm branch you sent. We are ready to make a full peace with you and to write to our officials to grant you releases.
13:38 All that we have agreed with you stands, and the strongholds you have built are to be yours.
13:39 We pardon your errors and wrongs up to this day, and the crown tax you owed. And if any other tax was collected in Jerusalem, it is no longer to be collected.
13:40 If any of you are fit to be enrolled among our courtiers, let them be enrolled. Let there be peace between us."
13:41 In the year 170 the yoke of the Gentiles was lifted from Israel.
13:42 The people of Israel began to write in their documents and contracts, "In the first year of Simon, great high priest, general and leader of the Jews."
## Gaza and the Citadel fall
13:43 In those days Simon camped against Gaza and surrounded it with his camps. He built a siege tower, brought it up to the city, battered one of the towers and captured it.
13:44 The men in the siege tower leaped out into the city, and there was a great uproar in the city.
13:45 The people in the city, with their wives and children, went up on the wall with their clothes torn. They cried out with a loud voice, begging Simon to give them terms of peace.
13:46 They said, "Do not treat us as our evil deeds deserve, but as your mercy allows."
13:47 Simon came to terms with them and did not fight them. But he drove them out of the city and cleansed the houses where the idols were. Then he entered it, singing hymns and giving praise.
13:48 He threw out all uncleanness from it and settled in it men who keep the law. He made it stronger and built a house there for himself.
13:49 The men in the Citadel in Jerusalem were kept from going out into the country to buy and sell. They grew very hungry, and many of them died of starvation.
13:50 They cried out to Simon to give them terms of peace, and he did. He drove them out from there and cleansed the Citadel from its pollutions.
13:51 On the twenty-third day of the second month, in the year 171, he entered it with praise and palm branches, with lyres and cymbals and harps, with hymns and songs, because a great enemy had been crushed and cast out of Israel.
13:52 He decreed that this day should be celebrated every year. He also made the Temple hill next to the Citadel stronger, and he lived there with his men.
13:53 Simon saw that his son John had grown to be a man, and he made him commander of all the forces. John lived in Gazara.
## Simon's peace
14:1 In the year 172 King Demetrius gathered his forces and went into Media to get help, so that he could fight Tryphon.
14:2 Arsaces, king of Persia and Media, heard that Demetrius had entered his territory. He sent one of his commanders to take him alive.
14:3 The commander went and defeated Demetrius's army. He seized Demetrius, brought him to Arsaces, and put him under guard.
14:4 The land was quiet all Simon's days. / He sought what was good for his nation. / His rule and his glory pleased them / all his days.
14:5 With all his glory he took Joppa for a harbor / and made an entry to the islands of the sea.
14:6 He widened the borders of his nation / and took control of the land.
14:7 He gathered many captives / and became master of Gazara, Beth-zur and the Citadel. / He removed the uncleanness from it, / and there was no one to stand against him.
14:8 They farmed their land in peace. / The land gave its crops, / and the trees of the plains their fruit.
14:9 The elders sat in the town squares, / all talking together about good things. / The young men dressed in splendor / and in the clothes of war.
14:10 He supplied the towns with food / and equipped them with the means of defense, / until the name of his glory was spoken / to the end of the earth.
14:11 He made peace in the land, / and Israel rejoiced with great joy.
14:12 Each one sat under his vine and his fig tree, / and no one was there to frighten them.
14:13 No one was left in the land to fight them, / and their kings were crushed in those days.
14:14 He gave strength to all the humble among his people. / He searched out the law / and removed every lawless and wicked man.
14:15 He made the sanctuary glorious / and added many vessels to the sanctuary.
## Rome and Sparta renew the treaty
14:16 It was heard in Rome, and as far away as Sparta, that Jonathan had died, and they grieved deeply.
14:17 But when they heard that his brother Simon had become high priest in his place and was ruling the country and the towns in it,
14:18 they wrote to him on bronze tablets to renew with him the friendship and alliance they had made with his brothers Judas and Jonathan.
14:19 These were read aloud before the assembly in Jerusalem.
14:20 This is a copy of the letter the Spartans sent: "The rulers and the city of the Spartans, to Simon the high priest, and to the elders, the priests and the rest of the Jewish people, our brothers: greetings.
14:21 The envoys who were sent to our people told us of your glory and honor, and we were glad that they came.
14:22 We have recorded what they said in our people's decisions, as follows: 'Numenius son of Antiochus and Antipater son of Jason, envoys of the Jews, came to us to renew their friendship with us.
14:23 The people were pleased to welcome the men with honor and to place a copy of their words in the public records, so that the people of Sparta may have a record of it.'" They wrote a copy of this to Simon the high priest.
14:24 After this Simon sent Numenius to Rome with a large gold shield weighing a thousand minas, to confirm the alliance with them.
## The decree for Simon
14:25 When the people heard these things, they said, "How shall we thank Simon and his sons?
14:26 For he and his brothers and his father's house stood firm, and they fought off Israel's enemies from them." So they established freedom for Israel. It was engraved on bronze tablets, and they set it on a pillar on Mount Zion.
14:27 This is a copy of the writing: "On the eighteenth of Elul, in the year 172, that is, the third year of Simon the high priest,
14:28 at Saramel, in a great assembly of the priests and the people, the rulers of the nation and the elders of the country, this was made known to us:
14:29 'Wars have often broken out in the country. But Simon son of Mattathias, a descendant of Joarib, and his brothers put themselves in danger. They stood up to the enemies of their nation, so that their sanctuary and the law might stand, and they brought their nation great glory.
14:30 Jonathan gathered their nation together and became their high priest, and then he was gathered to his people.
14:31 Their enemies meant to invade their country and wipe it out, and to lay hands on their sanctuary.
14:32 Then Simon rose up and fought for his nation. He spent much of his own money. He armed the soldiers of his nation and paid them wages.
14:33 He fortified the towns of Judea, and Beth-zur on the border of Judea, where the enemy's weapons had been kept before. He placed a garrison of Jewish men there.
14:34 He fortified Joppa, which is on the sea, and Gazara on the border of Azotus, where the enemy had lived before. He settled Jews there and supplied them with all they needed to restore those towns.
14:35 When the people saw how loyal Simon was, and the glory he meant to win for his nation, they made him their leader and high priest. They did this because he had done all these things, and because of the justice and loyalty he had kept toward his nation, and because he sought in every way to lift up his people.
14:36 In his days things went well under his hand, so that the Gentiles were driven out of their country. So were the men in the City of David in Jerusalem, who had made themselves a citadel. From it they used to go out and defile the area around the sanctuary, and they struck a great blow at its holiness.
14:37 He settled Jewish men in it and fortified it for the safety of the country and the city, and he raised the walls of Jerusalem higher.
14:38 In keeping with these things, King Demetrius confirmed him in the high priesthood.
14:39 He made him one of his Friends and honored him with great honor.
14:40 For he had heard that the Romans were calling the Jews friends, allies and brothers, and that they had met Simon's envoys with honor,
14:41 and that the Jews and the priests were pleased to have Simon as their leader and high priest forever, until a faithful prophet arises,
14:42 and that he should be their general, and that he should take charge of the sanctuary, and that by his authority men should be put in charge of their works, the country, the weapons and the strongholds,
14:43 and that he should take charge of the sanctuary, and that everyone should obey him, and that all contracts in the country should be written in his name, and that he should be clothed in purple and wear gold.
14:44 None of the people or the priests may set aside any of these things, or speak against what he says, or call a gathering in the country without him, or be clothed in purple or wear a gold clasp.
14:45 Whoever acts against these things or sets aside any of them will be liable to punishment.'"
14:46 All the people agreed to grant Simon the right to act according to these words.
14:47 Simon accepted. He consented to serve as high priest, to be general and ethnarch of the Jews and the priests, and to preside over them all.
14:48 They ordered that this writing be put on bronze tablets and set up in the enclosure of the sanctuary, in a secure place,
14:49 and that copies of it be placed in the treasury, so that Simon and his sons might have them.
## Antiochus VII writes to Simon
15:1 Antiochus son of King Demetrius sent a letter from the islands of the sea to Simon, priest and ethnarch of the Jews, and to the whole nation.
15:2 It read as follows: "King Antiochus, to Simon the high priest and ethnarch, and to the Jewish nation: greetings.
15:3 Some troublemakers have seized the kingdom of our fathers, and I intend to claim the kingdom back, so that I may restore it as it was before. I have hired a great number of soldiers and built warships.
15:4 I intend to land in the country, so that I may punish those who have ruined our country and laid waste many towns in the kingdom.
15:5 Now therefore I confirm for you all the exemptions that the kings before me granted you, and any other exemptions they granted you.
15:6 I permit you to strike your own coins as money for your country.
15:7 Jerusalem and the sanctuary are to be free. All the weapons you have made, and the strongholds you have built and now hold, are to remain yours.
15:8 Every debt you owe the king, and every royal due that may arise, is canceled for you from now on and for all time.
15:9 When we have established our kingdom, we will honor you and your nation and the Temple with great honor, so that your glory will be seen in all the earth."
15:10 In the year 174 Antiochus set out into the land of his ancestors. All the troops came over to him, so that few were left with Tryphon.
15:11 King Antiochus pursued him, and Tryphon fled and came to Dor, which is by the sea.
15:12 For he knew that troubles had piled up against him, and his troops had deserted him.
15:13 Antiochus camped against Dor, and with him were 120,000 warriors and 8,000 cavalry.
15:14 He surrounded the town, and the ships joined in from the sea. He pressed the town hard by land and by sea and let no one go out or come in.
## Rome's letter to the kings
15:15 Numenius and his companions came from Rome with letters to the kings and countries, in which this was written:
15:16 "Lucius, consul of the Romans, to King Ptolemy: greetings.
15:17 The envoys of the Jews, our friends and allies, have come to us to renew their old friendship and alliance. They were sent by Simon the high priest and by the Jewish people.
15:18 They brought a gold shield worth five thousand minas.
15:19 So we have decided to write to the kings and countries that they must not seek to harm them, or make war on them or their towns or their lands, or ally themselves with those who make war on them.
15:20 We have also decided to accept the shield from them.
15:21 So if any troublemakers have fled from their country to you, hand them over to Simon the high priest, so that he may punish them according to their law."
15:22 He wrote the same things to King Demetrius, to Attalus, to Ariarathes and to Arsaces,
15:23 and to all the countries: to Sampsames, to the Spartans, and to Delos, Myndos, Sicyon, Caria, Samos, Pamphylia, Lycia, Halicarnassus, Cos, Side, Arvad, Rhodes, Phaselis, Gortyna, Cnidus, Cyprus and Cyrene.
15:24 He also wrote a copy of them to Simon the high priest.
## Antiochus turns against Simon
15:25 King Antiochus camped against Dor on the second day. He kept attacking it and building siege engines, and he shut Tryphon in, so that he could not go out or come in.
15:26 Simon sent him two thousand picked men to fight on his side, along with silver and gold and plenty of equipment.
15:27 But Antiochus refused to accept them. He broke all the agreements he had made with Simon before and turned against him.
15:28 He sent Athenobius, one of his Friends, to negotiate with him. He said, "You are holding Joppa and Gazara and the Citadel in Jerusalem, which are towns of my kingdom.
15:29 You have laid waste their territory, done great harm in the land, and taken control of many places in my kingdom.
15:30 So now hand over the towns you have taken, and the taxes of the places you have seized outside the borders of Judea.
15:31 If not, pay five hundred talents of silver in their place, and for the damage you have done and the taxes of the towns, another five hundred talents. If not, they will come and make war on you."
15:32 Athenobius the Friend of the King came to Jerusalem. He saw Simon's splendor, his sideboard with gold and silver dishes, and his many attendants, and he was amazed. He gave them the king's message.
15:33 Simon answered him, "We have not taken anyone else's land or seized what belongs to others. We hold the inheritance of our ancestors, which our enemies wrongfully seized for a time.
15:34 Now that we have the opportunity, we are holding on to our inheritance, our ancestors' inheritance.
15:35 As for Joppa and Gazara, which you demand, they were doing great harm among the people and to our land. For them we will give a hundred talents." Athenobius did not answer him a word.
15:36 He went back to the king in a rage and reported these words to him, along with Simon's splendor and all he had seen. The king became furious.
15:37 Tryphon boarded a ship and fled to Orthosia.
15:38 The king made Cendebeus governor of the coast and gave him infantry and cavalry.
15:39 He ordered him to camp facing Judea, and he ordered him to build up Kedron and fortify the towns, and to make war on the people. The king himself went on pursuing Tryphon.
15:40 Cendebeus came to Jamnia and began to provoke the people, to invade Judea, and to take the people captive and kill them.
15:41 He built up Kedron and stationed cavalry and troops there, so that they could ride out and raid the roads of Judea, as the king had ordered him.
## John defeats Cendebeus
16:1 John went up from Gazara and told his father Simon what Cendebeus was doing.
16:2 Simon called his two oldest sons, Judas and John, and said to them, "I and my brothers and my father's house have fought Israel's wars from our youth to this day, and things went well in our hands, so that we rescued Israel many times.
16:3 But now I have grown old, and you, through mercy, are old enough. Take my place and my brother's. Go out and fight for our nation, and may the help from Heaven be with you."
16:4 He chose from the country twenty thousand warriors and cavalry. They marched against Cendebeus and spent the night at Modein.
16:5 They got up early in the morning and marched into the plain. There a large army of infantry and cavalry was coming to meet them, and a stream lay between them.
16:6 He and his people camped facing them. He saw that the people were afraid to cross the stream, so he crossed first. When the men saw him, they crossed after him.
16:7 He divided the people and put the cavalry in the middle of the infantry, for the enemy's cavalry was very large.
16:8 They blew the trumpets, and Cendebeus and his army were routed. Many of them fell wounded, and those who were left fled to the stronghold.
16:9 It was then that Judas, John's brother, was wounded. But John pursued them until he came to Kedron, which Cendebeus had built.
16:10 They fled to the towers in the fields of Azotus, and he set it on fire. About a thousand of them fell, and he came back to Judea in peace.
## Simon is murdered
16:11 Ptolemy son of Abubus had been appointed governor over the plain of Jericho. He had much silver and gold,
16:12 for he was the high priest's son-in-law.
16:13 His heart grew proud, and he wanted to take control of the country. He made a treacherous plan against Simon and his sons, to do away with them.
16:14 Simon was touring the towns in the country and seeing to their needs. He went down to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas, in the year 177, in the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat.
16:15 The son of Abubus received them with deceit into the little fortress called Dok, which he had built. He gave a great drinking party for them, and he hid men there.
16:16 When Simon and his sons were drunk, Ptolemy and his men got up, took their weapons, rushed in on Simon in the banquet hall, and killed him and his sons and some of his servants.
16:17 He committed a great act of impiety and paid back evil for good.
16:18 Ptolemy wrote these things down and sent them to the king, asking him to send troops to help him, so that he could hand over their country and the towns to him.
16:19 He sent others to Gazara to do away with John. He sent letters to the commanders of thousands to come to him, so that he could give them silver and gold and gifts.
16:20 He sent others to seize Jerusalem and the Temple hill.
16:21 But someone ran ahead and told John in Gazara that his father and his brothers had been killed, and he said, "He has sent men to kill you too."
16:22 When John heard it, he was utterly shocked. He seized the men who had come to kill him and put them to death, for he knew they were trying to kill him.
## The rest of John's story
16:23 The rest of John's story, his wars and the brave deeds he did, the walls he built and all he did,
16:24 look, they are written in the book of the days of his high priesthood, from the time he became high priest after his father.
