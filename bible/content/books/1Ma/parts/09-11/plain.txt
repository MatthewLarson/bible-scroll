## Bacchides returns
9:1 When Demetrius heard that Nicanor and his army had fallen in battle, he sent Bacchides and Alcimus into the land of Judah once more, and the right wing of his army with them.
9:2 They took the road to Gilgal and camped against Mesaloth in Arbela. They captured it and killed many people.
9:3 In the first month of the year 152 they camped against Jerusalem.
9:4 Then they moved on and went to Berea with twenty thousand men and two thousand horsemen.
9:5 Judas was camped at Elasa, and three thousand picked men were with him.
9:6 They saw how many soldiers the enemy had, and they were badly frightened. Many slipped out of the camp, until no more than eight hundred men were left.
9:7 Judas saw that his army had melted away while the battle was pressing on him. His heart was crushed, for he had no time to gather them again.
9:8 He lost heart, but he said to those who were left, "Let us get up and go against our enemies. Perhaps we can fight them."
9:9 They tried to turn him back. "We cannot," they said. "For now let us save our own lives. Later we will come back with our brothers and fight them. We are few."
9:10 But Judas said, "May I never do such a thing as run away from them! Our time has drawn near. Let us die with courage for our brothers' sake, and leave nothing to stain our glory."
## The death of Judas
9:11 The army marched out of its camp and took its stand to meet them. The cavalry was split into two groups. The slingers and archers went ahead of the army, and so did all the front-line fighters, the strongest men.
9:12 Bacchides was on the right wing. The phalanx closed in from both sides, and they blew their trumpets. Judas's men blew their trumpets too.
9:13 The ground shook with the noise of the armies, and the battle stayed locked from morning until evening.
9:14 Judas saw that Bacchides and the strongest part of his army were on the right. All the brave-hearted men joined him.
9:15 They crushed the right wing, and he chased them as far as Mount Azotus.
9:16 Those on the left wing saw that the right wing was crushed, and they turned and came after Judas and his men, close on their heels, from behind.
9:17 The fighting grew heavy, and many fell wounded on both sides.
9:18 Judas fell, and the rest ran away.
9:19 Jonathan and Simon carried away their brother Judas and buried him in his ancestors' tomb at Modein.
9:20 They wept for him there. All Israel beat their breasts for him in a great lament. They mourned many days and said,
9:21 "How the warrior has fallen, / the one who saved Israel!"
9:22 The rest of Judas's story, his wars, his brave deeds and his greatness, was never written down, for there was a great deal of it.
## Jonathan takes command
9:23 After Judas died, the lawless poked their heads up all through Israel's territory, and all who did wrong sprang up.
9:24 In those days a very severe famine came, and the countryside went over to their side.
9:25 Bacchides chose the ungodly men and made them masters of the country.
9:26 They hunted down and searched out Judas's friends and brought them to Bacchides, and he took revenge on them and made sport of them.
9:27 So Israel was in great distress, worse than any since the day a prophet was last seen among them.
9:28 All Judas's friends gathered and said to Jonathan,
9:29 "Since your brother Judas died, there is no man like him to go out against our enemies, against Bacchides and against those of our own nation who hate us.
9:30 So today we have chosen you to take his place as our ruler and leader, to fight our war."
9:31 At that time Jonathan accepted the leadership and took the place of his brother Judas.
## In the wilderness of Tekoa
9:32 Bacchides learned of it and tried to kill him.
9:33 When Jonathan, his brother Simon and all who were with him learned of this, they fled into the wilderness of Tekoa and camped by the water of the cistern of Asphar.
9:34 Bacchides learned of it on the Sabbath day, and he came with all his army across the Jordan.
9:35 Jonathan sent his brother at the head of the crowd to ask his friends the Nabataeans to keep their baggage for them, of which they had a great deal.
9:36 But the sons of Jambri came out of Medeba, seized John and everything he had, and went off with it.
9:37 After these things, word came to Jonathan and his brother Simon: "The sons of Jambri are holding a great wedding. They are bringing the bride from Nadabath with a large escort. She is the daughter of one of the great nobles of Canaan."
9:38 They remembered their brother John. They went up and hid under the cover of the mountain.
9:39 They looked up and saw a noisy crowd with much baggage. The bridegroom came out with his friends and his brothers to meet them, with drums and musicians and many weapons.
9:40 They rose against them from their ambush and killed them. Many fell wounded, and the rest fled to the mountain, and they took all their goods.
9:41 The wedding became a time of mourning, and the sound of their musicians became a lament.
9:42 They took full vengeance for their brother's blood and went back to the marsh of the Jordan.
## The battle at the Jordan
9:43 Bacchides heard of it and came on the Sabbath day to the banks of the Jordan with a large force.
9:44 Jonathan said to his brothers, "Let us get up now and fight for our lives. Today is not like the days before.
9:45 Look, the battle is in front of us and behind us. The water of the Jordan is on this side and that, with marsh and thicket, and there is no place to turn aside.
9:46 So now cry out to Heaven, so that you may be rescued from the hands of our enemies."
9:47 The battle was joined. Jonathan reached out his hand to strike Bacchides, but Bacchides turned away from him and drew back.
9:48 Then Jonathan and his men leaped into the Jordan and swam across to the other side, and the enemy did not cross the Jordan after them.
9:49 About a thousand of Bacchides' men fell that day.
## Bacchides fortifies Judea
9:50 Then they returned to Jerusalem. They built strong towns in Judea: the fortress at Jericho, and Emmaus, Beth-horon, Bethel, Timnath, Pharathon and Tephon, with high walls and gates and bars.
9:51 They put garrisons in them to make war on Israel.
9:52 They fortified the city, and Beth-zur, Gazara and the Citadel, and put troops and stores of food in them.
9:53 They took the sons of the leading men of the country as hostages and put them under guard in the Citadel in Jerusalem.
## The death of Alcimus
9:54 In the year 153, in the second month, Alcimus gave orders to tear down the wall of the inner court of the sanctuary. He tore down the works of the prophets. He had begun to tear it down,
9:55 but at that time Alcimus was struck, and his work was stopped. His mouth was shut and he was paralyzed. He could no longer say a word or give orders about his household.
9:56 Alcimus died at that time in great agony.
9:57 When Bacchides saw that Alcimus was dead, he went back to the king, and the land of Judah was quiet for two years.
## Bacchides' last campaign
9:58 Then all the lawless made a plan. "Look," they said, "Jonathan and his men are living in peace, without a care. So now let us bring Bacchides, and he will capture them all in a single night."
9:59 They went and laid the plan before him.
9:60 He set out to come with a large army and secretly sent letters to all his allies in Judea, telling them to seize Jonathan and his men. But they could not, because their plot became known.
9:61 They seized about fifty of the men of the country, the ringleaders in this wickedness, and he put them to death.
9:62 Then Jonathan, Simon and their men withdrew to Bethbasi in the wilderness. Jonathan rebuilt the parts of it that had been torn down and made it strong.
9:63 When Bacchides learned of it, he gathered his whole army and sent word to the men from Judea.
9:64 He came and camped against Bethbasi. He fought against it for many days and built siege engines.
9:65 But Jonathan left his brother Simon in the town and went out into the countryside, and he came with a band of men.
9:66 He struck down Odomera and his brothers and the sons of Phasiron in their tents. He began to attack and to advance with his forces.
9:67 Simon and his men came out of the town and set fire to the siege engines.
9:68 They fought against Bacchides and crushed him. They pressed him hard, because his plan and his attack had come to nothing.
9:69 They were furious with the lawless men who had advised him to come into the country, and they killed many of them. Then he decided to go back to his own land.
9:70 When Jonathan learned this, he sent envoys to him to make peace with him and to have the captives given back to them.
9:71 Bacchides agreed and did as Jonathan asked. He swore to him that he would not try to harm him all the days of his life.
9:72 He gave back to him the captives he had taken earlier from the land of Judah. Then he turned and went away to his own land, and he never came into their territory again.
9:73 So the sword came to rest in Israel. Jonathan settled at Michmash. He began to judge the people, and he wiped out the ungodly from Israel.
## Alexander Balas claims the throne
10:1 In the year 160, Alexander Epiphanes, son of Antiochus, came up and seized Ptolemais. They welcomed him, and he became king there.
10:2 King Demetrius heard of it. He gathered a very large army and went out to meet him in battle.
10:3 Demetrius sent Jonathan a letter in friendly words, to raise him to high honor.
10:4 For he said, "Let us be first to make peace with him, before he makes peace with Alexander against us.
10:5 He will remember all the harm we did to him and to his brothers and to his nation."
10:6 He gave him authority to raise troops and make weapons and to be his ally, and he ordered that the hostages in the Citadel be handed over to him.
10:7 Jonathan came to Jerusalem and read the letter aloud to all the people and to the men of the Citadel.
10:8 They were badly frightened when they heard that the king had given them authority to raise troops.
10:9 The men of the Citadel handed the hostages over to Jonathan, and they gave them back to their parents.
10:10 Jonathan made his home in Jerusalem and began to build and restore the city.
10:11 He told those doing the work to build the walls, and to circle Mount Zion with squared stones to fortify it, and they did so.
10:12 The foreigners in the fortresses Bacchides had built ran away.
10:13 Each of them left his post and went off to his own country.
10:14 Only in Beth-zur did some of those who had abandoned the law and the commandments stay behind, for it was their place of refuge.
## The bidding war: Alexander's letter
10:15 King Alexander heard of all the promises Demetrius had sent to Jonathan. People told him about the wars and brave deeds that Jonathan and his brothers had done, and the hardships they had borne.
10:16 He said, "Will we ever find another man like this? Let us make him our friend and ally now."
10:17 So he wrote a letter and sent it to him in these words:
10:18 "King Alexander to his brother Jonathan, greetings.
10:19 We have heard about you, that you are a strong and capable man, fit to be our friend.
10:20 So today we appoint you high priest of your nation. You are to be called Friend of the King, to take our side and to keep up friendship with us." Along with the letter they sent him a purple robe and a gold crown.
10:21 Jonathan put on the holy robe in the seventh month of the year 160, at the Feast of Booths. He gathered troops and made many weapons.
## Demetrius outbids him
10:22 Demetrius heard of these things and was upset. He said,
10:23 "What have we done? Alexander has beaten us to making friends with the Jews to strengthen himself.
10:24 I too will write them words of encouragement, and offer honors and gifts, so that they will be on my side to help me."
10:25 He sent them a message in these words: "King Demetrius to the nation of the Jews, greetings.
10:26 You have kept your agreements with us and remained in our friendship, and you have not gone over to our enemies. We have heard this and are glad.
10:27 Now keep on being faithful to us, and we will repay you with good things for what you do for us.
10:28 We will grant you many exemptions and give you gifts.
10:29 Now I set you free, and I release all the Jews, from the tribute, from the salt tax and from the crown taxes.
10:30 As for the third of the grain and the half of the fruit of the trees, which is mine by right, I release them from this day on. I will not take them from the land of Judah, or from the three districts added to it from Samaria and Galilee, from this day on, for all time.
10:31 Let Jerusalem and its territory be holy and free, and its tithes and tolls as well.
10:32 I also give up my authority over the Citadel in Jerusalem, and I hand it to the high priest, so that he may station in it men of his own choosing to guard it.
10:33 Every Jew taken captive from the land of Judah into any part of my kingdom I set free without payment. And let everyone cancel their taxes, even those on their livestock.
10:34 All the feasts and Sabbaths and new moons and appointed days, and the three days before a feast and the three days after a feast, are all to be days of exemption and release for all the Jews in my kingdom.
10:35 No one will have authority to collect from any of them or to trouble them about any matter.
10:36 Let Jews be enrolled in the king's forces, up to thirty thousand men, and they will be paid as all the king's forces are.
10:37 Some of them will be stationed in the king's great fortresses, and some of them will be put in positions of trust in the kingdom. Their officers and commanders are to be from among them, and they are to live by their own laws, just as the king has commanded in the land of Judah.
10:38 As for the three districts added to Judea from the country of Samaria, let them be joined to Judea, so that they are counted as under one ruler and obey no other authority but the high priest.
10:39 Ptolemais and the land belonging to it I have given as a gift to the sanctuary in Jerusalem, for the expenses the sanctuary needs.
10:40 I also give every year fifteen thousand shekels of silver from the king's revenues, from the places that belong to me.
10:41 And all the surplus that the officials have not paid in, as they did in the first years, they shall from now on give for the work on the house.
10:42 Besides this, the five thousand shekels of silver that were taken from the sanctuary's income every year, as in the first years, are also released, because they belong to the priests who serve there.
10:43 Whoever flees to the Temple in Jerusalem or anywhere within its bounds, because he owes the king money or for any other matter, is to be released, along with all he owns in my kingdom.
10:44 For building and restoring the structures of the sanctuary, the cost will be paid from the king's revenues.
10:45 For building the walls of Jerusalem and fortifying it all around, the cost will also be paid from the king's revenues, and for building the walls in Judea."
## Jonathan sides with Alexander
10:46 When Jonathan and the people heard these words, they did not trust them or accept them, because they remembered the great evil he had done in Israel and how harshly he had oppressed them.
10:47 They were pleased with Alexander, because he had been the first to speak words of peace to them, and they stayed his allies always.
10:48 King Alexander gathered large forces and camped opposite Demetrius.
10:49 The two kings met in battle. Demetrius's army fled, and Alexander chased him and overpowered them.
10:50 He pressed the battle hard until the sun went down, and Demetrius fell that day.
## Alexander marries Cleopatra
10:51 Alexander sent envoys to Ptolemy king of Egypt with this message:
10:52 "I have come back to my kingdom and taken my seat on the throne of my fathers. I have taken power, crushed Demetrius and taken control of our country.
10:53 I met him in battle, and he and his army were crushed by us, and we have taken our seat on the throne of his kingdom.
10:54 Now let us make friendship with each other. Give me your daughter as my wife, and I will become your son-in-law and give you and her gifts worthy of you."
10:55 King Ptolemy answered, "Happy is the day you came back to the land of your fathers and sat on the throne of their kingdom!
10:56 Now I will do for you what you wrote. But come to meet me at Ptolemais, so that we may see each other, and I will make the marriage with you, as you have said."
10:57 Ptolemy set out from Egypt with his daughter Cleopatra and came to Ptolemais in the year 162.
10:58 King Alexander met him, and Ptolemy gave him his daughter Cleopatra and held her wedding at Ptolemais with great splendor, as kings do.
10:59 King Alexander wrote to Jonathan to come and meet him.
10:60 He went in splendor to Ptolemais and met the two kings. He gave them and their Friends silver and gold and many gifts, and he won their favor.
10:61 Some men from Israel who were a plague, renegades, gathered against him to bring charges against him, but the king paid no attention to them.
10:62 The king gave orders to take off Jonathan's clothes and dress him in purple, and they did so.
10:63 The king seated him beside himself and said to his officers, "Go out with him into the middle of the city and announce that no one is to bring charges against him on any matter, and no one is to trouble him for any reason."
10:64 When his accusers saw the honor he was given, as the herald proclaimed it, and saw him dressed in fine linen, they all ran away.
10:65 The king honored him and listed him among the First Friends, and made him a general and governor of a province.
10:66 Jonathan returned to Jerusalem in peace and gladness.
## Apollonius challenges Jonathan
10:67 In the year 165, Demetrius son of Demetrius came from Crete to the land of his fathers.
10:68 King Alexander heard of it. He was deeply troubled and returned to Antioch.
10:69 Demetrius put Apollonius, the governor of Coele-Syria, in command. He gathered a large force and camped at Jamnia. He sent word to Jonathan the high priest:
10:70 "You alone rise up against us, and because of you I have become a laughingstock and a disgrace. Why do you lord it over us in the mountains?
10:71 Now if you trust in your forces, come down to us in the plain, and let us test each other there, for the strength of the cities is with me.
10:72 Ask and find out who I am, and who the others are who help us. They say, 'You have no place to plant your foot before us, for your fathers were routed twice in their own land.'
10:73 And now you will not be able to stand against the cavalry and such an army in the plain, where there is no stone or pebble, and nowhere to run."
10:74 When Jonathan heard Apollonius's words, he was stirred in his mind. He chose ten thousand men and set out from Jerusalem, and his brother Simon joined him to help him.
## The battle of Azotus
10:75 He camped against Joppa. The people of the city shut him out, because Apollonius had a garrison in Joppa. So they attacked it.
10:76 The people of the city were afraid and opened the gates, and Jonathan took control of Joppa.
10:77 Apollonius heard of it. He drew up three thousand cavalry and a large force and marched toward Azotus as if passing through, while at the same time he led them out into the plain, because he had a great number of horsemen and trusted in them.
10:78 Jonathan went after him toward Azotus, and the armies joined battle.
10:79 Apollonius had left a thousand horsemen hidden behind them.
10:80 Jonathan learned that there was an ambush behind him. They surrounded his army and threw javelins at the people from morning until evening.
10:81 But the people stood firm, as Jonathan had ordered, and their horses grew tired.
10:82 Then Simon brought up his force and attacked the phalanx, for the cavalry was worn out. They were crushed by him and fled.
10:83 The cavalry scattered over the plain. They fled to Azotus and went into Beth-dagon, the temple of their idol, to save themselves.
10:84 Jonathan burned Azotus and the towns around it and took their plunder. The temple of Dagon, and those who had fled into it, he burned with fire.
10:85 Those who fell by the sword, together with those who were burned, came to about eight thousand men.
10:86 Jonathan set out from there and camped by Ascalon, and the people of the city came out to meet him with great honor.
10:87 Then Jonathan and those with him returned, loaded with plunder.
10:88 When King Alexander heard these things, he honored Jonathan still more.
10:89 He sent him a gold brooch, such as is given to the Kinsmen of kings. He also gave him Ekron and all its territory as his own possession.
## Ptolemy turns on Alexander
11:1 The king of Egypt gathered forces as many as the sand on the seashore, and many ships. He tried to take control of Alexander's kingdom by trickery and add it to his own kingdom.
11:2 He set out for Syria with words of peace, and the people of the towns opened their gates to him and went out to meet him, for King Alexander had ordered them to meet him, because he was his father-in-law.
11:3 But as Ptolemy entered the towns, he left troops in each town as a garrison.
11:4 When he came near Azotus, they showed him the temple of Dagon burned down, Azotus and its suburbs torn down, the bodies thrown out, and the remains of those he had burned in the battle, for they had piled them in heaps along his road.
11:5 They told the king what Jonathan had done, to discredit him, but the king said nothing.
11:6 Jonathan met the king at Joppa with ceremony. They greeted each other and spent the night there.
11:7 Jonathan went with the king as far as the river called Eleutherus, and then returned to Jerusalem.
11:8 King Ptolemy took control of the coastal towns as far as Seleucia by the sea, and he kept plotting evil against Alexander.
11:9 He sent envoys to King Demetrius, saying, "Come, let us make a treaty with each other. I will give you my daughter whom Alexander has, and you will rule your father's kingdom.
11:10 I am sorry I gave him my daughter, for he tried to kill me."
11:11 He spoke ill of him because he wanted his kingdom.
11:12 He took his daughter away from him and gave her to Demetrius. Alexander's face changed, and the hatred between them came into the open.
11:13 Ptolemy entered Antioch and put on the crown of Asia. He put two crowns on his head, the crown of Asia and the crown of Egypt.
11:14 King Alexander was in Cilicia at that time, because the people of that region were in revolt.
11:15 Alexander heard of it and came against him to make war. Ptolemy marched out and met him with a strong force and routed him.
11:16 Alexander fled to Arabia to find shelter there, and King Ptolemy was raised high.
11:17 Zabdiel the Arab cut off Alexander's head and sent it to Ptolemy.
11:18 King Ptolemy died on the third day, and his troops in the fortresses were killed by the people of those fortresses.
11:19 So Demetrius became king in the year 167.
## Jonathan wins over Demetrius II
11:20 In those days Jonathan gathered the men of Judea to attack the Citadel in Jerusalem, and they set up many siege engines against it.
11:21 But some men who hated their own nation, renegades, went to the king and reported to him that Jonathan was besieging the Citadel.
11:22 When he heard it he was angry. As soon as he heard, he set out and came to Ptolemais. He wrote to Jonathan not to go on with the siege, and to come and meet him for talks at Ptolemais as quickly as possible.
11:23 When Jonathan heard this, he gave orders to keep up the siege. He chose some of the elders of Israel and some of the priests and put himself in danger.
11:24 He took silver and gold, clothing and many other gifts, and went to the king at Ptolemais, and he won his favor.
11:25 Some lawless men of the nation kept bringing charges against him,
11:26 but the king treated him as the kings before him had done and raised him high in front of all his Friends.
11:27 He confirmed him in the high priesthood and in all the other honors he had held before, and had him counted among the First Friends.
11:28 Jonathan asked the king to free Judea from tribute, with the three districts and Samaria, and promised him three hundred talents.
11:29 The king agreed and wrote Jonathan a letter about all these things, as follows:
11:30 "King Demetrius to his brother Jonathan and to the nation of the Jews, greetings.
11:31 We are sending you too a copy of the letter we wrote about you to Lasthenes our Kinsman, so that you may see it:
11:32 'King Demetrius to Lasthenes his father, greetings.
11:33 We have decided to do good to the nation of the Jews, who are our friends and keep what is just toward us, because of their goodwill toward us.
11:34 So we confirm to them the territory of Judea and the three districts of Aphairema, Lydda and Rathamin. These were added to Judea from Samaria, with everything that belongs to them, for all who sacrifice in Jerusalem, in place of the royal dues that the king used to receive from them each year from the produce of the land and the fruit of the trees.
11:35 And the other payments due to us from now on, from the tithes and the tolls due to us, and the salt pans, and the crown taxes due to us, all these we hand over to them.
11:36 Not one of these grants will be canceled from now on, for all time.
11:37 Now take care to make a copy of these things. Let it be given to Jonathan and put up on the holy mountain in a fitting and conspicuous place.'"
## Tryphon and the boy king
11:38 King Demetrius saw that the land was quiet before him and no one opposed him. So he sent all his forces home, each to his own place, except the foreign troops he had hired from the islands of the nations. All the troops who had served his fathers came to hate him.
11:39 Now Tryphon had been one of Alexander's men before. He saw that all the troops were grumbling against Demetrius, so he went to Simalkue the Arab, who was raising Antiochus, Alexander's young son.
11:40 He kept pressing him to hand the boy over to him, so that he could reign in his father's place. They told him all that Demetrius had done and how his troops hated him. And he stayed there many days.
## Jewish troops save Demetrius at Antioch
11:41 Jonathan sent to King Demetrius asking him to remove the men of the Citadel from Jerusalem, and the men in the fortresses, for they kept making war on Israel.
11:42 Demetrius sent word to Jonathan: "I will not only do this for you and your nation. I will greatly honor you and your nation when I find the right time.
11:43 Now you will do well to send me men to fight on my side, for all my troops have deserted me."
11:44 Jonathan sent him three thousand strong, able men to Antioch. They came to the king, and the king was glad they had come.
11:45 The people of the city gathered in the middle of the city, about a hundred and twenty thousand men, and they wanted to kill the king.
11:46 The king fled into the palace. The people of the city seized the city's streets and began to fight.
11:47 The king called on the Jews for help. They all rallied to him at once and spread out through the city. That day they killed about a hundred thousand in the city.
11:48 They set fire to the city and took a great deal of plunder that day, and they saved the king.
11:49 When the people of the city saw that the Jews had taken control of the city as they pleased, they lost heart and cried out to the king, begging him,
11:50 "Give us your right hand in peace, and make the Jews stop fighting us and the city."
11:51 They threw down their weapons and made peace. The Jews won glory in the eyes of the king and of everyone in his kingdom, and they returned to Jerusalem with much plunder.
11:52 King Demetrius sat on the throne of his kingdom, and the land was quiet before him.
11:53 But he proved false in everything he had said. He turned against Jonathan and did not repay the kindness Jonathan had shown him. He oppressed him harshly.
## Antiochus VI and Jonathan's campaigns
11:54 After this Tryphon came back, and with him Antiochus, who was still a young boy. Antiochus became king and put on the crown.
11:55 All the troops Demetrius had dismissed gathered to him. They fought against Demetrius, and he fled and was routed.
11:56 Tryphon took the elephants and gained control of Antioch.
11:57 The young Antiochus wrote to Jonathan: "I confirm you in the high priesthood and set you over the four districts, and you are to be one of the King's Friends."
11:58 He sent him gold dishes and table service, and gave him the right to drink from gold cups, to wear purple and to have a gold brooch.
11:59 He made his brother Simon governor from the Ladder of Tyre to the borders of Egypt.
11:60 Jonathan set out and traveled through the land beyond the River and through its towns. All the army of Syria rallied to him as allies. He came to Ascalon, and the people of the city met him with honor.
11:61 From there he went to Gaza, but the people of Gaza shut him out. He laid siege to it, burned its suburbs and plundered them.
11:62 The people of Gaza pleaded with Jonathan, and he gave them his right hand in peace. He took the sons of their rulers as hostages and sent them to Jerusalem. Then he traveled through the country as far as Damascus.
## The battle at Hazor
11:63 Jonathan heard that the officers of Demetrius had come to Kedesh in Galilee with a large army, intending to remove him from his post.
11:64 He went to meet them, and he left his brother Simon in the country.
11:65 Simon camped against Beth-zur. He fought against it many days and shut them in.
11:66 They asked him to give them his right hand in peace, and he did. He sent them out of it, took the town and put a garrison in it.
11:67 Jonathan and his army camped by the water of Gennesar. Early in the morning they went to the plain of Hazor.
11:68 There an army of foreigners came to meet him in the plain. They had set an ambush against him in the mountains, while they themselves came at him from the front.
11:69 The men in ambush rose up from their places and joined battle, and all Jonathan's men fled.
11:70 Not one of them was left except Mattathias son of Absalom and Judas son of Chalphi, the commanders of the army's forces.
11:71 Jonathan tore his clothes, put dirt on his head and prayed.
11:72 Then he turned back against them in battle and routed them, and they fled.
11:73 When his men who were running away saw it, they came back to him and joined him in the pursuit as far as Kedesh, to the enemy camp, and they camped there.
11:74 About three thousand of the foreigners fell that day. And Jonathan returned to Jerusalem.
