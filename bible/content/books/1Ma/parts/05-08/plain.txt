## Edom, Baean and Ammon
5:1 When the nations on every side heard that the altar had been rebuilt and the sanctuary made new again as it had been before, they were enraged.
5:2 They plotted to do away with the people of Jacob who lived among them, and they began putting people to death and wiping them out.
5:3 Judas made war on the descendants of Esau in Idumea, at Akrabattene, because they kept Israel hemmed in. He struck them a heavy blow, crushed their strength and took their plunder.
5:4 He also remembered the wickedness of the sons of Baean. They had been a trap and a snare to the people, lying in wait for them along the roads.
5:5 He shut them up in their towers and camped against them. He put them under the ban, and burned the towers of the place with fire, together with everyone inside.
5:6 Then he crossed over against the Ammonites. He found a strong force there and many people, with Timothy as their leader.
5:7 He fought many battles with them. They were broken before him, and he struck them down.
5:8 He captured Jazer and its villages, and then went back to Judea.
## Cries for help from Gilead and Galilee
5:9 The nations in Gilead gathered against the Israelites who lived in their territory, to wipe them out. But the Israelites fled to the stronghold of Dathema.
5:10 They sent a letter to Judas and his brothers. It said, "The nations around us have gathered against us to wipe us out.
5:11 They are getting ready to come and capture the stronghold where we have taken refuge, and Timothy is leading their army.
5:12 So come now and rescue us from their hands, for many of us have fallen.
5:13 All our brothers who were in the district of Tobias have been killed. The enemy has carried off their wives, their children and their goods as captives, and destroyed about a thousand men there."
5:14 The letter was still being read when other messengers arrived from Galilee with their clothes torn. Their report ran the same way.
5:15 They said, "Men from Ptolemais, Tyre and Sidon, and all Galilee of the foreigners, have joined forces against them to destroy us."
5:16 When Judas and the people heard these words, a great assembly gathered to decide what they should do for their brothers, who were in distress and under attack.
5:17 Judas said to his brother Simon, "Choose men for yourself, and go and rescue your brothers in Galilee. My brother Jonathan and I will go into Gilead."
5:18 He left Joseph son of Zechariah, and Azariah, a leader of the people, with the rest of the army in Judea to guard it.
5:19 He ordered them, "Stand at the head of this people, and do not join battle with the nations until we come back."
5:20 Three thousand men were assigned to Simon to go to Galilee, and eight thousand to Judas for Gilead.
## Simon in Galilee
5:21 Simon went into Galilee and fought many battles with the nations, and the nations were broken before him.
5:22 He chased them as far as the gate of Ptolemais. About three thousand men of the nations fell, and he took their plunder.
5:23 He took along the Jews from Galilee and Arbatta, with their wives and children and all they owned, and brought them to Judea with great joy.
## Judas in Gilead
5:24 Judas Maccabeus and his brother Jonathan crossed the Jordan and marched three days into the wilderness.
5:25 They met the Nabateans, who came to meet them in peace and told them everything that had happened to their brothers in Gilead:
5:26 that many of them were shut up in Bozrah and Bosor, in Alema, Chaspho, Maked and Carnaim. All these towns were strong and large.
5:27 Others were shut up in the rest of the towns of Gilead. The enemy had set the next day to attack the strongholds, capture them, and wipe out all these people in one day.
5:28 Judas and his army suddenly turned off by the wilderness road to Bosor. He captured the town and killed every male with the edge of the sword. He took all their plunder and burned the town with fire.
5:29 He set out from there by night and marched until he reached the stronghold.
5:30 At dawn they looked up, and there was a huge crowd, too many to count, carrying ladders and siege machines to capture the stronghold. They were attacking the people inside.
5:31 Judas saw that the battle had begun, and that the cry of the town was going up to heaven with trumpets and loud shouting.
5:32 He said to the men of his army, "Fight today for our brothers!"
5:33 He came out behind the enemy in three companies. He sounded the trumpets, and they cried out in prayer.
5:34 When Timothy's army realized it was Maccabeus, they ran from him. He struck them a heavy blow, and about eight thousand of their men fell that day.
5:35 Then he turned aside to Maapha and attacked it. He captured it, killed every male in it, took its plunder and burned it with fire.
5:36 From there he moved on and captured Chaspho, Maked, Bosor and the other towns of Gilead.
## The battle at Raphon
5:37 After these events Timothy gathered another army and camped facing Raphon, on the far side of the stream.
5:38 Judas sent men to spy out the camp. They reported to him, "All the nations around you have gathered to him. It is a very large force.
5:39 They have also hired Arabs to help them. They are camped on the far side of the stream, ready to come and fight you." So Judas went to meet them.
5:40 As Judas and his army came near the stream of water, Timothy said to the officers of his army, "If he crosses over to us first, we will not be able to stand against him, because he will be more than a match for us.
5:41 But if he is afraid and camps on the other side of the river, we will cross over to him."
5:42 When Judas came near the stream of water, he stationed the officials of the people beside the stream and gave them this order: "Do not let anyone stay in camp. Everyone is to go into the battle!"
5:43 He crossed over against them first, and all his people behind him. All the nations were broken before him. They threw away all their weapons and fled to the sacred precinct at Carnaim.
5:44 But Judas's men took the town first, and burned the precinct with fire, with everyone in it. So Carnaim was overthrown, and it could no longer stand against Judas.
## The road home through Ephron
5:45 Judas gathered all the Israelites in Gilead, from the least to the greatest, with their wives, their children and their goods, a very large company, to go to the land of Judah.
5:46 They came as far as Ephron. This large town guarded the way in, and it was very strong. There was no turning aside from it to the right or to the left; the only way was through the middle of it.
5:47 The people of the town shut them out and blocked the gates with stones.
5:48 Judas sent them a message of peace: "I will go through your land to reach our own land. No one will hurt you. We will only walk past on foot." But they would not open to him.
5:49 Judas had it announced in the camp that every man should take up his position where he was.
5:50 The fighting men took their positions, and attacked the town all that day and all that night, and the town was given into his hands.
5:51 He destroyed every male with the edge of the sword, tore the town up by its roots, and took its plunder. Then he passed through the town over the bodies of the dead.
5:52 They crossed the Jordan into the great plain facing Beth-shan.
5:53 All along the way Judas kept gathering the stragglers and encouraging the people, until he came to the land of Judah.
5:54 They went up to Mount Zion glad and joyful, and presented burnt offerings, for not one of them had fallen by the time they came home in peace.
## Joseph and Azariah defeated
5:55 In the days when Judas and Jonathan were in Gilead, and their brother Simon was in Galilee facing Ptolemais,
5:56 Joseph son of Zechariah and Azariah, the commanders of the forces, heard about the brave deeds and the fighting the others had done.
5:57 They said, "We too should win a name for ourselves. Let us go and fight the nations around us."
5:58 They gave orders to the men of the army who were with them, and marched against Jamnia.
5:59 Gorgias and his men came out of the town to meet them in battle.
5:60 Joseph and Azariah were routed and chased as far as the borders of Judea. That day about two thousand men of the people of Israel fell.
5:61 The people suffered a great defeat, because they had not listened to Judas and his brothers, but thought they would do brave deeds.
5:62 They were not of the seed of those men by whose hand Israel was given salvation.
5:63 But the man Judas and his brothers were held in great honor in the sight of all Israel and of all the nations, wherever their name was heard.
5:64 People crowded around them, praising them.
## Hebron and Azotus
5:65 Judas and his brothers went out and fought the descendants of Esau in the land to the south. He struck Hebron and its villages, tore down its stronghold and burned its towers on every side.
5:66 He set out to go into the land of the Philistines, and passed through Samaria.
5:67 That day some priests fell in battle. They wanted to do brave deeds, and they went out to battle without thinking it through.
5:68 Judas turned aside to Azotus, in the land of the Philistines. He tore down their altars, burned the carved images of their gods, plundered the towns and returned to Judea.
## The death of Antiochus
6:1 King Antiochus was traveling through the upper provinces. He heard that in Elymais, in Persia, there was a city famous for its wealth, its silver and gold,
6:2 and that the temple in it was very rich. Gold coverings were there, with breastplates and weapons, left there by Alexander son of Philip, the king of Macedon, the first to reign over the Greeks.
6:3 So he came and tried to take the city and plunder it. But he could not, because the people of the city had learned of his plan.
6:4 They rose up against him in battle. He fled, and left there in deep grief to go back to Babylon.
6:5 Then someone came to him in Persia with news that the armies that had marched into the land of Judah had been routed.
6:6 Lysias had gone first with a strong force, and had been driven back before the Jews. They had grown strong with the weapons they took from the armies they had cut down.
6:7 They had torn down the abomination that he had built on the altar in Jerusalem. They had surrounded the sanctuary with high walls as it was before, and his town Beth-zur too.
6:8 When the king heard this news, he was shocked and badly shaken. He took to his bed and fell sick from grief, because things had not turned out for him the way he had planned.
6:9 He lay there many days, because deep grief came over him again and again, and he reckoned that he was dying.
6:10 He called all his Friends and said to them, "Sleep has left my eyes, and my heart has sunk under my worries.
6:11 I have asked my heart, 'How far into trouble have I come, into what great waves, where I am now? For I was good-hearted and well loved while I held power.'
6:12 Now the evil I did in Jerusalem comes back to me. I took all the silver and gold vessels in it, and I sent men to wipe out those who lived in Judah, for nothing.
6:13 I know that because of these things these troubles have found me. And here I am, dying of deep grief in a land not my own."
6:14 He called Philip, one of his Friends, and put him in charge of his whole kingdom.
6:15 He gave him his crown, his robe and his signet ring, so that he would guide his son Antiochus and bring him up to be king.
6:16 King Antiochus died there in the year 149.
6:17 When Lysias learned that the king was dead, he made the king's son Antiochus king in his place. Lysias had brought him up from boyhood, and he named him Eupator.
## The siege of the Citadel
6:18 The men in the Citadel kept hemming Israel in around the holy place. They were always looking for ways to do harm and to give the nations a foothold.
6:19 Judas decided to destroy them. He called all the people together to besiege them,
6:20 in the year 150, and he built siege platforms and machines against them.
6:21 But some of the besieged got out, and some ungodly men from Israel joined them.
6:22 They went to the king and said, "How long will you put off doing justice and avenging our brothers?
6:23 We were glad to serve your father, to live by what he said and to follow his decrees.
6:24 Because of this the men of our own people turned against us. Any of us they found was put to death, and our family lands were plundered.
6:25 They have not stretched out their hand against us alone, but also against all their own territory.
6:26 And look, today they have camped against the Citadel in Jerusalem to capture it. They have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur.
6:27 If you do not strike them first, and quickly, they will do greater things than these, and you will not be able to hold them back."
## The battle of Beth-zechariah
6:28 When the king heard this, he was angry. He gathered all his Friends, the commanders of his army, and the officers in charge of the cavalry.
6:29 Hired forces came to him from other kingdoms and from the islands of the seas.
6:30 His army numbered a hundred thousand foot soldiers, twenty thousand horsemen, and thirty-two elephants trained for war.
6:31 They came by way of Idumea and camped against Beth-zur. They fought for many days and built siege machines. But the defenders came out and burned the machines with fire, and fought like men.
6:32 Judas pulled away from the Citadel and camped at Beth-zechariah, facing the king's camp.
6:33 The king rose early in the morning and moved his army at full charge along the road to Beth-zechariah. The forces made ready for battle and blew the trumpets.
6:34 They showed the elephants the blood of grapes and mulberries, to rouse them for the battle.
6:35 They shared out the beasts among the phalanxes. By every elephant they posted a thousand men armored in chain mail, with bronze helmets on their heads, and to every beast was assigned a chosen troop of five hundred horse.
6:36 These were already wherever the beast was to be. Where it went, they went too; they did not stray from it.
6:37 On their backs were strong wooden towers with roofs, one to each beast, girded on with gear. In each tower rode thirty-two warriors who fought from it, and each beast had its Indian.
6:38 The rest of the horse he set on both sides, on the two flanks of the army, stirring up the enemy and closing in among the phalanxes.
6:39 As the sun glittered on the gold shields, the hills glittered back from them and blazed like torches of fire.
6:40 One part of the king's army stretched out over the high hills, and some over the low ground, and they came on securely, in ranks.
6:41 All who heard it were shaken: the sound of that multitude, the multitude on the march, the weapons crashing together. It was a very great and strong army.
6:42 Judas and his army drew near to do battle, and six hundred men of the king's army fell.
6:43 Eleazar Avaran saw that one of the beasts was armored with royal armor, and that it stood taller than all the other beasts. It looked as if the king was on it.
6:44 He gave himself to save his people and gain an everlasting name for himself.
6:45 He ran at it boldly into the middle of the phalanx, killing men to the right and to the left, and they parted before him on this side and that.
6:46 He crept in under the elephant, set himself beneath it, and killed it. It fell to the earth on top of him, and he died there.
6:47 When Judas's men saw the strength of the kingdom and the fury of its forces, they turned away from them.
## Siege and peace
6:48 The king's army went up to Jerusalem to meet them, and the king camped in Judea and at Mount Zion.
6:49 He made peace with the people of Beth-zur, and they came out of the town, because they had no food there to last a siege, for it was a Sabbath year for the land.
6:50 The king took Beth-zur and stationed a garrison there to guard it.
6:51 He camped against the sanctuary for many days. He set up siege platforms there, and machines, fire-throwers and stone-throwers, and small catapults for shooting arrows and slingstones.
6:52 The defenders also made machines against their machines, and they fought for many days.
6:53 But there was no food in the holy place, because it was the seventh year, and those who had escaped to Judea from the nations had eaten up the last of the stores.
6:54 Only a few men were left in the holy place, because hunger had overpowered them, and the rest scattered, each to his own place.
6:55 Then Lysias heard that Philip, whom King Antiochus while he was still alive had appointed to bring up his son Antiochus to be king,
6:56 had come back from Persia and Media with the king's forces that had gone with him, and that he was trying to take over the government.
6:57 So they hurried and pressed to leave. Lysias said to the king, to the commanders of the army and to the men, "We grow weaker every day. Our food is short, the place we are besieging is strong, and the affairs of the kingdom press on us.
6:58 So now let us give these people our right hand, and make peace with them and with all their nation.
6:59 Let us grant that they may walk by their own laws, as before. For it was over their laws, which we did away with, that they grew angry and did all these things."
6:60 The plan pleased the king and the commanders. He sent word to them to make peace, and they accepted.
6:61 The king and the commanders swore an oath to them on these terms, and the defenders came out of the stronghold.
6:62 Then the king entered Mount Zion and saw how strong the place was. He broke the oath he had sworn, and gave orders to tear down the wall all around.
6:63 Then he left in haste and went back to Antioch. He found Philip in control of the city, fought against him and took the city by force.
## Demetrius becomes king
7:1 In the year 151 Demetrius son of Seleucus left Rome. He came with a few men to a town on the coast, and made himself king there.
7:2 As he was entering the royal house of his ancestors, the army seized Antiochus and Lysias to bring them to him.
7:3 When he learned of it, he said, "Do not show me their faces."
7:4 So the army killed them, and Demetrius took his seat on the throne of his kingdom.
## Alcimus and the Hasideans
7:5 Then all the lawless and ungodly men of Israel came to him. Their leader was Alcimus, who wanted to serve as priest.
7:6 They brought charges against the people before the king. They said, "Judas and his brothers have destroyed all your friends, and have driven us out of our land.
7:7 Now send a man you trust. Let him go and see all the ruin that Judas has brought on us and on the king's land, and let him punish them and everyone who helps them."
7:8 The king chose Bacchides, a Friend of the King, who governed the province Beyond the River. He was a great man in the kingdom and loyal to the king.
7:9 He sent him, with the ungodly Alcimus, and they set Alcimus up in the priesthood. The king ordered Bacchides to take vengeance on the Israelites.
7:10 They set out and came into the land of Judah with a large army. Bacchides sent messengers to Judas and his brothers with peaceful words, but with treachery.
7:11 But they paid no attention to their words, for they saw that they had come with a large army.
7:12 A group of scribes gathered before Alcimus and Bacchides to ask for fair terms.
7:13 The Hasideans were the first among the Israelites to seek peace from them.
7:14 For they said, "A priest of the line of Aaron has come with the army. He will not do us wrong."
7:15 He spoke peaceful words with them and swore to them, "We will not seek to harm you or your friends."
7:16 They believed him. But he seized sixty of them and killed them in one day, as in the words that he wrote:
7:17 "The flesh of your loyal ones and their blood / they spilled on every side of Jerusalem, / and there was nobody to bury them."
7:18 Fear and dread of them fell on all the people. They said, "There is no truth or justice in them. They have broken the agreement and the oath they swore."
7:19 Bacchides pulled out from Jerusalem and camped at Beth-zaith. He sent men who seized many of the men who had deserted to him, and some of the people. He slaughtered them and threw them into the great pit.
7:20 He put the country in Alcimus's charge, and left an army with him to support him. Then Bacchides went back to the king.
7:21 Alcimus fought hard for the high priesthood.
7:22 All who were troubling their people rallied to him. They took control of the land of Judah and did great harm in Israel.
7:23 Judas saw all the evil that Alcimus and those with him had done among the Israelites, more than the nations had done.
7:24 He went out through all the territory of Judea round about, and took vengeance on the men, and on those who had deserted. They were kept from going out into the countryside.
7:25 When Alcimus saw that Judas and those with him had grown strong, and knew that he could not stand against them, he went back to the king and brought wicked charges against them.
## Nicanor
7:26 The king sent Nicanor, one of his honored commanders, a man who hated Israel and was its enemy, and ordered him to wipe out the people.
7:27 Nicanor came to Jerusalem with a large army. He sent a treacherous message of peace to Judas and his brothers:
7:28 "Let there be no war between you and me. I will come with only a few men, so that I may look you in the face in peace."
7:29 He came to Judas, and they greeted each other in peace. But the enemy were ready to seize Judas.
7:30 Word reached Judas that Nicanor had come to him with treachery. He was afraid of him, and would not see his face again.
7:31 When Nicanor knew that his plan had been found out, he went out to meet Judas near Capharsalama.
7:32 About five thousand of Nicanor's men fell, and they fled to the City of David.
7:33 After this Nicanor went up to Mount Zion. Some of the priests came out of the holy place, with some of the elders of the people, to greet him in peace and to show him the burnt offering being offered for the king.
7:34 But he mocked them and laughed at them. He defiled them and spoke arrogantly.
7:35 In a rage he swore, "Unless Judas and his army are handed over to me now, then when I come back safe, I will burn this house down." And he went out in a rage.
7:36 The priests went in and stood before the altar and the temple. They wept and said,
7:37 "You chose this house, for your name to be called over it, to be a house of prayer and pleading for your people.
7:38 Bring vengeance on this man and on his army, and let them fall by the sword. Remember their blasphemies, and do not let them stand."
## The battle of Adasa
7:39 Nicanor left Jerusalem and camped at Beth-horon, and an army from Syria came to join him.
7:40 Judas camped at Adasa with three thousand men. And Judas prayed:
7:41 "When the king's men blasphemed, your angel went out and struck down a hundred eighty-five thousand of them.
7:42 Shatter this army in the same way in front of us today. Let those who are left know that he spoke evil against your holy place. Judge him by his own wickedness."
7:43 The armies met in battle on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar. Nicanor's army was crushed, and he himself was the first to fall in the battle.
7:44 When his army saw that Nicanor had fallen, they threw down their weapons and fled.
7:45 The Jews chased them a day's journey, from Adasa until they came to Gazara, and they sounded the signal trumpets behind them.
7:46 People came out from all the villages of Judea round about, and outflanked them. They turned them back toward their pursuers, and they all fell by the sword. Not one of them was left.
7:47 They took the spoils and the plunder. They cut off Nicanor's head, and the right hand he had stretched out so arrogantly, and brought them and hung them up near Jerusalem.
7:48 The people were overjoyed, and they kept that day as a day of great gladness.
7:49 They resolved to keep this day every year, on the thirteenth of Adar.
7:50 So the land of Judah was quiet for a few days.
## What Judas heard of Rome
8:1 Judas heard what the Romans were known for: that they were mighty in strength, that they were well disposed to all who sided with them, and that they made friends with anyone who joined them or came to them,
8:2 and that they were mighty in strength. He was told about their wars and the brave deeds they did among the Gauls, how they had conquered them and made them pay tribute,
8:3 and what they had done in the land of Spain to get hold of the silver and gold mines there.
8:4 By their planning and patience they had taken control of the whole region, though it was very far away from them. They had also overcome the kings who came against them from the ends of the earth, until they crushed them and struck them a heavy blow, and the rest paid them tribute every year.
8:5 Philip, and Perseus king of the Kittim, and the others who rose up against them, they had crushed in war and conquered.
8:6 Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, had gone to war against them with a hundred twenty elephants, with cavalry and chariots and a very large army, and he was crushed by them.
8:7 They took him alive, and made him and those who would reign after him pay a heavy tribute, give hostages and a set portion.
8:8 As for the country of India, Media and Lydia, some of their finest lands, they took these from him and gave them to King Eumenes.
8:9 The Greeks had planned to come and destroy them.
8:10 But the Romans learned of it, and sent a single general against them. They made war on them, and many of them fell wounded. The Romans took their wives and children captive and plundered them. They took control of their land, tore down their strongholds, plundered them and made them slaves to this day.
8:11 The other kingdoms and the islands, all who ever stood up against them, they destroyed and made their slaves.
8:12 But with their friends and with those who relied on them, they kept up friendship. They had conquered kingdoms near and far, and all who heard their name were afraid of them.
8:13 Whomever they wished to help and to make king, became king; whomever they wished, they removed. They had risen very high.
8:14 Yet in all this none of them wore a crown or dressed in purple to make himself great by it.
8:15 They had made themselves a council chamber, and every day three hundred twenty men met there in council. They took counsel without ceasing about the people, to keep them in good order.
8:16 Every year they entrusted their rule to one man, to be lord over their whole land. Everyone obeyed that one man, and there was no envy or rivalry among them.
## The embassy to Rome
8:17 So Judas chose Eupolemus son of John son of Accos, and Jason son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to set up friendship and an alliance with them,
8:18 and to lift the yoke from them, for they saw that the kingdom of the Greeks was forcing Israel into slavery.
8:19 They went to Rome, a very long journey. They entered the council chamber and spoke:
8:20 "Judas, who is also called Maccabeus, with his brothers and the people of the Jews, has sent us to you to set up an alliance and peace with you, and to be enrolled as your allies and friends."
8:21 The proposal pleased them.
## The treaty
8:22 This is the copy of the letter that they wrote in reply on bronze tablets and sent to Jerusalem, to be kept there by the Jews as a record of peace and alliance:
8:23 "Good fortune to the Romans and to the nation of the Jews, by sea and by land, forever! May sword and enemy stay far from them.
8:24 If war comes first to Rome, or to any of its allies anywhere in its dominion,
8:25 the nation of the Jews shall fight as their ally with all their heart, as the occasion requires of them.
8:26 To those who make war they shall not give or supply grain, weapons, money or ships, as Rome has decided. They shall keep their obligations, and take nothing in return.
8:27 In the same way, if war comes first to the nation of the Jews, the Romans shall fight as their ally with all their soul, as the occasion requires of them.
8:28 To those who fight alongside the enemy, no grain, weapons or money shall be given, as Rome has decided. The Romans shall keep these obligations, and without deceit.
8:29 On these terms the Romans have made this agreement with the Jewish people.
8:30 If after this either party decides to add or take away anything, they may do so as they choose, and whatever they add or take away shall be binding.
8:31 As for the wrongs that King Demetrius is doing to them, we have written to him: 'Why have you made your yoke heavy on our friends and allies the Jews?
8:32 If they appeal to us against you again, we will do them justice and fight you by sea and by land.'"
