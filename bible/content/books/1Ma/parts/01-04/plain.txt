## Alexander and the kings after him
1:1 Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian, came out of the land of Kittim and struck down Darius king of the Persians and Medes. After that he became king in his place. He had already been king over Greece.
1:2 He fought many wars, seized fortresses and slaughtered kings.
1:3 He went on to the ends of the earth and took plunder from a great many nations. The earth fell silent before him, and he was raised high, and his heart swelled with pride.
1:4 He gathered a very strong army and ruled over lands, nations and princes, and they paid him tribute.
1:5 After this he fell ill and took to his bed, and he knew that he was dying.
1:6 He called his officers, the nobles who had been brought up with him from his youth, and he divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.
1:7 Alexander had reigned twelve years when he died.
1:8 Then his officers took power, each in his own place.
1:9 After his death they all put on crowns, and their sons after them for many years, and evils multiplied on the earth.
## Antiochus and the renegades
1:10 From them came a sinful root, Antiochus Epiphanes, son of King Antiochus. He had been a hostage in Rome, and he became king in the year 137 of the kingdom of the Greeks.
1:11 In those days renegades went out from Israel and won many over. "Come," they said, "let us make a covenant with the nations around us, for ever since we cut ourselves off from them, many evils have found us."
1:12 The idea seemed good to them.
1:13 Some of the people were eager for it and went to the king, and they were given authority to follow the practices of the nations.
1:14 They built a gymnasium in Jerusalem, following the customs of the nations.
1:15 They made foreskins for themselves and turned away from the holy covenant. They yoked themselves to the nations and sold themselves to do evil.
## Antiochus invades Egypt
1:16 When his kingdom was firmly in hand, Antiochus planned to become king of the land of Egypt too, so that he would reign over both kingdoms.
1:17 He entered Egypt with a huge force, with chariots and elephants and cavalry and a great fleet.
1:18 He made war on Ptolemy king of Egypt. Ptolemy turned back before him and fled, and many fell wounded.
1:19 They captured the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and he took the plunder of the land of Egypt.
## The Temple robbed
1:20 After he had struck Egypt, Antiochus turned back in the year 143. He went up against Israel and against Jerusalem with a huge force.
1:21 In his arrogance he entered the sanctuary. He took away the gold altar and the lampstand that gave light, with all its equipment,
1:22 the table of the Presence Bread, the cups for drink offerings, the bowls, the gold incense dishes, the curtain, the crowns, and the gold decoration on the front of the Temple. He scraped it all off.
1:23 He took the silver and the gold and the vessels people prized, and he took whatever hidden treasures he found.
1:24 Taking it all, he went away to his own land. And they committed a massacre and spoke with great arrogance.
## A lament for Israel
1:25 Great mourning came upon Israel / in every place where they lived.
1:26 Rulers and elders groaned; / young women and young men grew faint; / the beauty of the women was changed.
1:27 Every bridegroom took up a lament; / she who sat in the bridal room mourned.
1:28 The land shook for those who lived in it, / and the whole house of Jacob was clothed with shame.
## The Citadel
1:29 Two full years later the king sent an officer to collect tribute in the towns of Judah. He came to Jerusalem with a huge force.
1:30 His words to them were friendly, but they were a trick. Once they trusted him, he fell on the city without warning, dealt it a great blow, and wiped out many of the people of Israel.
1:31 He took the plunder of the city and set it on fire, and he tore down its houses and the walls around it.
1:32 He took the women and children captive, and they seized the livestock.
1:33 Then they built up the City of David with a great strong wall and strong towers, and it became their Citadel.
1:34 They stationed a sinful people there, renegades, and these made themselves strong in it.
1:35 They stored weapons and food in it. They gathered the plunder of Jerusalem and laid it up there, and it became a great snare.
1:36 It became an ambush against the sanctuary, / an evil adversary to Israel at all times.
1:37 They shed innocent blood all around the sanctuary, / and they defiled the sanctuary.
1:38 Because of them the people of Jerusalem fled. / She became a home for strangers; / she became a stranger to her own offspring, / and her children deserted her.
1:39 Her sanctuary was laid waste like a desert; / her feasts were turned into mourning, / her Sabbaths into a disgrace, / her honor into contempt.
1:40 As great as her glory had been, / so great her dishonor grew; / her pride was turned into mourning.
## The king's decree
1:41 Then the king wrote to his whole kingdom: all were to be one people,
1:42 and each was to give up his own customs. All the nations accepted the king's word.
1:43 Many even from Israel were glad to take up his worship. They sacrificed to idols and profaned the Sabbath.
1:44 The king sent letters by messengers to Jerusalem and the towns of Judah, telling them to follow customs foreign to the land,
1:45 to stop burnt offerings, sacrifice and drink offering in the sanctuary, to profane Sabbaths and feasts,
1:46 to defile the sanctuary and the holy ones,
1:47 to build altars and sacred precincts and idols, to sacrifice pigs and unclean animals,
1:48 to leave their sons uncircumcised, and to make themselves detestable with every kind of uncleanness and profanity,
1:49 so that they would forget the law and change all its rules.
1:50 "Anyone who does not act according to the king's word will die."
1:51 He wrote all this to his whole kingdom. He appointed inspectors over all the people, and he ordered the towns of Judah to offer sacrifice, town by town.
1:52 Many of the people joined them, everyone who was abandoning the law, and they did evil in the land.
1:53 They drove Israel into hiding, into every place where they could take refuge.
## The abomination of desolation
1:54 On the fifteenth day of Kislev, in the year 145, they built an abomination of desolation on the altar. And in the towns of Judah all around they built altars.
1:55 They burned incense at the windows of the houses and in the streets.
1:56 Any scrolls of the law they found, they ripped apart and burned.
1:57 Wherever a scroll of the covenant was found with anyone, or if anyone held to the law, the king's decree put him to death.
1:58 They used their power against Israel, against those who were found in the towns, month after month.
1:59 On the twenty-fifth day of the month they offered sacrifice on the pagan altar that stood on top of the altar of sacrifice.
1:60 Following the decree, they put to death the women who had had their children circumcised.
1:61 They hung the babies from their mothers' necks, and also their families and those who had circumcised them.
1:62 Yet many in Israel held strong and steeled themselves not to eat what was unclean.
1:63 They accepted death rather than be made unclean by food and profane the holy covenant. And they died.
1:64 And a very great wrath fell on Israel.
## Mattathias of Modein
2:1 In those days Mattathias son of John, son of Simeon, a priest of the line of Joarib, arose. He came from Jerusalem, and he made his home in Modein.
2:2 He had five sons: John, called Gaddi,
2:3 Simon, called Thassi,
2:4 Judas, called Maccabeus,
2:5 Eleazar, called Avaran, and Jonathan, called Apphus.
## Mattathias mourns
2:6 He saw the blasphemies being committed in Judah and in Jerusalem,
2:7 and he said, "Woe is me! Why was I born to see this, / the shattering of my people / and the shattering of the holy city, / and to sit there while it is handed over to enemies, / the sanctuary into the hands of strangers?
2:8 Her Temple has become like a man without honor;
2:9 the vessels of her glory are carried off as captives. / Her infants are killed in her streets, / her young men by the enemy's sword.
2:10 What nation has not taken her palaces for its own / and laid hands on her plunder?
2:11 All her ornaments have been stripped away; / she was free, and she has become a slave.
2:12 Look how our holy place, / our beauty and our glory, lies in ruins; / the nations have profaned them.
2:13 Why go on living?"
2:14 Mattathias and his sons tore their clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourned deeply.
## The test at Modein
2:15 The king's officers who were enforcing the apostasy came to the town of Modein to make them sacrifice.
2:16 Many from Israel came to them, and Mattathias and his sons gathered there too.
2:17 The king's officers spoke up and said to Mattathias, "You are a leader in this town, honored and great, and backed by sons and brothers.
2:18 Now come forward first and do what the king commands, as all the nations have done, and the leaders of Judah, and those who are left in Jerusalem. Then you and your house will be counted among the Friends of the King, and you and your sons will be honored with silver and gold and many gifts."
2:19 Mattathias answered in a loud voice, "Even if all the nations in the king's realm listen to him, and each one turns away from the worship of its ancestors and chooses to follow his commands,
2:20 yet I and my sons and my brothers will walk in the covenant of our ancestors.
2:21 Never may we abandon the law and its rules!
2:22 We will not obey the king's law and turn aside from our worship to the right or to the left."
2:23 When he had finished saying these words, a Jewish man came forward in the sight of all to offer incense on the altar in Modein, as the king had commanded.
2:24 Mattathias saw it and was filled with zeal. His insides trembled, and he let his anger rise, as justice demanded. He ran and slaughtered the man on the altar.
2:25 At the same time he killed the king's man who was forcing them to sacrifice, and he tore down the altar.
2:26 He was zealous for the law, as Phinehas had been against Zimri son of Salu.
2:27 Then Mattathias cried out in a loud voice through the town, "Everyone who is zealous for the law and stands by the covenant, come out and follow me!"
2:28 They fled to the hills, he and his sons, leaving behind all they owned in the town.
## A thousand die on the Sabbath
2:29 Then many who sought what is right and just went down into the desert to settle there,
2:30 they and their sons, their wives and their livestock, because their troubles had grown so hard to bear.
2:31 Word reached the king's men and the forces that were in Jerusalem, the City of David, that men who had defied the king's command had gone down to the hiding places in the desert.
2:32 Many ran after them and overtook them. They camped opposite them and prepared to attack them on the Sabbath day.
2:33 They said to them, "That is enough. Come out and obey the king's word, and you will live."
2:34 They answered, "We will not come out. We will not obey the king's word and profane the Sabbath day."
2:35 The enemy quickly attacked them.
2:36 But they did not answer them. They threw no stone at them and did not seal off their hiding places.
2:37 "Let us all die in our integrity," they said. "Heaven and earth bear witness for us that you destroy us without a trial."
2:38 So the enemy rose against them in battle on the Sabbath. They died, with their wives and children and animals, about a thousand human lives.
## Fighting on the Sabbath
2:39 When Mattathias and his friends learned of it, they mourned for them deeply.
2:40 They said to one another, "If we all do as our brothers did and do not fight the nations for our lives and our rules, they will soon wipe us off the land."
2:41 That day they agreed: "Whoever comes to make war on us on the Sabbath day, we will fight him face to face. We will not all die the way our brothers died in the hiding places."
2:42 Then a company of Hasideans joined them, strong fighters from Israel, each one ready to give himself for the law.
2:43 All who were fleeing from the troubles joined them and gave them support.
2:44 They formed an army and struck down sinners in their anger and lawless men in their rage. The rest fled to the nations for safety.
2:45 Mattathias and his sons went around and tore down their altars.
2:46 Any boys still uncircumcised whom they found within Israel's borders, they circumcised by force.
2:47 They chased the sons of arrogance, and the work succeeded in their hands.
2:48 They rescued the law from the hands of the nations and the hands of kings, and they did not let the sinner raise his horn.
## Mattathias's last words
2:49 As the time drew near for Mattathias to die, he said to his sons, "Pride and contempt have taken firm hold now. This is a season of overthrow and blazing anger.
2:50 So now, my children, be zealous for the law, and give your lives for the covenant of our ancestors.
2:51 Remember the deeds our ancestors did in their generations, and receive great glory, glory that lasts forever.
2:52 Abraham: was he not found faithful in the test, and was it not reckoned to him as righteousness?
2:53 Joseph kept the commandment when he was hard pressed, and he became lord of Egypt.
2:54 Phinehas our father, because he was zealous with zeal, received the covenant of a holy priesthood.
2:55 Joshua, because he carried out the word, became a judge in Israel.
2:56 Caleb bore witness before the assembly, and he received land as his inheritance.
2:57 David, for his mercy, inherited the throne of a kingdom that lasts forever.
2:58 Elijah, because he was zealous with zeal for the law, was taken up as if into heaven.
2:59 Hananiah, Azariah and Mishael trusted, and they were kept safe from the flame.
2:60 Daniel, for his integrity, was rescued from the lions' mouth.
2:61 So think it through, generation after generation: none who hope in him will grow weak.
2:62 Do not fear the words of a sinful man, for his glory will go to dung and worms.
2:63 Today he will be lifted high, and he will not be found, for he has gone back to his dust, and his plans have come to nothing.
2:64 My children, be strong and be men in keeping the law, for in it you will be honored.
2:65 Here is your brother Simon. I know he is a man of good counsel. Listen to him always. He will be a father to you.
2:66 Judas Maccabeus has been mighty and strong from his youth. He will be the commander of your army, and you will wage the war of the peoples.
2:67 Bring to your side all who keep the law, and take vengeance for your people.
2:68 Repay the nations what they deserve, and give heed to the law's commands."
2:69 Then he blessed them and was gathered to his ancestors.
2:70 He died in the year 146, and his sons buried him in the tomb of his ancestors at Modein. All Israel mourned for him with great lamentation.
## Judas Maccabeus
3:1 Then his son Judas, called Maccabeus, rose up in his place.
3:2 All his brothers helped him, with all who had joined his father, and they gladly fought Israel's war.
3:3 He made his people's glory wide. / He dressed in a breastplate like a giant / and belted on his gear of war. / He joined battle after battle; / his sword sheltered the camp.
3:4 In his deeds he was like a lion, / like a young lion that roars on the hunt.
3:5 He tracked down the lawless and hunted them; / he set fire to those who troubled his people.
3:6 The lawless shrank back for fear of him; / all who did lawless deeds were thrown into panic; / and deliverance went forward in his hand.
3:7 He brought bitterness to many kings / and gladness to Jacob by what he did; / his memory will be a blessing forever.
3:8 He passed through Judah's towns, / destroying the ungodly in the land, / and he turned anger away from Israel.
3:9 His name was known to the end of the earth; / he gathered those who were perishing.
## Apollonius and Seron
3:10 Apollonius gathered the nations, with a large force from Samaria, to fight against Israel.
3:11 Judas learned of it and went out to meet him. He struck him down and killed him. Many fell wounded, and the rest fled.
3:12 They took their gear, and Judas took the sword of Apollonius. He fought with it all the rest of his days.
3:13 Seron, the commander of the army of Syria, heard that Judas had gathered a band, a company of the faithful, who went out with him to war.
3:14 He said, "I will make a name for myself and win glory in the kingdom. I will make war on Judas and those with him, who scorn the king's word."
3:15 So he set out again, and with him went up a strong army of the ungodly, to help him take vengeance on the Israelites.
3:16 He came near the ascent of Beth-horon, and Judas went out to meet him with a very small force.
3:17 When they saw the army coming to meet them, they said to Judas, "We are so few. How can we fight a crowd this size? And we have had no food today, so we are worn out."
3:18 Judas said, "It is easy for many to be trapped by a few. To Heaven it makes no difference whether it saves by many or by few.
3:19 Winning a war is not a matter of how big an army is. Strength comes from Heaven.
3:20 They are coming against us full of insolence and lawlessness, to destroy us and our wives and our children, and to plunder us.
3:21 We, though, are fighting for our lives and our laws.
3:22 He will shatter them in front of us himself. So do not be afraid of them."
3:23 When he had finished speaking, he rushed on them suddenly, and Seron and his army were crushed before him.
3:24 They pursued them down the descent of Beth-horon to the plain. About eight hundred of them fell, and the rest fled to the land of the Philistines.
3:25 Fear of Judas and his brothers began to fall, and terror came upon the nations around them.
3:26 His name reached the king, and every nation told of Judas's battles.
## The king's treasury runs low
3:27 The news enraged King Antiochus. He sent out orders and gathered every force in his kingdom, a very strong army.
3:28 He opened his treasury and paid his troops for a year, and he ordered them to be ready for a year for every need.
3:29 But he saw that the money in his treasuries was running out. The tribute from the country was small, because of the strife and disaster he had brought on the land by doing away with the laws that had stood from the earliest days.
3:30 He was afraid that, as had happened once or twice, he would not have enough for the expenses and the gifts he used to give before with an open hand.
3:31 He was deeply troubled, and he decided to go to Persia, to collect the tribute of those lands and raise a great deal of money.
3:32 He put Lysias, a noble of royal blood, over the king's affairs from the Euphrates River to the border of Egypt,
3:33 and to bring up his son Antiochus until he came back.
3:34 He handed over to him half of his forces and the elephants, and he gave him orders about everything he wanted done, and about those who lived in Judea and Jerusalem:
3:35 he was to send an army against them to root out and wipe out the strength of Israel and the remnant of Jerusalem, and to remove their memory from the place,
3:36 to settle foreigners in all their territory, and to divide their land among them.
3:37 The king took the half of his forces that remained and set out from Antioch, his royal city, in the year 147. He crossed the Euphrates River and went on through the upper provinces.
## The army at Emmaus
3:38 Lysias chose Ptolemy son of Dorymenes, Nicanor and Gorgias, powerful men among the Friends of the King,
3:39 and he sent with them forty thousand men and seven thousand horsemen to go into the land of Judah and destroy it, as the king had commanded.
3:40 They set out with all their forces and came and camped near Emmaus in the lowland.
3:41 The merchants of the region heard the report of them. They took a great deal of silver and gold, and servants, and came to the camp to buy the Israelites as slaves. Forces from Syria and from the land of the Philistines joined them.
## The prayer at Mizpah
3:42 Judas and his brothers saw that troubles had multiplied and that the armies were camping within their borders. They also learned of the royal orders, which commanded that the people be brought to ruin and an end.
3:43 They said to one another, "Let us raise up our people and our holy place from their ruin."
3:44 The assembly came together to get ready for war and to pray, asking for mercy and pity.
3:45 Jerusalem lay empty like a desert; / none of her children went in or came out. / The sanctuary was trampled down, / foreigners held the Citadel, / and it was a lodging for the nations. / Joy was taken away from Jacob; / the flute and the harp fell silent.
3:46 They gathered and came to Mizpah, opposite Jerusalem, because in the past Mizpah had been a place of prayer for Israel.
3:47 That day they fasted. They dressed in sackcloth, put ashes on their heads and tore their clothes.
3:48 They spread out the scroll of the law, to search it on matters the nations used to ask of the images of their idols.
3:49 They brought the priests' garments, the firstfruits and the tithes, and they brought forward the Nazirites who had completed their days.
3:50 They shouted toward Heaven: "What are we to do with these? Where can we take them?
3:51 Your holy place is trampled and profaned, / and your priests are in mourning and humiliation.
3:52 The nations have come together against us to wipe us out. / You know their plans against us.
3:53 How can we stand before them / unless you help us?"
3:54 They sounded the trumpets and shouted with a loud voice.
## Judas readies the army
3:55 After this Judas appointed leaders of the people, commanders of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties and of tens.
3:56 He told those who were building houses, or were engaged to be married, or were planting vineyards, or were afraid, each to go back home, as the law says.
3:57 Then the army set out and camped south of Emmaus.
3:58 Judas said, "Arm yourselves and be brave men. Be ready by morning to fight these nations who have gathered against us to wipe out both us and our holy place.
3:59 Better for us to die fighting than to watch the evils that come on our nation and our holy place.
3:60 But whatever the will in Heaven is, so he will do."
## Victory at Emmaus
4:1 Gorgias took five thousand men and a thousand picked horsemen, and this force set out by night
4:2 to fall on the camp of the Jews and strike them suddenly. Men from the Citadel were his guides.
4:3 Judas heard of it, and he and his warriors set out to strike the king's army at Emmaus
4:4 while its forces were still scattered away from the camp.
4:5 Gorgias came to Judas's camp by night and found no one there. He searched for them in the hills, for he said, "These men are running away from us."
4:6 When day came, Judas was seen in the plain with three thousand men. They lacked the shields and swords they would have wanted.
4:7 They saw the nations' camp: strong, its men in breastplates, horsemen circling it, all of them practiced in war.
4:8 Judas said to the men with him, "Do not be afraid of how many they are, and do not let their charge scare you.
4:9 Remember how our ancestors were saved at the Red Sea, when Pharaoh pursued them with an army.
4:10 So now let us shout to Heaven. Perhaps he will want us still, and remember the covenant with our ancestors, and smash this army in front of us today.
4:11 Then every nation will know that Israel has someone who redeems and rescues it."
4:12 The foreigners looked up and saw them coming against them.
4:13 They went out of the camp to battle, and Judas's men blew the trumpets.
4:14 They clashed, and the nations were crushed and fled into the plain.
4:15 All those at the rear fell by the sword. They pursued them as far as Gazara and the plains of Judea, Azotus and Jamnia, and about three thousand of them fell.
4:16 Judas and his force turned back from the pursuit,
4:17 and he said to the people, "Don't crave the plunder. Another battle is in front of us.
4:18 Gorgias and his army are close by in the hills. Stand up to our enemies now and fight them; afterward you can take the plunder without fear."
4:19 Judas was still saying this when a detachment appeared, peering out from the hills.
4:20 The detachment saw that their army had been put to flight and that the camp was being burned, for the smoke they could see showed what had happened.
4:21 When they understood this they were terrified. And when they also saw Judas's army in the plain, ready for battle,
4:22 they all fled to the land of the Philistines.
4:23 Then Judas went back to plunder the camp. He took much gold and silver, blue and purple cloth, purple from the sea, and great wealth.
4:24 On their way back they sang hymns and blessed Heaven: "For his loyal love is good forever."
4:25 That day a great rescue came to Israel.
4:26 Those of the foreigners who had escaped came and told Lysias everything that had happened.
4:27 When he heard it, he was shaken and discouraged, because things had not gone with Israel as he wished, and what the king had ordered him had not come about.
## Lysias defeated at Beth-zur
4:28 The next year he mustered sixty thousand picked men and five thousand horsemen to finish them off in war.
4:29 They came into Idumea and camped at Beth-zur, and Judas met them with ten thousand men.
4:30 He saw that the army was strong, and he prayed: "Blessed are you, Savior of Israel. You broke the mighty man's charge by the hand of your servant David, and you gave the camp of the Philistines into the hands of Jonathan son of Saul and the man who carried his armor.
4:31 Trap this army in the hands of your people Israel. Let them be put to shame with all their troops and horsemen.
4:32 Give them a coward's heart. Let their bold strength melt away. Make them reel when they are broken.
4:33 Bring them down by the sword of those who love you, and let all who know your name sing your praise."
4:34 They clashed, and about five thousand men of Lysias's army fell; they fell right in front of them.
4:35 When Lysias saw his lines routed and the boldness of Judas's men, and how ready they were either to live or to die nobly, he went back to Antioch. He hired mercenaries, planning to come back to Judea with an even larger army.
## The sanctuary made clean
4:36 Judas and his brothers said, "Our enemies have been crushed. Now let us go up and make the holy place clean, and dedicate it again."
4:37 The whole army gathered, and they went up to Mount Zion.
4:38 They found the sanctuary deserted, the altar profaned and the gates burned down. Plants had grown up in the courtyards as in a forest or on a hillside, and the priests' rooms had been pulled down.
4:39 They tore their clothes, mourned with great lamentation and put ashes on their heads.
4:40 They fell face down on the ground, sounded the signal trumpets and cried out to Heaven.
4:41 Then Judas assigned men to fight those in the Citadel until he had cleansed the holy place.
4:42 He picked priests who were without blemish and who loved the law.
4:43 They purified the holy place and took the stones that had been defiled out to an unclean place.
4:44 They discussed what to do with the altar of burnt offering, which had been profaned.
4:45 They came to a good decision: to tear it down, so that it would not be a disgrace to them, since the nations had defiled it. So they tore down the altar
4:46 and stored its stones on the Temple mount in a suitable place, until a prophet should come to give a ruling about them.
4:47 They took whole stones, as the law says, and built a new altar on the pattern of the first.
4:48 They rebuilt the holy place and the inner parts of the house, and he consecrated the courts.
4:49 They made new holy vessels, and they brought the lampstand, the altar for burnt offerings and incense, and the table into the Temple.
4:50 They burned incense on the altar and lit the lamps of the lampstand, and light shone in the Temple.
4:51 They laid loaves on the table and spread out the curtains, and so they completed every task they had taken on.
## The dedication
4:52 They got up at dawn on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month, the month of Kislev, in the year 148,
4:53 and they offered sacrifice on the new altar of burnt offering they had made, as the law commands.
4:54 It was dedicated at the same season and on the same day that the nations had profaned it, with songs and harps and lyres and cymbals.
4:55 All the people fell on their faces. They worshiped and blessed Heaven, who had given them success.
4:56 They celebrated the dedication of the altar for eight days. They offered burnt offerings with joy, and they sacrificed peace offerings and offerings of praise.
4:57 They adorned the face of the Temple with gold crowns and little shields. He renewed the gates and the priests' rooms, and they hung doors in them.
4:58 The people were overjoyed, and the shame the nations had brought on them was turned away.
4:59 Judas and his brothers and the whole assembly of Israel decided that the days of the dedication of the altar should be kept at their season every year, for eight days. They were to begin on the twenty-fifth day of the month of Kislev and be kept with joy and gladness.
## Zion and Beth-zur fortified
4:60 At that time they built high walls and strong towers around Mount Zion, so that the nations could not come and trample it down as they had done before.
4:61 They stationed a force there to guard it, and they fortified Beth-zur to guard it, so that the people would have a fortress facing Idumea.
