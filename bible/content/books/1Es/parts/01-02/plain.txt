## Josiah's Passover
1:1 Josiah held the Passover in Jerusalem for his Lord. They slaughtered the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.
1:2 He put the priests in their divisions, dressed in their robes, in the temple of the LORD.
1:3 He spoke to the Levites, Israel's temple servants, and told them to make themselves holy to the LORD when the holy ark of the LORD was placed in the house built by King Solomon, David's son.
1:4 "You will not have to carry it on your shoulders. Now serve the LORD your God, and care for his nation Israel. Get yourselves ready by your families and your tribes, as David king of Israel wrote, and as befits the greatness of Solomon his son.
1:5 Take your stand in the holy place, in the divisions of your Levite families, in front of your brothers the Israelites.
1:6 In good order, slaughter the Passover, get the sacrifices ready for your brothers, and keep the Passover by the LORD's command given to Moses."
1:7 Josiah gave the people who were there thirty thousand lambs and young goats, and three thousand calves. They came from the king's own goods, a gift he had promised to the people, the priests and the Levites.
1:8 Hilkiah, Zechariah and Esyelus, the officers in charge of the temple, gave the priests two thousand six hundred sheep and three hundred calves for the Passover.
1:9 Jeconiah, Shemaiah, Nethanel his brother, Sabias, Ochiel and Joram, commanders of thousands, gave the Levites a thousand sheep and seven hundred calves for the Passover.
1:10 This is how it was done. The priests and the Levites stood in good order, holding the unleavened bread, by tribes and by their ancestors' divisions, in front of the people, to present to the LORD as it is written in the scroll of Moses. They did the same in the morning.
1:11 They roasted the Passover in the fire, as is right. The sacrifices they boiled in bronze pots and kettles, with a sweet smell, and took them around to everyone among the people.
1:12 Afterward they made things ready for themselves and for their brothers the priests, Aaron's descendants.
1:13 For the priests were sending up the fat until late at night, so the Levites made things ready for themselves and for their brothers the priests, Aaron's descendants.
1:14 The temple singers, Asaph's descendants, were at their posts, as David had arranged, with Asaph, Zechariah and Eddinus, the king's men.
1:15 At each gate stood the gatekeepers. None of them could leave his own shift, for their brothers the Levites made things ready for them.
1:16 So everything for the LORD's sacrifice was finished that day, to hold the Passover and to offer the sacrifices on the LORD's altar, by King Josiah's command.
1:17 The Israelites to be found there kept the Passover at that time, and the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days.
1:18 No Passover like it had been held in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet.
1:19 None of Israel's kings had held a Passover like the one Josiah held, with the priests, the Levites, the Jews, and all Israel who were found living in Jerusalem.
1:20 It was in the eighteenth year of Josiah's reign that this Passover was held.
## Josiah's upright heart
1:21 Josiah's works stood upright before his Lord, for they came from a heart full of godliness.
1:22 The things of his time are written in the records of earlier times: about those who sinned and acted ungodly against the Lord more than any nation or kingdom, and the ways they grieved him. And the words of the Lord rose up against Israel.
## Josiah dies at Megiddo
1:23 When Josiah had done all this, Pharaoh king of Egypt came to stir up war at Carchemish on the Euphrates. Josiah went out to meet him.
1:24 The king of Egypt sent him a message: "What do I have to do with you, king of Judea?
1:25 It is not against you that the Lord God has sent me out, for my war is on the Euphrates. The Lord is with me now. Yes, the Lord is with me and hurries me on. Get out of the way, and do not stand against the Lord."
1:26 But Josiah did not turn back in his chariot. He set out to fight him, and he did not listen to the words of Jeremiah the prophet from the mouth of the Lord.
1:27 He went to war with him on the plain of Megiddo, and the officers came down against King Josiah.
1:28 The king said to his servants, "Get me out of the battle, for I am badly weakened." At once his servants carried him out of the battle line.
1:29 He climbed into his second chariot. They brought him back to Jerusalem, and there he departed this life. He was buried in the family tomb.
1:30 All Judea mourned for Josiah. Jeremiah the prophet sang a lament over Josiah, and the chief men and the women have sung laments for him to this day. It was given out as a custom, to be kept forever by the whole race of Israel.
1:31 All this is recorded in the scroll of the histories about Judea's kings, along with each single thing Josiah did, his glory, and his understanding in the LORD's law. What he did, and what is told here, stands written in the scroll of the kings of Israel and Judah.
## The last kings of Judea
1:32 The people of the nation took Jeconiah son of Josiah and made him king in place of Josiah his father. He was twenty-three years old.
1:33 He reigned in Israel and Jerusalem for three months. Then the king of Egypt removed him from the throne in Jerusalem,
1:34 and he fined the nation one hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.
1:35 Then the king of Egypt set up Jehoiakim, his brother, as king of Judea and Jerusalem.
1:36 Jehoiakim bound the nobles. Zarius his brother he seized and brought up out of Egypt.
1:37 Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he became king of Judea and Jerusalem. He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.
1:38 Next, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up. He bound him in bronze fetters and took him away to Babylon.
1:39 Some of the LORD's holy vessels Nebuchadnezzar also took and carried off, and he put them in his temple in Babylon.
1:40 What is told about him, his uncleanness and his impiety, is written in the scroll of the chronicles of the kings.
1:41 In his place his son Jehoiakim reigned. He was eight years old when he was made king.
1:42 He reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem. He did what was evil in the LORD's eyes.
1:43 A year later Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him to Babylon, along with the holy vessels of the LORD.
1:44 He set up Zedekiah as king of Judea and Jerusalem, a man of twenty-one, and Zedekiah reigned for eleven years.
1:45 He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and he paid no respect to the words spoken by Jeremiah the prophet from the mouth of the LORD.
1:46 King Nebuchadnezzar had put him under oath by the name of the Lord, but he swore falsely and revolted. He made his neck stiff, and his heart, and broke the laws of the LORD, the God of Israel.
## Jerusalem falls
1:47 The leaders of the people and of the priests, too, did many ungodly and lawless things, worse than all the unclean ways of all the nations. They made the LORD's temple unclean, the temple set apart in Jerusalem.
1:48 The King of their ancestors sent them his messenger to call them back, for he felt pity for them and for his tent.
1:49 They, though, sneered at his messengers. On the very day the Lord spoke, they were making fun of his prophets, until at last, angry with his nation for its impious deeds, he ordered the kings of the Chaldeans to come up against them.
1:50 They killed their young men with the sword all around the holy temple. They spared no young man or young woman, no old man or boy. They handed them all over into their hands.
1:51 All the Lord's holy vessels, big and small, the LORD's chests and the king's storehouses, they took and carried off to Babylon.
1:52 They set the Lord's house on fire and tore down the walls of Jerusalem. Its towers they burned with fire,
1:53 and they wrecked every precious thing in it. Those who were left he took away to Babylon with the sword.
1:54 There they became slaves to him and to his sons until the Persians began to reign. This was to fulfill the LORD's word by Jeremiah's mouth:
1:55 until the land has had the pleasure of its Sabbaths. All the time it lies desolate it will keep Sabbath, until seventy years are filled up.
## Cyrus sends the exiles home
2:1 In the first year that Cyrus reigned over the Persians, to fulfill the LORD's word by Jeremiah's mouth,
2:2 the LORD roused the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians. He sent a proclamation through his whole kingdom, and in writing as well:
2:3 "Cyrus king of the Persians says this: The Lord of Israel, the Lord Most High, has appointed me king of the whole inhabited world.
2:4 He has made it known to me that I am to build him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judea.
2:5 So if any of you are from his nation, may his Lord be with him. Let him go up to Jerusalem in Judea and build the house of the Lord of Israel. He is the Lord who has made his dwelling in Jerusalem.
2:6 All who live in the places around, the people of his place, are to help him with gold and silver and gifts, with horses and livestock, along with the other things given by vow for the temple of the Lord in Jerusalem."
2:7 Then the heads of the families of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin set things in order, with the priests and the Levites: all those whose spirit the Lord had roused to go up and build a house for the LORD in Jerusalem.
2:8 Those around them helped them in every way, with silver and gold, horses and livestock, and a great many vowed gifts from many whose minds had been stirred.
## The vessels come home
2:9 King Cyrus took out the LORD's holy vessels, the ones Nebuchadnezzar had carried off from Jerusalem and set up in his idol temple.
2:10 Cyrus king of the Persians brought them out and handed them over to Mithridates, his treasurer.
2:11 Through him they were handed over to Sanassar, the leader of Judea.
2:12 This was their count: 1,000 gold cups for drink offerings, 1,000 silver cups for drink offerings, 29 silver censers, 30 gold bowls, 2,410 silver bowls, and 1,000 other vessels.
2:13 All the vessels of gold and silver that were brought came to 5,469.
2:14 Samanassar brought them up with the people from the captivity, from Babylon to Jerusalem.
## A letter against Jerusalem
2:15 In the time of Artaxerxes king of the Persians, Belemus, Mithridates, Tabellius, Rathymus, Beeltethmus and Samellius the scribe, with the rest of their colleagues who lived in Samaria and the other places, wrote against those living in Judea and Jerusalem. This is the letter they wrote:
2:16 "To the lord King Artaxerxes, from your servants Rathymus the recorder, Samellius the scribe, the rest of their council, and those in Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
2:17 Now let the lord king know that the Jews who came up to us from you have come to Jerusalem. They are settling in the rebellious and evil city. They are repairing its marketplaces and walls and laying the foundation of a temple.
2:18 So if this city is built and its walls are finished, they will refuse to pay tribute, and they will even stand against kings. And because the temple work is moving ahead, it seems good to us not to let this pass, but to bring it before the lord king, so that, if you approve, your fathers' books may be searched.
2:19 You will find in the records what is written about these things, and you will learn that this city was a rebel city that troubled kings and cities, and that the Jews were rebels who set up sieges in it from long ago. That is the reason this city was destroyed.
2:20 So now we are informing you, lord king, that if this city is built and its walls are set up again, you will have no way out to Coele-Syria and Phoenicia anymore."
## The king's reply
2:21 Then the king wrote back to Rathymus the recorder, Beeltethmus, Samellius the scribe and the rest of their colleagues who live in Samaria, Syria and Phoenicia, as follows:
2:22 "I have read the letter you sent me. So I gave an order for a search, and it was found that this city has stood against kings since long ago,
2:23 that its people have carried on revolts and wars in it, and that strong, harsh kings were in Jerusalem, lords over Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, who taxed them.
2:24 So now I have ordered that those men be stopped from building the city, and that care be taken that nothing more is done against this order, so that the mischief does not grow any more and trouble kings."
2:25 When King Artaxerxes' letter had been read, Rathymus, Samellius the scribe and their colleagues set out in haste for Jerusalem with horsemen and a crowd drawn up for battle, and set about stopping the builders. Work on the temple in Jerusalem stopped, and it stayed stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of the Persians.
