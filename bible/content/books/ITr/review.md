# Hostile review: Ignatius to the Trallians (ITr)

Reviewer: separate hostile-reviewer agent, 2026-11-13. Checked: plain.txt against `texts/orig/ITr.json` verse by verse; Lake's Greek, English and footnotes in the local OCR (`.cache/dl/lake-ia/apostolicfathers01lake.txt` 11082-11700, with `TheApostolicFathersV1.txt` as the second scan); Lightfoot's text and apparatus (`.cache/dl/lightfoot-ign/p2v2.txt`, Trallians at lines 15099-19000); Lightfoot's Christian Ministry (`lightfoot-ministry/lm1901.txt`); the writer's saved copies of the Catechism, Augsburg VII, Article XIX, Westminster 25.2, the 1662 Ordinal, the 1689 Confession and Guy 1964 (`.scratch/`); and the published IEp, ISm, IRo and IMg. `node tools/build.js --check ITr` gives 0 errors and 0 warnings.

## Verdict

The book is careful and honest in tone, its quotations check out, and the translation is mostly close to Lake's printed Greek. The problems are in the text-critical layer. The writer used Lake's nine footnotes and a few of Lightfoot's remarks, but left out several of the most important differences in Lightfoot's apparatus, among them 3:1 (G reads "Son of the Father" where Lake prints "type"), 1:0 (G reads "flesh and blood"), 7:2 (G drops a whole clause) and 12:2. Several variant entries carry wrong or invented witness labels. The progress file says "No departure from Lake", but 1:1 follows the long recension and Lightfoot against Lake's own Greek. The 3:1 "views" note has all five quotations right, but its Orthodox row rests on an encyclopedia fragment and not on any Orthodox document. Two chapter pages say things about the later creeds and about "readings" of 3:1 that no opened source supports. I found no invented source, fragment or reading, so there are no critical findings. The Greek, the Lightfoot and Lake quotations, the Catechism, Augsburg, Article XIX, Westminster 25.2, 1689 26.8, the Ordinal, and the Guy and Ministry quotations all check against opened text.

## Major findings

1. **MAJOR, variants.json (missing entry), 3:1: G reads "Son of the Father", not "type".** Lightfoot's apparatus: `τοὺς διακόνους ὡς ἰησοῦν χριστόν, ὡς καὶ τὸν ἐπίσκοπον ὄντα υἱὸν τοῦ πατρός G; ... existentem filium patris L`. He says "the corrupt υἱὸν of GL must certainly be rejected in favour of τύπον", which is found in S and g. L also reads "as a mandate of Jesus Christ" for "as Jesus Christ" (`ὡς ἐντολὴν`), and Lightfoot explains it as a blend of two readings. He also thinks the second ὡς awkward and floats `ἔντυπον`. This is the verse the whole views note and chapter 3 are built on, and it has no variant entry. Note 12 mentions Roberts and Donaldson's "an appointment of Jesus Christ" only as "a different text". Note 13 says nothing about G. Fix: add a 3:1 variant (τύπον S g Antioch; υἱόν G L; ἐντολήν L for the deacons) and say in note 13 that "type" rests on the Syriac and the long recension against the Greek manuscript.

2. **MAJOR, variants.json (missing entry), 1:0: "flesh and spirit" or "flesh and blood".** Lightfoot: `πνεύματι] g; αἵματι GLA`. He writes "the existing Greek text ἐν σαρκὶ καὶ αἵματι τῷ πάθει ... can hardly stand; and I have thought it best to adopt from the interpolator's text πνεύματι", and he gives the alternative of dropping τῷ πάθει (the Armenian supports it). The digital Lake text prints πνεύματι with no footnote in `app.json`, so the writer's footnote-driven method missed it. The Plain English Translation ("peace in flesh and spirit through the suffering") follows the long recension against the Medicean manuscript. Roberts and Donaldson (in `texts/en`) read "flesh, and blood, and passion". Fix: add the entry and say so in the 1:0 notes or in the `canon` text-section.

3. **MAJOR, plain.txt / notes.json / progress.md, 1:1: "in him I saw your whole congregation" departs from Lake's Greek and the progress file says it does not.** Lake prints `ὥστε με ... ἐν αὐτῷ θεωρεῖσθαι` with the footnote `θεωρεῖσθαι G, θεωρῆσαι g` (both scans). The Greek is passive or middle ("be beheld"). Lightfoot prints θεωρῆσαι ("see") and says "This reading is to be preferred. There seems to be no good authority for the middle θεωρεῖσθαι". The translation "I saw" is the long recension's and Lightfoot's reading. Note 6 prints θεωρεῖσθαι beside "I saw" and says "The long recension reads 'to see' for 'to behold'; the sense is the same", which blurs a real grammatical difference. Progress.md says "No departure from Lake (3:3, 6:2, 7:1, 12:3 follow his printed text)". Fix: add a variant entry for 1:1 and say in note 6 that the translation follows Lightfoot's θεωρῆσαι, or translate θεωρεῖσθαι literally. Correct progress.md.

4. **MAJOR, variants.json (missing entries), 7:2 and 12:2.**
   - 7:2: Lightfoot: `ὁ δὲ ... ὢν οὐ καθαρός ἐστιν] ... non mundus est L; om. G (doubtless owing to homœoteleuton)`. So "whoever is outside the altar is not pure" is absent from the Greek manuscript and comes from the Latin (and g). Lightfoot also corrects `καὶ διακόνου GL` to `διακόνων`, because it needs the plural to go with πρεσβυτερίου (he calls the alternative of ejecting "καὶ διακόνου" as an interpolation possible, since A lacks it). Note 28 treats the sentence as plain Greek.
   - 12:2: G and L read `εἰς τιμὴν πατρός, Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ καὶ τῶν ἀποστόλων`. Lightfoot says that on the Greek manuscript's text one must punctuate "to the honour of the Father of Jesus Christ, and of the Apostles", because "the former connexion would almost necessarily require a connecting particle". g and A add `καὶ εἰς τιμήν` before Ἰησοῦ. The Plain English Translation ("to the honor of the Father, of Jesus Christ and of the apostles") follows Lake's English and the long recension's sense, and gives no hint of the issue.
   Fix: add both entries.

5. **MAJOR, variants.json, 3:3: the witness labels are wrong.**
   - Reading 1 is labelled "Lake's text, restored from the Armenian and the long recension" for `οὐκ εἰς τοῦτο ᾠήθην`. In Lightfoot, A and g restore ἀγαπῶν and συντονώτερον (the first half of the sentence). G reads `εἰς τοῦτο φήθην` with no οὐκ. The `οὐκ` is Lake's own, with no footnote. Lake says only "confused and corrupt in all the authorities".
   - Reading 2 is labelled "Lightfoot's text" but prints his bracketed conjecture `[ἀλλ᾽ οὐχ ἱκανὸν ἑαυτόν]` as text, and drops his `οὕτως` (`ἀγαπῶν ὑμᾶς οὕτως φείδομαι`), which the witness line itself mentions. Lightfoot says of the words in brackets: "I have hazarded a conjecture in accordance with the general sense of A", and "What these were it is impossible to say".
   - Chapter 3 `deep` repeats the first error ("a text mended from the Armenian and the long recension").
   Fix: relabel each reading from Lightfoot's apparatus, mark the bracketed words as his conjecture, and say that Lake's `οὐκ` has no manuscript support.

6. **MAJOR, variants.json, 6:2 (two entries): several errors against Lightfoot.**
   - Reading 4 prints `οἳ καὶ ἰῷ` (relative). Lightfoot's note says "For the construction οἱ (i.e. οἱ αἱρετικοί understood from the preceding αἵρεσις)": it is the article. Lake's printed text and Lake's footnote give no accent change either. Reading 1 prints `οἱ ἑαυτοῖς`. Use `οἱ καὶ ἰῷ`.
   - Reading 2 is labelled "G, with the Latin (L)" for `οἱ καιροί`. That is Lake's "G(L)". Lightfoot's apparatus gives L as `quae et inquinatis implicat`, a further corruption of G (he reconstructs οικαιροιπαρεμπλεκουσιν read as οἱ καὶ ῥυπαρ' ἐμπλέκουσιν). The `b` field says this and contradicts the witness label.
   - Lightfoot also prints `ἀδεῶς` (his own conjecture, "without apprehension") for Lake's ἡδέως, and Dam-Rup has `καὶ παρεμπλέκουσιν`. Neither is given.
   - Second 6:2 entry: Lightfoot's decisive reason for κακῇ is Philadelphians 2 (`ἡδονῇ κακῇ`, quoted in his apparatus), not the Parallels. The "why" says κἀκεῖ and κακῇ "differ by a breathing and a letter", which is the writer's own inference. Lightfoot says G is written `κακεῖ`, and the Greek manuscript's punctuation `ἡδονῇ·` is his.
   Fix: correct the readings and witness lines, add Philadelphians 2 as the reason.

7. **MAJOR, variants.json and notes.json, 7:1: "God Jesus Christ" is presented one-sidedly and the witnesses overstated.** The variant lists "Lake's text (G L g)" for `θεοῦ Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ`. Lightfoot says g "seems however to have been in the text used by the interpolator" and quotes it as `εἶναι ἀχωρίστους θεοῦ ... αἰδεῖσθε δὲ καὶ τὸν ἐπίσκοπον ὑμῶν ὡς χριστόν`: g has θεοῦ and has Christ in another place, so it is not a witness to the full phrase. The note and variant record only that Lightfoot "prints the word in brackets". Lightfoot's actual judgement (p. 168 note) is: "Probably this word should be omitted with the Armenian Version. Though Ignatius frequently speaks of Jesus Christ as God, it may be questioned whether he ever so styles Him without some explanatory or qualifying phrase", and, if kept, it "should perhaps be separated from Χριστοῦ". Note 26 then closes with "Elsewhere Ignatius plainly calls Jesus God", citing Ephesians 18:2, which argues against Lightfoot's doubt without stating it. Fix: state Lightfoot's view and his qualifier point, fix the g label, and say in note 26 what the Greek at Ephesians 18:2 has that 7:1 lacks, or drop the closing sentence.

8. **MAJOR, notes.json 14 (views, 3:1): the Orthodox row is not from an Orthodox document and says almost nothing.** The only source is `The A to Z of the Orthodox Church` (Scarecrow Press, 2010, a reference book). The saved copy (`.scratch/IMg/web/atoz.txt`) is a one-paragraph excerpt from a Russian website (azbyka.ru) ending in "Читать далее", about Christ on the altar and "the importance of the bishop who presides at the altar". It does not discuss 3:1 or the "not called a church" sentence, and the note gives no quotation. This is the works brief's lesson 6: a church row sourced from anything but that church's own document. The five other rows are real quotations from each church's own document, but the Reformed, Lutheran, Baptist and Anglican quotations do not discuss Ignatius or this verse (allowed by the brief if framed as "the question it answers", and the note does say "define the church in other words"). Fix: replace the Orthodox row with a document the Orthodox churches themselves issue (for example the OCA's own life or liturgical text on Ignatius, or a Synodal or patriarchal statement on the church and the bishop), or say plainly that no Orthodox statement was found. Do not paraphrase a secondary source as the church's reading.

9. **MAJOR, chapters.json ch. 3 `readings`, and book.json `sections`: readings of 3:1 are attributed to churches that never read it that way, and Lightfoot is shortened.**
   - "Read as a definition of the church ... the 1662 Ordinal says 'from the Apostles' time ...'" gives the Ordinal as a reading of 3:1. It is a statement about orders. "Read as a word to one church: Ignatius tells the Trallians what a church is called among them; the Reformation confessions define the church ..." puts three confessions under a reading ("among them") that no source supports, and Article XIX (Anglican) falls under both readings. Matched against book.json `sections` ("the Reformation confessions define the church otherwise"), this implies the Protestant documents answer 3:1 by rejecting it, which none of them says.
   - Chapter 3 `views.H`: "Lightfoot granted the force of the language and explained it by 'the needs and distractions of the age'". Ministry p. 87-88 says the language, "if taken literally, would invest the episcopal office" with "the crushing despotism", and that the needs and distractions are "the extenuating fact". The note (14) quotes "extravagant" fairly; the chapter page does not.
   Fix: drop the "word to one church" heading or source it to a named scholar, say which documents discuss Ignatius and which only answer the question, and give Lightfoot's judgement in full.

10. **MAJOR, chapters.json ch. 9 `readings` and `views.C`, notes.json 32: the later creeds.** Chapter 9: "The points come in the order of the later creeds: descent from David, birth, suffering under Pontius Pilate, crucifixion, death, resurrection", and `views.C`: "The churches' creeds keep these points". No creed was opened (sources.json has none), and the brief said "later creeds only from texts opened". The claim is also wrong for the two main creeds: neither the Apostles' nor the Nicene Creed mentions descent from David. Note 32 is more careful ("Later creeds keep several of the same points (Pontius Pilate, crucified, died, rose)") but still has no source. Fix: open the creeds and quote them, or remove all three sentences.

## Minor findings

11. **MINOR, plain.txt 8:1.** "make yourselves new" renders `ἀνακτήσασθε`. Lightfoot: "recover, refresh ... doubtless the right reading", and "If the other verb (ἀνακτίζειν) had been used, the words would have been ἀνακτίσατε". "Make new" is the sense of the rejected ἀνακτίζω (G has `ἀνακτίσασθε`, which Cotelier corrected). Lake's English "be renewed" blurs the two. Suggest "recover yourselves" or "be refreshed", with a variant for G. Chapter 8's summary repeats it.

12. **MINOR, plain.txt 5:2.** "the ranks of the rulers" for `τὰς συστάσεις τὰς ἀρχοντικάς`. Lake has "gatherings"; Lightfoot "assemblages, musterings". "Ranks" is closer to Lightfoot's reading of τοποθεσίας ("dispositions ... in their several ranks"), so it moves a word from one half to the other. The brief itself said "gatherings of the rulers".

13. **MINOR, plain.txt 4:2.** "I do love the thought of suffering" adds "the thought of" (`ἀγαπῶ τὸ παθεῖν`, "I love to suffer"). The topic and chapter text repeat "the thought of suffering".

14. **MINOR, variants (further omissions from Lightfoot's apparatus).** 2:3 `μυστηρίων] g; μυστήριον G` (the versions all have the plural; note 10 builds on "mysteries" without saying the Greek manuscript has the singular). 5:2 `δύναμαι νοεῖν] gS,SA; δυνάμενος GL` (Lake prints Pearson's emendation). 8:2 `τὸ ἐν θεῷ G; τὸ ἔνθεον Dam-Vat` (Lightfoot prints ἔνθεον). 2:1 Lightfoot prints `κατὰ ἀνθρώπους` (plural), Lake `κατὰ ἄνθρωπον`. 4:2 `ὁ διάβολος` added by g and `διάβολος` by Dam-Vat-Rup Anton (relevant to Lake's "the jealousy of the devil" in the note). 6:1 S reads `gratiarum actionis` (εὐχαριστικῇ?) for χριστιανῇ. 13:1 G alone has καὶ before οὐκ. These are lower weight than findings 1-7 but the brief asks for every difference a careful reader should know.

15. **MINOR, variants.json, 12:3 and 13:3.** 12:3: Lightfoot gives `οὗ περίκειμαι` as the reading of "Gg", and L `qua conor potiri`, A "accipere (sortes) ad quas vocatus sum"; the entry gives "Lake's text (G)" only. Lightfoot's note on κλῆρος ("i.e. the glory of martyrdom") is not used in the gloss "the share God has set for him". 13:3: Lightfoot's apparatus puts `ἁγνίζετε ὑμῶν` at the Trallians verse itself as the reading of "GL" (not only in the Ephesians note), and gives g `ἀσπάζεται ὑμᾶς` and A "desiderat erga vos". The entry cites only the Ephesians note.

16. **MINOR, chapters.json ch. 11 `deep` spec box and book.json `spec`.** The "cross as the tree of life" theory is labelled as Claude's, but Lightfoot's note at 11:2 already says "For the Cross is the true ξύλον ζωῆς" (line 561 of the OCR). The statement "Lake's margin gives only Matthew 15:13" is true, but the theory is not new and Lightfoot should be credited. In book.json `spec`, "Neither Lake nor Lightfoot links the passages" is a negative claim that cannot be verified from what was read and should be reworded or dropped.

17. **MINOR, notes.json 2.** "'In the fullness' may mean God's fullness (Lake) or simply 'fully'; the Greek does not say." Lightfoot defends "in the pleroma, the sphere of the Divine graces" and lists "in the fulness of Christian good wishes" (Zahn) among explanations he says "seem to be excluded by the use of the word or by the grammar". The note should say who holds "simply fully" or drop it.

18. **MINOR, glosses.json.** "unwavering in endurance: steady under pressure" is fine; "offshoots: shoots that grow up from the roots, not from the plant itself" is unsourced (Lightfoot's note on `παραφυάδας` is not used); "council of elders: the church's board of elders" is anachronistic and "board" is modern. "the lot: the share God has set for him" drops what Lightfoot says it means (martyrdom).

19. **MINOR, glosses/chapters `readers` and `settings`.** Several `readers` lines are inference presented as fact: ch. 9 "a city ... whom traveling teachers could easily reach", ch. 12 "a man they had met only through Polybius", ch. 3 "Trallians who had seen Polybius's 'very bearing' heard him held up". Lightfoot's remark on Jews at Tralles (Josephus, Ant. 14.10.20) is a better-sourced reason for the warning and is not used.

20. **MINOR, book.json facts "Date".** To the Romans is dated "the 24th of August" in Lake's English (IRo 10:3 says "Lake renders 24 August"); Lightfoot's introduction to Romans says "August 23rd". The fact row follows Lake and does not note the difference. "(as Lake reports it)" on the Chronicle is a named, opened source and acceptable.

21. **MINOR, book.json `canon` `sections` and note 14.** The Reformed row quotes only the membership definition of Westminster 25.2. The next sentence of the confession (25.3, "Unto this catholic visible church Christ hath given the ministry") is the part that bears on offices, and the Augsburg quotation omits that VII goes on to say agreement on the gospel and sacraments is enough for the church's unity. Both are in the saved copies. Not a ranking, but the Reformed and Lutheran positions would be shown more fairly with these.

22. **MINOR, texts.** `texts/orig/ITr.json` 12:1 has `οἲ` (U+1F32, smooth breathing and grave), a slip for `οἱ` (Lake's printed relative, masculine because "churches" stands for "men", as Lake's "men who"). `texts/` is generated and not for the writer to edit, but the digital file has other unfixed slips (3:3 ὤν, the second ὤν of 7:2). The content does not quote 12:1 in Greek; `grep οἲ` over the book is empty.
