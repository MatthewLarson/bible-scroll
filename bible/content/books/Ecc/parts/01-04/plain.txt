## Vapor of vapors
1:1 The words of Qohelet, son of David, king in Jerusalem.
1:2 "Vapor of vapors," says Qohelet, "vapor of vapors! Everything is vapor."
1:3 What gain is there for a person in all his toil that he toils under the sun?
## Nothing new under the sun
1:4 A generation goes and a generation comes, / and the earth stands forever.
1:5 The sun rises and the sun goes down, / and it pants its way back to its place, / where it rises once more.
1:6 It goes to the south / and turns around to the north. / Turning, turning goes the wind, / and back on its rounds the wind returns.
1:7 All the streams go to the sea, / and still the sea is not full. / To the place where the streams go, / there they go back to flow again.
1:8 All words are weary; / no one can say it all. / No eye has its fill of seeing, / no ear is filled up with hearing.
1:9 Whatever has been, that is what will be; / whatever has been done, that is what will be done. / Nothing at all is new under the sun.
1:10 Is there a thing of which someone says, / "Look, this is new"? / It already was, long ages ago, / before our time.
1:11 There is no memory of the earlier ones, / and of the later ones still to come / there will be no memory either / among those who come later still.
## The king sets out to search
1:12 I, Qohelet, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
1:13 I put my mind to seeking and exploring, with wisdom, everything that is done under the heavens. It is an evil task, which God has given human beings to busy themselves with.
1:14 I have looked at all the things that are done under the sun. And see: everything is vapor and chasing the wind.
1:15 The bent cannot be set straight, / and what is not there cannot be counted.
1:16 I talked it over with myself. "Look," I said, "I have become great, and I have gained wisdom beyond all who were over Jerusalem before me. My mind has seen a great deal of wisdom and knowledge."
1:17 So I set myself to learn what wisdom is, and to learn what madness and folly are. I learned that this too is chasing the wind.
1:18 For where wisdom is great, frustration is great, / and the one who adds to knowledge adds to pain.
## The test of pleasure
2:1 I thought to myself, "Come now, let me test you with joy. See what is good!" And look, this too was vapor.
2:2 About laughter I said, "It is madness," and about joy, "What does this accomplish?"
2:3 I explored in my mind how to indulge my body with wine, while my mind kept steering by wisdom, and how to take hold of folly. I wanted to see what is good for human beings to do under the heavens during the numbered days of their life.
2:4 I made my works great. I built myself houses; I planted myself vineyards.
2:5 I made gardens and parks for myself, and in them I planted trees that bear every kind of fruit.
2:6 I made pools of water for myself, to water a forest where young trees were springing up.
2:7 I acquired slaves, men and women, and others were born in my household and belonged to me. I also had livestock, cattle and sheep, in great numbers, a larger number than anyone in Jerusalem before me had.
2:8 I also gathered silver and gold for myself, and the treasure of kings and of the provinces. I got myself men and women who sang, and the delights of human beings: a woman, and many women.
2:9 I became great, and I kept adding, beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem. Through it all my wisdom stood by me.
2:10 I did not refuse my eyes anything they asked for. I held my heart back from no joy, since my heart took joy in all my toil. This was my share from all my toil.
2:11 Then I turned and looked at all the works my hands had done, and at the toil I had toiled to do them. And see: everything was vapor and chasing the wind. There was no gain under the sun.
## The wise and the fool die alike
2:12 Then I turned to look at wisdom, and at madness and folly. For what of the person who comes after the king? It is what people have already done.
2:13 And I saw that wisdom gives a gain over folly, like the gain that light gives over darkness.
2:14 The wise man's eyes are in his head, and the fool goes about in the dark. Yet I also came to know that one fate meets every one of them.
2:15 Then I thought to myself, "The fool's fate will meet me too. Why then have I been so very wise?" And I told myself that this too is vapor.
2:16 For the wise man is not remembered forever, any more than the fool. The days are coming when all of it will already be forgotten. Oh, how the wise man dies along with the fool!
2:17 So I hated life. What is done under the sun seemed evil to me, for everything is vapor and chasing the wind.
## Toil left to another
2:18 I hated all the toil I had toiled at under the sun. I will have to leave it to whoever comes after me.
2:19 And who knows if he will be a wise man or a fool? Yet he will rule over all my toil, the toil and the wisdom I spent under the sun. This too is vapor.
2:20 So I turned around and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil I had toiled at under the sun.
2:21 There may be a person whose toil is done with wisdom, knowledge and skill, and he has to hand over his share to someone who never toiled for it. This too is vapor, and a great evil.
2:22 For what comes to a person from all his toil, and from the chasing of his heart, that he toils at under the sun?
2:23 All his days are pains, and his task is frustration; even at night his heart does not lie down. This too is vapor.
2:24 There is nothing good for a person but to eat and drink and let himself see good in his toil. I saw that this too comes from God's hand.
2:25 For who can eat, and who can enjoy, apart from me?
2:26 To the person who is good in his eyes, God gives wisdom, knowledge and joy. But to the sinner he gives the task of gathering and piling up, so as to give it to the one who is good in God's eyes. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.
## A time for everything
3:1 Everything has its season, / and every matter under the heavens has its time:
3:2 a time to give birth and a time to die, / a time to plant and a time to tear up what was planted,
3:3 a time to kill and a time to heal, / a time to pull down and a time to build,
3:4 a time to weep and a time to laugh, / a time to wail and a time to dance,
3:5 a time to throw stones away and a time to gather stones, / a time to embrace and a time to stay far from embracing,
3:6 a time to search and a time to give up as lost, / a time to keep and a time to throw away,
3:7 a time to tear and a time to sew, / a time to keep quiet and a time to speak,
3:8 a time to love and a time to hate, / a time of war and a time of peace.
## What God has given
3:9 What gain is there for the one who does the work, in what he toils at?
3:10 I have seen the task that God has given human beings to busy themselves with.
3:11 Everything he has made beautiful in its time. He has also put forever in their hearts, yet no human can find out the work God has done, from the beginning to the end.
3:12 I know that there is nothing good for them except to be glad and to do good in their life.
3:13 And also, when anyone eats and drinks and sees good in all his toil, it is God's gift.
3:14 I know that all that God does will be forever. There is no adding to it and no taking away from it. God has acted so that people will fear him.
3:15 What is has already been, and what is to be has already been. And God seeks out what is pursued.
## Humans and animals
3:16 I saw this too under the sun: wickedness was there in the place of judgment, and wickedness was there in the place of righteousness.
3:17 I thought to myself, "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for every matter has its time, and so does every deed, there."
3:18 I thought to myself about human beings, "This is so that God may test them, and so that they may see that on their own they are animals."
3:19 For human beings have a fate, and animals have a fate, and they share one fate. The death of the one is like the death of the other. All of them have one breath. Humans have no edge over animals, for everything is vapor.
3:20 Everything goes to one place. Everything came from the dust, and everything goes back to the dust.
3:21 Who knows whether the spirit of human beings goes up above, and whether the spirit of animals goes down below to the earth?
3:22 And I saw that nothing is better than this: a person should be glad in what he does, since that is his share. For who will bring him to look at what will come after him?
## The oppressed and the envious
4:1 Then I turned and saw all the acts of oppression that are done under the sun. I saw the tears of the oppressed, with no one to comfort them. Their oppressors had power in their hands, and still the oppressed had no one to comfort them.
4:2 And I counted the dead happy, those who died long ago, above the living who are alive still.
4:3 Better off than both of them is the one who has never yet existed, who has not seen the evil that is done under the sun.
4:4 I looked at all toil and all the skill that goes into work, and saw that it is rivalry between a person and his neighbor. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.
4:5 The fool sits with folded hands / and eats up his own flesh.
4:6 One hand filled with rest is better / than two fists full of toil and chasing the wind.
## One alone, or two
4:7 Then I turned and saw vapor under the sun:
4:8 There is one person, with no second: he has no son and no brother. Yet there is no end to all his toil, and his eye never has its fill of riches. "For whom am I toiling," he asks, "and keeping myself from good things?" This too is vapor, and an evil task.
4:9 Two are better than one, since together they get a good return for their toil.
4:10 If one of them falls, the other can help his friend up. But pity the one who falls with no second person to help him up.
4:11 And if two lie down side by side, there is warmth for them. But for one by himself, how will he get warm?
4:12 Someone may overpower the one who is alone, but two will stand up to him. And a thread twisted three times does not snap quickly.
## The poor young man and the old king
4:13 Better a young man who is poor and wise than a king who is old and a fool and no longer knows how to take a warning.
4:14 For he came out of the prison house to reign, though in his kingdom he had been born poor.
4:15 I saw all the living, everyone walking about under the sun, on the side of the young man, the second one, who would stand up in his place.
4:16 There was no end to all the people, to all whom he led. Yet those who come later will not be glad in him. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.
