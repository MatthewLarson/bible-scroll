## One fate for all
9:1 I laid all this on my heart and set out to make it all clear: that the righteous and the wise, with all they do, are in God's hand. Love, or hate: a human being knows neither. Everything lies ahead of them.
9:2 Everything goes alike for everyone. One fate comes to the righteous and to the wicked, to the good, to the clean and to the unclean, to the one who offers sacrifices and to the one who does not. As it goes for the good, so it goes for the sinner. As it goes for the one who swears oaths, so it goes for the one who is afraid of an oath.
9:3 Here is an evil in everything that is done under the sun: one fate comes to everyone. And the hearts of human beings are full of evil, and madness fills their hearts all their lives. Then it is off to the dead.
9:4 Whoever is joined to the company of the living has something to trust in. It is better to be a living dog than a dead lion.
9:5 Yes, the living know that they will die, but the dead do not know a thing. There are no more wages for them, since their memory is forgotten.
9:6 Their love, their hate, their jealousy: all of it has already perished. And they have no share any longer, forever, in anything that is done under the sun.
## Eat your bread in joy
9:7 Come, eat your bread in joy, and with a good heart drink your wine, for what you do has already pleased God.
9:8 Let your clothes be white all the time, and do not let your head go without oil.
9:9 See life with a woman you love, all the days of your vapor of a life, given you under the sun, all your days of vapor. For that is your share in life and in your toil that you toil under the sun.
9:10 Whatever is in your power to do, do it with your strength. For there is no doing and no reckoning, no knowledge and no wisdom in Sheol, the place where you are going.
## Time and chance
9:11 Once more I saw under the sun that the race does not belong to the swift, or the battle to the warriors. Bread does not go to the wise, or wealth to the discerning, or favor to those who know. For time and chance meet them all.
9:12 For people do not even know their time. Like fish caught fast in an evil net, like birds held in a trap, so human beings are snared by an evil time, when it drops on them without warning.
## The poor wise man
9:13 This also I have seen under the sun, a piece of wisdom that struck me as great.
9:14 There was a small town with few men inside. A great king came to it. He ringed it in and piled up great siege works against it.
9:15 A poor man who was wise was found in it, and by his wisdom he rescued the town. But not a soul remembered that poor man.
9:16 For my part I said, "Wisdom is better than power." But a poor man's wisdom is looked down on, and no one listens to his words.
9:17 The words of the wise, spoken calmly, get a better hearing / than a ruler's shouting among fools.
9:18 Wisdom is better than weapons of battle, / but one sinner can wreck a great deal of good.
## A little folly
10:1 Dead flies turn a perfumer's oil foul and frothy; / a little folly weighs more than wisdom, more than honor.
10:2 The wise man's heart goes toward his right hand, / the fool's heart toward his left.
10:3 Even on the road, as the fool walks along, he is senseless, / and he tells everyone he is a fool.
10:4 If the ruler's spirit rises up against you, / do not let go of your place, / for calm lets great wrongs go.
## Folly on high
10:5 There is an evil I have seen under the sun, like a mistake that comes out from the one in power:
10:6 folly is placed on many a high seat, while the rich sit down in low ones.
10:7 I have seen slaves up on horses, and princes on the ground, walking as slaves walk.
## Every task has its risk
10:8 The one who digs a pit falls into it, / and the one who breaks through a wall, a snake bites him.
10:9 The one who digs out stones gets hurt by them; / the one who splits wood is put at risk by it.
10:10 If the iron is blunt / and he has not whetted its face, / he will have to put more muscle into it; / but wisdom's gain is that it brings success.
10:11 If the snake bites when no spell has been whispered, / there is no gain for the master of the tongue.
## The fool's many words
10:12 Words from a wise mouth are grace, / but a fool's own lips gulp him down.
10:13 The first words from his mouth are folly, / and his last are evil madness.
10:14 Yet the fool piles up words. / No one knows what is going to happen, / and what will come after him, who can tell him?
10:15 Fools' toil wears them out, / for none of them knows how to get to town.
## Kings and princes
10:16 Woe to you, land! Your king is a lad, / and your princes eat at daybreak.
10:17 Happy are you, land! Your king is a son of free men, / and your princes eat at the right time, / to be strong and not to be drunk.
10:18 With laziness upon laziness the rafters sink, / and when hands hang low, the house drips.
10:19 People make a meal for laughter, / and wine makes life glad, / and silver answers for it all.
10:20 Not even in your thoughts should you curse a king, / and not even in your bedroom curse someone rich, / for a bird of the sky will carry off the sound, / and a master of wings will tell the tale.
## Send out your bread
11:1 Send your bread out over the surface of the water, / for when many days have passed, you will find it.
11:2 Give a share to seven, and even to eight, / for you do not know what evil is coming on the earth.
11:3 If the clouds fill with rain, / they empty it out on the earth; / and if a tree falls, in the south or in the north, / the place where the tree falls is where it will be.
11:4 Whoever keeps an eye on the wind will not sow, / and whoever stares at the clouds will not reap.
11:5 You do not know which way the wind goes, and it is the same with the bones in the womb of a pregnant woman. So too you do not know what God does, the God who makes everything.
11:6 Plant your seed at dawn, / and when evening comes, do not let your hand rest, / for you cannot tell which will do well, this one or that, / or whether both will turn out good together.
## Light is sweet
11:7 Light is sweet, and seeing the sun is good for the eyes.
11:8 If a person lives a great many years, let him be glad in all of them. But let him keep in mind the days of darkness, because they will be many. All that comes is vapor.
11:9 Young man, be glad while you are young. Let your heart do you good in the days of your prime. Walk on in your heart's ways and in what your eyes see. But know this: for all of it God will bring you into judgment.
11:10 Put frustration away from your heart, and send trouble away from your flesh, for boyhood and black hair are vapor.
## Before the silver cord is torn
12:1 Remember your Creator in the days of your prime, / before the days of evil come / and the years arrive when you will say, / "There is no delight for me in them";
12:2 before the sun goes dark, and the light, / and the moon and the stars, / and the clouds come back after the rain;
12:3 on the day that the guards of the house shake, / and the men of strength are bent, / and the women grinding stop because they are few, / and the women who look out through the lattice windows grow dark;
12:4 when the double doors onto the street are shut, / and the sound of the mill drops low, / and one wakes up at a bird's call, / and all the daughters of song sink down;
12:5 when heights frighten them, / and terrors wait on the road, / and the almond tree is in bloom, / and the locust is weighed down, / and the caper berry fails; / for the human is on his way to his house of forever, / and the mourners circle about in the street;
12:6 before the cord of silver is torn, / and the bowl of gold is crushed, / and the jar breaks at the spring, / and the wheel is crushed at the cistern,
12:7 and the dust goes back to the earth as it was, / and the spirit goes back to God, who gave it.
## Vapor of vapors
12:8 "Vapor of vapors," says Qohelet, "everything is vapor."
## The last word
12:9 Beyond this: Qohelet was wise, and he kept on teaching knowledge to the people. He weighed, he searched, he put many proverbs in order.
12:10 Qohelet tried to find words that delight, and what was written was upright, words of truth.
12:11 The words of the wise are like ox goads, and like nails driven deep are the masters of collections. They were given by a single shepherd.
12:12 And beyond these, my son, take warning: making many books has no end, and much study wears out the flesh.
12:13 The last word, now that everything has been heard: Fear God and keep his commands, because this is the whole of being human.
12:14 For God will bring everything that is done into the judgment over all that is hidden, whether good or evil.
