# Hostile review: 1 Thessalonians (1Th), whole book (flat folder)

Reviewer: separate hostile reviewer, 2026-10-29. `node tools/build.js --check 1Th` gives 0 errors, 0 warnings.
Lead decisions not treated as findings: 3:13 "his holy ones"; 1:1 to be aligned with 2 Thessalonians' greeting.

## Verdict

This is a careful, mostly honest book. The Plain follows the Greek closely and is clearly built from the literal line. I found no invented source, reading or manuscript. The 2:14-16 treatment is fair in spirit: it gives the interpolation case, the replies, the Gilliard comma argument, the pagan-slur background and the later misuse, and it labels Claude's theory. The facts I could check (the 2:7 witnesses, the Pearson/Schmidt history, Nostra Aetate, Weima/Bammel/White, Chrysostom's quotes on 4:4 and 4:17, Augustine's Letter 130, the 128 BC and 41 BC dates, the DTS quotes, Tacitus Hist. 5.5, the rapture proponents named) hold up. The weak points are these. (1) At 2:16 the Plain turns a Greek purpose/result construction into "result" without saying so, in the one verse where that choice matters most. (2) Several tradition claims on the rapture, intermediate-state and gifts cards are uncited, uneven or only sourced to Wikipedia or an unverifiable leaflet. (3) Some phrases in 5:8, 5:10, 5:11, 5:23 and 4:1 sit too close to the NIV. (4) Witness lists and several scholarly names rest on Wikipedia alone. (5) A few absolute or unsourced statements need support or softening. There are no critical findings.

Count: 0 critical, 6 major, 14 minor.

## Findings

### Major

**1. Major. chapter 2 / plain.txt 2:16 and notes 2:16. "The result is that they keep filling up the measure of their sins."**
Greek: `εἰς τὸ ἀναπληρῶσαι αὐτῶν τὰς ἁμαρτίας πάντοτε`. `εἰς τὸ` + infinitive can express purpose or result, and most translations and commentators read purpose ("so as always to fill up"). The Plain chooses "result" with no note. It also renders `πάντοτε` ("always") as "keep ... -ing", which loses the force. This is the harshest clause of the harshest passage. Choosing the milder grammar silently is a softening, and the opposite choice would be a sharpening. The note on 2:16 "filling up the measure of their sins" explains only the image, not the grammar. The fix: either render so that both stay open (for example "so that they always fill up the measure of their sins" with a note giving purpose versus result and who reads which) or keep "result" and add a note stating that the Greek construction allows purpose and that most translations take it as purpose. Check the same choice against 2 Thessalonians and Romans for consistency of the construction. Note: `εἰς τέλος` ("in the end") is handled well in note 36.

**2. Major. chapters.json ch.4 `traditions[0]` (rapture card). Uncited and uneven tradition claims.**
(a) The Catholic entry's last sentence ("Being caught up to meet the Lord is part of that one coming") has no source; the cited Catechism 675 and 677 speak of the final trial and Passover, not of 4:17. (b) The Reformed entry's last sentence ("Reformed teachers read 4:17 as the gathering of the church at that coming") and the Lutheran entry's gloss are uncited claims about what those traditions say about this verse; the confessions quoted say Christ comes once but do not mention 4:17. Under the project rule, each needs a source that actually addresses the verse, or the sentence must go. (c) The Orthodox entry rests on "Dimitri Cozby, The Rapture, Missionary Leaflet E47 (Holy Trinity Orthodox Mission, 2001)" (sources.json `cozby-rapture`). I could find Fr. Dimitri Cozby's articles on the rapture (an OCA priest, died 2019) but could NOT verify the leaflet number E47, the publisher "Holy Trinity Orthodox Mission" or the year 2001. Unverifiable bibliographic detail must be corrected to what a source shows or removed. A priest's article is also not a church statement; say so ("an OCA priest's article"), or add a liturgical or conciliar source. (d) The card has no entry labelled amillennial or postmillennial, though the brief called for them; the churches' "one return" readings are in effect amillennial, and the reader should be told so. (e) Three of eight slots (Dispensationalist, Pentecostal, Evangelical) are detailed and the confessional churches get one or two lines each; the lengths are not even. (f) The Pentecostal entry generalizes the Assemblies of God alone to "Pentecostal". AG article 14 says the rapture is "followed by" Christ's visible return and millennial reign; the card's phrase "comes before Christ's visible return" is fair, but the label should be "Assemblies of God (Pentecostal)" or other Pentecostal bodies should be cited. (g) The Baptist entry says "many American Baptists" hold pretribulation views, sourced only to the Wikipedia "Rapture" page; "American Baptists" also names a specific denomination (ABCUSA) whose view is not shown. Use "many Baptists in the United States" and a real source or cut it.

**3. Major. chapters.json ch.2 deep dive "1 Thessalonians 2:13-16" and variants.json 2:15 `b`. Absolute and secondhand claims.**
(a) "No manuscript, version or early writer lacks the verses." The manuscript half is the standard claim and Wikipedia's article does not contradict it, but "early writer" is a universal negative nobody checked (Marcion's text of 1 Thessalonians, for example, is known only through Tertullian and Epiphanius). Under STYLE rule 2 it must be verified in an opened source or reduced to "no known manuscript or version". (b) "Recent surveys (Johnson 2016, Gupta 2019, Brookins 2021) report that most scholars in the last thirty years accept the verses as Paul's." The Wikipedia article (the only source, `wiki-1th`) names Johnson, Gupta, Brookins and Jensen. In my search Jensen's 2019 review (Currents in Biblical Research 18.1) was the one clearly confirmed article, and I could not confirm "Gupta 2019". Johnson 2016 and Brookins 2021 appeared only in a Wikipedia mirror's reference list, with no stated position. Replace the three years with what a source shows, and cite Jensen's review directly (title, journal, year) as the survey. (c) The sentence "Since the middle of the 1800s some scholars have argued that these verses are not Paul's" (F. C. Baur is named in book.json authorship) has no source key. (d) The replies list leaves out the argument most often made for authenticity, that the passage fits the letter's own setting (suffering from countrymen, 2:14; Paul's earlier "wrath" language in 1:10) and that Paul never says "the Jews" elsewhere as a blanket term but does so in Galatians 1:13-14 about his own past. At least note that the reply "Paul's other harsh words" (Gal 5:12, Phil 3:2) are addressed to different targets. This is a request to strengthen the replies, which are now thinner than the case.

**4. Major. chapters.json ch.4 `traditions[1]` (intermediate state) and ch.5 `traditions[0]` (gifts). Entries do not answer the card's own question.**
The card asks whether the dead in Christ are conscious with the Lord before the resurrection. The Catholic entry (purgatory and prayer) and the Orthodox entry (prayer for the departed) never say whether the dead are conscious; only the Reformed and Adventist entries answer. The Catholic Church does teach that the soul is with God or in purification after death, and the Orthodox have their own account; the entries should state it from a source (Catechism 1021-1022 is the obvious pointer, but I did not open it). Lutheran, Baptist and Anglican views are absent, though the Lutheran confessions and Lutheran theologians are known to differ from the Reformed on this (do not assert from memory; find a source or leave the church out and say the card shows a selection). On the gifts card the Pentecostal entry (baptism in the Spirit, tongues) does not address 5:19-21 (testing prophecy) or "who tests them"; only the Catholic entry does. Orthodox, Lutheran and Baptist are missing. Add entries or add one line saying the card shows only the views for which a source was opened.

**5. Major. plain.txt 5:11, 5:8, 5:10, 5:23, 4:1. Drift toward the NIV (checked in my head only, not copied).**
- 5:11: the whole clause "build each other up, just as you are in fact doing" follows the NIV's wording and clause order almost exactly, with only "comfort" for the NIV's first verb. The Greek (`οἰκοδομεῖτε εἷς τὸν ἕνα, καθὼς καὶ ποιεῖτε`) allows many other renderings.
- 5:8: "put on faith and love as a breastplate, and the hope of salvation as a helmet" matches the NIV's construction; the Greek has a noun phrase "the breastplate of faith and love", which the NIV restructured the same way.
- 5:10: "He died for us so that, awake or asleep, we may live along with him" has the NIV's opening and shape.
- 5:23: "through and through" for `ὁλοτελεῖς` is the NIV's phrase.
- 4:1 "you are in fact walking that way" with 5:11 "in fact doing": "in fact" for `καὶ` in `καθὼς καὶ περιπατεῖτε` is an NIV habit, used twice.
Rebuild at least 5:11, 5:8 and 5:10 from the literal line (`node tools/literal.js 1Th 5:8-11`) with different structure, and replace "through and through" (for example "completely" or "in every part").

**6. Major. variants.json 2:7 `why` and `b`; book.json "The text". Overstated or unsupported claims in the headline variant.**
(a) The `why` says "Infants" has "the oldest and widest support". The Byzantine majority, Alexandrinus, a corrector of Sinaiticus and many minuscules (the "gentle" list) are numerically wider; "widest" needs to be "earliest, and spread across Alexandrian, Western and Coptic witnesses" if that is what is meant. (b) The witness list is taken wholly from Wikipedia and the NET note, which the entry admits. I compared it to Wikipedia: P65, Sinaiticus first hand (א*), B, C*, G and I(Ψ*), plus it, vg, cop, eth are listed for "infants"; the gentle list matches too, so the entry is faithful to its source. But Wikipedia is not an apparatus, and its own 3:2 line is garbled (it lists "Byz" under the plain "co-worker of God" reading). Cross-check the 2:7, 3:2 and 3:9 lists against the NA28 apparatus or Metzger's Textual Commentary and cite that, or add to the `b` that the lists are secondhand. (c) STEP's own data shows NA27, NA28 and Westcott-Hort print "infants" and Tyndale House, SBL and Tregelles print "gentle"; the brief's wording "NA28 changed to infants" is not supported by this data (NA27 already printed it). Do not repeat it. (d) book.json "The text" names Papyrus 46 and 65 as the oldest copies; Papyrus 30 (3rd century, which as I recall carries parts of 4:12-5:28) is not mentioned. I did not verify P30's contents this session, so either confirm and add it or say "among the oldest".

### Minor

**7. Minor. variants.json 3:9 witnesses.** Wikipedia's list for "to the Lord" is א*, D*, F, G, a, b, vg, bo. The entry omits F (Augiensis) and the Bohairic. It names "some Latin witnesses" only. Add them or say the list is partial.

**8. **9. Minor. notes 5:17, Augustine.** The note says Augustine "praised the monks of Egypt for 'very frequent prayers, but these very brief'." In the New Advent text of Letter 130 (chapter 10, section 20) the words are "The brethren in Egypt are reported to have very frequent prayers, but these very brief", with no praise and "brethren", not "monks". The "desire without intermission" quote is in chapter 9, section 18 and is accurate. Use "reports that the brethren in Egypt" and quote with the translation named. The date "AD 412" I did not verify.

**10. Minor. plain.txt 4:4.** `εἰδέναι ἕκαστον ὑμῶν ... κτᾶσθαι` is "know how to gain control of", but the Plain says "learn how". "Know" is the Greek verb and fits Paul's "as you know" reminders. Change to "know how".

**11. Minor. plain.txt 5:3 and the notes.** The Plain says "Peace and safety" (Greek `ἀσφάλεια`) but the note, topics, the imperial-cult contribution and Weima and White all discuss "peace and security". Add a gloss ("safety, security") so the reader can link the Plain to the debate.

**12. Minor. notes 4:17 (apantesis), chapters.json ch.4 deep dive.** The note says Cosby (1994) and Plevnik (1999) "argued that the word was not a technical term and that the passage shows believers taken to be with the Lord". The Cosby abstract says the term is not technical, the main elements of the Hellenistic receptions are missing, and the details come from Christian pictures of the parousia; "believers taken to be with the Lord" is Plevnik's emphasis (the faithful are brought in, not the Lord). Separate the two. Gundry's 1996 reply in the same journal (the parallels are closer than Cosby allows) is the main reply and is not named; add it so the debate is two-sided.

**13. Minor. notes 5:23 and chapters.json ch.5 deep dive, Catechism 367.** Both say the Catechism "says Paul's distinction ... does not introduce a duality". As I recall the text (not opened here), the Catechism says that "the Church teaches that this distinction does not introduce a duality into the soul", i.e. the Church's teaching about the distinction, not Paul's. Verify against the Catechism text and correct the attribution. Berkhof's "do not prove the point" is confirmed by search results (Manual of Christian Doctrine).

**15. Minor. Gallio and the dating (chapters.json ch.1 deep dive; book.json facts).** The deep dive says the Delphi letter "places Gallio's term about AD 51-52". The step from this to "the letter was probably written about AD 50" is not explained (Paul's Gallio hearing came part way through the 18 months, so the letter would be earlier). **20.
