## Greeting
1:1 From Paul, a slave of Christ Jesus, called as an apostle and set apart for the good news of God.
1:2 God promised this good news ahead of time through his prophets, in the holy Scriptures.
1:3 It is about his Son, who, according to the flesh, came from the offspring of David,
1:4 and who, according to the Spirit of holiness, was marked out as Son of God with power by his resurrection from the dead: Jesus Christ our Lord.
1:5 Through him we received grace and the work of an apostle, to bring about the obedience of faith among all the Gentiles, for the sake of his name.
1:6 You also are among them, called as Jesus Christ's own people.
1:7 To all God's loved ones in Rome, called to be saints. Grace to you and peace from God our Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ.
## Paul's thanks and plans
1:8 To begin with, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for every one of you, because people are talking about your faith in the whole world.
1:9 God, whom I serve with my spirit in the good news of his Son, is my witness that I never stop mentioning you.
1:10 In my prayers I always ask that somehow, now at last, I may succeed in coming to you, if God wills it.
1:11 For I long to see you, so that I may share some spiritual gift with you to make you strong.
1:12 That is, so that among you I may be encouraged together with you, each of us by the other's faith, yours and mine.
1:13 Brothers, I want you to know that I have often intended to visit you, but until now something has stopped me. I wanted to gather some fruit among you too, as I have among the other Gentiles.
1:14 I am in debt both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to the wise and to the foolish.
1:15 So, for my part, I am eager to tell the good news to you in Rome as well.
## The good news
1:16 For I am not ashamed of the good news. It is God's power for salvation for everyone who believes, first for the Jew and for the Greek as well.
1:17 In it God's righteousness is revealed, from faith to faith, as it is written: "The righteous will live by faith."
## God's anger against humanity
1:18 For God's anger is revealed from heaven against all the ungodliness and unrighteousness of people who hold down the truth by their unrighteousness.
1:19 What can be known about God is plain among them, because God has made it plain to them.
1:20 From the world's beginning, his unseen qualities, his everlasting power and divine nature, are visible to the mind in what he has made. This leaves them with no defense.
1:21 They knew God, yet they did not praise him as God, and they were not thankful. Instead their reasoning came to nothing, and their senseless hearts were darkened.
1:22 They said they were wise, and became fools.
1:23 They traded the glory of the immortal God for a likeness: an image of mortal man, of birds, of four-footed animals and of crawling things.
1:24 So, in the desires of their hearts, God handed them over to impurity, so that their bodies were dishonored among themselves.
1:25 They swapped the truth of God for the lie. They revered and served the created thing rather than the One who created it. He is blessed forever. Amen.
1:26 That is why God handed them over to shameful passions. Their women changed the natural use for one that goes against nature.
1:27 In the same way the men gave up the natural use of the woman and burned in their desire for one another. Men did shameful things with men, and received in themselves the payment their error deserved.
1:28 And because they did not think it worth keeping God in their knowledge, God handed them over to a mind that fails the test, to do what is not fitting.
1:29 Every kind of unrighteousness, wickedness, greed and evil fills them. They are loaded with envy, murder, strife, deceit and spite. They whisper behind backs,
1:30 they slander, they hate God, they are insolent, proud and boastful. They invent new kinds of evil, and they disobey their parents.
1:31 They have no sense, they break their promises, they have no family love, they have no mercy.
1:32 They know God's righteous decree: people who practice such things deserve death. Even so, they do these things themselves, and they also applaud others who do them.
## God judges without favorites
2:1 So you have no defense, whoever you are, you who sit in judgment. Another person is the one you judge, but you condemn yourself, because you who judge do the very same things.
2:2 We know that God's judgment on those who practice such things is according to the truth.
2:3 You who judge people who practice such things, and do the same yourself: do you suppose you will escape God's judgment?
2:4 Or do you make light of the riches of his kindness, his forbearance and his patience? Do you not realize that God's kindness is leading you to repentance?
2:5 You are treasuring up anger for yourself, because of your hardness and your heart that will not repent. It is for the day of anger, when God's righteous judgment is revealed.
2:6 He will pay back each person according to his works.
2:7 To those who patiently do good works and seek glory, honor and immortality, he will give eternal life.
2:8 But for those who are self-seeking, who disobey the truth and let unrighteousness win them over, there will be anger and fury.
2:9 There will be trouble and distress for every human soul that does evil, for the Jew first and for the Greek as well.
2:10 But there will be glory, honor and peace for everyone who does good, for the Jew first and for the Greek as well.
2:11 For with God there are no favorites.
2:12 Those who sinned without the law will also perish outside the law, and those who sinned inside the law will be judged through the law.
2:13 Hearing the law does not make people righteous with God. It is the doers of the law who will be justified.
2:14 Gentiles do not have the law. But when they do by nature what the law contains, they are a law for themselves, though they lack the law.
2:15 These people display the work of the law, written in their hearts. Their conscience testifies with it, and their reasonings, one against another, either accuse them or defend them.
2:16 This will happen on the day when God judges what people keep secret, through Christ Jesus, as my good news says.
## The Jew and the law
2:17 Suppose you are called a Jew. You rest on the law, and you boast in God.
2:18 You know his will, and you can test what matters, because the law has taught you.
2:19 You are convinced that you are a guide to the blind, a light to people in darkness,
2:20 a trainer of the foolish, a teacher of infants, because in the law you have the very form of knowledge and truth.
2:21 Well then, you teach someone else. Do you fail to teach yourself? You announce, "Do not steal." Do you steal?
2:22 You say, "Do not commit adultery." Do you commit adultery? You detest idols. Do you rob temples?
2:23 You boast in the law. Do you dishonor God by breaking the law?
2:24 For, as it is written, "The name of God is insulted among the Gentiles because of you."
2:25 Circumcision is of value if you do the law. But if you are a lawbreaker, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.
2:26 So if the uncircumcised keep the requirements of the law, will his uncircumcision not count as circumcision?
2:27 And the man who is uncircumcised by nature, yet fulfills the law, will judge you, who with the written letter and circumcision are a lawbreaker.
2:28 For a person is not a Jew who is one on the outside, and real circumcision is not the outward mark in the flesh.
2:29 No, a Jew is one on the inside, and circumcision is of the heart, in spirit and not in letter. His praise comes not from human beings but from God.
## The Jew's advantage
3:1 Then what is the Jew's advantage? Or what good is circumcision?
3:2 Much, in every way. First of all, they were put in charge of the oracles of God.
3:3 What of it, if some disbelieved? Will their unbelief make God's faithfulness useless?
3:4 By no means! Let God be true, and every human being a liar, as it is written: "So that you may be proved right in your words, and win your case when you are judged."
3:5 But if our injustice shows God's righteousness, what are we to say? Is God unjust when he brings his anger down? I am speaking as people do.
3:6 By no means! Otherwise, how will God judge the world?
3:7 But if God's truth overflowed to his glory through my lie, why am I still judged as a sinner?
3:8 And why not say, as we are slandered for saying and as some claim we say, "Let us do evil so that good may come"? The condemnation of such people is just.
## No one is righteous
3:9 What then? Are we ahead? No, not in any way. For we have already made the charge against Jews and Greeks alike: all of them are under sin.
3:10 As it is written: "No one is righteous. Not a single one.
3:11 No one has understanding. No one searches for God.
3:12 All have swerved aside. All together they have become useless. No one does good; there is not even one."
3:13 "Their throat is a grave lying open; their tongues have been busy with deceit." "The venom of snakes is under their lips."
3:14 "Cursing and bitterness fill their mouths."
3:15 "Their feet run fast to spill blood.
3:16 Wreckage and misery are on their roads,
3:17 and the way of peace they never knew."
3:18 "There is no fear of God in front of their eyes."
3:19 We know that everything the law says is spoken to those who are inside the law. The purpose is to close every mouth and to make the whole world answerable to God.
3:20 No flesh will be justified in his sight by works of the law. What the law brings is knowledge of sin.
## God's righteousness revealed
3:21 But now, apart from the law, God's righteousness has been made plain, and the Law and the Prophets give witness to it.
3:22 This is God's righteousness, reaching all who believe by way of faith in Jesus Christ. There is no distinction.
3:23 For all sinned, and all lack the glory of God.
3:24 They are justified freely, by his grace, through the redemption found in Christ Jesus.
3:25 God put him forward as the place of atonement, through faith, by his blood. This was to show his righteousness, because in his forbearance God had passed over sins done earlier.
3:26 His purpose was to display his righteousness in the present time, showing that he is righteous himself and that he justifies the one who has faith in Jesus.
## Boasting is shut out
3:27 Where, then, is boasting? It is shut out. Through what kind of law? The law of works? No, through the law of faith.
3:28 For we reckon that a person is justified by faith, apart from works of the law.
3:29 Is God God for Jews alone? Is he not God for Gentiles too? He is, for Gentiles too,
3:30 if it is true that God is one. He will justify the circumcised from faith, and the uncircumcised through that faith.
3:31 Do we, then, make the law useless through faith? By no means! Instead, we make the law stand.
## Abraham was justified by faith
4:1 What then are we to say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, found?
4:2 If Abraham was justified by works, he has a reason to boast, though not with God.
4:3 For what does the Scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness."
4:4 To the one who works, wages are not reckoned as a favor but as a debt.
4:5 But the person who does not work, and puts his trust in the One who justifies the ungodly, has his faith reckoned as righteousness.
4:6 David too speaks of the blessedness of the person to whom God reckons righteousness without any works:
4:7 "Blessed are those whose lawless acts have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered over.
4:8 Blessed is the man whose sin the Lord will never reckon."
## Before circumcision
4:9 Does this blessing come only to the circumcised, or also to the uncircumcised? For we say, "Faith was reckoned to Abraham as righteousness."
4:10 How was it reckoned? When he was circumcised, or when he was uncircumcised? Not when he was circumcised, but when he was uncircumcised.
4:11 And he received circumcision as a sign, a seal on the righteousness that came from his faith when he was still uncircumcised. In this way he became the father of every believer who is uncircumcised, so that righteousness would be reckoned to them also,
4:12 and the father of the circumcised, of those who are not just circumcised but also walk in the steps of that faith which our father Abraham showed in his uncircumcised state.
## The promise comes by faith
4:13 The promise that he would inherit the world was given to Abraham and his offspring, not through the law but through the righteousness of faith.
4:14 If those who rely on the law are heirs, then faith is emptied and the promise is canceled.
4:15 For the law produces anger, and transgression does not exist where there is no law.
4:16 For this reason it comes from faith, so that it will be by grace, and the promise will be certain for all his offspring: not only those who belong to the law, but also those who belong to the faith of Abraham. He is the father of us all,
4:17 as it is written: "I have made you the father of many nations." He is father in the sight of the God he believed, who makes the dead alive and calls the things that are not as though they were.
4:18 Against hope, he believed in hope, so that he became the father of many nations, as he had been told: "So will your offspring be."
4:19 He was about a hundred years old, and he looked at his own body, which was dead, and at the deadness of Sarah's womb. Yet his faith did not grow weak.
4:20 He did not doubt God's promise in unbelief. He grew strong in faith and gave glory to God.
4:21 He was completely sure that what God had promised, God was also able to do.
4:22 This is the reason it was reckoned to him as righteousness.
4:23 Now it was not written for his sake alone that it was reckoned to him,
4:24 but for ours too. It is going to be reckoned to us who believe in the one who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,
4:25 who was handed over for our trespasses and raised for our justification.
