## Paul in Corinth
18:1 After these things, Paul moved on from Athens and came to Corinth.
18:2 He found a Jew there named Aquila, a man of Pontus by birth. Aquila and his wife Priscilla had just come from Italy, since Claudius had given orders that all the Jews must leave Rome. Paul went to them,
18:3 and since he practiced the same craft, he lodged in their home and went to work. Their craft was making tents.
18:4 Sabbath after Sabbath he argued his case in the synagogue, and he kept persuading Jews and Greeks alike.
18:5 Once Silas and Timothy had come down from Macedonia, Paul was taken up wholly with the word. He solemnly declared to the Jews that the Messiah is Jesus.
18:6 But they set themselves against him and kept insulting him. So he shook out his clothes and said to them, "Your blood on your own head! I am clean. From now on, I am going to the Gentiles."
18:7 So he moved over from there and went into the home of someone named Titius Justus, who worshiped God. His house shared a wall with the synagogue.
18:8 Crispus, the synagogue leader, came to believe in the Lord, with all his household. And many of the Corinthians, as they listened, were coming to faith and being baptized.
## The Lord speaks in the night
18:9 In the night the Lord said to Paul through a vision, "Have no fear. Go on speaking, and do not go quiet,
18:10 for I am with you. No one will lay a hand on you to do you harm, for a large people in this city belongs to me."
18:11 He settled there a year and six months, teaching them God's word.
## Paul before Gallio
18:12 When Gallio was serving as proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose up with one mind against Paul and led him before the judgment seat.
18:13 They said, "He is talking people into worshiping God in a way that goes against the law."
18:14 Paul was about to speak. But Gallio said to the Jews, "You Jews, if it were some wrong done, or some wicked piece of villainy, it would make sense for me to hear you out.
18:15 But if these are disputes over a word and names and the law you follow, you deal with it. I have no wish to be a judge of such matters."
18:16 So he drove them off from the judgment seat.
18:17 Then everyone seized Sosthenes, the synagogue leader, and began to beat him right before the judgment seat. None of these things mattered at all to Gallio.
## Back to Antioch
18:18 Paul remained a good many days more. Then he said goodbye to the brothers and sailed off toward Syria, and Priscilla and Aquila were with him. He had had his hair cut short at Cenchreae, for he was keeping a vow.
18:19 When they reached Ephesus, he left those two there. But Paul went into the synagogue himself and held discussions with the Jews.
18:20 They asked him to remain longer, but he did not consent.
18:21 Instead he took his leave, saying, "I will turn back to you again, God willing." And he put out to sea from Ephesus.
18:22 After coming down to Caesarea, he went up and paid his respects to the church, then went down to Antioch.
18:23 He spent some time there and then set out. He went through the Galatian country and Phrygia, one place after another, making all the disciples stronger.
## Apollos
18:24 Now a Jew called Apollos, an Alexandrian by birth, arrived in Ephesus. He was a learned speaker and strong in the Scriptures.
18:25 He had received instruction in the way of the Lord. With a spirit on fire, he spoke and taught the facts about Jesus accurately, but the only baptism he knew was John's.
18:26 This man started speaking out freely in the synagogue. Priscilla and Aquila heard him, and they drew him to their side and laid out God's way for him more accurately.
18:27 He decided to go on to Achaia, and the brothers urged him on. They wrote to the disciples there to receive him. Once he got there, he was a great help, through grace, to those who had come to believe,
18:28 for with great force he proved the Jews wrong, out in public, using the Scriptures to show that the Messiah is Jesus.
## Disciples who knew only John's baptism
19:1 It happened, while Apollos was in Corinth, that Paul crossed the upland country and came to Ephesus, where he came across some disciples.
19:2 He said to them, "When you came to believe, did you receive the Holy Spirit?" They answered him, "Why, we never even heard whether there is a Holy Spirit."
19:3 He said, "Then into what were you baptized?" "Into the baptism of John," they said.
19:4 Paul said, "John baptized with a baptism of repentance. He kept telling the people to believe in the one coming after him, that is, in Jesus."
19:5 Hearing this, they were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.
19:6 Then Paul laid hands on them, and the Holy Spirit came on them. They began to speak in tongues and to prophesy.
19:7 All together, the men numbered about twelve.
## Two years in the school of Tyrannus
19:8 He went into the synagogue, and for three months he spoke out freely, holding discussions and trying to convince them about the kingdom of God.
19:9 But some grew hard and would not be persuaded, and they spoke badly of the Way in front of the whole gathering. So he stepped away from them and set the disciples apart, and every day he held discussions in the school of Tyrannus.
19:10 This lasted two years. As a result, everyone living in Asia heard the Lord's word, Jews and Greeks alike.
## The sons of Sceva
19:11 God was doing acts of power, and no ordinary ones, through Paul's hands.
19:12 Sweat cloths or work aprons from his skin were even carried off to the sick, and their illnesses left them and the evil spirits went out.
19:13 Some traveling Jewish exorcists also tried naming the name of the Lord Jesus over people who had evil spirits. They would say, "I put you under oath by Jesus, the one Paul preaches!"
19:14 The ones doing this were the seven sons of Sceva, a Jewish high priest.
19:15 But the evil spirit gave them this answer: "I know Jesus, and I am acquainted with Paul. But you, who are you?"
19:16 The man with the evil spirit in him sprang at them, got the better of them all, and was too strong for them. They fled out of that house stripped and wounded.
19:17 Word of this reached everyone living in Ephesus, Jews and Greeks alike. Fear came over them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus grew great in their eyes.
19:18 Many who had come to faith kept coming forward, openly admitting and reporting their practices.
19:19 Quite a few of those who had practiced magic gathered up their books and set them on fire where everyone could see. When the prices were added up, the total was fifty thousand silver coins.
19:20 In this way, by the Lord's might, the word kept growing and gaining strength.
## Paul makes his plans
19:21 When these things were complete, Paul set his mind, in the Spirit, to travel through Macedonia and Achaia and then go on to Jerusalem. He said, "Once I have been there, I must see Rome as well."
19:22 He sent ahead into Macedonia two of the men who served him, Timothy and Erastus, but he himself held back in Asia for a time.
## The riot of the silversmiths
19:23 Around that time the Way became the cause of no small commotion.
19:24 There was a silversmith by the name of Demetrius who made little silver shrines of Artemis, and he brought the craftsmen a great deal of work.
19:25 He gathered these men, and the workers in trades like theirs, and told them, "Gentlemen, you understand that our good living comes out of this work.
19:26 You can see and hear for yourselves what this Paul has done. Not just in Ephesus but through nearly all of Asia, he has won over a large crowd and drawn them away. He says that gods produced by hands are not gods.
19:27 The risk is not just that this line of ours will get a bad name. The temple of the great goddess Artemis may also come to count for nothing. She herself may even be torn down from her greatness, she whom all Asia and the whole world revere."
19:28 Hearing this, they were full of fury and kept shouting, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"
19:29 Confusion filled the city. With one impulse the people charged into the theater, hauling along Gaius and Aristarchus, two Macedonians who were traveling with Paul.
19:30 Paul meant to go in before the people's assembly, but the disciples kept him from it.
19:31 Some of the Asiarchs too, who were on friendly terms with him, sent a message pleading with him not to put himself at risk in the theater.
19:32 Meanwhile people were yelling, one this and another that. The assembly was in a muddle, and the greater part had no idea what they had gathered for.
19:33 Some in the crowd briefed Alexander, since the Jews were pushing him to the front. Alexander signaled with his hand and wanted to give a defense before the people.
19:34 But once they saw he was a Jew, a single cry went up from them all. For some two hours they kept shouting, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"
19:35 The city's chief clerk calmed the crowd and said, "You Ephesians, who on earth does not know this? The city of the Ephesians is the temple warden of the great Artemis, and of what fell from the sky.
19:36 No one can argue with these facts. So you must stay calm and not do anything reckless.
19:37 You brought these men here, yet they are not temple robbers, and they have not spoken against our goddess.
19:38 If Demetrius and his fellow craftsmen have a case against someone, court days are held and there are proconsuls. Let the parties accuse each other there.
19:39 Anything beyond that you are looking for will have to be resolved in the lawful assembly.
19:40 As it is, we are at risk of being accused of an uprising over today's events. There is no cause for it, and we will not be able to explain this disorderly gathering."
19:41 Having said this, he sent the assembly home.
## Through Macedonia and Greece
20:1 Once the uproar was over, Paul summoned the disciples and urged them on. Then he said goodbye to them and left to travel to Macedonia.
20:2 He passed through those parts, urging them on with many words, and came to Greece.
20:3 He stayed three months. He was about to put out to sea for Syria, but the Jews laid a plot against him. So he made up his mind to go back by way of Macedonia.
20:4 Traveling with him were Sopater son of Pyrrhus, a Berean; from the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; Gaius of Derbe and Timothy; and from Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.
20:5 These men came and were waiting for us in Troas.
20:6 We ourselves put out from Philippi when the days of Unleavened Bread were over, and within five days we reached them at Troas. There we spent seven days.
## Eutychus at Troas
20:7 On the first day of the week we had gathered to break bread, and Paul was holding a discussion with them. Since he was going to leave the next day, he stretched his talk out until midnight.
20:8 A good many lamps were in the upstairs room where we had met.
20:9 A young man called Eutychus was sitting on the window ledge. As Paul kept talking longer and longer, deep sleep weighed him down. Sleep finally carried him off, and he fell from the third story to the ground and was lifted up dead.
20:10 Paul went down, fell on him and wrapped his arms around him. "Stop the commotion," he said. "His life is still in him."
20:11 He went back up, broke the bread and had something to eat, and kept talking with them a long while, until dawn. Then he left.
20:12 They led the boy away alive, and their comfort was beyond measure.
## From Troas to Miletus
20:13 We went on ahead to the ship and put out for Assos, where we were to pick Paul up. That was how he had arranged it, since he meant to make the trip on foot himself.
20:14 He joined us at Assos. We took him on and came to Mitylene.
20:15 From there we sailed off and on the next day reached a point across from Chios. A day later we put in at Samos, and on the day after we came to Miletus.
20:16 Paul had made up his mind to sail on past Ephesus and not lose time in Asia. He was pressing on so that, if he could, he would be in Jerusalem for the day of Pentecost.
## Paul's farewell to the elders of Ephesus
20:17 From Miletus he sent word to Ephesus and summoned the church's elders.
20:18 When they had come to him, he said: "You know for yourselves how I was with you the whole time, from the first day I arrived in Asia.
20:19 I worked as the Lord's slave with all humility, through tears and through the trials that the Jews' plots brought on me.
20:20 You know I held nothing back that would do you good. I announced it to you and taught you openly and in one home after another.
20:21 I solemnly urged Jews and Greeks alike to repent toward God and to trust in our Lord Jesus.
20:22 And now, look, I am on my way to Jerusalem, bound in the Spirit. I do not know what will meet me there,
20:23 only that city after city the Holy Spirit solemnly tells me that chains and troubles lie ahead for me.
20:24 But I give no thought to my life, as if it were precious to me, if only I can finish my race and the service the Lord Jesus gave me. That service is to bear witness to the good news about the grace of God.
20:25 And now, look, I know this: you will see my face no more, all of you I went among as I preached the kingdom.
20:26 So I solemnly declare to you on this very day that I am clean of everyone's blood.
20:27 I did not hold back from announcing to you all of God's purpose.
20:28 Watch out for yourselves and for the whole flock. The Holy Spirit has placed you within it as overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which he acquired through his own blood.
20:29 I know that once I am gone, vicious wolves will get in among you and show the flock no mercy.
20:30 And out of your own group men will stand up and say twisted things, so they can pull the disciples away to follow them.
20:31 Keep awake, then. Remember that for three years, night and day, I did not let up from warning each one of you with tears.
20:32 And now I put you in the care of God and of the word of his grace, able to build up and to give the inheritance among all who have been made holy.
20:33 Silver, gold, clothes: I set my heart on no one's.
20:34 You know for yourselves that these hands met my needs and the needs of the people with me.
20:35 In every way I gave you an example: by toiling like this we must come to the aid of the weak. And we must keep in mind the words of the Lord Jesus, for he himself said, 'Giving is more blessed than receiving.'"
20:36 After saying this, he got down on his knees with them all and prayed.
20:37 They all broke into loud weeping. They threw their arms around Paul's neck and kissed him again and again,
20:38 hurting most of all over what he had said, that they were no longer going to look on his face. Then they escorted him to the ship.
