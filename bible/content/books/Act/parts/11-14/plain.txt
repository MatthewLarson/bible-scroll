## Peter explains himself in Jerusalem
11:1 The apostles and the brothers across Judea heard that even the Gentiles had welcomed the word of God.
11:2 Peter went up to Jerusalem, and there the people of the circumcision argued with him.
11:3 "You went in among uncircumcised men," they said, "and you ate with them!"
11:4 So Peter set it all out for them from the start, in order:
11:5 "I was praying in the town of Joppa, and while in a trance I saw a vision: some kind of container coming down, like a great linen sheet let down from heaven by its four corners. It came all the way to me.
11:6 I fixed my eyes on it and studied it, and I saw the four-footed animals of the earth, the wild beasts, the creeping things and the birds of the sky.
11:7 Then I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Get up, Peter; kill and eat.'
11:8 But I said, 'Not at all, Lord! Not once has anything common or unclean gone into my mouth.'
11:9 A second time the voice answered from heaven: 'What God has made clean, you must not call common.'
11:10 This happened three times, and then the whole thing was pulled up into heaven again.
11:11 Just then three men stood outside the house where we were. They had been sent to me from Caesarea.
11:12 The Spirit told me to go along with them and not to make distinctions. These six brothers here came with me as well, and we went into the man's house.
11:13 He reported to us that he had seen the angel standing in his home, who said to him, 'Send to Joppa and have Simon brought here, the one they call Peter.
11:14 He will speak words to you, and through them you and your whole household will be saved.'
11:15 I had only begun to speak when the Holy Spirit fell on them, the same way he fell on us in the beginning.
11:16 And the Lord's words came back to me, how he used to say, 'John baptized in water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit.'
11:17 If God gave them the same gift he also gave us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I? Did I have the power to stop God?"
11:18 On hearing this they went quiet, and they glorified God. "Then God has given even the Gentiles repentance that brings life!" they said.
## The church in Antioch
11:19 Those scattered by the trouble that arose over Stephen traveled on as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus and Antioch. They spoke the word to nobody but Jews.
11:20 Some of them, though, were men from Cyprus and Cyrene. When they reached Antioch, they spoke to the Hellenists too, announcing the Lord Jesus as good news.
11:21 The Lord's hand was with them. Many people believed, a great number, and turned to the Lord.
11:22 Word about them reached the ears of the church in Jerusalem, and the church sent Barnabas off all the way to Antioch.
11:23 He came and saw the grace of God, and he was glad. He kept urging them all to stay close to the Lord with determined hearts.
11:24 He was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, and a sizable crowd was added to the Lord.
11:25 Barnabas then set out for Tarsus to track Saul down.
11:26 He found him and brought him to Antioch. For a whole year the two of them gathered with the church there and taught a sizable crowd. And Antioch was the first place where the disciples came to be called "Christians."
## Help for Judea in a famine
11:27 Around that time, prophets came down to Antioch from Jerusalem.
11:28 One of them, Agabus by name, got up and indicated through the Spirit that a great famine was going to strike the whole inhabited world. It did come, in the days of Claudius.
11:29 Each of the disciples, as far as his means allowed, resolved to send aid to the brothers who lived in Judea.
11:30 They did it, and Barnabas and Saul were the ones who delivered it to the elders.
## James killed, Peter in prison
12:1 At about that time, King Herod seized some people from the church to do them harm.
12:2 James, John's brother, he executed with the sword.
12:3 Seeing that the Jews liked it, he went further and had Peter taken too. It was the time of the Feast of Unleavened Bread.
12:4 Once he had him in hand, he put him in prison and gave him to four squads, four soldiers in each, to guard. He planned to bring him out before the people after the Passover.
12:5 So Peter was held in the prison. But the church kept praying to God for him, and praying hard.
## An angel sets Peter free
12:6 On the very night before Herod meant to bring him out, Peter lay asleep with a soldier on either side of him, fastened with two chains. Guards at the door were keeping watch over the prison.
12:7 Just then an angel of the Lord stood beside him, and light blazed in the cell. He struck Peter on the side and woke him, saying, "Up, quickly!" The chains dropped off his hands.
12:8 The angel said to him, "Put on your belt and tie on your sandals." He did so. Then the angel told him, "Throw your cloak on and follow me."
12:9 Peter went out and kept following, unaware that what the angel was doing was actually happening. He thought he was having a vision.
12:10 They went past a first guard post and a second, and reached the iron gate that opens onto the city. On its own the gate swung open for them. Out they went and down one street, and all at once the angel left him.
12:11 Peter came to his senses and said, "Now I truly know it: the Lord sent out his angel and snatched me from Herod's grip, and from all that the people of the Jews were waiting for."
12:12 Once he had taken it in, he went to the home of Mary, mother of the John they called Mark. Many had gathered there and were praying.
12:13 He knocked on the door in the gate, and a slave girl called Rhoda came to see who was there.
12:14 She knew it was Peter's voice, and for pure joy she left the gate shut and ran inside to tell them Peter was standing outside it.
12:15 "You're raving," they said to her. She held to it that it was true. Then they said, "It is his angel."
12:16 Meanwhile Peter went on knocking. They opened up, saw him and were stunned.
12:17 With a wave of his hand he signaled them to be quiet, and he told them the story of how the Lord had led him out of prison. "Let James and the brothers know about this," he said. Then he went out and left for somewhere else.
12:18 At daybreak the soldiers were in quite an uproar over what had become of Peter.
12:19 Herod hunted for him but did not find him. He put the guards on trial and ordered them led away. After that he left Judea, went down to Caesarea, and spent some time there.
## The death of Herod
12:20 Herod was furious with the people of Tyre and Sidon. They came to him as one, won over Blastus, who was in charge of the king's bedroom, and asked for peace, since their region was fed from the king's territory.
12:21 On a set day, Herod dressed in his royal clothes, took his seat on the platform and made a speech to them.
12:22 And the crowd kept crying out, "It's a god speaking, not a man!"
12:23 Then and there an angel of the Lord struck him, because he did not give the glory to God. Worms devoured him, and he died.
## The word keeps growing
12:24 But the word of God kept growing and multiplying.
12:25 Barnabas and Saul went back, once they had completed their service in Jerusalem. They took with them John, the one called Mark.
## Barnabas and Saul sent out
13:1 The church in Antioch had prophets and teachers in it: Barnabas; Simeon, known as Niger; Lucius the Cyrenian; Manaen, who had been raised alongside Herod the tetrarch; and Saul.
13:2 Once, as they were serving the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, "Now set Barnabas and Saul apart for me, for the work I have called them to do."
13:3 They fasted and prayed, put their hands on the two men, and let them go.
## Cyprus and the magician
13:4 So they were sent out by the Holy Spirit. They went down to Seleucia, and from there they took a ship to Cyprus.
13:5 At Salamis they began announcing God's word in the synagogues of the Jews. John was with them too, as an assistant.
13:6 They went through the whole island as far as Paphos. There they found a man, a magician, a Jewish false prophet. His name was Bar-Jesus.
13:7 This man belonged to the circle of the proconsul Sergius Paulus, a man of good sense. Sergius Paulus summoned Barnabas and Saul, eager to hear God's word.
13:8 But Elymas the magician (that is how his name is translated) set himself against them, trying to steer the proconsul away from the faith.
13:9 Then Saul, who also went by Paul, was filled with the Holy Spirit. He fixed his eyes on him
13:10 and said, "You are packed with every lie and every scheme, you son of the devil, you enemy of all righteousness! Will you not quit bending the straight paths of the Lord?
13:11 Look now: the hand of the Lord is on you. You will go blind and not see the sun for a time." At once mist and darkness dropped over him, and he turned this way and that, looking for someone to take him by the hand.
13:12 The proconsul saw what had happened and came to faith, struck with wonder at the Lord's teaching.
## A sermon at Pisidian Antioch
13:13 From Paphos, Paul and the men with him put out to sea and reached Perga in Pamphylia. There John parted from them and headed back to Jerusalem.
13:14 They themselves traveled on from Perga and arrived at Pisidian Antioch. When the Sabbath came, they went into the synagogue and took their seats.
13:15 When the Law and the Prophets had been read, the synagogue leaders sent someone to tell them, "Brothers, if any of you has a word to encourage the people, speak up."
13:16 Paul rose, signaled with his hand, and said: "Israelites, and you who fear God, listen.
13:17 The God of this people Israel picked out our fathers. He lifted the people high while they were living as foreigners in the land of Egypt. Then with his arm raised high he brought them out of it.
13:18 He bore with their ways in the wilderness for some forty years.
13:19 He tore down seven nations in the land of Canaan and gave his people their land to inherit.
13:20 It came to some four hundred and fifty years in all. After that he gave them judges, up to Samuel the prophet.
13:21 Then they asked for a king, and for forty years God gave them Saul son of Kish, a man from the tribe of Benjamin.
13:22 He removed Saul and raised up David as their king. God bore witness to him in these words: 'I have found David son of Jesse, a man to suit my heart, who will carry out everything I want.'
13:23 Out of this man's offspring, as he had promised, God has brought Israel a Savior, Jesus.
13:24 Ahead of his arrival John had announced to the whole people of Israel a baptism of repentance.
13:25 When John was nearing the end of his race, he would say, 'What do you take me to be? I am not he. No, look, after me someone is coming, and I am not good enough to untie the sandal on his feet.'
13:26 Brothers, sons of Abraham's family, and you here who fear God: the word about this salvation was sent out to us.
13:27 Jerusalem's residents and their leaders failed to know him, or the voices of the prophets read out Sabbath after Sabbath. And by condemning him they made those voices come true.
13:28 They found no charge that called for death, and still they begged Pilate to do away with him.
13:29 Once they had carried out everything written about him, they took him down off the tree and placed him in a tomb.
13:30 But God raised him up from among the dead.
13:31 Over many days he was seen by those who had traveled up with him from Galilee to Jerusalem, and these are now his witnesses before the people.
13:32 As for us, we are telling you good news about the promise that was made to the fathers:
13:33 God has made it come true for us, their children, by raising Jesus. That is what is written in the second psalm: 'You are my son. This day I have fathered you.'
13:34 That he raised him from the dead, never to go back to decay, God declared like this: 'I will give you the holy things of David, the ones that are sure.'
13:35 For this reason he says in another place as well: 'You will not allow your holy one to see decay.'
13:36 David, you see, served his own generation by God's plan, and then he fell asleep. He was added to his fathers, and he did see decay.
13:37 The one God raised up, though, never saw decay.
13:38 So, brothers, you should know this: it is through him that the forgiveness of sins is being announced to you.
13:39 There were things from which you could not be justified by the law of Moses. From all of them, everyone who believes is justified in him.
13:40 Be careful, then, so that the saying in the Prophets does not come true:
13:41 'See, you who sneer! Stand amazed, and vanish! / In your own days I am at work on a deed, / a deed you would never believe / if someone told you about it.'"
## Turning to the Gentiles
13:42 As the two were going out, the people kept urging them to tell them these things again on the next Sabbath.
13:43 When the synagogue let out, many Jews and God-worshiping proselytes followed Paul and Barnabas. The two talked with them and tried to persuade them to hold on to the grace of God.
13:44 When the following Sabbath came, nearly the entire city gathered to hear the word of the Lord.
13:45 Seeing the crowds, the Jews were filled with jealousy. They spoke against what Paul said and hurled insults.
13:46 Paul and Barnabas spoke out fearlessly: "God's word had to be told to you first. But you are pushing it away, and so you are judging yourselves unworthy of eternal life. Look, we are turning to the Gentiles.
13:47 That is the command the Lord has given us: 'I have set you as a light for the nations, / so that you may be salvation / to the farthest end of the earth.'"
13:48 On hearing this the Gentiles rejoiced and glorified the word of the Lord. And as many as had been assigned to eternal life came to believe.
13:49 The Lord's word was carried all through that region.
13:50 The Jews, though, worked up the prominent women who worshiped God, and the first men of the city. They roused a persecution against Paul and Barnabas and threw them out of their territory.
13:51 The two shook the dust from their feet at them and moved on to Iconium.
13:52 Meanwhile the disciples went on being filled with joy and the Holy Spirit.
## Iconium
14:1 The same thing happened in Iconium. They went into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke, and a great crowd of Jews and Greeks came to believe.
14:2 The Jews who would not be persuaded, though, roused the Gentiles and made their minds bitter toward the brothers.
14:3 So they spent a good while there, speaking out freely, trusting in the Lord. He backed up the word of his grace by granting that signs and wonders happen at their hands.
14:4 The city's population split in two: some were with the Jews and some with the apostles.
14:5 When Gentiles and Jews, together with their rulers, made a push to abuse them and stone them,
14:6 the two became aware of it and took refuge in Lystra and Derbe, towns of Lycaonia, and the country around.
14:7 And there they went on announcing the good news.
## Taken for gods at Lystra
14:8 A man sat at Lystra whose feet had no strength. He was lame from his mother's womb and had never once walked.
14:9 He was listening as Paul spoke. Paul fixed his eyes on him and saw that he had faith to be saved,
14:10 and called out loudly, "Get up on your feet, upright!" He leaped up and started walking around.
14:11 Seeing what Paul had done, the crowds raised their voices in Lycaonian: "The gods have taken the likeness of men and come down to us!"
14:12 They named Barnabas "Zeus," and Paul "Hermes," because he was the one who led in speaking.
14:13 The priest of Zeus, the Zeus whose shrine stood in front of the city, brought bulls and wreaths of flowers to the gates. Along with the crowds he was ready to offer sacrifice.
14:14 The apostles Barnabas and Paul heard about it. Tearing their clothes, they dashed out among the crowd, crying out,
14:15 "Men, why are you doing this? We are human too, with feelings just like yours. We have come with good news, so that you will turn from these empty things to the God who lives, who made heaven and earth and the sea and all that is in them.
14:16 In the generations gone by, he allowed every nation to walk its own roads.
14:17 Even so, he did not leave himself without a witness. He kept doing good: giving you rains from the sky and seasons that bear fruit, and filling your hearts with food and good cheer."
14:18 Saying all this, they only just managed to stop the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.
## Paul stoned
14:19 Then Jews arrived from Antioch and Iconium. They talked the crowds around, pelted Paul with stones and hauled him outside the town, believing he had died.
14:20 The disciples formed a circle around him, and he stood up and walked back into the town. On the following day he set out with Barnabas for Derbe.
## Back the way they came
14:21 After bringing the good news to that city and making a good many disciples, they turned back through Lystra, Iconium and Antioch.
14:22 They made the disciples' souls stronger and urged them to keep on in the faith. "We must go through many troubles to enter the kingdom of God," they told them.
14:23 They chose elders for them in each church. With prayer and fasting, they entrusted them to the Lord they had put their faith in.
14:24 They crossed Pisidia and arrived in Pamphylia.
14:25 After speaking the word in Perga, they made their way down to Attalia.
## Home to Antioch
14:26 From there they took ship for Antioch. That was where they had first been handed over to God's grace for the work, and now they had finished it.
14:27 When they arrived, they called the church together and told them how much God had done alongside them, and how he had opened to the Gentiles a door to faith.
14:28 They spent quite a while there with the disciples.
