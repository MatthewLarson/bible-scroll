## Tertullus accuses Paul before Felix
24:1 Five days after that, Ananias the high priest came down, bringing some elders and an advocate, a certain Tertullus. They filed a formal complaint against Paul with the governor.
24:2 When Paul had been called in, Tertullus began to accuse him. "Thanks to you, we live in great peace," he said, "and your foresight is bringing improvements to this nation.
24:3 We receive it gladly, most excellent Felix, in every way and in every place, and we are full of thanks.
24:4 I don't want to hold you up any longer, so I ask you, as the kind man you are, to listen to a few words from us.
24:5 This man, we found, is a pest. He starts uprisings among all the Jews across the empire, and he stands at the head of the sect of the Nazarenes.
24:6 He even attempted to make the temple unholy, and we took hold of him.
24:7 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
24:8 If you question him yourself, you can find out from him the facts about everything we charge him with."
24:9 The Jews joined the attack too, declaring that it was all exactly so.
## Paul's defense before Felix
24:10 Then the governor nodded to Paul to speak, and he answered. "I know you have sat as judge over this nation for many years. So I take heart as I speak in my own defense.
24:11 You can confirm it: it has been no more than twelve days since I went up to worship at Jerusalem.
24:12 No one caught me debating with anybody in the temple or gathering a mob, not in the synagogues and not around the city.
24:13 And what they now charge me with, they have no way to prove to you.
24:14 But I will admit this to you: by the Way, which they call a sect, I serve our ancestors' God. I believe everything in line with the Law and everything written in the Prophets.
24:15 My hope rests on God, a hope these men also look forward to: that a resurrection is coming, of the righteous and the unrighteous alike.
24:16 That is why I work hard at keeping my conscience free of blame toward God and toward people, all the time.
24:17 Many years went by, and then I arrived to give alms for my people and to present offerings.
24:18 That is what I was doing when they came across me in the temple. I had been purified, and there was no crowd with me and no uproar. But there were some Jews from Asia,
24:19 and those men ought to be standing here in front of you, making their accusation, if they really hold anything against me.
24:20 Otherwise, let the men here tell what crime they discovered when I stood in front of the Sanhedrin,
24:21 except for one sentence I called out while standing among them: 'I am being judged by you today over the resurrection of the dead!'"
## Felix and Drusilla
24:22 Felix had quite accurate knowledge about the Way, and he postponed the case. He told them, "When Lysias the commander comes down, I will settle the matters between you."
24:23 He gave the centurion orders to hold Paul under guard, but to let him have some relief and not to stop his own people from caring for him.
24:24 A few days after that, Felix came with Drusilla, his own wife, a Jewish woman. He had Paul brought to him and heard him out on the subject of faith in Christ Jesus.
24:25 Paul reasoned about righteousness, about self-control and about the judgment that lies ahead, and Felix became afraid. "Go away for now," he answered. "When I find a chance, I will call you back."
24:26 He was also hoping, all the while, that Paul would hand him some money. That was why he had him brought in so often and kept talking with him.
24:27 Two full years went by, and then Porcius Festus took over from Felix. Felix wanted to store up favor with the Jews, so he left Paul behind in chains.
## Festus and the Jerusalem plot
25:1 So Festus took up his post in the province, and three days later he left Caesarea and went up to Jerusalem.
25:2 There the chief priests and the foremost men among the Jews filed a formal complaint against Paul with him, and they kept pleading with him.
25:3 They wanted a favor that would hurt Paul: to have him summoned to Jerusalem. They were setting a trap to kill him on the road.
25:4 Festus's answer was that Paul was being held under guard at Caesarea, and that he himself would be heading there before long.
25:5 "So," he said, "your men with power to act should come down along with me. If there is something out of place in this man, they can accuse him."
## Paul appeals to Caesar
25:6 He spent no more than eight or ten days with them and then went down to Caesarea. On the following day he sat on the judgment seat and gave the order for Paul to be brought.
25:7 As he arrived, Jews down from Jerusalem crowded around him. They hurled many heavy charges at him, but they could not prove any of them.
25:8 Paul spoke in his own defense: "I have not sinned in any way, not against the Jews' law, not against the temple, and not against Caesar."
25:9 Festus, though, wanted to store up favor with the Jews. He said to Paul in reply, "Would you agree to go up to Jerusalem and be judged there, in front of me, about these matters?"
25:10 Paul said, "I am standing at Caesar's judgment seat. That is where I must be judged. I have wronged the Jews in nothing, and you know that better than anyone.
25:11 If I am a wrongdoer and have done something worth the death penalty, I am not trying to get out of dying. But if none of their accusations holds up, no one is able to make a gift of me to them. I appeal to Caesar!"
25:12 Festus discussed it with his advisers and then answered, "Caesar is the one you appealed to. To Caesar you will go!"
## Festus consults King Agrippa
25:13 After some days had gone by, King Agrippa came down to Caesarea with Bernice to welcome Festus.
25:14 They stayed on for a good many days, and Festus brought up Paul's affairs with the king. "Felix left a certain man here in custody," he said.
25:15 "While I was at Jerusalem, the Jews' chief priests and elders filed a complaint about him and demanded that he be condemned.
25:16 My answer to them was that Romans do not have a custom of making a gift of any man before the accused has his accusers in front of him, face to face, and gets room to defend himself against the charge.
25:17 They gathered here, and I lost no time. The very next day I sat on the judgment seat and had the man brought in.
25:18 His accusers stood up around him, but the charges they brought were not the evil deeds I had suspected.
25:19 What they had against him were questions about their own religion, and about one Jesus, who had died, and who Paul kept saying is alive.
25:20 Not knowing how to inquire into such things, I asked if he cared to go to Jerusalem and be judged there over them.
25:21 Paul, though, appealed to be kept under guard for the verdict of the emperor. So I gave orders to hold him until I can send him up to Caesar."
25:22 Agrippa told Festus, "I have been wanting to hear the man for myself." "You will hear him tomorrow," Festus said.
## Paul is brought before Agrippa
25:23 The following day, Agrippa and Bernice arrived in grand display and went into the hall of hearings, along with the commanders and the city's most important men. Festus gave the order, and Paul was led in.
25:24 Then Festus said, "King Agrippa, and all you men present here with us: look at this person. The entire mass of the Jews appealed to me about him, in Jerusalem and here as well. They kept crying out that he must not stay alive one more day.
25:25 As I understood it, he had done nothing that deserves death. But this man himself appealed to the emperor, so I made up my mind to send him.
25:26 Yet I have nothing sure to put in writing about him to our lord. That is why I have brought him out in front of you all, and above all in front of you, King Agrippa. Once he has been questioned, I may have something to write.
25:27 It makes no sense to me to send off a prisoner and not spell out the charges against him."
## Paul speaks before Agrippa
26:1 Agrippa told Paul, "You may now speak on your own behalf." So Paul held out his hand and spoke in his defense.
26:2 "King Agrippa, about everything the Jews charge me with, I count myself happy that today I am going to answer for myself in front of you,
26:3 most of all since you know well every custom and every debate among the Jews. So please, hear me out with patience.
26:4 Every Jew knows my life from my youth on. From the start I spent it among my own nation, and in Jerusalem.
26:5 They have known me from early on, and they could tell you, if they wanted to, that I lived as a Pharisee, following the most exacting party of our religion.
26:6 And now it is for hoping in the promise God gave our fathers that I stand here being judged.
26:7 Our twelve tribes, serving God with all their strength night and day, hope to see that promise reached. And over this hope, O king, I am being accused, and by Jews!
26:8 Why is it judged beyond belief among you all that God raises dead people?
26:9 As for me, I once thought to myself that I had to do a great deal against the name of Jesus the Nazarene.
26:10 I did it, too, in Jerusalem. Armed with authority from the chief priests, I shut up many of the saints in prisons. When they were being executed, I added my vote against them.
26:11 Over and over, in every synagogue, I had them punished and kept trying to push them into blasphemy. My rage at them was beyond all bounds, and I went on persecuting them even as far as foreign cities.
## The light on the Damascus road
26:12 "That was my business when I was traveling to Damascus, carrying authority and orders from the chief priests.
26:13 In the middle of the day, O king, out on the road, I saw a light that came from heaven, outshining the sun, and it shone all around me and around those making the trip with me.
26:14 All of us dropped to the ground. Then I heard a voice speaking to me in Hebrew: 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? Kicking against the goads is hard on you.'
26:15 Then I asked, 'Who are you, Lord?' The Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
26:16 Now rise, and stand up on your feet. This is why I have shown myself to you: to choose you as my helper and my witness, a witness to what you have seen of me and to what I will yet show you.
26:17 I will keep taking you out of danger from the people and from the Gentiles. To them I am sending you,
26:18 to open their eyes, so they turn back from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to God. Then they will receive the forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who have been made holy by faith in me.'
26:19 And so, King Agrippa, I did not refuse to obey the vision from heaven.
26:20 Instead, I brought the message first to the people in Damascus and in Jerusalem, then through the whole countryside of Judea, and to the Gentiles: they must repent, turn to God, and live out works that are worthy of repentance.
26:21 Because of these things, Jews took hold of me while I was in the temple and kept trying to do away with me.
26:22 God's help has been with me right up to this day, and here I stand, giving witness to the lowly and the great. I tell them only what the prophets said was going to take place, and Moses too:
26:23 that the Messiah was to suffer, and that he, the first one raised out of death, was going to announce light to the people and to the Gentiles."
## Festus interrupts
26:24 While Paul was giving this defense, Festus called out at the top of his voice, "You're raving, Paul! All those books of yours are turning you into a madman!"
26:25 "I am not raving, most excellent Festus," Paul said. "No, the words I am speaking are true, and sound of mind.
26:26 The king understands these matters, and so I speak to him openly. I cannot believe that any of this has slipped past him. This thing was not done off in a corner.
26:27 Do you believe the prophets, King Agrippa? I know you believe them."
26:28 Agrippa said to Paul, "With so little, you would talk me into making myself a Christian?"
26:29 Paul replied, "Little or much, my prayer to God is this: that you, and not you alone, but everyone who hears me today, would come to be just what I am, apart from these chains."
## Innocent, but bound for Rome
26:30 The king stood up, and so did the governor, Bernice, and the people seated with them.
26:31 As they went out, they talked it over among themselves. "Nothing this man does is worth death or chains," they said.
26:32 And Agrippa told Festus, "The man might have been released, if only he had not appealed to Caesar."
## Setting sail for Rome
27:1 Once our sailing for Italy was settled, Paul and certain other prisoners were turned over to a centurion of the Emperor's Cohort, a man named Julius.
27:2 We went aboard a ship whose home port was Adramyttium. It was about to call at towns along the coast of Asia, and we set sail. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.
27:3 On the following day we came into port at Sidon. Julius was considerate toward Paul and allowed him to visit his friends and receive their care.
27:4 Setting out from there, we kept to the sheltered side of Cyprus, since the winds were blowing against us.
27:5 We crossed the waters that lie off Cilicia and Pamphylia and reached Myra, in Lycia.
27:6 There the centurion came across a ship out of Alexandria bound for Italy, and he moved us onto it.
27:7 For many days we crept along, and it was hard work to get as far as Cnidus. The wind would not let us go on that way, so we ran down along the sheltered side of Crete, near Salmone.
27:8 Hugging the coast with great effort, we reached a spot known as Fair Havens. The town of Lasea was close by.
## Paul's warning at Fair Havens
27:9 By now a lot of time had gone by. Going on by sea had become risky, since the Fast was already past. So Paul gave them a warning.
27:10 "Men," he said, "I can tell this voyage will bring damage and heavy loss, and not just to the cargo and the ship. Our own lives are at stake too."
27:11 The centurion, though, trusted the helmsman and the shipowner more than the things Paul was saying.
27:12 The harbor was a poor place to spend the winter, so most of them were for setting out from there, in the hope that they could somehow make it to Phoenix to winter. Phoenix is a Cretan harbor that looks out toward the southwest and the northwest.
## The storm
27:13 A light south wind sprang up, and they thought their plan was as good as done. They pulled up anchor and ran along close in by Crete.
27:14 Not long after, a typhoon-like wind came rushing down off Crete. It is known as Euraquilo, the Northeaster.
27:15 The ship was caught in it and could not look the wind in the eye. We let her go and were swept along.
27:16 We ran in behind a little island named Cauda, where it was sheltered, and there, with a great struggle, we just barely got control of the skiff.
27:17 The crew hauled it up and then used cables as braces, lashing them around the hull. They were afraid of being thrown onto the shoals of Syrtis, so they let down the gear and in that way drifted on.
27:18 The storm tossed us so violently that on the next day they started heaving the cargo into the sea.
27:19 On the third day, with their own hands, they flung away the ship's gear.
27:20 For many days no sun or stars could be seen, and a storm of no small size kept pressing on us. From then on, every hope that we would be saved was being stripped away.
## An angel's promise
27:21 Nobody had eaten for a long time. Then Paul stood up in the middle of them. "Men," he said, "you ought to have obeyed me and not put out from Crete. Then you would not have gained this harm and this loss.
27:22 Even so, I now call on you to be of good cheer. Not one life among you will be lost, only the ship.
27:23 For this past night an angel of the God who owns me, the God I worship, stood at my side.
27:24 'Don't be afraid, Paul,' he said. 'You have to stand before Caesar. And look: God has made you a gift of everyone who is sailing with you.'
27:25 So cheer up, men! I believe God, and it will turn out just the way it was told to me.
27:26 We are going to have to be cast up on some island, though."
## Bread before dawn
27:27 It was the fourteenth night, and we were drifting back and forth across the Adria. Around the middle of the night the sailors began to suspect that some land was coming close to them.
27:28 They dropped a weighted line and measured twenty fathoms. They went on a little farther, dropped the line again and measured fifteen fathoms.
27:29 They were afraid we might be thrown somewhere onto rocky ground, so they threw out four anchors from the stern and kept praying for the day to dawn.
27:30 Meanwhile the sailors were looking for a way to get off the ship. They let the skiff down into the sea, making it look as if they meant to run out anchors from the bow.
27:31 Paul told the centurion and the soldiers, "If these men do not stay on board, there is no way for you to be saved."
27:32 So the soldiers chopped through the skiff's ropes and let it drop away.
27:33 While they waited for day to come, Paul kept begging everyone to take some food. "This is the fourteenth day," he said, "that you have been watching and waiting with no food. You have taken nothing at all.
27:34 So I appeal to you to take some food; this is for your rescue. Not a hair will be lost from the head of one of you."
27:35 Having said this, he took a loaf, thanked God before everyone, broke it, and started eating.
27:36 Everyone's spirits rose, and they too took some food.
27:37 Counting everyone, there were two hundred seventy-six souls on the ship.
27:38 Once they had their fill of food, they made the ship lighter by dumping the wheat into the sea.
## Shipwreck
27:39 At daybreak no one knew what land it was. But they spotted an inlet with a beach, and they made plans to drive the ship up onto it, if they could.
27:40 They cut away the anchors and abandoned them to the sea. At the same time they loosed the lines lashing the steering oars. They raised the small sail at the front to catch the wind and headed for the shore.
27:41 But they hit a place where two seas meet and drove the vessel aground there. The bow jammed and stayed stuck, unable to move, while the stern was being smashed apart by the force of the waves.
27:42 The soldiers made up their minds to kill the prisoners, so that none of them could swim off and slip away.
27:43 But the centurion, who wanted to bring Paul safely through, stopped them from doing what they intended. He gave orders that anyone able to swim should throw himself into the water first and head for land.
27:44 The others were to go, some on boards and some on things from the ship. And so it turned out that all were brought safely through to land.
## The viper on Malta
28:1 Now that we had been brought safely through, we discovered the island's name was Malta.
28:2 The local people were kind to us beyond the ordinary. They lit a fire and brought us all in to it, because rain had set in and it was cold.
28:3 Paul had collected an armful of sticks and laid them on the fire. A viper came out from the heat and clamped onto his hand.
28:4 The local people saw the beast dangling from his hand and began saying to each other, "Surely this fellow is a murderer! He came safely through the sea, but Justice won't let him go on living."
28:5 Paul, though, flicked the beast off into the fire and felt no ill at all.
28:6 They kept expecting him to puff up with fever, or to drop dead all at once. They waited a long while and watched nothing out of place happen to him. Then they turned around and began saying he was a god.
## Healing in Publius' house
28:7 Around that spot there were estates owned by the first man of the island, whose name was Publius. He took us in and treated us as friends for three days.
28:8 As it happened, Publius' father was laid up with bouts of fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, put his hands on him, and healed him.
28:9 After that, all the others on the island who had illnesses kept coming, and they were cured.
28:10 They showered us with honors, and as we set sail they loaded on everything we would need.
## Rome at last
28:11 Three months later we put to sea on a ship from Alexandria that had spent the winter at the island. Its emblem was the Twins, the sons of Zeus.
28:12 We landed at Syracuse and stayed on three days.
28:13 From there we cast off and made it to Rhegium. One day later a wind blew in from the south, and by the second day we were at Puteoli.
28:14 There we met some brothers, who urged us to remain with them for seven days. And that is how we came to Rome.
28:15 Word about us had reached the brothers from there, and they came out to meet us at Appius' Market and at Three Taverns. When Paul caught sight of them, he gave thanks to God and his heart grew bold.
28:16 Once we had entered Rome, Paul was given permission to stay on his own, along with the soldier assigned to guard him.
## Paul and the Jews of Rome
28:17 After three days Paul invited the men who led the Jews there to come together. Once they were gathered he spoke to them. "Brothers, I did nothing hostile to our people or to the ways handed down from our fathers. Yet from Jerusalem I was handed over, a prisoner, into Roman hands.
28:18 The Romans questioned me and were ready to let me go, since I had done nothing that called for death.
28:19 But the Jews spoke against it, and I had no choice but to appeal to Caesar. It was not that I had some accusation to lay against my own nation.
28:20 That is why I have called for you, to see you face to face and talk with you. This chain is around me on account of Israel's hope."
28:21 They answered him, "No letters about you have reached us from Judea. Not one of the brothers who has arrived has brought word or spoken a word of harm about you.
28:22 We think it right to hear from you what your thinking is. As for this sect, we know it is spoken against everywhere."
## Isaiah's words to Rome
28:23 So they fixed a day with him, and a larger crowd came to where he was staying. From early morning till evening he laid it all out for them. He bore solemn witness to the kingdom of God and tried to win them over about Jesus, using both the law of Moses and the Prophets.
28:24 Some were persuaded by his words, while others kept refusing to believe.
28:25 At odds with one another, they started to go, but not before Paul said one more thing: "The Holy Spirit put it well when he spoke through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers.
28:26 He said, 'Go to this people and say, "You will listen and listen, but never understand. / You will look and look, but never see.
28:27 For this people's heart has become sluggish. / They hardly hear with their ears, / and they have shut their eyes tight, / so that they never see with their eyes / or hear with their ears / or understand with their heart / and turn, and I heal them."'
28:28 So you must know this: this salvation from God has been sent out to the Gentiles. They will listen."
28:29 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
## Two years, unhindered
28:30 For two full years Paul stayed on in quarters he paid for himself. He welcomed everyone who came in to see him,
28:31 proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things about the Lord Jesus Christ, with complete freedom to speak, and no one stopped him.
