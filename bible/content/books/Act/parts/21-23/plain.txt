## On to Tyre
21:1 Once we had been pulled away from them and had put to sea, we ran a straight course to Cos. The next day we reached Rhodes, and from there Patara.
21:2 There we found a ship bound across the sea for Phoenicia. We went aboard and sailed.
21:3 Cyprus came into view, and we kept it on our left as we sailed on toward Syria. We put in at Tyre, since the ship was unloading its freight there.
21:4 We searched out the disciples and stayed there seven days. Through the Spirit they kept telling Paul not to set foot in Jerusalem.
21:5 When we had finished out our days there, we left and went on our way. Every one of them came with their wives and children to see us off, all the way out of the city. We went down on our knees on the shore and prayed,
21:6 and then said goodbye to one another. We climbed on board the ship, and they went back home.
## Agabus at Caesarea
21:7 As for us, our sailing ended when we came from Tyre down to Ptolemais. We greeted the brothers there and spent one day with them.
21:8 Setting out the next day, we came to Caesarea, where we entered the home of Philip the evangelist and stayed with him. He was one of the Seven.
21:9 This man had four daughters, virgins who prophesied.
21:10 We stayed on there for some days. Then a man came down from Judea, a prophet by the name of Agabus.
21:11 He came over to us, picked up Paul's belt, and bound his own feet and hands with it. "The Holy Spirit says this," he told us. "The man this belt belongs to, the Jews in Jerusalem will bind him just like this, and they will hand him over into the hands of the Gentiles."
21:12 At these words, we and the local people kept begging Paul not to go up to Jerusalem.
21:13 Paul answered, "What are you doing, crying like this and crushing my heart? For the name of the Lord Jesus I am prepared to be bound in Jerusalem, and not only bound, but to die there."
21:14 We could not persuade him, so we said no more except, "May the Lord's will be done."
## James and the elders
21:15 When those days were over, we got our baggage ready and began the journey up to Jerusalem.
21:16 Some disciples from Caesarea traveled with us too. They took us to the man who would be our host, Mnason, a Cypriot who had been a disciple from the early days.
21:17 We reached Jerusalem, and the brothers there gave us a glad welcome.
21:18 On the following day Paul went with us to visit James. All the elders had gathered there as well.
21:19 Paul greeted them, then went through, item by item, everything God had done among the Gentiles by means of his service.
21:20 They heard him and gave glory to God. Then they told him, "Brother, you can see how many tens of thousands among the Jews have come to believe, and they are, all of them, zealous for the law.
21:21 But they have been told things about you. The story is that you teach all the Jews who live among the Gentiles to desert Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children and not to live by the customs.
21:22 What now? There is no doubt they will hear you have arrived.
21:23 So take our advice and do this. Four men among us have taken a vow on themselves.
21:24 Bring these men with you, go through the purification together with them, and cover their costs, so they can have their heads shaved. That way everyone will see that the rumors they have heard about you amount to nothing. They will see that you too walk in line with the law and keep it.
21:25 Now concerning the believers from the Gentiles, we wrote and gave our ruling: they must guard themselves from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what is strangled and from sexual sin."
21:26 Paul did so. He took the men along the next day, was purified with them, and went into the temple. There he gave word of the date when their days of purification would be finished, the day the offering would be brought for each one of them.
## A riot in the temple
21:27 The seven days were about to run out when the Jews from Asia caught sight of him in the temple. They threw the whole crowd into turmoil and grabbed him,
21:28 yelling, "Israelites, help! Here is the man who goes everywhere teaching everybody against the people, against the law and against this place. On top of that, he has brought Greeks into the temple and made this holy place common."
21:29 They said this because they had already seen Trophimus, a man from Ephesus, in the city with Paul, and they took it for granted that Paul had brought him into the temple.
21:30 The entire city was set in motion, and people rushed in from all sides. They took hold of Paul and hauled him outside the temple, and right away the doors were shut.
21:31 They were out to kill him. But a report went up to the commander of the cohort: all Jerusalem was in chaos.
21:32 Without a moment's delay he took soldiers and centurions with him and charged down on the crowd. As soon as the people saw the commander and his soldiers, they quit beating Paul.
21:33 The commander stepped up and took hold of him, and gave orders to fasten him with two chains. Then he tried to find out who the man might be and what he had done.
21:34 But in the crowd people were yelling, one this, another that. With all the noise he could not find out anything for sure, so he gave orders to take Paul into the barracks.
21:35 By the time he got to the stairs, the crowd had turned so violent that the soldiers ended up carrying him.
21:36 The whole mass of people was coming after them, screaming, "Away with him!"
21:37 Just as Paul was about to be led into the barracks, he spoke to the commander. "Am I allowed to say something to you?" "You know Greek?" the commander said.
21:38 "So you are not that Egyptian, the one who started an uprising a while back and took the four thousand men of the Sicarii out to the wilderness?"
21:39 Paul said, "I am a Jewish man, a Tarsian from Cilicia, and a citizen of a city that is far from unknown. Please give me leave to speak to the people."
21:40 The commander allowed it. Paul, standing on the stairs, waved his hand to quiet the people, and a deep silence fell. Then he called out to them in Hebrew. He said:
## Paul speaks from the stairs
22:1 "My brothers and fathers, listen to me as I now give you my defense."
22:2 Hearing him address them in Hebrew, they grew quieter still. He went on:
22:3 "I am a Jewish man. I was born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but I grew up here in this city. I sat at the feet of Gamaliel and was schooled in every detail of our ancestral law. I was zealous for God, the same as every one of you is today.
22:4 I persecuted this Way, even to the point of death. Men and women alike, I put them in chains and handed them over to the prisons.
22:5 The high priest will back me up on this, and so will the full body of elders. I even got letters from them addressed to the brothers in Damascus. I was on my way there to arrest the people in that city too and bring them in chains to Jerusalem for punishment.
22:6 "But as I traveled and was coming close to Damascus, around midday, all at once a powerful light from heaven flashed all around me.
22:7 I dropped to the ground and heard a voice speaking to me: 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?'
22:8 'Who are you, Lord?' I answered. And he told me, 'I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you are persecuting.'
22:9 The men with me did see the light, but the voice of the one who spoke to me they did not hear.
22:10 'Lord, what am I to do?' I asked. The Lord answered me, 'Get up and go on into Damascus. Everything that has been laid out for you to do will be told to you there.'
22:11 The glory of that light had left me unable to see, so my companions took me by the hand, and that is how I came into Damascus.
22:12 "There was a man there named Ananias. He was devout, as the law measures it, and every Jew living in Damascus spoke well of him.
22:13 He came to me, stood at my side, and said, 'Brother Saul, see again!' At that same instant I could see again, and I looked up at him.
22:14 He said, 'The God of our ancestors chose you for this: to know his will, to see the Righteous One, and to hear a voice coming from his own mouth.
22:15 You are going to be a witness for him to all people, telling what you have seen and heard.
22:16 And now, why wait? Stand up and have yourself baptized, and have your sins washed away as you call on his name.'
22:17 "Later I came back to Jerusalem. As I prayed in the temple, a trance came over me,
22:18 and I saw him. He was telling me, 'Hurry! Leave Jerusalem without delay. They will not welcome what you testify about me.'
22:19 'Lord,' I said, 'they know for themselves that I used to go through the synagogues jailing and flogging people who believed in you.
22:20 When they were spilling the blood of Stephen, your witness, I was right there. I stood by, I agreed with it, and I kept watch over the cloaks of the men who were killing him.'
22:21 He told me, 'Go. I am going to send you out to the Gentiles, far away.'"
## A Roman citizen
22:22 They had listened to him until he said this. Then they began to shout at the top of their voices, "Take such a man off the face of the earth! It was never right for him to live!"
22:23 They kept screaming and throwing their cloaks off, and they tossed dust up into the air.
22:24 So the commander gave orders to take Paul inside the barracks. He said to get the truth out of him under the whip, so that he could learn the reason for all this shouting against him.
22:25 They had stretched him out and tied him with the straps when Paul spoke to the centurion standing nearby. "Does the law let you whip a Roman," he asked, "and one who has not been found guilty?"
22:26 Hearing that, the centurion went straight to the commander with the news. "What are you about to do?" he said. "The man is a Roman."
22:27 The commander came over to him. "Tell me," he said, "are you really a Roman?" "Yes," said Paul.
22:28 The commander replied, "It cost me a great deal of money to acquire this citizenship." Paul said, "I, though, was born to it."
22:29 At once the men who were going to question him stepped away from him. The commander was frightened as well, once he understood that Paul was a Roman and that he had tied him up.
## Before the Sanhedrin
22:30 On the next day the commander still wanted to know for sure what the Jews were charging him with. So he released him and ordered the chief priests and all the Sanhedrin to come together. Then he brought Paul down and had him stand in front of them.
23:1 Paul fixed his eyes on the Sanhedrin and said, "Brothers, right up to this day I have lived as a citizen before God with a conscience that is good in every way."
23:2 The high priest Ananias told the men beside Paul to hit him in the mouth.
23:3 Then Paul said to him, "God will be the one to strike you, you wall painted with whitewash! You sit there to judge me by the law, and then you break the law and give orders for me to be struck?"
23:4 The men standing near said, "You dare to abuse the high priest of God?"
23:5 Paul answered, "Brothers, I had no idea he was the high priest. It stands written, 'You will not say bad things about a ruler of your people.'"
23:6 Paul was aware that one part was made up of Sadducees and the other of Pharisees. So he called out in the Sanhedrin, "Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees! I am being judged because of hope and the resurrection of the dead."
23:7 The words were hardly out of his mouth when the Pharisees and Sadducees began fighting, and the gathering split in two.
23:8 Sadducees claim there is no resurrection, no angel, and no spirit. Pharisees affirm both.
23:9 The shouting grew loud. Some scribes who belonged to the Pharisees' side got up and fought it out: "We find nothing bad about this man. And if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel..."
23:10 As the fight grew fiercer, the commander feared they would pull Paul apart between them. He ordered his troops down to snatch him out of their midst and take him into the barracks.
23:11 That next night the Lord came and stood beside him. "Be brave," he said. "You have borne full witness to me in Jerusalem, and in the same way you must bear witness in Rome too."
## The plot of the forty
23:12 When daylight came, the Jews got together in a plot. They put themselves under a curse, swearing that they would neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.
23:13 Over forty men were part of this sworn plot.
23:14 They came to the chief priests and the elders and told them, "We have put ourselves under a curse, a solemn curse, not to taste a thing until we have killed Paul.
23:15 So here is what you should do. You and the Sanhedrin should send word to the commander to bring Paul down to you, as if you planned to look into his case more closely. We will be ready to do away with him before he ever gets close."
23:16 Paul's nephew, his sister's son, got wind of the ambush. He showed up at the barracks, went in, and told Paul.
23:17 Paul called over one of the centurions. "Bring this young man to the commander," he said. "He has news for him."
23:18 The centurion took him along to the commander and said, "Paul, the man in custody, called me in. He asked me to lead this young fellow to you. He has something he wants to say to you."
23:19 Taking him by the hand, the commander led him off to one side, out of hearing, and asked, "What do you have to tell me?"
23:20 He said, "The Jews have made an agreement. They will ask you to take Paul down to the Sanhedrin tomorrow, making out that they intend to question him more closely.
23:21 Don't go along with them. Over forty of their men are setting a trap for him, and they have put themselves under a curse to neither eat nor drink until they have done away with him. They are all set now. All they are waiting for is your promise."
23:22 The commander sent the young man away with an order: "Don't breathe a word to anyone that you brought this to me."
## By night to Caesarea
23:23 He summoned a pair of centurions and said, "Have two hundred soldiers ready to march as far as Caesarea, and seventy cavalry and two hundred spearmen, starting at the third hour of the night.
23:24 Have animals on hand as well for Paul to ride, so they can get him through safely to Felix, the governor."
23:25 He also wrote a letter, along these lines:
23:26 From Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor, Felix. Greetings.
23:27 The Jews had taken hold of this man and were on the point of killing him. I arrived with the troops and pulled him out, since I had found out that he is a Roman.
23:28 Because I wished to find out the reason for their accusations against him, I took him down to their Sanhedrin.
23:29 I discovered that the charges concerned disputes about their law. Nothing he was accused of called for death or chains.
23:30 Then I was tipped off that there would be an attempt on the man's life, so I sent him straight to you. I have also told his accusers to make their charges against him in your court.
23:31 The soldiers followed their orders. They picked Paul up and took him overnight as far as Antipatris.
23:32 The next day the foot soldiers went back to the barracks and left the cavalry to go on with him.
23:33 On reaching Caesarea, the riders handed the letter to the governor and brought Paul before him too.
23:34 The governor read it through and asked which province Paul came from. Told that it was Cilicia,
23:35 "I will hear you out fully," he said, "once your accusers have come too." He ordered Paul held under guard in Herod's Praetorium.
