## Ananias and Sapphira
5:1 There was a man named Ananias. He and his wife Sapphira sold some property.
5:2 He kept back some of the money for himself, and his wife knew about it too. He brought just a part of it and set it at the apostles' feet.
5:3 Peter said, "Ananias, how did Satan come to fill your heart? You lied to the Holy Spirit and held back part of what the land brought in.
5:4 The land was yours as long as you kept it, wasn't it? And once you sold it, the money was still yours to decide about. Why did you plan this in your heart? It was not people you lied to. It was God."
5:5 At these words Ananias fell to the ground, and his life left him. Great fear took hold of everyone who heard about it.
5:6 The younger men stood up, wound a cloth around him, took him out and buried him.
5:7 Some three hours passed, and then his wife came in, with no idea of what had happened.
5:8 Peter spoke to her. "Tell me, is this what the two of you got for the land?" "Yes," she said, "this much."
5:9 Then Peter said to her, "Why did you two make a pact to put the Lord's Spirit to the test? Look, at the door are the feet of the men who buried your husband, and they will carry you out."
5:10 Right then she dropped at his feet, and her life left her. When the young men came in, they found her dead. They carried her out and laid her in the grave next to her husband.
5:11 The whole church was struck with great fear, and so was everyone who heard of these things.
## Signs and wonders in Solomon's Porch
5:12 Through the apostles' hands many signs and wonders kept happening among the people. With one mind they all used to gather in Solomon's Porch.
5:13 None of the others had the courage to attach themselves to them. Still, the people praised them.
5:14 Even more people who trusted in the Lord were added, crowds of men and women alike.
5:15 People even brought their sick out into the open streets and set them on cots and sleeping mats. They hoped that as Peter went by, his shadow at least would pass over some of them.
5:16 People from the towns surrounding Jerusalem came flocking in as well. They brought those who were sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, and every one of them was healed.
## The apostles jailed and set free
5:17 Then the high priest rose up, and all his people with him, who made up the Sadducee party. Jealousy filled them.
5:18 They laid hands on the apostles and put them in the public lockup.
5:19 That night, though, the doors of the prison were opened by an angel of the Lord, who led them out and said,
5:20 "Go and take your stand in the temple. Tell the people every word of this life."
5:21 So, having heard this, at dawn they went into the temple and began teaching. Meanwhile the high priest and his circle arrived. They summoned the Sanhedrin, the full council of elders of the Israelites, and sent word to the prison to have the apostles brought in.
5:22 The guards went, but they did not find them in the prison. So they came back with the news:
5:23 "The jail was shut, and locked as securely as could be. The sentries were standing at the doors. Yet when we opened them, there was nobody inside."
5:24 This news left the commander of the temple and the chief priests baffled. What was going on, they wondered, and where would it end?
5:25 Then somebody arrived with a report: "Listen! Those men you locked in the prison are in the temple right now. They are standing there teaching the people!"
5:26 So the commander set out with the guards and brought them back. They used no force, since they feared the people might stone them.
## Before the Sanhedrin
5:27 They brought the apostles in and made them stand in front of the Sanhedrin, and the high priest began to question them.
5:28 He said, "We ordered you, and ordered you firmly, to stop teaching in this name. Instead, look: your teaching is all over Jerusalem, and you are set on making us answer for this man's blood!"
5:29 Peter and the apostles answered, "Obeying God has to come before obeying people.
5:30 The God of our ancestors raised up Jesus, the man you put to death by hanging him on a tree.
5:31 God has lifted this man to his right hand as Founder and Savior, so that Israel might be given repentance and forgiveness of sins.
5:32 We ourselves are witnesses of these events. The Holy Spirit is a witness too, the Spirit God has given to people who obey him."
## Gamaliel's advice
5:33 Hearing this tore at them, and they wanted to kill the apostles.
5:34 Then one member of the Sanhedrin stood up, a Pharisee named Gamaliel. He taught the law, and the whole people honored him. He gave an order to take the men out for a short time.
5:35 Then he spoke to them. "Israelites, watch yourselves. Think about what you are going to do with these men.
5:36 A while back, Theudas came forward. He said he was someone important, and some four hundred men sided with him. But he was killed. Everyone he had won over scattered, and the whole thing came to nothing.
5:37 After that man, Judas the Galilean appeared at the time of the registration, and he pulled people away to follow him. He died too, and the ones he had won over were scattered.
5:38 So here is my advice now: keep your hands off these men and leave them alone. If this plan or this work has a human source, it will be torn down.
5:39 But if it comes from God, you will not be able to tear them down. You might even turn out to be fighting against God."
5:40 Gamaliel won them over. They summoned the apostles, had them whipped, and warned them not to speak in Jesus' name. Then they released them.
5:41 They walked out from before the Sanhedrin glad at heart, because they had been found worthy to be dishonored for the sake of the Name.
5:42 Day in and day out, in the temple and in people's homes, they kept on teaching. They did not stop announcing the good news: the Messiah is Jesus.
## Seven chosen to serve
6:1 In those days, as the disciples kept multiplying, the Hellenists began grumbling against the Hebrews: their widows were being passed over in the daily serving.
6:2 So the Twelve called together the whole body of the disciples. "It is not good for us to set aside the word of God in order to serve tables," they said.
6:3 "Brothers, look among yourselves for seven men with a good name, men full of the Spirit and wisdom. We will put them in charge of this task.
6:4 As for us, we will hold fast to prayer and to serving the word."
6:5 The whole gathering approved this plan. They picked Stephen, who was full of faith and the Holy Spirit, along with Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert from Antioch.
6:6 They brought the seven to stand before the apostles. Then they prayed and laid their hands on them.
6:7 And the word of God kept growing. In Jerusalem the number of disciples multiplied enormously, and a big crowd of priests were obeying the faith.
## Stephen seized
6:8 Stephen, a man full of grace and power, was doing wonders and great signs among the people.
6:9 Some men stood up to argue with Stephen. They belonged to the synagogue called the Freedmen's, and to the Cyrenians and the Alexandrians, and some were from Cilicia and Asia.
6:10 They were no match for the wisdom and the Spirit he spoke with.
6:11 So they planted some men who said, "We heard him say blasphemous things against Moses and against God."
6:12 They also roused the people, the elders and the scribes. Then they rushed at Stephen, dragged him off and took him to the Sanhedrin.
6:13 They put up lying witnesses, who said, "This man keeps on talking against the holy place and the law.
6:14 We heard him say that this Jesus, the Nazarene, will tear down this place and alter the customs that Moses passed on to us."
6:15 Everyone seated in the Sanhedrin stared hard at him. They saw his face, and it looked like an angel's face.
## Stephen answers: Abraham and the patriarchs
7:1 Then the high priest said, "Is what they say true?"
7:2 Stephen answered, "My brothers and fathers, listen. While our father Abraham was in Mesopotamia, before he settled in Haran, the God of glory appeared to him.
7:3 He told him, 'Go out of your land and away from your kin, and come on to whatever land I show you.'
7:4 So he went out of the Chaldeans' land and made his home in Haran. After his father's death, God moved him on from there to this land, the one you live in now.
7:5 He gave him no share of it to inherit, not even room to plant a foot. Still, he promised to give it to him to hold, and to his offspring after him, though Abraham did not yet have a child.
7:6 What God said was this: his offspring would be foreigners living in someone else's land. The people there would make slaves of them and treat them badly for four hundred years.
7:7 'And whatever nation they are slaves to, I myself will judge,' God said. 'After these things they will come out and serve me in this place.'
7:8 And he gave him a covenant of circumcision. And so Abraham fathered Isaac and circumcised him on the eighth day. Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered the twelve patriarchs.
7:9 Out of jealousy the patriarchs sold Joseph away into Egypt. Yet God was with him.
7:10 He pulled him out of all his hardships and gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh, the king of Egypt. Pharaoh appointed him governor over Egypt and over his whole household.
7:11 Then famine hit the whole of Egypt and Canaan. The hardship was great, and our ancestors could not find enough to live on.
7:12 Jacob heard that grain could be had in Egypt, so he sent our ancestors off on a first trip.
7:13 The second time, Joseph let his brothers recognize him, and Pharaoh found out what family Joseph came from.
7:14 Joseph sent and called for his father Jacob and the whole clan: seventy-five people.
7:15 Jacob went down into Egypt. He came to his end there, and so did our ancestors.
7:16 They were moved to Shechem and put in the tomb Abraham had paid silver for, buying it from Hamor's sons at Shechem.
## Moses rejected and sent
7:17 The time of the promise God had openly made to Abraham was coming close. Meanwhile the people grew and became many in Egypt,
7:18 until a different king rose to rule Egypt, a king who knew nothing of Joseph.
7:19 This king outsmarted our people and treated our ancestors cruelly, making them put their babies out in the open, so that they would not live.
7:20 This was when Moses was born. He was a fine child in God's eyes. For three months he was nursed in his father's home.
7:21 Then he was put out, and Pharaoh's daughter picked him up and reared him to be her son.
7:22 Moses was schooled in all the wisdom of the Egyptians. He had power in his words and in his actions.
7:23 As he was turning forty, he made up his mind to go and see how his brothers, the Israelites, were doing.
7:24 He saw a man being treated unjustly and came to his aid. He paid back the wrong done to the beaten-down man by striking the Egyptian dead.
7:25 He expected his brothers to grasp that God was bringing them salvation by his hand. They did not grasp it.
7:26 On the following day he showed up while some of them were fighting, and he tried to bring them back to peace. 'Men,' he said, 'you are brothers! Why do you harm one another?'
7:27 The one who was in the wrong against his neighbor shoved Moses aside. 'Who put you in charge as ruler and judge over us?' he said.
7:28 'You don't mean to kill me, do you, the same way you killed that Egyptian yesterday?'
7:29 That remark sent Moses running. He went to live as a foreigner in the land of Midian, and there he fathered two sons.
7:30 Forty years went by. Then, in the wilderness of Mount Sinai, an angel appeared to him in a flame of fire from a thornbush.
7:31 Moses wondered at what he saw. He was moving closer to look carefully when the voice of the Lord sounded:
7:32 'I am the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob.' Moses began to shake and did not have the nerve to look closely.
7:33 The Lord said to him, 'Untie your sandals from your feet. The spot you are standing on is holy ground.
7:34 I have seen, yes seen, how badly my people in Egypt are treated. I have heard them groan, and I have come down to snatch them out. So come now: I am sending you to Egypt.'
7:35 They had disowned this Moses. 'Who put you in charge as ruler and judge?' they said. Yet this is the man God has sent to be both ruler and redeemer, with the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the thornbush to help him.
7:36 He is the one who brought them out, working wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, at the Red Sea, and in the wilderness for forty years.
7:37 This is the same Moses who told the Israelites, 'God will raise up a prophet for you from among your brothers, a prophet like me.'
7:38 He was there with the assembly in the wilderness, together with the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai and with our ancestors. He received living sayings to hand on to us.
## The calf, the tent and the house
7:39 Our ancestors had no wish to obey him. They shoved him aside and turned their hearts back toward Egypt.
7:40 They told Aaron, 'Make us gods that will go ahead of us. This Moses, who brought us out of the land of Egypt, we have no idea what has become of him.'
7:41 In those days they made a calf. They brought a sacrifice up to the idol and held a celebration over what their own hands had made.
7:42 So God turned and handed them over to serve the army of heaven, just as the book of the prophets has it written: 'You did not offer me slaughtered animals and sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness, did you, house of Israel?
7:43 You carried Moloch's tent along, and the star of the god Rephan, figures you made so you could bow down to them. So I will deport you to the far side of Babylon.'
7:44 In the wilderness our ancestors had with them the tent of the testimony. It was built the way the One who spoke to Moses ordered, following the model Moses had seen.
7:45 The next generation of our ancestors received it and carried it in with Joshua, when they took possession of the nations' land, the nations God pushed out ahead of our ancestors. So it was until David's days.
7:46 David found favor with God. He asked to be allowed to find a dwelling for the God of Jacob.
7:47 Solomon, though, built him a house.
7:48 But the Most High does not make his home in what hands have made. The prophet puts it this way:
7:49 'Heaven is a throne for me, and the earth is a stool for my feet. What sort of house will you build me, says the Lord, or what place could be my place of rest?
7:50 Was it not my hand that made all of this?'
## You always resist the Spirit
7:51 Stiff-necked men, uncircumcised in your hearts and in your ears! You are always fighting the Holy Spirit, just as your ancestors did. You are no different.
7:52 Was there one prophet your ancestors did not hound? They killed the ones who told in advance that the Righteous One would come. And you, now, have turned traitor against him and murdered him.
7:53 You were given the law by the orders of angels, and you never kept it."
## The stoning of Stephen
7:54 As they listened, it tore at their hearts, and they gnashed their teeth at him.
7:55 But Stephen was full of the Holy Spirit. He fixed his eyes on heaven and saw God's glory, with Jesus standing at God's right hand.
7:56 "Look!" he said. "I see the heavens opened wide, and the Son of Man standing at God's right hand."
7:57 They let out a great shout, held their ears shut, and with one mind they charged at him.
7:58 They drove him out of the city and started stoning him. The witnesses put their cloaks down at the feet of a young man whose name was Saul.
7:59 They went on stoning Stephen as he called out in prayer, "Lord Jesus, welcome my spirit."
7:60 He knelt down and shouted at the top of his voice, "Lord, do not count this sin against them!" And with these words he fell asleep.
