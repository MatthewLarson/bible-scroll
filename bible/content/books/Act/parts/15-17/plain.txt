## Must Gentiles Be Circumcised?
15:1 Some people came down from Judea and kept teaching the brothers this: "If you are not circumcised as Moses' custom requires, you cannot be saved."
15:2 This led to a serious quarrel and debate between them and Paul and Barnabas. So they arranged for Paul and Barnabas and some others from their group to go up to Jerusalem, to the apostles and elders, about this dispute.
15:3 The church sent them on their way, and as they went through Phoenicia and Samaria they told the whole story of the Gentiles' turning to God. And they made all the brothers very glad.
15:4 On their arrival in Jerusalem they were received by the church, the apostles and the elders. They reported how much God had done, working with them.
15:5 Then some from the Pharisees' party who had become believers rose to their feet. "It is necessary to circumcise them," they said, "and to order them to keep the law of Moses."
## The Meeting in Jerusalem
15:6 The apostles and elders came together to look into this matter.
15:7 When there had been a long argument, Peter got up. "Brothers," he said to them, "you yourselves know that from the first days God made his choice among you. It was through my mouth that the Gentiles were to hear the message of the good news and believe.
15:8 God, the knower of hearts, vouched for them. He gave them the Holy Spirit, as he had given him to us.
15:9 He drew no line at all between us and them; by faith he made their hearts clean.
15:10 So why are you now testing God, by laying on the disciples' necks a yoke that neither our ancestors nor we ourselves had the strength to carry?
15:11 Rather, it is through the grace of the Lord Jesus that we believe we are saved, the same way they are."
15:12 The whole gathering went quiet. They listened as Barnabas and Paul told how many signs and wonders God had worked among the Gentiles through them.
## James Gives His Judgment
15:13 After they fell silent, James responded: "Brothers, hear me.
15:14 Simeon has told how God first showed his care by taking from the Gentiles a people for his name.
15:15 The prophets' words are in tune with this, just as it stands written:
15:16 'After these things I will come back / and build again the fallen tent of David. / I will build again its torn-down parts, / and I will stand it up straight,
15:17 so that those who are left of humankind may seek out the Lord, / and all the nations over whom my name has been called, / says the Lord, who is doing these things,
15:18 things known from ages past.'
15:19 So my decision is this: we should not keep bothering those Gentiles who are turning to God.
15:20 Rather, we should write to tell them to keep away from the pollution of idols, from sexual sin, from what is strangled and from blood.
15:21 After all, for generations long past Moses has had his preachers in town after town: he is read aloud in the synagogues every Sabbath."
## The Letter to the Gentile Believers
15:22 Then the apostles and elders, together with the whole church, resolved to pick men from among them. They would send them to Antioch along with Paul and Barnabas. They chose Judas, known as Barsabbas, and Silas, men who led among the brothers.
15:23 They sent this in writing with them: "From the apostles and the elders, who are brothers, to the brothers of Gentile birth in Antioch, Syria and Cilicia: greetings.
15:24 We have heard that some who came from us have troubled you with their talk and thrown your souls into turmoil. We gave them no orders.
15:25 So we met and all agreed to choose men and send them to you, together with our dear Barnabas and Paul,
15:26 who have handed over their lives for the sake of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
15:27 So we have sent Judas and Silas. They will report the same things to you in person.
15:28 For the Holy Spirit has decided, and so have we, to place no heavier load on you than these necessary things:
15:29 keep away from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what is strangled and from sexual sin. If you keep yourselves clear of these, you will be doing well. Stay well."
15:30 The men were dismissed and went down to Antioch. There they called the whole body of believers together and handed over the letter.
15:31 Reading it, they were glad for the comfort it brought.
15:32 Judas and Silas were prophets too, and they spoke at length to urge the brothers on and make them firm.
15:33 They stayed for a while, and then the brothers let them go in peace, back to those who had sent them.
15:34 [The oldest manuscripts do not have this verse.]
15:35 Paul and Barnabas, though, spent time in Antioch. Along with many others, they taught the word of the Lord and told it as good news.
## Paul and Barnabas Part Ways
15:36 After a few days Paul said to Barnabas, "Come on, let's turn back and look in on the brothers, town by town, everywhere we announced the word of the Lord. Let's see how they are getting on."
15:37 Barnabas wanted to bring John, the one called Mark, with them as well.
15:38 But Paul did not think it fitting to take him along. The man had walked away from them in Pamphylia and had not kept on with them in the work.
15:39 The quarrel became so heated that the two split up. Barnabas took Mark along and set sail for Cyprus.
15:40 Paul picked Silas and set out, after the brothers had handed him over to the Lord's grace.
15:41 He traveled through Syria and Cilicia, making the churches stronger.
## Timothy Joins Paul
16:1 He reached Derbe, and Lystra too. And look, a disciple was living there, Timothy by name. His mother was a Jewish believer, and his father a Greek.
16:2 He had a good name among the brothers at Lystra and Iconium.
16:3 Paul wanted this man to set out with him. So he took Timothy and circumcised him, on account of the Jews living in that area, since everyone knew his father was Greek.
16:4 Going from town to town, they handed on the decrees decided by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem, for the believers to keep.
16:5 So the churches grew firmer in the faith, and day after day their numbers swelled.
## The Call to Macedonia
16:6 They crossed the Phrygian and Galatian country, and the Holy Spirit kept them from speaking the word in Asia.
16:7 Arriving opposite Mysia, they kept trying to travel into Bithynia, and the Spirit of Jesus would not let them.
16:8 So they skirted Mysia and came down to Troas.
16:9 In the night a vision appeared to Paul. A Macedonian man stood there, begging him, "Cross over to Macedonia and help us!"
16:10 As soon as he had seen the vision, we looked for a way to set out for Macedonia. We had worked out that God had summoned us to tell them the good news.
## Lydia at Philippi
16:11 Setting off from Troas, we ran a straight course to Samothrace, and on the next day to Neapolis,
16:12 and from there to Philippi, one of the foremost cities of its district of Macedonia, and a colony. We spent some days in that city.
16:13 On the Sabbath day we walked out of the gate to a river. We expected to find a place of prayer there. We sat down and began speaking with the women who had gathered.
16:14 A woman named Lydia was listening. She sold purple cloth and came from the city of Thyatira, and she worshiped God. The Lord opened up her heart to take in what Paul was saying.
16:15 She was baptized, and her household with her. Then she pressed us: "If you have decided that I am a believer in the Lord, come into my home and stay." And she would not take no for an answer.
## The Slave Girl with a Python Spirit
16:16 Once, on our way to the place of prayer, a slave girl came to meet us. She had a Python spirit, and her fortune-telling earned a lot of money for her masters.
16:17 She kept trailing behind Paul and the rest of us and shouting, "These people are slaves of the Most High God! They are telling you a way to be saved!"
16:18 She did this day after day. At last Paul, thoroughly annoyed, turned around and spoke to the spirit: "I order you, in the name of Jesus Christ, to come out of her!" It left her then and there.
16:19 Her masters saw that their hope of income had gone out with it. They grabbed Paul and Silas and hauled them into the public square, before the city's rulers.
16:20 They stood them before the magistrates and said, "These fellows are making serious trouble in our city. They are Jews,
16:21 and they are announcing customs that we, as Romans, are not allowed to take up or follow."
16:22 The crowd rose up against them too. The magistrates ripped the clothes off them and gave the order to beat them with rods.
16:23 They laid many blows on them and then threw them into prison, ordering the jailer to keep them under tight guard.
16:24 With orders like that, he threw them into the innermost cell and locked their feet in the wooden stocks.
## An Earthquake at Midnight
16:25 Around midnight, as they prayed, Paul and Silas sang God's praises, and the prisoners were listening in.
16:26 All at once a strong earthquake struck, and it rocked the prison to its foundations. Right away every door swung open, and the chains fell off all the prisoners.
16:27 The jailer was jolted awake. Seeing the doors of the prison standing open, he pulled out his sword, ready to take his own life, for he believed the prisoners had fled.
16:28 But Paul shouted at the top of his voice, "Do yourself no harm! Every one of us is still here!"
16:29 He asked for lamps, sprang inside, and shaking all over threw himself down at the feet of Paul and Silas.
16:30 He led them outside and asked, "Sirs, what do I have to do to be saved?"
16:31 They answered, "Put your trust in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household."
16:32 They told the word of the Lord to him and to everyone in his house.
16:33 Right then, in the middle of the night, he took them and washed off their wounds. Then without delay he was baptized, and all his people with him.
16:34 He led them up into his home and laid a table for them, and he celebrated with his entire household, since he had put his faith in God.
## Roman Citizens
16:35 At daybreak the magistrates sent the rod-bearers with a message: "Set those men free."
16:36 The jailer passed the message on to Paul: "The magistrates have sent orders for you to be let go. You can leave now. Go in peace."
16:37 But Paul told them, "We are Romans. They flogged us in front of everyone with no verdict against us, and they threw us into prison. And now they are pushing us out on the quiet? Certainly not! They can come here in person and lead us out."
16:38 The rod-bearers took this answer back to the magistrates. Hearing that the men were Romans, they grew frightened.
16:39 They came and appealed to them. Then they led them out and kept asking them to go away from the city.
16:40 Leaving the prison, they went into Lydia's home. They saw the brothers there and encouraged them, and then they went on their way.
## Thessalonica
17:1 They traveled the road through Amphipolis and Apollonia and reached Thessalonica. The Jews had a synagogue there.
17:2 Paul, following his usual practice, went in to join them. For three Sabbaths he held discussions with them, arguing from the Scriptures.
17:3 He opened up their meaning and laid out the evidence that the Messiah had to suffer and to rise again from the dead. "And this man is the Messiah," he said, "Jesus, whom I am announcing to you."
17:4 Some of them were won over and cast in their lot with Paul and Silas. So did a large number of Greeks who worshiped God, and many women of the first rank.
17:5 The Jews, though, burned with jealousy. They recruited some low men who loitered in the marketplace, gathered a crowd and set the whole city in a turmoil. They stormed Jason's house, trying to bring the two out before the assembly of the people.
17:6 Not finding them, they hauled Jason and some of the brothers in front of the city rulers, yelling, "These men who have thrown the whole world into turmoil have now come here too,
17:7 and Jason has taken them in as guests! They all break Caesar's decrees by saying that Jesus is another king."
17:8 By saying this they threw the crowd and the city rulers into alarm.
17:9 The officials made Jason and the others post a bond, and then released them.
## Berea
17:10 That same night the brothers wasted no time in sending Paul and Silas off to Berea. On arrival the two went to the Jews' synagogue.
17:11 The Jews here had a nobler spirit than the ones at Thessalonica. They welcomed the message with real eagerness, and day by day they studied the Scriptures closely to find out whether it was really so.
17:12 Many of them, then, came to believe, and so did a good number of Greeks, prominent women and men alike.
17:13 When the Jews from Thessalonica found out that Paul had announced God's word in Berea as well, they came there too, shaking up the crowds and throwing them into confusion.
17:14 At once the brothers sent Paul away to make his way to the coast. Silas and Timothy stayed behind in Berea.
17:15 The men taking Paul brought him all the way to Athens. They went back carrying his order that Silas and Timothy should join him as fast as they could.
## Paul in Athens
17:16 As Paul waited for them in Athens, his spirit was stirred to anger inside him when he saw how the city was smothered in idols.
17:17 So he argued in the synagogue with the Jews and those who worshiped God, and every day in the marketplace with whoever happened by.
17:18 Some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also took him on. Some asked, "What could this seed-picker be trying to say?" Others said, "He seems to be an announcer of foreign deities." They said this because he was telling the good news of Jesus and the resurrection.
17:19 So they took hold of him and led him to the Areopagus. "Can we learn what this new teaching of yours is?" they asked.
17:20 "The things you put into our ears sound odd to us, so we want to know what they are supposed to mean."
17:21 All the Athenians, and the foreign visitors staying there, had time for nothing except saying or hearing the newest thing.
## The Speech on the Areopagus
17:22 Paul stood up in the middle of the Areopagus and said: "Men of Athens, I see how very devoted to the gods you are, in every way.
17:23 As I went about, I took a good look at the things you hold sacred. Among them I found an altar with this written on it: 'To an unknown god.' So what you revere without knowing it, that is what I announce to you.
17:24 God, who made the universe and all that is in it, is Lord of heaven and earth. He does not dwell in shrines that hands have made.
17:25 Nor do human hands tend to him, as if he were short of anything. It is he who gives life and breath and all things to everyone.
17:26 He made every nation of humankind out of one person, to live across the whole surface of the earth. He fixed their set seasons and the limits of the places where they would live,
17:27 so that they would look for God, in the hope that they might grope for him and find him. Yet he is never far off from a single one of us.
17:28 For in him we live and move and exist. Even some of the poets of your own people have said it: 'For we are his offspring too.'
17:29 So if we are God's offspring, we should not suppose that the Divine looks like gold or silver or stone, a carving shaped by human craft and invention.
17:30 God has looked past those times of not knowing. But now he tells human beings, all of them in every place, to repent,
17:31 because he has set a day when he will judge the world in the right way, through a man he has marked out for this. And he has given everyone grounds for faith in it by raising him from the dead."
17:32 At the words "resurrection of the dead," some began to sneer. Others said, "We'll listen to you on this subject another time."
17:33 And so Paul left their company.
17:34 Some men, though, joined him and became believers. Among them were Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman called Damaris, and others along with them.
