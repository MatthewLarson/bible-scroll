# Hostile review: Jeremiah 39-45

Reviewer: separate agent (did not write the part). Checked against texts/orig/Jer.json, the writer's literal lines, texts/auto/Jer.json (Ketiv/Qere), `tools/dss.py` (2Q13 and 4Q72a, run directly), Swete's Greek via `tools/lxx.py`, the site's 2 Kings 25 and Jeremiah 52 Plain English Translation, and web checks. `VERBOSE=1 node tools/build.js --check Jer/39-45` reports "122 verses, 65 notes, 32 variants, 27 glosses, 7 chapter pages, 17 topic contributions" with no error or warning in this part. The 6 errors printed are all Ezekiel 16-19 source-key clashes (another lane); the one warning is Jer/book.json 1:1 (part 01-06). The proposed core `baruch` exists once (content/proposals/prophets/topics/baruch.json); parts 32-38 and 39-45 each only contribute a section to it, so there is no duplicate. No reader-facing text says "from memory" or "not rechecked".

## Verdict

The translation is mostly faithful to the Hebrew and in plain style, and the witness work is better than in earlier parts: I confirmed the 2QJer and 4QJer-d readings the writer cites (43:6, 43:9, 43:10, 44:13), the Greek minus of 39:4-13, and the main facts (BM 114789, Mishnah Taanit 4:6 with Bavli 28b, Herodotus 2.161-169, the Radak gloss). But there is one critical fabricated attribution (Josephus said to take Jeremiah and Baruch to Babylon), a wrong Ketiv in a variant entry, a wrong explanation of the 42:12 Greek reading, an undeclared Greek-derived word in the Plain English Translation (41:9), a quotation of Chronicles that does not match the site's own Plain English Translation, a false claim that four words recur together in four passages, and the drift audit is a script stamp rather than an audit (it logs "matched/kept (Hebrew-forced wording...)" for 72 verses with no reason, and several of those verses have the sentence shape of the ESV or NIV). Term and parallel alignment (42:10 "grieved", the call verbs, 39:5 against 52:9, "royal family" against 2 Kings "royal line") also need work. Fixable in one pass.

Count: 1 critical, 11 major, 14 minor.

## Critical

1. **Critical. notes.json 43:6 ("Nebuzaradan ... had left with Gedaliah").** The note says "Another view, found in Josephus (Antiquities 10.9.7), says Nebuchadnezzar later took both men [Jeremiah and Baruch] to Babylon." Josephus does not say this. Antiquities 10.180-182 (the passage cited; the 23rd year) says the king attacked Coele-Syria, the Ammonites and Moabites, then Egypt, killed its king, set up another, and took the Jews there captive to Babylon. It speaks only of "the prophet" foretelling it and names neither Jeremiah nor Baruch as carried off. (The idea that Jeremiah and Baruch went to Babylon is a later Jewish tradition, for example Seder Olam Rabbah; and Josephus's own account has Jeremiah staying in Egypt.) This is a misattributed source. Fix: delete the claim, or move it to the rabbinic tradition and cite it exactly (Seder Olam Rabbah 26, after checking). Cite Josephus as "Antiquities 10.180-182" (the 10.9.7 chapter numbering belongs to Whiston and should be given as such). The same Josephus citation in notes 41:1 and 43:11 (23rd year) is correct and can stay, but add "and says nothing of Jeremiah".

## Major

2. **Major. variants.json 42:20 (Ketiv/Qere); notes.json 42:20.** The Ketiv is given as הִתְעֵתִי "I have led astray". texts/auto/Jer.json says the Ketiv is `hit.'e.tei/m` "I caused to err / them", so the written form is a verb with the object "you" (I led you astray), not a bare first-person verb. The Hebrew in the entry is truncated (the final mem is missing), and the Plain English Translation-column wording "been led astray by me, at the cost of your lives" is not a rendering of anything. Fix: give the real Ketiv consonants from the auto file, translate "I led you astray" (the Qere "you led yourselves astray"), and make the note say the same. Also add Swete's Greek line as it is ("you acted wickedly against your own lives") only as a witness to the Qere's sense.

3. **Major. variants.json 42:12 "why".** It says "The Hebrew verb can also be read 'I will have mercy' with different vowels." It cannot: the consonants וְרִחַם are the third-person Piel/perfect "and he will have mercy"; "I will have mercy" would need וְרִחַמְתִּי. 2Q13 (preserved, unbracketed) reads exactly the Masoretic וְרִחַם. So the Greek ("I will have mercy ... I will bring you back") is a free rewording or a different Vorlage, and the claim that it is the same consonants is wrong. Fix: say 2QJer and the MT agree, the Greek makes God the sole actor, and the reason is unknown (probably the translator or a different text avoiding the awkward switch to Babylon's mercy). Delete the vowel argument.

4. **Major. plain.txt 41:9 "the big one King Asa had made".** The Hebrew הוּא אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה הַמֶּלֶךְ אָסָא has no "big" (the writer's own literal line has none). Swete's Greek 48:9 has "a great cistern" (φρέαρ μέγα); the word entered the Plain English Translation from the Greek without a variant entry and without a note, against "follow the Hebrew base" and the brief's rule on departures. Fix: remove "big", or if it is wanted for sense, drop it and put the Greek reading in the 41:9 variant (which now only covers the Greek's omission of "by the hand of Gedaliah").

5. **Major. plain.txt 40:5 "But Jeremiah still had not turned away."** The Hebrew וְעוֹדֶנּוּ לֹא יָשׁוּב means "while he had not yet turned back / returned." "Turned away" in this book is the idiom for apostasy (the shuv wordplay of 3:12-14; 8:4-5), so a reader will hear "had not turned away [from his stubbornness / from the offer]". The note says the Plain English Translation means "had not yet turned to leave", which the sentence does not say. Fix: "While Jeremiah was still not turning to go, Nebuzaradan said ..." (or similar), and keep shuv/shuvah visible: the next word is "Go back".

6. **Major. plain.txt 39:5 against 52:9 and 2 Kings 25:5-6.** The brief requires matching where the Hebrew is the same. 39:5 and 52:9 share וַיְדַבֵּר אִתּוֹ מִשְׁפָּטִים, rendered "there the king gave his judgments against him" (39:5), "he pronounced sentence on him" (52:9), "his case was judged" (2 Kings 25:6). "Against" is not in the Hebrew (אִתּוֹ is "with him"), and the gloss "put him on trial and sentenced him" is the sense, not the wording. Likewise וַיִּרְדְּפוּ ... וַיַּשִּׂגוּ is "chased after ... caught" here but "went after ... caught" in 52:8 and 2 Kings 25:5. Fix: align 39:5 to the 52:9 wording and 52:8 verbs (or change all three together and tell part 50-52 and the 2 Kings owner). Also add the note the brief asks for listing the differences between 39:4-10 and 52:7-16 / 2 Kings 25:4-12 (39:6 has "nobles" חֹרִים where 52:10 has "officials"; 39:8 has no Temple; 39:10 "vineyards and fields" where 52:16 has "to tend vines and to plow"). Right now only the Temple omission is discussed.

7. **Major. drift-audit.txt and plain.txt: the audit is not credible; some verses keep ESV/NIV sentence shape.** 72 of 122 verses are logged "matched/kept (Hebrew-forced wording or no close match)" with no verse-specific reason, which is the pattern earlier reviews found to hide drift. By my own comparison in the head (not quoting the versions), these verses follow a modern version's sentence build more than the Hebrew requires, and should be rebuilt from `literal.js` with a one-line reason logged for each:
   - 40:1: the two-sentence rebuild ("after Nebuzaradan ... let him go from Ramah. He had taken him there bound in chains, among all the exiles ... being marched off") matches the NIV's restructure, and "let him go from Ramah" repeats the ESV's phrase for שִׁלַּח ("sent"). The Hebrew is one clause: "after Nebuzaradan sent him from Ramah, when he took him, and he was bound in chains".
   - 40:2: "took Jeremiah and said to him ... spoke this calamity against this place" is the ESV's frame word for word apart from "chief" for "captain".
   - 40:4: "from the chains on your hands ... come, and I will ... If you do not want to come ... don't. Look, the whole land is in front of you" follows the NIV's "then don't. Look, the whole country lies before you"; "good and right to go" adds "think". 
   - 40:15: "Let me go and strike down Ishmael ... No one will know ... Why should he kill you? Then all the Judeans gathered around you would be scattered, and the remnant of Judah would perish" keeps both versions' "would be scattered ... would perish" conditional, which the Hebrew (indicative waw-consecutive questions) does not force.
   - 41:8: "Ten men among them said to Ishmael, 'Do not kill us! We have stores ... wheat, barley, oil and honey.' So he held back and did not kill them along with their companions" has the ESV's order and wording ("ten men among them", "stores", "did not ... with their companions").
   - 39:8: "The Chaldeans burned the king's house and the people's houses with fire, and they tore down the walls of Jerusalem" has the ESV/NIV build (house of the king and the house/houses of the people, then the walls). The Hebrew has the singular "house of the people"; "houses" is an interpretation.
   Also check 39:16-17 and 42:5-6 for the same shape. The fix is a real second audit with a reason per verse, not a re-run of the script.

8. **Major. notes.json 39:5 (the Chronicles quotation).** The note puts in quotation marks that Chronicles says Zedekiah "rebelled against King Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God". The site's 2 Chronicles 36:13 reads "Against King Nebuchadnezzar, too, he rebelled, though Nebuchadnezzar had put him under oath by God." The brief and CLAUDE.md say a note that quotes another book's verse uses the Plain English Translation exactly. (The build did not warn, but the rule stands.) Fix: use the 2 Chronicles Plain English Translation wording or drop the quotation marks and paraphrase.

9. **Major. notes.json 42:18 ("Four words for a ruined people").** The note says the same four words appear together "at 24:9, 25:18, 29:18, 42:18 and 44:12". They do not. The Hebrew of 24:9 is "terror (zeva'ah), evil, disgrace, byword, taunt, curse"; 25:18 is "ruin, horror, hissing, curse"; 29:18 is "curse (alah), horror, hissing, disgrace" with others. Only 42:18 and 44:12 carry the quartet 'alah, shammah, qelalah, cherpah in this form (and 44:8 a part of it). The card's "p" and "b" both repeat the error. Fix: list only 42:18 and 44:12, and say the other passages use related lists with different members.

10. **Major. Terms and cross-part consistency.**
    - 42:10 and the term: content/proposals/prophets/terms.json fixes nicham of the LORD as "change one's mind (about the harm planned)" (26:3, 13, 19; 15:6). The Plain English Translation at 42:10 reads "I am grieved over the harm I have done to you". The note explains the choice but not why it departs from the shared term. Either use "change my mind" with a note on the אל construction, or register a documented exception (the 42:10 Greek ἀναπέπαυμαι "I have been eased" is also unmentioned).
    - The call verbs: notes 33 and 65 build an argument on the verbs of 1:10 recurring ("rendered the same way throughout"), but the Plain English Translation of 1:10 is "pull up and pull down, to destroy and demolish, to build and to plant", 24:6 is "build them up and not demolish them ... plant ... not pull them up", while 42:10 and 45:4 say "tear down / uproot" (as do 18:7, 31:28). The English reader cannot see the echo that the note claims. Align 1:10 and 24:6 (parts 01-06, 21-25) or change 42:10, 45:4 and the notes.
    - "Disease" (deber): note 36 says the Plain English Translation's "disease" in every case. 21:9 and 38:2 render the same triple with "plague"; 29:18 has "disease". Align across parts.
    - "Loot": note 12 says the idiom is "loot" four times; 38:2 reads "prize of war" while 21:9 and 39:18/45:5 read "loot". Align 38:2.

11. **Major. "Where Christians differ" card at 44:17 is thinly sourced.** Orthodox and Baptist answers have empty source lists (`[]`); the Reformed answer cites the Westminster Confession and Heidelberg Catechism for worship only, then says "some Reformed writers have drawn a line ... others reject the comparison" with no named writer; the Lutheran answer rests on the Apology of the Augsburg Confession XXI. The brief and STYLE require each tradition's answer to be sourced to a catechism, confession or named teacher. Fix: name the Orthodox source (for example a liturgical text or a named teacher such as Bulgakov or Ware; check), cite a Baptist confession (2nd London Confession 22.2 on worship; check the number), and name the Reformed writers or drop the sentence. Consider adding Anglican (Article XXII; the Church of England has Marian feasts) since it holds a distinct view. Also the X line in the chapter "views" ("Catholic writers answer that the two have nothing in common") is an unsupported generalization; name a source or soften it.

12. **Major. variants.json 43:9, and the other Qumran/Greek rows.**
    - The 43:9 entry has three readings but no 2QJer row, though 2Q13 is the witness that matters: it reads `קח ... וטמנתם בפתח בית פרעה בתחפנחס`, that is, it has *no* "in the mortar, in the brick terrace" but does have "house of Pharaoh". That puts 2QJer with the Greek (which also lacks malben) against 4QJer-d and the MT. The "why" says this in prose but the readings table hides it. Add a 2QJer row.
    - The 4QJer-d row prints "[in the mortar in the brick terrace]" in the Plain English Translation column without brackets; those words (`[במלט] [במלבן]`) are restored in the transcription, so they are not the scroll's reading. Show only what is read.
    - 4Q72a at 43:7 reads `תחפחס` (Tahpahes) where the MT has `תחפנחס`: a Qumran spelling variant that is not recorded.
    - Greek 47:1 attaches "from Daman" (ὁ ἐκ Δαμάν) where the MT has "from Ramah" (probably Ramah misread as Daman): not recorded though Ramah has its own note.
    - Greek 48:1 has "son of Elasa" where the MT has Elishama, and lacks "one of the king's chief men": not recorded.
    - **40:9: the Greek reads "the servants (παίδων) of the Chaldeans", the reading of 2 Kings 25:24 (מֵעַבְדֵי), where the MT of Jeremiah has מֵעֲבוֹד "from serving".** This is the one real Greek reading in the Gedaliah oath that matches the parallel and changes the sense; the site's 2 Kings Plain English Translation says "Don't fear the Chaldeans' servants". No variant, no parallel note.
    - Ketiv/Qere in the auto file are only partly used. 39:12 (`אִם`, written, not read) is mentioned in a note but has no variant entry; 43:10 שפרור/שפריר and 43:11 are silent.

13. **Major. Missing parallel note for 2 Kings 25:22-26 and its differences.** The brief says to list each difference between the Jeremiah and the earlier 2 Kings text. No note does for 40:5-41:3, 41:10, 41:17-18. Differences the Plain English Translation already exposes and does not explain: 2 Kings 25:23 "Jaazaniah" for 40:8 "Jezaniah" (noted in 40:8, but only as a spelling); "servants" against "serving" (item 12); "one of the royal line" (2 Kings 25:25) against "who was of the royal family" (41:1) for the identical Hebrew מִזֶּרַע הַמְּלוּכָה, to be aligned; 2 Kings 25:25 lacks "chief men of the king", the meal, the 80 mourners, the cistern and Johanan's pursuit. A single note at 41:1 listing these would do.

## Minor

14. **Minor. notes.json 39:3 and sources.json `bm-114789-nabu-sharrussu-ukin`.** The tablet number, the 10th year of Nebuchadnezzar (595 BC), the receipt of gold for Esangila and Jursa's 2007 identification all check out (British Museum; BM 114789, acquired 1920). But the source string puts "Nabu-sharrussu-ukin, the chief court officer" in quotation marks as the tablet's words; the museum says "chief eunuch" (rab ša-rēši), and "Rab-saris" is the Hebrew form of the same title. Also "(Babylon ...)" as provenance is not given by the museum (the sources disagree: Sippar, Babylon), so say "provenance not stated". The note also never mentions that 39:13 gives a *different* spelling "Nebushazban the Rab-saris" for what is probably the same officer; the reader sees two Rab-sarises with different names. One sentence is needed.

15. **Minor. notes.json 43:7 (Herodotus).** "Herodotus mentions its garrison (Histories 2.30, 2.107)." 2.30 (Psammetichus's garrison at Daphnae) is right; 2.107 (Sesostris at Pelusian Daphnae) mentions the place, not a garrison. Say "2.30" for the garrison and "2.107" for the name, or drop 2.107. Also 44:30 note: "2.161-169" is right for Apries/Amasis and his strangling (2.169); fine.

16. **Minor. notes.json 44:17 and chapters.json 44.** The figurines are "the thousands of clay pillar figurines" in the note and "many hundreds" in the chapter deep text. Kletter's catalog has about 850. Use "many hundreds" in both.

17. **Minor. chapters.json 42 (J line).** "Jewish readers have noticed the contrast with the later Jewish settlements in Egypt, at Elephantine and Alexandria, where Deuteronomy 17:16 was read as a warning but did not stop the move." No Jewish source is named; it reads as an invented tradition. Remove, or name a source (for example a Talmudic discussion of Egypt: Bavli Sukkah 51b-52a on the Alexandrian synagogue is the usual passage, to be verified before use).

18. **Minor. topics.json `jeremiah`, "uncommon".** "Jewish and Christian tradition both say Jeremiah died in Egypt." The Jewish side is the Lives of the Prophets and some midrash; other rabbinic tradition has him taken to Babylon or returning. Say "the Lives of the Prophets, and Christian writers who follow it (Tertullian, Jerome), say he was stoned in Egypt; Jewish tradition is divided". The wording also needs the `l` link tag used in note 43:6.

19. **Minor. chapters.json 44, "spec" box.** The "Claude's theory" that Jeremiah's reply (44:21) answers the women's "we and our fathers, our kings and our officials" with the same list is close to the standard observation (Holladay and Lundbom both comment on the matched lists in 44:17 and 44:21); the brief says to use `spec` only if unpublished. Check these two commentaries; if the parallel is in print, relabel as `com` and cite.

20. **Minor. variants.json 42:1 and 43:2 "why".** The 42:1 reasoning ("Jezaniah son of the Maacathite appears in 40:8 and the Hebrew keeps the two chapters apart") confuses 42:1 (Jezaniah son of *Hoshaiah*) with 40:8 (Jezaniah/Jaazaniah son of the *Maacathite*). Many scholars read 42:1 as an error for "Azariah son of Hoshaiah" (43:2), as the Greek does (Azariah son of Maaseiah in both). Tell the reader that the Greek's single man may be the original, not a harmonization.

21. **Minor. variants.json 39:1 "why".** "The Greek has probably lost the word 'year' and the 'tenth month' in copying" is a guess stated as likely; the Greek also gives "ninth month" (a different count). Say the cause is unknown and that Ziegler's text was not checked here.

22. **Minor. chapters.json 39 (views, H).** Stray punctuation: "the chief court officer of 39:3,." Also the three-month shape of the claim "a tablet of 595 BC names Nabu-sharrussu-ukin" is fine, but say "generally identified" rather than "names" for the 39:3 man.

23. **Minor. Unsourced factual claims in notes.** 39:8 (a burned layer, arrowheads and ash in the City of David from 586) has no source key; 41:9 (cisterns cut in rock at Tell en-Nasbeh) is fine but unkeyed; note 30 says Chimham "was given land near Bethlehem" (2 Samuel 19:37-40; 1 Kings 2:7 do not say this; it is an inference from Jeremiah 41:17 and Gedaliah's text, so say so). 24. **Minor. Baruch and 2 Baruch labeling.** chapters.json 45 (J and X lines), notes 43:3 and 45:5 say "Baruch and 2 Baruch" were written in his name. Baruch is tier "dc" in works.json (accepted by Catholic and Orthodox churches) and 2 Baruch is tier "other"; say for whom each is Scripture, and use the `l` link tag for them as 43:6 does for the Lives of the Prophets.

25. **Minor. chapters.json settings.** Chapter 41 has no settings card, though it contains Ishmael's words to the mourners (41:6, 8) and the arrival of the 80 men (the eighty are a group with a purpose that needs "who were they and why"). The 43:8-13 card's "about 586 or 582 BC" should say why 582 is considered. The 39:15-18 card says Ebed-melech was "a court official"; the text says "the Cushite" and 38:7 "a eunuch/court official" is only in chapter 38: fine, but say where you get it.

26. **Minor. Weak "echoes".** chapters.json 40 echoes include Hebrews 11:24-26 and the X line links Luke 19:41-44 to Jeremiah's choice to stay: neither is a Jeremiah usage. Drop or label as the writer's association. Chapter 42 X line ties "build and plant" to 1 Corinthians 3:6-9 and 2 Corinthians 10:8: the 2 Corinthians 10:8 "building up, not tearing down" echo is real, the 1 Corinthians one is a general metaphor: label as parallel, not use.

27. **Minor. glosses.json.** "Rab-mag: a top Babylonian title; no one is sure what it meant" is fine. The 39:5 gloss ("put him on trial and sentenced him") is an interpretation of an idiom the Plain English Translation already renders as a paraphrase: if 39:5 is aligned (item 6), reword the gloss to explain the literal "spoke judgments with him".
