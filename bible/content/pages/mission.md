We believe everyone deserves the chance to explore ancient religious writings with the same depth of historical,
language and cultural understanding that scholars have.

For centuries, studying these writings in depth has meant learning ancient languages, buying expensive academic
books, or relying on religious authorities and scholars to explain them. Codex Collection is here to put that
knowledge within reach of anyone curious enough to look.

We are not trying to convince anyone that traditional interpretations are wrong. We are trying to give you what
you need to weigh them for yourself. So we don't tell you what you must believe. We give you the evidence,
explain what is known and what is still uncertain, and leave the conclusions to you.

## Don't replace one authority with another

Every translation involves interpretation, even one built on excellent scholarship. Choosing one English word over
another can change how you picture a passage. So instead of asking you to trust that our translation is correct,
we show you why a passage was translated the way it was, and what else it could say.

Take Genesis 1:2. The Hebrew word *ruach* can mean wind, breath or spirit. The phrase *ruach elohim* has been read
as "the Spirit of God," "a wind from God" and "a mighty wind." The choice changes how you picture the scene, and
the Hebrew alone does not settle it. Wherever a choice like that comes up, the note sits right in the text.

## Five commitments

1. **Accessible, not oversimplified.** The translations read at about an 8th-grade level. The notes explain hard
   scholarship in plain words without hiding the nuance that matters.
2. **Evidence first.** Claims about history and language are weighed by the evidence: manuscripts, archaeology,
   ancient languages. Religious interpretations get a fair hearing, and the views of different churches are set
   side by side without ranking them.
3. **Transparency over certainty.** When manuscripts disagree, scholars debate a meaning, or the historical
   record is incomplete, we say so plainly. What most scholars agree on is labeled as the consensus, along with
   who disagrees.
4. **Context without indoctrination.** We explain how Jewish, Christian and scholarly traditions understand a
   passage without asking you to adopt any of them.
5. **Your own conclusions.** We give you the tools to investigate, compare, question and decide, including
   conclusions that differ from ours.

## What you'll find in each writing

- **A Plain English translation** made from the original Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek or Ge'ez, checked against
  well-known English translations.
- **Color-coded notes** woven into the text: original words, history and culture, connections to other
  scripture, symbols, and the main schools of interpretation.
- **Manuscript differences.** Where the old copies disagree in a way that matters, you can see each reading,
  where it comes from, and switch the verse to read it.
- **Who it was first for, and why.** The original audience of each book, letter, sermon, law and prophecy.
- **"Where Christians differ."** Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist and other views side by side
  wherever they really conflict.
- **Topic pages.** One page per subject across the whole collection, filtered to the passage you just read.
- **Writings outside the Bible**, always labeled with who, if anyone, treats them as scripture.

## How it is made

The translations and notes are drafted with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, working from the
original-language texts and scholarly sources. Each book is then checked by a separate AI reviewer whose only job
is to find mistakes, and the problems it finds are fixed before the book is published. Ideas that are the AI's own,
rather than established scholarship, are labeled "Claude's theory," and you can hide them.

That process catches a lot, but it does not make the work infallible. We never call our translation the "most
accurate." We would rather show you our sources and our reasons, so you can check them. If you find a mistake,
we want to know.

## Our sources

- **Hebrew:** the Leningrad Codex (AD 1008, the oldest complete Hebrew Bible), as edited by STEPBible, with its
  marginal readings and other manuscript witnesses marked.
- **Greek:** the SBL Greek New Testament, compared with the Byzantine majority text and the Textus Receptus
  behind the King James Version.
- **English for comparison:** the Berean Standard Bible, King James Version, World English Bible, American
  Standard Version and Young's Literal Translation, plus links to others on Bible Gateway.
- **Word-by-word data** from STEPBible (Tyndale House, Cambridge) and **cross references** from OpenBible.info.

Every note names the scholarly works it draws on, so you can follow any claim back to where it came from.
