# Habakkuk: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-twelve.md and tools/brief-twelve2.md first. Book id `Hab`, 3 chapters, 56 verses (17, 20, 19). Prophets lane. One part, flat folder `content/books/Hab/`. One writer, who owns book.json. One review.md.

**Numbering.** English and Hebrew agree. Verify in texts/orig/Hab.json.

**Form.** A dialogue with God, not a message to a people: complaint (1:2-4), answer (1:5-11), second complaint (1:12-2:1), answer (2:2-5), five woes (2:6-20), a prayer-psalm (3), with the musical notes. Chapter 3 is headed "A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, according to *shigionoth*" and ends "For the choir director, on my stringed instruments" (3:19): the technical terms stay in the Plain English Translation with a gloss (*shigionoth*, *selah* at 3:3, 9, 13). Prose only in the headings; everything else ` / `. Habakkuk is a prophet who talks back; do not tidy that.

**Lenses: H, C, J, X, F.**
- H: Jerusalem under Jehoiakim after Josiah's death (609 BC), Egypt's defeat at Carchemish (605), Babylon ("the Chaldeans", *Kasdim*, 1:6; the term "Chaldeans" is in content/terms.json, note at first use) as the coming rod; the date c. 605-597 BC (Josiah and Jehoiakim: the Plain English Translation of 2 Kings 23-24 and 2 Chronicles 35-36 and Jeremiah 25-26 is the model); an earlier date (Josiah, Assyria as the oppressor? rare) and a later (the Greeks? with the Qumran pesher on the Kittim) are labeled.
- C: unity (ch. 3 later? it is in the Greek, Nahal Hever and MurXII but not in 1QpHab; the pesher stops at 2:20), the identity of the wicked in 1:4, 1:13 and 2:4-5 (Judahite oppressors? Assyrians? Babylonians? the "righteous" and "wicked" of 1:4 and 1:13), the text of 2:4 (below), the "tiqqun soferim" at 1:12 (a scribal correction by the Rabbis: the original "You do not die" became "we shall not die"; the Rabbinic tradition lists it among the eighteen: state the readings and the tradition's source, Mekhilta, Exodus 15:6, verify before citing), the antiquity of ch. 3 (Albright, Cross, Hiebert).
- J: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi on 1:12 and 2:4; Habakkuk 2:4 in the Talmud (Makkot 23b-24a: the 613 commandments reduced to six, three, two and finally one: "the righteous shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4): verify); Habakkuk 3 is the haftarah on the second day of Shavuot (Pentecost) in the Diaspora; the Targum Jonathan; Habakkuk 2:20 and 3:17-19.
- X: **Habakkuk 2:4 in Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11 and Hebrews 10:37-38 (Hebrews follows the Greek with a different order and adds "he who is coming will come")**; Acts 13:41 quotes Habakkuk 1:5 in the Greek form ("look, you scoffers, wonder and perish"), not the Hebrew "among the nations": note the difference and the pesher's *bogedim* "traitors" (1QpHab 2:1-10); Luther's whole theology of justification and Calvin; Augustine; Jerome; Origen; Cyril; Theodoret; the Habakkuk 3 liturgical use (Lauds in the Roman rite, Byzantine Holy Saturday Odes, Ode 4 of the Septuagint canticles: Ode 4 is Habakkuk 3:2-19 in the Greek Odes); Habakkuk 1:12-13 and the theodicy.
- F: 2:14 ("the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD", Isaiah 11:9), 2:3 ("it will surely come"), 3:3-15, 3:16-19.
