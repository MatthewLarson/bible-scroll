# James: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-nt.md, brief-pauline.md, brief-pauline2.md and brief-general.md first. Book id `Jas`. 5 chapters,
108 verses. Lenses H C L J T. One part, flat folder `content/books/Jas/`, one writer, owns book.json. Model:
content/books/Pro/ for the wisdom side (James is the New Testament's wisdom book), content/books/Jud/ for the form.

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1:1 | "James, a slave of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the Dispersion" |
| 1:2-18 | Trials and testing; wisdom asked of God; the poor and the rich; temptation from one's own desire; every good gift |
| 1:19-27 | Quick to hear; doers of the word, the mirror; the perfect law of freedom; pure religion: orphans and widows |

**Terms (with brief-general.md):** δίψυχος "double-minded" (1:8; 4:8, gloss "two-souled"); πειρασμός / πειράζω "trial"
(1:2, 12) and "tempt" (1:13-14), with the note that the Greek word is one; ἐπιθυμία "desire"; συναγωγή (2:2) "your
synagogue" with a note (the readings: a Jewish-Christian assembly, a meeting for judgment (Ward: verify), simply "your
assembly"); ἐκκλησία (5:14) "the church"; νόμος βασιλικός "the royal law" (love-your-neighbor topic aka); νόμος τέλειος
ὁ τῆς ἐλευθερίας "the perfect law of freedom" (1:25; 2:12, with "complete" the gloss: brief-general.md says τέλειος is
"complete" in James; the writer decides between "complete" and "perfect" for this phrase and records why); θρησκεία
"religion" (gloss "worship, religious practice"); μοιχαλίδες (4:4) "adulteresses" (literal, with the note on the
prophets' image of Israel as an unfaithful wife; the KJV's added "adulterers": a variant); ὁ κύριος σαβαώθ (5:4) "the
Lord of armies" (check how the site renders YHWH tsevaot and Romans 9:29, then follow it); προσευχῇ προσηύξατο (5:17)
"he prayed earnestly" with the Hebrew idiom note.

**Setting (H):** James "the Lord's brother" (Galatians 1:19; 2:9, 12; Acts 12:17; 15:13-21; 21:18; 1 Corinthians
15:7), leader of the Jerusalem church, killed in AD 62 (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200: verify the passage and the
year; Hegesippus in Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23: verify); "the twelve tribes in the Dispersion" (Jewish Christians
outside the land: literally, or the church as the new Israel: views); the poor laborers and absentee landowners of
5:1-6 (Roman Palestine and the Mediterranean world). **C:** authorship (James of Jerusalem; a later writer; a two-stage
view in which James' teaching was edited later (Davids: verify)), the date (among the earliest New Testament writings,
the 40s, or late first century), the language (good Greek for a Galilean: the arguments), the relation to Paul (James
answering Paul, answering a distortion of Paul, unaware of Paul, or Paul answering James: views, with named scholars),
the "straw epistle" (Luther's 1522 preface: "a right strawy epistle" in comparison with John, Romans, Galatians and 1
Peter; quote only from an opened text), the slight Christian content (Jesus named twice: 1:1; 2:1). **L:** paraenesis,
the diatribe (an imagined opponent, 2:18), catchword links between sections (Dibelius: verify), the vivid images
(waves, flower, mirror, bit, rudder, spark, spring, fig tree, mist, rust). **J:** James as Jewish wisdom (Proverbs,
Sirach, Wisdom of Solomon), the "two ways" and the evil inclination (*yetzer hara*, 1:14-15: views), the Torah kept
whole (2:10; rabbinic parallels: verify), the Akedah tradition that Abraham's faith was tested (1 Maccabees 2:52:
verify; Sirach 44:20), Rahab in Jewish tradition, Elijah's prayer, oaths. **T:** the canon (Origen first names it
as Scripture (verify), Eusebius lists it among the disputed books (verify), the Peshitta), Luther and the Lutheran
churches' later handling of James, the Council of Trent's use of James 2:24 (session 6: verify), the anointing of 5:14
(Trent session 14 on extreme unction; the Catechism 1510-1513: verify), and the Orthodox Holy Unction.
