# 2 Clement: brief for the 2 Clement writer

Book id `2Cl` (works.json `2-clement`, tier `early`). 20 chapters, 118 verses, no greeting (the text begins at 1:1,
"Brothers, we must think of Jesus Christ as of God"). In Lake's words (introduction, vol. 1 OCR lines 6724-6815, opened for
this plan) it is "a letter only in form, and scarcely in that, for the writer distinctly states (cf. Cap. XIX) that he is
reading aloud, and implies that he is doing so in a meeting for religious worship: it is thus clear that it is really more a
sermon than a letter". Its aim, Lake says, is "to inculcate a high Christology, a pure life, and a belief in the resurrection
of the flesh", and "it cannot have been written by the author of I. Clement". It names no author, no city and no event.

## Lenses (fixed)
- **H Historical-critical**: who wrote it, where and when, from Lake's three views (Harnack: Soter of Rome's letter to Corinth,
  about 166-174, Eusebius 4.23.11; Lightfoot: a Corinthian homily, the Isthmian games behind chapter 7; others: Alexandria and
  the Gospel of the Egyptians) and his date ("between 120 and 170 A.D. ... ±150 A.D. is most usually accepted"); how it came
  to stand after 1 Clement in A and C; anything later only from a work opened.
- **N New Testament and the sayings of Jesus**: the Gospel sayings it quotes, "another Scripture" (2:4), the sayings in no
  Gospel, its use of Paul and of 1 Peter (Lake's margin), and what it shows about which writings a church of the 100s read
  aloud as Scripture (only from sources opened).
- **P Preaching and practice**: a sermon read in worship (19:1, the "Readers"), repentance while there is time (8-9; 13; 16-17),
  baptism as "the seal" kept pure (6:9; 7:6; 8:6), self-control and the flesh (8-9; 14-15), almsgiving (16:4), the elders'
  warnings (17:3, 5), the church as spirit and flesh (14). How churches today use the sermon: only from sources that say so.
Use a lens only where it has something distinct to say.
