# 2 Thessalonians: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-nt.md, brief-pauline.md, brief-pauline2.md and brief-rom.md first. Book id `2Th`. 3 chapters, 47 verses (12, 17, 18). Lenses H C L J T. One part, flat folder `content/books/2Th/`, one writer, owns book.json. 1 Thessalonians is being written at the same time: its Plain English Translation does not exist yet. Translate 1:1-2 (nearly the same as 1 Thess 1:1) and 3:18 from your Greek and list the pair in your report.

**Setting (H):** the same church as 1 Thessalonians, soon after or later; the persecution; the claim that the day had come; the disorderly members. **C (full weight):** authorship: Paul soon after 1 Thessalonians (Wanamaker even put 2 Thessalonians first: verify), or a later writer imitating 1 Thessalonians (Wrede, Trilling, Menken: verify), with the arguments (close literary dependence and a cooler tone; a different eschatology from 1 Thess 5:1-3, signs before the end against "like a thief"; 2:2 and 3:17 read as a pseudonymous author's self-authentication, or Paul's real worry about a forged letter); the order of the two letters; the temple of 2:4 and whether the letter assumes the Jerusalem temple still stands (date). **L:** the apocalyptic form, the judgment scene, the chiasm proposals. **J:** Dan 7:25; 8:9-12; 9:27; 11:31, 36-37 (Antiochus IV) and 12:11 (Plains exist; quote exactly), Caligula's statue plan of AD 40 (Philo, *Embassy to Gaius*; Josephus *Antiquities* 18.261-309: verify), Isa 2:10, 19, 21 (1:9, "from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might"), Isa 11:4 (2:8), Isa 66:15 (flaming fire), Ezek 28:2 (the ruler of Tyre who says "I am a god"), Jewish apocalyptic of a final enemy (Belial in the Dead Sea Scrolls: verify). **T:** the restrainer's identity across history (the Roman Empire: Tertullian; Augustine's confession that he did not know (*City of God* 20.19: verify); the Holy Spirit and the church (dispensational); the preaching of the gospel; Paul himself; the archangel Michael; God's own restraint; the law), the man of lawlessness (a future individual; the papacy in Luther, Calvin and the Westminster Confession 25.6 (original 1646 text; later American revisions removed the line: verify); Nero redivivus; a corporate power; the preterist reading of AD 70), Catholic and Orthodox use of 2:15 for tradition, Protestant replies, the work ethic of 3:10 (Lenin's 1917 slogan, the Soviet constitution of 1936: verify; Captain John Smith at Jamestown: verify before citing; skip if unchecked).

**Places for full treatment:** 1:5-10 (eternal destruction, *olethros aionios*: annihilationist, eternal conscious punishment and universalist readings); 1:7 (revelation, *apokalypsis*); **2:1-12** (deep dive: *apostasia* as rebellion or "departure" (the rapture reading of some dispensationalists: say so, labeled); "the man of lawlessness" / "of sin" (variant); "son of destruction" (John 17:12 Plain English Translation: quote exactly); "takes his seat in the temple of God": the Jerusalem Temple, a rebuilt temple, the church, or figurative; *to katechon / ho katechon*; "the mystery of lawlessness is already at work"; 2:8 "the appearing of his coming"; 2:11 God sends the delusion); **2:13** ("firstfruits" *aparchen* against "from the beginning" *ap' arches*: one letter's difference; a full variant with the editions); **2:15** (*paradoseis*); 3:6-15 (the disorderly: idle, or unruly; the end-time enthusiasm explanation and the patronage explanation: views); 3:14-15 (church discipline: "do not regard him as an enemy"); 3:17 (Paul's own hand).
