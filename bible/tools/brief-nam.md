# Nahum: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-twelve.md and tools/brief-twelve2.md first. Book id `Nam`, 3 chapters, 47 verses (15, 13, 19). Prophets lane. One part, flat folder `content/books/Nam/`. One writer, who owns book.json. One review.md.

**Numbering.** English 1:15 = Hebrew 2:1, and English 2:1-13 = Hebrew 2:2-14 (Hebrew chapter 1 has 14 verses). Verify in texts/orig/Nam.json; explain at 1:15 and in `facts`. Chapter 3 is the same.

**Form.** Poetry throughout. 1:1 is the heading, "the oracle (*massa'*) of Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite"; 1:2-8 is a partial acrostic hymn (the first half of the Hebrew alphabet in 1:2-8; Gunkel's old theory, with a damaged text; later authors doubt it: give the views); 1:9-2:2 turns between Judah and Nineveh; 2:3-13 is the fall; 3:1-19 is the woes. The book is in the form of a victory song and a funeral song.

**Lenses: H, C, J, X** (F dropped on purpose: the book looks at a city's fall already past or imminent in its own time, and the churches' use is as a type; say so in book.json).
- H: the Assyrian empire's end. Thebes (No-amon, 3:8-10) fell to Ashurbanipal in 663 BC, so the book is after that (the commonest view); Nineveh fell in 612 BC to the Babylonians and Medes (the Babylonian Chronicle, "Fall of Nineveh Chronicle", ABC 3; 2 Kings 23:29 for Josiah's death; Zephaniah 2:13-15; Tobit 14; Diodorus (Ctesias) on the Tigris flood: label as later and not a source of the Plain English Translation); 1:12-15 a lifting of the Assyrian yoke from Judah; the date between 663 and 612 (the commonest 650s-620s), with the Elkosh debate (Galilee? Judah? Mesopotamia: Alqosh? Capernaum "village of Nahum"? Jerome's tradition; call it unknown).
- C: the acrostic, the unity (1:2-8 as a separate hymn), the date, Nahum as a prophet of "false prophecy" or of cult (Haldar), the relation to Isaiah 52:7 at 1:15 and to Jonah, and the violence of 3:5-7 (the nakedness and shame of the city as a woman: same debate as Hosea 2 in the Plain English Translation of Hosea; do not soften it and do not defend it).
- J: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum Jonathan on Nahum, the haftarah use (check), Nahum in Talmud (Megillah, Sanhedrin? verify exact citations), Nahum and the destruction of Sennacherib's army (Isaiah 37), Nahum 1:15 as Messianic ("the herald").
- X: Jerome on Nahum, Cyril of Alexandria, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret, Calvin on Nahum, Luther; Nahum 1:15 in Romans 10:15 (Isaiah 52:7 is the actual source in Paul: say so); the church's puzzle over a book of unrelieved vengeance after Jonah (Nahum is the second word on Nineveh a century and a half later).
- Qumran: the Nahum Pesher (4QpNah, 4Q169) is the only pesher that names historical people: "Demetrius king of Greece" and "Antiochus", "the Lion of Wrath" (understood as Alexander Jannaeus) and "those who seek smooth things" (the Pharisees, on the usual reading): read the printout with `tools/dss.py Nam <ch>` and report only what it shows, in a note of type `echo` on 2:12-14 (Hebrew 2:13-14) and 3:1-4.

**Places for full treatment.**
- 1:2-8 "a jealous and avenging God" (*'el qanno' venoqem*; *qannoh* "jealous", *naqam* "avenge": the Plain English Translation must not make God petty; Exodus 20:5; Deuteronomy 32:35-43), 1:3 "slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty" (the creed of Exodus 34:6-7 recast with the mercy clauses dropped: the Plain English Translation of Exodus is the model; list the differences from Joel 2:13 and Jonah 4:2, whose Plain English Translation is the model); the theophany (1:3-6: whirlwind, storm; the sea and rivers dry, as at Exodus 14 and Joshua 3); 1:7 "good... a refuge in a day of trouble... he knows those who take refuge in him".
- 1:9-14 the Hebrew is hard and the speaker shifts between Nineveh, the plotter "against the LORD" (*yoetz beliya'al*, "worthless counselor"), and Judah; 1:12 "Though they are at full strength and many... I have afflicted you, I will afflict you no more" (the Plain English Translation must not guess who "you" is: Judah in the common view; give the views).
- 1:15 (Hebrew 2:1) "Look! On the mountains, the feet of one who brings good news" (*mevasser*; Isaiah 52:7 is the model; Romans 10:15 and the Greek *euangelizomenou*; "Judah, celebrate your festivals, pay your vows").
- 2:1-13 (Hebrew 2:2-14) the destroyer (*mefits*) comes up against the city; 2:3-5 the red shields, chariots like torches, the Nineveh gate and the river gates opened (2:6: the flood; Babylonian Chronicle; Diodorus); 2:7 "Huzzab" (*hutsab*: a name, a verb or a queen? hard: variants); 2:8 the pool; 2:11-13 the lions' den (Nineveh as a lion; Assyrian lion imagery: palaces of Nineveh).
- 3:1-7 "Woe to the city of blood" (*hoy*, the Plain English Translation's term), the harlot with her sorceries (3:4: Isaiah 23:15-17 for Tyre; Revelation 17: Babylon, see the Plain English Translation of Rev only to say they share the image); 3:5-6 shame and filth (Jeremiah 13:26; Isaiah 47:3; Ezekiel 16:37: the Plain English Translation of each is the model); 3:8-10 No-amon (Thebes) fell though she was strong (the Nile; Cush and Put); 3:11-19 the fortress figs, the locusts (3:15-17: compare Joel's Plain English Translation), the shepherds asleep, "your wound is not healed" and the closing question "who has not suffered your endless cruelty?" (3:19, the book's last words; Jonah 4:11's question and this answer: the book is a pair).
