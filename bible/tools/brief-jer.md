# Jeremiah: shared brief for the part writers

Book id `Jer`. This book belongs to the **prophets lane** (tools/lanes.md): you write only in content/books/Jer/, and shared-file additions go in content/proposals/prophets/. Nine parts, all written at the same time (no part can see another's notes):
- 01-06: the call, the first oracles against Judah, the foe from the north. **This writer owns book.json.**
- 07-12: the temple sermon, the Queen of Heaven, idols and the living God (10, with its Aramaic verse 10:11), the broken covenant and the first plot against Jeremiah.
- 13-20: the linen loincloth, drought and lament, the confessions (15:10–21; 17:14–18; 18:18–23; 20:7–18), no marriage for the prophet, the potter, the broken jar, Pashhur.
- 21-25: words to the last kings (21–22), the righteous Branch (23:1–8), the false prophets (23:9–40), the good and bad figs, the seventy years and the cup of wrath (25).
- 26-31: the temple sermon trial (26), the yoke and Hananiah (27–28), the letter to the exiles (29), the Book of Consolation (30–31) with the new covenant (31:31–34).
- 32-38: the field at Anathoth (32), the promises of 33 (33:14–26, which the Greek lacks), Zedekiah and the slaves (34), the Rechabites (35), the burned scroll (36), Jeremiah in prison and the cistern (37–38).
- 39-45: the fall of Jerusalem (39), Gedaliah and his murder (40–41), the flight to Egypt (42–43), the Queen of Heaven again (44), the word to Baruch (45).
- 46-49: the oracles against the nations: Egypt, Philistia, Moab, Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar and Hazor, Elam.
- 50-52: the oracle against Babylon, Seraiah's scroll thrown in the Euphrates (51:59–64), and the fall of Jerusalem retold (52).

**Brief and drift audit.**
- Follow tools/agent-writer.md exactly, with STYLE.md and PLAN.md: the drift audit, the References rule, "Who it was for, and why", "Where Christians differ" and "Books outside the 66".
- Compare against the NIV and ESV only in your head. Never write out their text; log only verse number, outcome and a few words of reason.
- Rebuild every verse from `node tools/literal.js Jer <ch>`. Familiar lines (1:5; 17:9; 29:11; 31:31–34; 33:3; Lamentations-like laments) may stay only where the Hebrew forces them. Isaiah's reviews still found 5 to 25 drifted verses in every part, often with a modern version's sentence shape or an added word; audit honestly, line by line, and check the sentence shape, not only the words.

**Your part.**
- Write plain.txt, notes.json, variants.json, glosses.json, chapters.json, topics.json and sources.json in content/books/Jer/parts/<your part>/ for your chapters only. Read STYLE.md "Large books written in parts".
- The lenses are fixed in book.json: H (History and fulfillment), C (Critical), J (Jewish tradition), X (Christian), F (Still to come). Only the 01-06 writer edits book.json.
- book.json covers: Jeremiah and his times (Josiah to the exile in Egypt; Anathoth and the priests of Abiathar's line; Baruch the scribe); how the book was made (the poetry, the prose sermons, the stories, the scroll of 36; Duhm and Mowinckel's sources A, B and C and the later debate; Baruch's part); **the two editions**: the Hebrew (Masoretic) text and the shorter Greek, about one-seventh shorter with the oracles against the nations after 25:13 and in another order, and the Qumran copies (4QJer-b and -d agree with the Greek's shorter text, 4QJer-a, -c and -e with the Masoretic), with Tov's and Bogaert's view that the Greek translates an earlier edition and the minority view that the Greek abridged; the chronology (name the one used; match the site's 2 Kings and content/topics/regnal-chronology.json); the book in Judaism (the Ninth of Av, Bava Batra 15a's tradition that Jeremiah wrote Kings and Lamentations), in the New Testament and in the church; and the books later linked to his name (Lamentations, Baruch, the Letter of Jeremiah, 2 Baruch, 4 Baruch / Paralipomena of Jeremiah: use the works.json names, add new works to content/proposals/prophets/works.json).
- The other writers may wait on book.json's lead, facts and audience before `--check` is fully clean, so the 01-06 writer fills those first, before translating.

**Numbering.** plain.txt uses English verse numbers. The Masoretic Text and English agree almost everywhere in Jeremiah (texts/orig/Jer.json `heb` says where not). The Greek numbers chapters 25–51 differently; when you cite the Greek, give its chapter and verse as well ("Greek 32:15", Swete's numbering; `python3 tools/lxx.py` explains the map and has a find mode).

**Poetry and prose.** Jeremiah mixes poetry (most of 2–20, 30–31, 46–51) and prose (the sermons and stories). Poetry: one verse per line, ` / ` between poetic lines. Prose: no ` / `. Follow the Hebrew layout of the Leningrad Codex and the scholarly consensus on which verses are verse; a verse set as poetry in a prose chapter gets a note only if it matters. Keep Jeremiah's wordplays where English can (1:11–12 shaqed/shoqed "almond/watching"; 23:33 massa' "oracle/burden"; 19:7 baqqoti/baqbuq "I will empty/jar"; the atbash ciphers Sheshach for Babel, 25:26; 51:41, and Leb-qamai for Kasdim, 51:1).

**Terms.** Re-read content/terms.json and content/proposals/prophets/terms.json before you start, and follow both: koh amar YHWH "The LORD says this"; ne'um YHWH "the LORD declares"; massa' "oracle"; hoy "Woe to"; "Daughter Zion"; bat 'ammi "my dear people"; "the Chaldeans"; torah "teaching" or "law" by context as the Isaiah entry says; "the LORD of Armies"; "the Lord GOD"; hesed "loyal love"; Sheol; 'elilim; 'iyyim "the islands". Jeremiah's own words: shuv "turn/return" (Jeremiah's great pun on turning back and turning away, 3:12–14, 22; 8:4–5); sheqer "lie/falsehood"; berit "covenant"; tophet "Topheth"; malkat hashamayim "the Queen of Heaven". If you need a new shared term, check both files and the other lanes' proposals, then add it to content/proposals/prophets/terms.json with a short read-modify-write and say so in your report.

**Textual evidence.**
- The Leningrad Codex is the base. Use texts/auto/Jer.json for Ketiv/Qere and marked readings.
- **The Greek** is a witness of the first rank in Jeremiah, not just a free translation. `python3 tools/lxx.py Jer <greek ch>` prints Swete (Codex Vaticanus; Swete's numbering), and `python3 tools/lxx.py Jer find <word>` finds a passage. On this site "the Greek" means the critical text (Ziegler's Göttingen Jeremiah, 1957, or Rahlfs); a reading only Swete's codex has is named by that codex.
- **How to show the Greek's minuses.** The Greek lacks about 2,700 words of the Hebrew. Give a variant entry (with `plain` "" for an omission) wherever a whole verse or a clause with content is missing (for example 10:6–8, 10:10; 17:1–4; 27:7, 27:19–22; 29:16–20; 33:14–26; 39:4–13; 51:44b–49a; 52:2–3, 52:15, 52:27b–30), and wherever the Greek reads differently in substance. Do not make an entry for each missing "the LORD declares", name, title or epithet ("Nebuchadnezzar", "the prophet", "king of Babylon"): summarize those for each chapter in one chapter-page paragraph, with a few examples. Say which edition each Qumran copy supports where it survives.
- **Qumran.** Read the scrolls yourself: `.cache/dl/tfenv/bin/python tools/dss.py Jer <ch>` (run from bible/; its header explains the marks; restored words in brackets are never a scroll's reading; "no scroll preserves this verse" can mean a scroll lacks it, as 4QJer-b lacks 10:6–8 and 10:10). The copies are 2QJer, 4QJer-a to -e (4Q70–4Q72b).
- The Targum of Jeremiah (Targum Jonathan; Hayward's translation in The Aramaic Bible, 1987), the Vulgate and the Peshitta are later witnesses. Sefaria's texts of Rashi, Radak and the Targum can be read with WebFetch on sefaria.org (Bash cannot reach it).
- Say once, on your first chapter page, which witnesses you read yourself. No reader-facing text may say "from memory", "not rechecked" or "I have seen it myself": verify, or write "reported by <source>".

**Lessons from the Isaiah reviews** (apply them from the start):
- Witness evidence must be exact. For each variant, list every scroll that preserves the place, including those that agree with the Masoretic Text, and never say "the Greek supports" a reading until you have checked that the Greek's wording comes from that Hebrew. Isaiah had one variant with the Greek on the wrong side.
- Depart from the Masoretic Text only on strong evidence (more than one independent witness, or a clear scribal slip), and say plainly when the witnesses are split. Otherwise follow the MT and give the other reading as a variant.
- A "Where Christians differ" card needs a real conflict between churches' teaching, not a split among interpreters inside every church. Give every major view, including the ones easy to forget (postmillennial on the millennium; Lutheran beside Reformed), and source each answer to that church's own catechism, confession or named teachers.
- Do not say what "most modern translations" or "most scholars" do without checking.
- Name the medieval Jewish commentators' readings exactly; they often disagree with one another (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimchi and Abravanel each read Isaiah 42:1 differently). Read them on Sefaria when you can.
- Settings cards must be specific to the passage: who heard these words, when and why, never a repeated stock paragraph. Jeremiah gives many dates (1:2; 3:6; 21:1; 25:1; 26:1; 27:1; 28:1; 32:1; 35:1; 36:1; 39:1; 45:1; 46:2; 49:34; 51:59): use them, and where the book's order or a heading's date is debated, give the views. Add `readers` for the exiles and later readers who read the collected book.
- One chronology for the kings, named, matching the site's Kings.
- The fall of Jerusalem is "586 BC", as in the site's 2 Kings; where it matters, say once that some scholars date it 587 BC.

## Lead decisions (after the writers, before the fixes; every fixer applies these)

- **dever/deber:** "disease" everywhere in Jeremiah ("sword, famine and disease": 14:12; 21:7, 9; 24:10; 27:8, 13; 29:17-18; 32:24, 36; 34:17; 38:2; 42:17, 22; 44:13). 21:9 and 38:2 change from "plague" to "disease". "Plague" is Ezekiel's word (Leviticus 26:25 model) and is not changed there.
- **"Terror all around"** (magor missaviv): the same English at 6:25, 20:3, 20:10, 46:5, 49:29 (6:25 changes to match the shared term).
- **Wedding phrase** (7:34; 16:9; 25:10; 33:11): "the sound of the groom and the sound of the bride", every time (qol = "sound").
- **"The LORD declares" vs "says":** ne'um YHWH is always "the LORD declares"; koh amar YHWH is "The LORD says this". 49:18 (Hebrew `amar`) stays "the LORD says" and 50:40 (Hebrew `ne'um`) stays "the LORD declares": the English follows the Hebrew, so the two differ there on purpose (correction by the lead; an earlier draft of this decision said to change 49:18).
- **Earlier text is the model** for parallel pairs (6:22-24/50:41-43; 10:12-16/51:15-19; 16:14-15/23:7-8 (16 is model); 23:5-6/33:15-16 (23 is model); 23:19-20/30:23-24 (23 is model); 30:10-11/46:27-28 (30 is model); 49:18/50:40 (49 is model); 39:1-10/52:4-16 (39 is model)). The later part changes its Plain to the earlier's wording where the Hebrew is the same, and each remaining difference is named in a note.
- **Sources and 'from memory':** a fact that cannot be verified is removed or written as "reported by <named source>". Never "from memory".
- **Greek:** "the Greek" means Swete's Vaticanus unless Ziegler was read; say so on each part's first chapter page.
