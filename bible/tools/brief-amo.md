# Amos: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-twelve.md first. Book id `Amo`, 9 chapters, 146 verses (15, 16, 15, 13, 27, 14, 17, 14, 15). Prophets lane. One part, flat folder `content/books/Amo/`. The writer owns book.json. One review.md.

**Shape (use these unit names in the outline and settings).** Superscription and motto (1:1-2); oracles against the nations (1:3-2:16: Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, Moab, Judah, Israel; "for three rebellions, and for four"); three "Hear this word" sermons (3, 4, 5-6) with the woes (5:18; 6:1); five visions (7:1-3 locusts; 7:4-6 fire; 7:7-9 plumb line; 8:1-3 summer fruit; 9:1-4 the LORD beside the altar) with the Amaziah interlude (7:10-17); the epilogue of restoration (9:11-15).

**Lenses:** H, C, J, X, F.
- H: Amos of Tekoa preaching at Bethel in the long peace of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:23-29) and Uzziah (2 Chronicles 26), "two years before the earthquake" (1:1; Zechariah 14:5; excavation evidence of an 8th-century earthquake: cite only if you can name the source); land accumulation, debt slavery, bribery, false weights (2:6-8; 8:4-6); the Assyrian threat (4:2-3; 5:27; 6:14).
- C: composition (the prophet's own oracles; Judean additions argued for 1:9-12, 2:4-5, the hymns 4:13, 5:8-9, 9:5-6, and the epilogue 9:11-15; Wolff, Paul, Andersen-Freedman, Jeremias, Hayes, Coggins), the oracles' rhetoric, the visions, the date of 1:1.
- J: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum; Talmud (verify any citation, e.g. Makkot 24a on Amos 5:4); haftarot (verify from a printed source or leave out).
- X: Acts 7:42-43; Acts 15:15-18; Jerome, Cyril, Theodoret, Luther, Calvin; the social gospel; Martin Luther King, Jr.'s use of 5:24 (cite only a source you can name); Catholic social teaching; liberation theology; Quaker and Anabaptist readings.
- F: the day of the LORD (5:18-20), the booth of David (9:11), the restoration (9:13-15): dispensational, amillennial and Jewish readings.

**Places for full treatment.**
- 1:1 *noqedim* "sheep breeders" (2 Kings 3:4); 7:14 "I am no prophet, nor the son of a prophet, but a herdsman and one who tends sycamore figs": the Hebrew has no verb, so "I was no prophet" or "I am no prophet" changes the sense; weigh and state, other reading in a note. 1:2 the LORD roars from Zion (= Joel 3:16, align).
- 1:3-2:16 each crime (threshing Gilead with iron sledges; deporting a whole people; forgetting the brothers' covenant; ripping open pregnant women; burning Edom's king's bones to lime; Judah rejecting the LORD's teaching; Israel selling the righteous for silver, trampling the poor, father and son with the same girl, garments taken in pledge, Nazirites made to drink and prophets silenced), 2:9-11 the Amorites, 2:13 the cart; the formula "for three rebellions, and for four" (numerical-sayings; Proverbs 30); the historical references (Hazael, 2 Kings 10:32-33; 13:3-7; Philistine slave trade; Tyre's treaty, 1 Kings 5; 9:11-14; Edom; Ammon's raids on Gilead).
- 3:1-8 "You only have I known" (election with moral consequence), the cause-and-effect questions, "The lion has roared; who will not fear?"; 3:12 the shepherd saving two legs or a piece of an ear; 3:9-15 Samaria's palaces, Bethel's altars. 4:1 "cows of Bashan"; 4:4-5 "Go to Bethel and rebel"; 4:6-11 the refrain "yet you did not return to me" and the five disasters; 4:12 "Prepare to meet your God, Israel"; 4:13 hymn.
- 5:1-3 the funeral song; 5:4-6 "Seek me and live", not Bethel, Gilgal or Beersheba; 5:7 justice turned to wormwood; 5:8-9 Pleiades and Orion (*kimah*, *kesil*; Job 9:9; 38:31); 5:10-13 the gate and bribes; 5:14-15 "perhaps the LORD will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph" (*ulay*: Joel 2:14; Jonah 3:9); 5:18-20 "Woe to those who long for the day of the LORD" (darkness, not light; the lion and the bear); 5:21-24 "I hate, I despise your festivals... let justice roll on like waters and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream": build it from the Hebrew, not from the memorized English; 5:25-27 the forty wilderness years and "Sikkuth your king and Kiyyun your star-god" (the Hebrew vowels, Mesopotamian astral gods, the Greek "the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Rephan", "beyond Damascus" in Hebrew and "beyond Babylon" in Acts 7:43; the Damascus Document's use: verify before citing).
- 6:1-7 the complacent "at ease in Zion", ivory beds, harps, "not grieved over the ruin of Joseph", first to go into exile; 6:8 "the pride of Jacob"; 6:12 "Do horses run on a rock?" (text uncertain); 6:13 Lo-debar and Karnaim ("by our own strength" pun); 6:14 "the Brook of the Arabah" (the Bible Atlas point is wrong).
- 7:1-9 visions one to three; "the LORD relented" (7:3, 6: same English as Joel and Jonah; topic gods-regret); *'anak* "plumb line" (a rare word, debated); 7:9 "Isaac" for Israel. 7:10-17 Amaziah and the prophet's reply; 7:11 "Jeroboam will die by the sword" is not what 2 Kings 14:29 says: do not harmonize, give the views. 8:1-3 *qayits/qets* "summer fruit/end" pun; 8:4-6 "buying the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals", the false ephah; 8:9 the sun set at noon (Matthew 27:45); 8:11-12 the famine of hearing the LORD's words; 8:14 "As your god lives, Dan" and "the way of Beersheba".
- 9:1-4 the Lord beside the altar; 9:7 "Are you not like the Cushites to me?... Did I not bring up Israel from Egypt, and the Philistines from Caphtor and the Arameans from Kir?" (topic election); 9:8-10 not utterly destroyed, the sifting; 9:11-12 "the fallen booth of David... that they may possess the remnant of Edom" (Hebrew) versus the Greek "that the rest of humankind may seek" (Acts 15:16-18): the two readings turn on one consonant pair in *yirshu/yidreshu* and *'edom/'adam*; weigh: a different Hebrew text, interpretation, or the original (read dss.py for 4QXII scrolls and the Damascus Document and 4QFlorilegium citations; say only what you verify); 9:13-15 the plowman overtaking the reaper, mountains dripping new wine (= Joel 3:18, align), "never again uprooted".
