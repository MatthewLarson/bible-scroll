# Ignatius to the Ephesians: brief for the Ephesians writer

Book id `IEp` (works.json `ignatius-ephesians`, proposed in `content/proposals/works/works.json`, tier `early`; the old entry
`letters-of-ignatius` stays for the collection). 21 chapters, 47 verses plus `1:0`, the greeting ("Ignatius, also called
Theophorus, to the church ... which is in Ephesus of Asia"). The first of the seven letters in Lake's order, written from
Smyrna on the way to Rome (Lake's introduction, vol. 1 OCR lines 8686-8700, following Eusebius, Church History 3.36), and the
longest. Lake: the immediate purpose of each letter but Romans "is to thank the recipients for the kindness which they had
shown to Ignatius" (here the Ephesians sent their overseer Onesimus and others to meet him, 1:3-2:1), and three further aims
run through the letters: "to strengthen respect for the bishop and presbyters", to protest "against the form of heresy called
docetism", and to secure help for his church in Antioch (lines 8708-8753).

## Text and witnesses
- Base text: Lake's Greek (`texts/orig/IEp.json` `grc`, with `fixed`: 23 slips corrected in 16 verses), the short (middle) recension.
  Lake's witnesses (introduction, lines 8756-8949): G, the Codex Mediceus Laurentianus at Florence (Vossius, 1646); g, the
  interpolated long recension; L, Grosseteste's Latin (about 1250; Ussher, 1644); A, the Armenian (from a lost Syriac); S, the
  Syriac (fragments of the full text, and Cureton's abridgment of Ephesians, Romans and Polycarp, 1845, which Lightfoot "and
  others showed ... to be merely an abridgment"). Lake: the text "may be regarded as fairly accurate, though it is probably by
  no means so good as that of I. Clement".
- Lake's footnotes (`texts/src/IEp.app.json`, 16): enter as variants those that change the meaning, witnesses exactly as Lake
  gives them. Above all **7:2**: Lake prints ἐν ἀνθρώπῳ θεός ("God in man"), "justified by early patristic quotation, and
  (slightly corrupted) by A", where G and L read ἐν σαρκὶ γενόμενος θεός ("God come in the flesh"): a full variant entry and a
  note, with what each reading says about Christ. Also 1:1 (A's "truly immaculate will"), 1:2, 1:3 (GL "your bishop in the
  flesh"), 5:1, 5:3 (θεοῦ G, Lightfoot; θεῷ LS), 6:2, 8:1, 9:1-2 (Lightfoot's emendations), 10:3, 15:3, 18:2, 19:3, and the two
  at 20:2 (Zahn's and Lightfoot's emendation; Theodoret's "one Jesus Christ").
- English check texts: Roberts and Donaldson's shorter column (`texts/en/IEp.json`, by chapter) and Lake's own English on his
  facing pages (vol. 1 OCR from line 8997), with his English footnotes (Theophorus, lines 9025-9027; at 1:1 his "brotherly" is Zahn's rendering,
  and Lightfoot suggests "natural" or "congenial", lines 9029-9030).
- Digital forms left as they are (brief-apfathers2.md): 4:1 ᾅδεται and 4:2 ᾅδητε (sing), 11:2 συνῄνεσαν, 8:2 οὐδε (Lake's own
  misprint).

## Lenses (fixed)
- **H Historical-critical**: Ignatius and his journey (Eusebius 3.36 and the Chronicle, as Lake reports them); the three
  recensions and why the short one is accepted, with the dissenters only from works opened; Ephesus's church and its overseer
  Onesimus (and whether he is Philemon's Onesimus: the site's `onesimus` topic asks; only from sources opened); the date.
- **N New Testament**: Paul (12:2 "who in every letter remembers you": what Ignatius knew of Paul's letters, from sources
  opened), 1 Corinthians (Lake's margin in 15-18), John (5:2 and the bread of God; 7:2 and the Word made flesh), Matthew (the
  star of 19 beside Matthew 2; 14:2 the tree known by its fruit, Matthew 12:33; 17:1 the ointment, Matthew 26:7), Romans 1:3
  (18:2; 20:2), Proverbs 3:34 (5:3) as James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 quote it.
- **C Church order and the churches**: the overseer, the council of elders and the deacons (2:2; 3:2 "the overseers appointed
  to the ends of the earth"; 4:1; 5:1-3; 6:1; 20:2); the thanksgiving and the one bread (5:2; 13:1; 20:2); how the churches read
  these chapters today (one bishop in each city as apostolic order, or a second-century development; the Supper as "medicine of
  immortality"), each reading only from a source that discusses Ignatius.
