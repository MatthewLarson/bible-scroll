# Zephaniah: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-twelve.md and tools/brief-twelve2.md first. Book id `Zep`, 3 chapters, 53 verses (18, 15, 20). Prophets lane. One part, flat folder `content/books/Zep/`. One writer, who owns book.json. One review.md.

**Numbering.** English and Hebrew agree. Verify in texts/orig/Zep.json.

**Form.** Poetry throughout (` / `) except the heading (1:1, prose). Three movements: judgment on Judah and the whole earth (1:2-18), the call to seek the LORD and the oracles against the nations (2:1-15), Jerusalem's guilt, the nations' purified lips and the remnant, and the joy song (3). The day of the LORD (1:7-18) is the center.

**Lenses: H, C, J, X, F.**
- H: "in the days of Josiah" (640-609 BC; the Plain of 2 Kings 22-23 and 2 Chronicles 34-35 is the model); the idolatry Josiah purged (1:4-6: the Baal, the *kemarim* "priests of the idols" (2 Kings 23:5), the host of heaven, Milcom/Malcam), the Scythian-invasion theory (Herodotus: a conjecture, give it as a view), the fall of Nineveh (2:13-15, 612 BC, Nahum's Plain pair), the Philistine cities (2:4-7), Moab and Ammon (2:8-11), Cush (2:12), Assyria; the date before or after the 622 reform (1:4-6 suggests before, the commonest view); the genealogy in 1:1 (four generations to "Hezekiah": the king? the Plain follows the Hebrew and the note gives the debate; "Cushi" = "the Cushite").
- C: authorship and the post-exilic additions (2:8-11? 3:9-20?), the unity (Eissfeldt, Ball, Berlin, Sweeney, Roberts, Hubbard, Szeles, Robertson), the structure of 1:2-3 (the reversal of creation, Genesis 1:20-26: fish, birds, beasts), the text of the hard verses (2:1-2, 2:14, 3:1, 3:8, 3:18).
- J: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum; Zephaniah 1:14-18 is the haftarah read on Tisha b'Av? (no: check); the Talmud on Zephaniah; 3:9 "a pure language" in Jewish tradition (Hebrew as the language of the age to come; check) and "from beyond the rivers of Cush" (3:10).
- X: the *Dies irae* (the Latin hymn of the Requiem Mass, attributed to Thomas of Celano or earlier, begins with the Vulgate of Zephaniah 1:15-16: *dies irae, dies illa*), Jerome on Zephaniah, Cyril of Alexandria, Theodoret, Calvin and Luther; Zephaniah 1:3 and the end; 3:9 and Pentecost (Acts 2: the reversal of Babel); 3:12-13 "a humble and poor people" (Matthew 5:3-5; Luke 6:20: label as an echo only); 3:14-17 joy and the Annunciation (Luke 1:28, 31: "Rejoice, the Lord is with you" echoes 3:14-17 in the Greek; label as the church's reading); 3:17 "He will rejoice over you with singing."
- F: 1:14-18 (the day of the LORD), 2:3, 3:8-13, 3:14-20.

**Places for full treatment.**
- 1:1 heading (the genealogy). 1:2-3 "I will sweep away everything from the face of the earth" (*asoph asef*: a pun in the Hebrew on "gathering" and "sweeping"; a note); the Greek reads a different line in 1:3. 1:4-6 the idols and the host of heaven (Deuteronomy 4:19; 17:3; 2 Kings 21:3-5; 23:4-5, 12; Jeremiah 7:18; 8:2; 19:13: the Plain of 2 Kings and Jeremiah are the models); *Malkam/Milkom* in the Hebrew and "their king" in the Greek (a variant entry); *kemarim* (also Hosea 10:5); those who "bow down on the roofs", swear by the LORD and by Milcom.
- 1:7-13 "Be silent before the Lord GOD, for the day of the LORD is near... the LORD has prepared a sacrifice and consecrated his guests" (the sacrifice as judgment: Isaiah 34:6; Jeremiah 46:10; Ezekiel 39:17; the guests); the officials in foreign clothes; "everyone who leaps over the threshold" (1 Samuel 5:5); the Fish Gate, the Second Quarter (*hamishneh*, Huldah's house: 2 Kings 22:14), the Mortar (*hammakhtesh*; the market district); 1:12 "who are thickening on their dregs" (wine left on the lees; Jeremiah 48:11) who say "the LORD will not do good or ill"; 1:13 the futility curse (Deuteronomy 28:30, 39; Amos 5:11; Micah 6:15).
- 1:14-18 the day of the LORD "a day of wrath" (*yom 'evrah*), "a day of darkness and gloom" (Joel 2:2; Amos 5:18-20; Isaiah 13; Ezekiel 30:3; the topic `day-of-the-lord`); the end of "all the inhabitants of the earth" (a complete end, *kalah ... nivhalah*); 1:18 "the whole earth will be consumed in the fire of his jealousy". The Dies irae (reception). Card: `top: day-of-the-lord`: preterist (Jerusalem, Babylon), historicist, futurist (dispensational), amillennial; Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist.
- 2:1-3 "gather yourselves, gather, O shameless nation... seek the LORD, all you humble of the earth... perhaps you will be hidden on the day of the LORD's anger" (*'ulay*, "perhaps"; the verb *tisatheru*); hard Hebrew in 2:1-2: variants.
- 2:4-15 the oracles against the nations: Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod and Ekron (the wordplays: Gaza will be abandoned (*'azzah 'azuvah*), Ekron uprooted (*teaker*)); the Kerethites (the Cretans, the sea-coast, 2:5-6), Moab and Ammon (their taunts; "like Sodom... like Gomorrah", 2:9; Genesis 19:37-38), Cush ("my sword", 2:12), Assyria and Nineveh (2:13-15: "this is the carefree city that said in her heart, 'I am, and there is no one else'", the Plain of Isaiah 47:8, 10 for Babylon; the animals (*qa'at*, *qippod*) in the capitals, 2:14: the Plain gives "desert owl" and "hedgehog" as plain English with a note on the uncertain birds: Isaiah 34:11 is the model; the Greek has "chameleon"/"hedgehog"). Pair with Nahum's Plain (written alongside) and list the pair.
- 3:1-7 "Woe to the rebellious, defiled city" (*hoy*; *moreah*, *nig'alah*, *ha'ir hayyonah*: "the oppressing city" or "the dove city"? the Greek reads "the dove"; the Plain follows the Hebrew with *yonah* = "oppress" and gives the other); the officials as roaring lions, the judges as wolves of the evening, the prophets reckless and treacherous, the priests profane the holy (3:3-4: Ezekiel 22:23-31); 3:5 "the LORD is righteous in her midst; he does no wrong; every morning he brings his justice to light".
- 3:8-13 "Therefore wait for me... until the day I rise up as a witness" (the Hebrew *le'ed* "as a witness"; the Greek and Syriac read "for plunder" (*la'ad*); the Plain follows? decide); "I will turn the peoples to a pure lip, so that all of them may call on the name of the LORD and serve him shoulder to shoulder" (3:9; Genesis 11:1-9; Acts 2; Hebrew *safah berurah*: a pure language or a pure speech? label; a card: `top: salvation` or `election`: inclusivist and exclusivist readings, Catholic Nostra aetate? only if you can cite the document exactly; do not invent), 3:10 beyond the rivers of Cush; 3:11-13 the proud removed and "a people humble and poor" left, "the remnant of Israel" (the topic `remnant`) who "do no wrong and speak no lies".
- 3:14-20 the joy song: "Sing, Daughter Zion... the LORD has taken away the judgments against you" (3:14-15, and the Plain's term "Daughter Zion"); 3:17 "The LORD your God is in your midst, a warrior who saves; he will rejoice over you with gladness; he will quiet you in his love (the Hebrew *yacharish be'ahavato*; the Greek, Syriac and Qumran? read "he will renew you in his love": hard; weigh with the Hebrew, a full variant entry); he will exult over you with singing"; 3:18 (hard Hebrew, a variant entry); 3:19-20 "I will deal with all who afflict you... I will gather you... when I restore your fortunes before your eyes" (the *shuv shevut* idiom).
