# Ignatius to Polycarp: brief for the Troas writer (second letter)

Book id `IPo` (works.json `ignatius-polycarp`, proposed in `content/proposals/works/works.json`, tier `early`). Not to be confused
with `Pol` (Polycarp's own letter to the Philippians) or `MPo` (the Martyrdom of Polycarp). 8 chapters, 21 verses plus `1:0`, the
greeting ("Ignatius, also called Theophorus, to Polycarp, overseer of the church of the Smyrnaeans, or rather one who is overseen
by God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ"). Written from Troas (8:1, "because I sailed suddenly from Troas to Neapolis"; Lake's
introduction, vol. 1 OCR lines 8694-8698, following Eusebius: "Polycarp the bishop of Smyrna"). The one letter of the seven written to
one man (Lake's list, lines 8690-8698), and its "you" changes: chapters 1-5 speak to Polycarp (singular), chapter 6 to the church
(plural), and 7-8 move between the two (Lake's footnotes, lines 14260 and 14368, and his Greek footnote at 8:1, "The combination of
singular and plural is very strange"). Polycarp's own letter, already on the site, answers part of it: Polycarp to the Philippians
13:1-2 (the Philippians and Ignatius asked that a letter go to Syria; the letters of Ignatius sent to Philippi).

**One writer, two letters**: you write this after Ignatius to the Philadelphians (`brief-iph.md`, which has the shared reading list
and your duties). Own both book.json and sources.json files.

## Parts
None: `content/books/IPo/` flat, 1:0-8:3. Sections (from the Greek and Lake's English, vol. 1 OCR 13806-14398): the greeting with its
play on "overseer" (1:0); his mind set "as on an immovable rock"; "vindicate your office", care for union, bear with all, pray
without ceasing, "bear the sicknesses of all as a perfect athlete" (1); be gentle with the troublesome: "not every wound is healed by
the same plaster"; wise as the serpent and pure as the dove; the time calls for you as pilots for winds; God's athlete, the prize
incorruption (2); stand firm "as an anvil that is struck"; wait for the one above time, timeless, invisible, made visible for us,
untouchable, beyond suffering, who suffered for us (3); widows, more frequent meetings, slaves not to be freed at the church's
expense (4); flee evil arts; husbands and wives; continence without boasting; marriage "with the consent of the overseer" (5); to the
church: pay attention to the overseer; labor together; the soldier's pay, baptism as arms, faith as helmet, love as spear, endurance
as full armor; "deserter", "deposits", "back pay" (6); Antioch at peace; call a council and elect "God's runner" for Syria; "A
Christian has no power over himself, but gives his time to God" (7); write to the churches on the road ahead, since he sailed
suddenly from Troas to Neapolis; greetings to the wife of the procurator (or of Epitropus), Attalus, the one to be sent to Syria,
Alce; farewell (8).

## Text and witnesses
- Base text: Lake's Greek (`texts/orig/IPo.json` `grc`, with `fixed`: 10 slips corrected in 7 verses, and the restored **2:3**: the
  digital file ran it into 2:2; both scans begin section 3 at ὁ καιρὸς ἀπαιτεῖ), the short (middle) recension, witnesses G, g, L, A,
  and S, which for this letter includes Cureton's Syriac abridgment (Lake's introduction, lines 8839-8851: Cureton found in 1845
  "a Syriac text of a collection of three epistles, Ephesians, Romans, and Polycarp", which "Lightfoot however and others showed ...
  to be merely an abridgment"). Fixed for this plan: 5:1 began with Lake's stray section number; 8:1 has Lake's brackets back
  ("(οἱ μὲν δυνάμενοι ... ἔργῳ)", from both scans and his English, "(let those who can send messengers ... a memorable deed)"); the
  margin reference at 1:3 is Matthew 8:17 and at 6:1 2 Timothy 2:4 (both scans). Still doubtful: the margin at 5:1 ("Eph.3 15. 20"
  in the digital file; one scan reads "Eph.5 25. 30"): open the page.
- Lake's footnotes (`texts/src/IPo.app.json`, 6): enter as variants those that change the meaning, witnesses exactly as Lake gives
  them: 2:2 (ὁ om. G, "but the parallelism with ἡ περιστερά shows that this is only an accident"), 7:1 (διὰ τὴν προσευχήν G, διὰ τῆς
  προσευχῆς Lg; and **ἀναστάσει GL, αἰτήσει gA**: "your disciple at the resurrection" or "through your intercession"), 8:1 (ἐπεί GA,
  ἐπεὶ οὖν Lg; and the note on singular and plural, with Lake's "The punctuation given is in the main Lightfoot's, but even so the
  sentence is unsatisfactory"). 6:2's note is on the Latin words (Terms).
- English check texts: Roberts and Donaldson's shorter column (`texts/en/IPo.json`) and Lake's English on his facing pages, with his
  English footnotes (vol. 1 OCR): 1:3 "athlete" (line 13922, quoted in brief-apfathers3.md); 2:3 "The general meaning of this passage
  is fairly clear, but the details are hopelessly obscure. Possibly something has dropped out of the text" and θέμα "a 'money-prize'"
  (14032-14037); 6:2 the Latin words (14207) and the soldiers' deposits (14254-14258); 7:1 "Or perhaps 'a disciple at your
  resurrection'" (14260); 8:1 Neapolis, "The modern Cavalla, on the coast of Macedonia" (14366-14367), and "Modern English obscures the
  fact that this 'you' is plural. The others are singular" (14368-14369); 8:2 "Or, perhaps, 'of Epitropus'" (14373).

## Quotations
Follow brief-apfathers.md and brief-apfathers3.md. 2:2 takes up Matthew 10:16 (in the singular, to one man): the site's wording where
the Greek agrees, with a note on the change. 1:3 "bear the sicknesses of all" echoes Matthew 8:17 (Isaiah 53:4). 5:1 "to love their
wives as the Lord loves the church" echoes Ephesians 5:25, 29. The rest are echoes: never bend Ignatius to them.

## Where Christians differ
No book card. **One chapter card is possible, at chapter 5** (brief-apfathers3.md, Where Christians differ): "Ignatius says those who
marry should do so 'with the consent of the overseer' (5:2). Must Christians marry with the church's consent, and must a minister
witness the marriage?", top `marriage`. Read first the site's cards on marriage as a sacrament (Ephesians 5:32), on marrying
unbelievers (2 Corinthians 6; Ezra 10), on divorce (Matthew 5 and 19; 1 Corinthians 7; Deuteronomy 24) and on how closely related a
