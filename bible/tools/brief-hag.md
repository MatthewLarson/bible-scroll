# Haggai: brief for the writer

Read tools/brief-twelve.md and tools/brief-twelve2.md first. Book id `Hag`, 2 chapters, 38 verses (15, 23). Prophets lane. One part, flat folder `content/books/Hag/`. One writer, who owns book.json. One review.md.

**Numbering.** English and Hebrew agree on verse counts, but check texts/orig/Hag.json: the Hebrew text puts the date of 1:15 ("on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month in the second year of Darius") with ch. 1, and 2:1 begins "in the seventh month"; verify that the English matches. State the rule in book.json `facts`.

**Form.** Mostly prose: dated oracle reports, each opened with a date formula ("In the second year of King Darius, in the sixth month, on the first day of the month, the word of the LORD came by Haggai the prophet to Zerubbabel... and Joshua...") and short quoted speech. Four dated messages: 1:1-11 (29 August 520 BC, by the usual conversion: state it as such), 1:12-15 (the people's response, 21 September 520), 2:1-9 (21st of the seventh month: 17 October 520), 2:10-19 (24th of the ninth month: 18 December 520), 2:20-23 (the same day). There is a rhetorical poetry in 2:6-9 and 2:21-23, which are poetry in the Leningrad Codex layout? Follow the layout and the scholarly consensus; ` / ` only for real poetry. The repeated formulas ("consider your ways", *simu levavkem 'al darkeikhem*, 1:5, 7; 2:15, 18; "the word of the LORD came by the hand of Haggai"; "declares the LORD of Armies") keep the same English each time.

**Lenses: H, C, J, X, F** (F only for 2:6-9, 2:21-23; say so in book.json).
- H: the Persian period: Darius I (522-486), the second year 520 BC; Zerubbabel (governor of Judah, *pechah*; grandson of Jehoiachin: 1 Chronicles 3:17-19; Ezra 3:2; Matthew 1:12-13; Luke 3:27) and Joshua (Jeshua) son of Jehozadak the high priest; the return from Babylon under Cyrus (538, Ezra 1) and the stopped Temple (Ezra 4:24-5:2; 6:14-15: Ezra's Plain English Translation is the model, including its spelling of "Jeshua"; check, and use the site's Plain English Translation for every name); the dedication in 515 BC; Zechariah (1:1, 7) prophesying at the same time; the drought and poor harvest (1:6, 9-11; 2:16-17); the fall of Jehoiachin's signet (Jeremiah 22:24-25).
- C: the book as a compilation by a later editor ("Haggai the prophet" in the third person; "the people" vs "the remnant"), the relation to Zechariah 1-8 and Ezra 5-6 (Ackroyd, Meyers, Petersen, Boda, Kessler, Verhoef, Wolff's *Haggai*, Redditt, Mason), the date formulas, the "this people" of 1:2 and "this people... this nation" of 2:14 (who? the Judeans; the Samaritans of Ezra 4:1-5 (Rothstein's old thesis, rejected by most); give the views), the "former glory" of 2:3, the "desire of all nations" (2:7).
- J: Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum Jonathan on Haggai; the Talmud (Yoma 9b: the five things the Second Temple lacked, the ark, the fire, the Shekinah, the Holy Spirit, the Urim and Thummim; Megillah 3a on the prophets of the exile; Sukkah? and Bava Batra 14b-15a on the men of the Great Assembly writing Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi: verify before citing); the Second Temple's glory "greater" (2:9) on Talmudic debate (Bava Batra 3a-b; verify); the haftarah (1 Kings 7:51-8:21 for Shabbat Hanukkah? no: Haggai 2:1-9? check) and Zechariah's Hanukkah haftarah.
- X: Haggai 2:6-9 in Hebrews 12:26-27 (the Greek LXX "I will shake once more"); "the desire of all nations" in the Vulgate (*desideratus cunctis gentibus*; Advent "O Emmanuel" antiphon? "O Come, Desire of Nations" hymn: label as reception); Jerome's and Cyril's reading; Calvin; Zerubbabel as a type of Christ (Matthew 1; Ephesians 2:20-22; Hebrews 12); Haggai and the church as God's temple (1 Corinthians 3:16; 1 Peter 2:5); Haggai 2:23 in Sirach 49:11-12 (Zerubbabel, the signet ring); Haggai 1:4 and the "prosperity gospel" misuse of 1:6-11 (a modern reception, to be mentioned only as a misuse and with the Hebrew).
- F: 2:6-9 (the shaking and the glory of this house), 2:21-23 (Zerubbabel as a signet).

**Places for full treatment.**
- 1:1-11 "This people says, 'The time has not come to rebuild the house of the LORD'" (the dating formulas; *zeh 'am*, "this people" as a rebuke, Isaiah 6:9; 8:6); "Is it time for you to live in your paneled houses while this house lies in ruins?" (1:4, *batim sefunim*, cf. 1 Kings 6:9, 15; 7:7; Jeremiah 22:14); "Consider your ways" (1:5, 7); the futile harvest (1:6 "you wear clothes but are not warm; the wage earner puts his wages into a bag with holes"; Deuteronomy 28:38-40; Micah 6:14-15; Zephaniah 1:13: the Plains are models); 1:8 "Go up to the hills, bring wood and build the house, that I may take pleasure in it and be glorified" (*ve'ekkaved*; the *kethiv/qere*, the *he* missing: a variant entry); the drought (1:10-11: Deuteronomy 11:13-17; 28:22-24; Leviticus 26:19-20).
- 1:12-15 the response of Zerubbabel, Joshua and "all the remnant of the people" (the term `remnant`, *she'erit*); "the LORD stirred up the spirit" (*he'ir*; Ezra 1:1, 5; 1 Chronicles 5:26; the Plain English Translation of Ezra is the model); 1:13 "I am with you" (*'ani 'ittekhem*, the Plain English Translation's wording: Isaiah 41:10; Exodus 3:12); 1:14 "they came and worked on the house".
- 2:1-9 "Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? How do you see it now? Is it not in your eyes as nothing?" (the old people who remember Solomon's Temple, destroyed 586, now 66 years back; Ezra 3:12-13; Zechariah 4:10; Ezekiel 40-48 as the unbuilt model); "Be strong... for I am with you" (Joshua 1:6-9; 1 Chronicles 28:10); 2:5 "the word that I cut with you when you came out of Egypt" (*berit* "covenant" is missing from the Hebrew and the Greek adds a verb; Exodus 19-24; textual note); 2:6-7 "Once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth, the sea and the dry land; I will shake all the nations, and **the treasures of all nations will come, and I will fill this house with glory**" (*hemdat kol-hagoyim*, "the desire of all nations", singular; the verb *uva'u* is plural; the Vulgate and many older English Bibles read a person, "the Desired of all nations"; the Hebrew grammar favors "treasures" (*hamudot* in a few manuscripts and in Targum, Kimhi); the Greek (LXX) has "the chosen things of all the nations"; a **full note and variant**, and a Where Christians differ card: `top: messiah` (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed (Calvin took the plural), Baptist, dispensational), citing each tradition's own teachers; do not rank). 2:8 "The silver is mine and the gold is mine" (Psalm 50:10; 1 Chronicles 29:11-16; the Temple's wealth); 2:9 "the latter glory of this house will be greater than the former" (the Greek adds a sentence about peace; the Jewish debate whether Herod's Temple fulfilled it: Bava Batra 3a-b; Josephus *Antiquities* 15.11 on Herod's rebuilding: verify the citation).
- 2:10-14 the priests' ruling: holy meat carried in a garment does not make other food holy, but one made unclean by a corpse makes it unclean (Leviticus 6:27 [Hebrew 6:20]; 22:4-7; Numbers 19:11-22); "so it is with this people, with this nation before me" (2:14). Treat the hard question squarely: who is "this people" (Judah, who offer worship while the Temple is unbuilt? the Samaritans? the returnees' offerings?) with the views (Rothstein, Ackroyd, Meyers and Meyers, Kessler); the Plain English Translation does not pick a referent the Hebrew does not name.
- 2:15-19 "from this day on I will bless you" (the turn: "consider from this day... the seed still in the barn... the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate and the olive tree have not borne; from today I will bless"): the date (24th of the ninth month = 18 December 520, the day the foundation was laid: 2:18 the sense of "the day the foundation of the LORD's temple was laid" is debated between the 520 events and the earlier (538 BC) laying in Ezra 3; label).
- 2:20-23 the second message on the same day to Zerubbabel: "I am about to shake the heavens and the earth... I will overthrow the throne of kingdoms... Horses and riders will fall, each by the sword of his brother" (the Holy War ending of Judges 7:22; Exodus 14-15; Ezekiel 38:21); "On that day... I will take you, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, my servant... and make you like a signet ring (*khotam*), for I have chosen you" (Jeremiah 22:24-25: Jehoiachin as the signet torn off, whose grandson Zerubbabel is now chosen: the Plain English Translation of Jeremiah and Chronicles is the model; 1 Chronicles 3:17-19; Sirach 49:11; the Messianic reading: Zerubbabel as the Davidic hope, as a type, or as a hope unfulfilled (the later Jewish and Christian readings); Matthew 1:12-13 and Luke 3:27 for the genealogy; the topic `zerubbabel` exists, contribute). A Where Christians differ card on 2:23: `top: zerubbabel` or `messiah`.
