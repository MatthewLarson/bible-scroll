# Ezekiel: shared brief for the part writers

Book id **`Ezk`** (the STEPBible code, as in texts/catalog.json, texts/orig/Ezk.json and tools/dss.py; the planning request said "Eze", which the build does not know. `tools/lxx.py` alone uses `Eze` for the Greek). This book belongs to the **prophets lane** (tools/lanes.md): you write only in content/books/Ezk/, and shared-file additions go in content/proposals/prophets/. Nine parts (48 chapters, 1,273 verses), all written at the same time (no part can see another's notes):
- 01-07 (140 verses): the vision at the Chebar canal (1), the call and the scroll (2–3:15), the watchman (3:16–27), the sign acts of the siege, the shaved hair and the famine (4–5), oracles to the mountains (6), "the end has come" (7). **This writer owns book.json.**
- 08-15 (158): the vision of the Temple abominations (8), the marking and the slaughter (9), the glory leaves (10–11), exile sign acts (12), false prophets and prophetesses (13), elders with idols; Noah, Daniel and Job (14), the useless vine (15).
- 16-19 (133): the foundling bride (16), the two eagles and the cedar shoot (17), individual responsibility (18), the lament for the princes (19).
- 20-24 (188): the history of rebellion and the "not good" statutes (20), the sword of Babylon (21; English 20:45–49 = Hebrew 21:1–5), the bloody city (22), Oholah and Oholibah (23), the pot and the death of Ezekiel's wife; the siege begins (24).
- 25-28 (100): Ammon, Moab, Edom, Philistia (25), Tyre (26–27), the ruler of Tyre and the king of Tyre (28:1–19), Sidon and the promise to Israel (28:20–26).
- 29-32 (97): the seven oracles on Egypt (29–32), including the dated 29:17–21 (the latest dated word, 571 BC), the dirge for Pharaoh (32) and the descent to Sheol.
- 33-39 (197): the watchman again and the news of the fall (33), the shepherds (34), Mount Seir (35), the new heart and spirit (36), the valley of dry bones and the two sticks (37), Gog of Magog (38–39).
- 40-43 (122): the visionary tour of the new Temple (40–42) and the return of the glory (43).
- 44-48 (138): the closed east gate, priests and Levites (44), the land, the leader, the festivals (45–46), the river from the Temple and the borders and tribal portions of the land, "The LORD is There" (47–48).

The breaks follow Ezekiel's own dated visions and the book's three-part shape (judgment on Jerusalem 1–24; nations 25–32; restoration 33–48), not the chapter size alone. 33–39 stays one part (197 verses) because its pieces lean on each other (33:30–33 the people who hear but do not do; the two shepherd chapters; the new covenant; the dry bones; Gog), and splitting it would split the dry bones from the new heart of 36 and the order question of the Greek. If a writer for 20-24 or 33-39 finds the load too heavy, report it to the lead; do not re-cut parts on your own.

- Compare against the NIV and ESV only in your head. Never write out their text; log only verse number, outcome and a few words of reason.

Keep the repetition (it is the book's voice). Do not smooth it into variety. Where the Hebrew is uncertain, a note (`kind` `deb` or `mis`) says so; do not hide it in smooth English.

**Poetry and prose.** Ezekiel is mostly prose (the visions, the sign acts, most oracles) with real poetry in the laments and songs: 19 (the qinah for the princes), 26:17–18, 27:3–9, 25–36 (the ship of Tyre), 28:12–19 (the lament for the king of Tyre), 31, 32:2–16 and parts of 21:9–17 (the sword song), 15:2–8 and 24:3–5 (the pot song, the vine) and 7:2–27. Poetry: one verse per line, ` / ` between poetic lines. Prose: no ` / `. Follow the Leningrad Codex layout and the consensus on which verses are verse; say in one chapter-page paragraph when a chapter's genre changes inside it. Keep Ezekiel's wordplays and puns where English can (Ezekiel's own mashal "riddle/parable/proverb" labels at 12:22–23; 16:44; 17:2; 18:2–3; 20:49; 24:3; the rhythm of 7:2–7 "an end, the end has come" qets ba'/ha-qets; shofet "judge" and the sword) and explain the rest in a note.

**Textual evidence.**
- The Leningrad Codex is the base. Use texts/auto/Ezk.json for Ketiv/Qere and marked readings.
- **Qumran and Masada.** Read the scrolls yourself: `.cache/dl/tfenv/bin/python tools/dss.py Ezk <ch>` (run from bible/; the header explains the marks; restored words in brackets are never a scroll's reading; "no scroll preserves this verse" can mean a scroll lacks it). The copies are 1QEzek (1Q9), 3QEzek (3Q1), 4QEzek-a (4Q73), 4QEzek-b (4Q74), 4QEzek-c (4Q75), 11QEzek (11Q4) and the Masada scroll (Mas 1d, which preserves 35:11–38:14 and is the oldest witness to the end of Ezekiel 36–38 in the Hebrew that matches the MT almost exactly). Which survive for a given verse is what dss.py shows; read it before stating a scroll's reading, and name every scroll that preserves a place, even when it agrees with the MT. The Qumran Ezekiel scrolls mostly agree with the MT; the few differences are the ones to list. Do not claim a scroll's order or length for a chapter it does not preserve.
- **The Greek.** On this site "the Greek" means the critical Old Greek (Ziegler's Göttingen Ezekiel, 1952, with Rahlfs). `python tools/lxx.py Eze <ch>` prints Swete (Codex Vaticanus; where it is missing, Alexandrinus); name a reading only Swete's codex has by that codex. Ezekiel's Greek is a literal translation by more than one translator (Thackeray's two or three hands, 1–27 and 28–39 and 40–48, is the older view; Ziegler, Tov and Lust accept at least two: say "reported by" and verify before stating the divisions), about four to five percent shorter than the MT (checked against Tov, whom the writer must verify: see Tov in Lust ed., Ezekiel and His Book, BETL 74, 1986; do not state the percentage without checking).
- **Papyrus 967** (Chester Beatty IX–X, with the Scheide leaves; early third century AD; the oldest Greek witness to Ezekiel; also a witness to Daniel and Esther). It is a witness of the first rank for Ezekiel 11–48. Its notable features (verify each against Ziegler's apparatus, Lust's "Ez 36–40 in the oldest Greek text" (CBQ 1981) and Tov's "Recensional differences between the MT and LXX of Ezekiel" (ETL 1986) before you write them): it lacks 12:26–28; it puts chapter 37 after 38–39 (the order 36, 38–39, 37, 40–48: the resurrection chapter and Gog swapped, so that the vision of the dry bones comes just before the Temple); it lacks 36:23c–38 (the long Greek plus of other manuscripts, the section with "I will sprinkle clean water on you", 36:25–27, is NOT in 967 on this reckoning, check: 36:23c–38 is a Greek lack of 967, not a plus; the writer for 33-39 must read 967 in the apparatus or a translation before saying which side has the addition); and it differs at 7, 19:... and 32:25–26. Give variant entries for each, with 'plain' "" for an omission, and describe the order of 36–39 in a variant on 37:1 and 38:1 and a chapter-page paragraph; say plainly that scholars read the order two ways (the MT's order being the earlier, with 967 moving the resurrection next to the Temple for a reason of theology: Lust; or 967 preserving an older arrangement: Block's summary of Lust and Bogaert). The 40–48 writer and the 33–39 writer both note it.
- **The order of 40–48**: only one order in all witnesses. Say where the Greek differs (the gates and chambers of 40–42 are a thicket of different measurements in the Greek; the Greek omits parts of 40:... and 45:... per the apparatus; verify before writing a variant).
- The Targum of Ezekiel (Targum Jonathan; Levey's translation in The Aramaic Bible, 1987), the Vulgate, the Peshitta and the later Greek versions (Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion, from Origen's Hexapla) are later witnesses. Sefaria's texts of Rashi, Radak (Kimchi), Ibn Ezra, Metzudot and the Targum can be read with WebFetch on sefaria.org (Bash cannot reach it).
- **Tiqqune soferim and the Jewish tradition of correction.** Ezek 8:17 ("they put the branch to my/their nose": zemorah) is listed in some medieval sources (Mekhilta, Midrash on Samuel) as a scribal alteration; verify and cite or leave it out.
- Say once, on your first chapter page, which witnesses you read yourself. No reader-facing text may say "from memory", "not rechecked" or "I have seen it myself": verify, or write "reported by <source>".
