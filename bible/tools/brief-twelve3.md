## Zechariah (parts 01-08 and 09-14)

**Form.** 1-8 is mostly prose, dated: 1:1-6 (October-November 520), 1:7-6:15 (11th month, 24 Feb 519: the eight night visions with interpreting angel and the crowning), 7:1-8:23 (4th day of the 9th month, 7 Dec 518: the fasting question). 9-14 is two oracles headed *massa'* (9:1 and 12:1), poetry and prose mixed, with no dates. Follow the Leningrad Codex layout and consensus on prose and poetry. Visions: keep the same English for the repeated formula ("I looked up and saw", "the angel who spoke with me").

**Part 01-08 (owns book.json).** The eight visions: the horsemen among the myrtles (1:7-17), four horns and four craftsmen (1:18-21), the man with the measuring line (2:1-13), Joshua the high priest, Satan and the filthy clothes, the Branch (3), the gold lampstand and the two olive trees, Zerubbabel's "not by might" (4), the flying scroll (5:1-4), the woman in the basket (the ephah, Shinar, 5:5-11), four chariots (6:1-8), the crowns and the Branch (6:9-15); the fasting question and the answer (7-8); the Zechariah ten-men line (8:23). book.json: lead, facts, authorship and date, the Persian setting, audience (the returned community of Yehud, Zerubbabel and Joshua, the Temple builders; who 9-14 was for is the critical question), outline, the structure of the book, the unity debate (Proto- Deutero- Trito-Zechariah: Mede 1653, Hitzig, Stade, Mitchell, Meyers and Meyers, Petersen, Redditt, Boda, Sweeney, Merrill; conservative defenders of unity: Baldwin, Kline, Hill, Archer; give each fairly), the Greek-period theory for 9:1-8 (Alexander) or the Persian one, the Branch (*tsemach*) in Jeremiah 23:5; 33:15, Isaiah 4:2, 11:1; Zerubbabel as Branch; the Satan (*hassatan*, "the accuser") in Job 1-2 and 1 Chronicles 21:1, the topics `satan` (check). **Part 09-14.** 9:1-8 the oracle against Hadrach, Damascus, Tyre, Philistia (Alexander's march, 332 BC, one view; others earlier); 9:9-10 the humble king on a donkey ("righteous and saved/victorious" *nosha'*; Matthew 21:5; John 12:15; the colt; Genesis 49:11); 9:11-17 the blood of the covenant, the prisoners of hope, Judah as bow, Greece (*Yavan*, 9:13, the Greek text), the LORD's arrow; 10 shepherds and idols (teraphim), the LORD cares for his flock; 11 the three shepherds in a month, the staffs Favor (*no'am*) and Union (*hovelim*), the thirty pieces of silver (11:12-13) thrown "to the potter in the house of the LORD" (Matthew 27:3-10), the worthless shepherd; 12-13 "they will look on me, the one they pierced" (12:10), mourning like Hadad-rimmon in Megiddo (12:11; Josiah? ## Malachi (one part)

**Form.** Prose-and-poetry disputation: the six "But you say, 'How have we...?'" exchanges (1:2-5, 1:6-2:9, 2:10-16, 2:17-3:5, 3:6-12, 3:13-4:3), each with a charge, an objection and a reply, plus the closing (4:4-6, Moses and Elijah). Keep one English for the repeated formulas ("But you say, ..."). *Malakhi* is "my messenger" (3:1) and probably the name of the book rather than the prophet (the Greek and the Targum treat it as a title; Targum: "Ezra the scribe"? check before saying); say so as a view.

**Date and audience.** Persian period, after the Temple's rebuilding (515 BC) and before Nehemiah or around the time of his second term (Nehemiah 13: tithes 13:10-14, Sabbath, mixed marriage 13:23-27), about 500-450 BC: give the views. Audience: the priests and people of Yehud (1:6 "O priests"); the book is about worship, the priesthood, marriage and divorce, justice and the tithe. The H lens is Persian-period Judah; Ezra and Nehemiah's Plain English Translation is the model.
