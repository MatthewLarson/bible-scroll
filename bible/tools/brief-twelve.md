**Numbering (important).** plain.txt uses English (KJV) verse numbers, and so do notes, chapters and references. The Hebrew Bible numbers some verses differently. Each writer checks `texts/orig/<id>.json` (`heb` field) and states the map in a note at the first affected verse and in book.json `facts`: Hosea 1:10-11 = Hebrew 2:1-2 (Hebrew chapter 2 therefore runs 2:3-25 in English 2:1-23); Hosea 11:12 = Hebrew 12:1 and English 12:1-14 = Hebrew 12:2-15; Hosea 13:16 = Hebrew 14:1 and English 14:1-9 = Hebrew 14:2-10; Joel 2:28-32 = Hebrew 3:1-5 and English chapter 3 = Hebrew 4 (so Hebrew Joel has 4 chapters, 21 verses in the last); Jonah 1:17 = Hebrew 2:1 and English 2:1-10 = Hebrew 2:2-11. Amos and Obadiah agree with the Hebrew. Verify every figure against the data, not this list. When you cite the Greek, give its chapter and verse as well. The Greek of Hosea-Joel-Amos follows the Hebrew numbering except as the printout shows.

**The Book of the Twelve.** The Hebrew Bible counts these twelve short books as one scroll (Ben Sira 49:10 praises "the Twelve Prophets"; Bava Batra 13b-15a on the scroll and its writers; Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.8, counts 22 books and the Twelve is one of them; check each before citing). The order differs: the Masoretic Text runs Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum...; the Septuagint runs Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel, Obadiah, Jonah, Nahum... Read both orders as a signal of how the Twelve were read (catchwords and links: Joel 3:16 = Amos 1:2; Joel 3:18 = Amos 9:13; Amos 9:12 "Edom" opening Obadiah; Obadiah's day of the LORD; Joel 2:13 = Jonah 4:2). Redaction-critical readings (Nogalski, Schart, Wolfe, Redditt; the "Twelve as a unit" debate against Ben Zvi and Barton) are debated: label them `views`, the Twelve as one book is not a consensus. `book-of-the-twelve` is a topic core (new, in proposals/prophets/topics/). The Hosea 01-07 writer owns it; others contribute to it.

**Lenses (fixed; use these keys in every chapter's `views`).** `H` History and fulfillment, `C` Critical, `J` Jewish tradition, `X` Christian, `F` Still to come. Jonah drops F and adds `L` Literary and genre (see brief-jon.md). Each book's book.json gives its own `desc` for each lens, in the Jeremiah and Daniel manner. The wording of H, C, J, X is the same across the five: H the 8th century (Hos, Amo, Jon), the Assyrian rise and fall of Samaria; or the 6th-5th century (Oba, Jol), the Babylonian fall and Persian times; C the critical and compositional questions; J the Jewish reading (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum Jonathan, the Talmud, the haftarot and Yom Kippur use); X the church's reading (fathers, Jerome's Commentary on the Twelve, Cyril of Alexandria, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret, Aquinas, Luther, Calvin's lectures on the Twelve, the lectionary).
