# Hebrews: brief for the writers

Read tools/brief-nt.md, brief-pauline.md, brief-pauline2.md and brief-general.md first. Book id `Heb`. 13 chapters,
303 verses. Lenses H C L J T. Two parts, written at the same time:

| Part | Chapters | Verses | Owns |
|---|---|---|---|

| `parts/08-13/` | 8:1-13:25 | 174 | topic cores `hall-of-faith` (see Topics); the `diatheke` and `skene` terms entries (see below) |

Hebrews is one argument, so the two writers must agree on the vocabulary of the sanctuary and the priesthood. Part
08-13 cannot read 01-07's Plain: both translate from the Greek with the terms in brief-general.md and the decisions
below, and list in their reports every place the other part's wording should match (the lead aligns them).

**Fixed for both parts:** ἀρχιερεύς "high priest"; ἱερεύς "priest"; κατὰ τὴν τάξιν Μελχισέδεκ "after the pattern of
Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4 Plain; the Greek τάξις is "order": note at 5:6 that "order" is the usual English, and that the
site's Psalm 110:4 renders the Hebrew *dibrati* "pattern"); σκηνή "tent" (gloss "tabernacle"); τὰ ἅγια "the sanctuary";
ἅγια ἁγίων "the most holy place"; καταπέτασμα "the veil"; ὑπόδειγμα "copy" and σκιά "shadow" (8:5; 10:1); τύπος
"pattern" (8:5, Exodus 25:40); ἀντίτυπος "copy" (9:24; 1 Peter 3:21 is in another round: note the word); εἰκών "image"
(10:1); ἐφάπαξ / ἅπαξ "once for all" / "once"; κρείττων "better" every time (the book's key word: 1:4; 6:9; 7:7, 19,
22; 8:6; 9:23; 10:34; 11:16, 35, 40; 12:24); ὑπόστασις "being" at 1:3 (gloss "nature, essence") and "assurance" at 11:1
(gloss "substance, the underlying reality, a title deed": note on the readings) and 3:14 "confidence"; ἔλεγχος (11:1)
"proof" (gloss "conviction"); παρρησία "boldness" (terms.json: check the Acts entry; in Hebrews "confidence to enter":
note); ὁμολογία "confession"; κατάπαυσις "rest" and σαββατισμός (4:9) "Sabbath rest" (a word found first here: verify);
ἀρχηγός "Founder" (brief-general.md); μέτοχοι "partners" (1:9; 3:1, 14; 6:4; 12:8); τελειόω "make perfect" (gloss);
οἰκουμένη "the world" (1:6; 2:5 "the world to come"); ὁ αἰὼν μέλλων "the age to come" (6:5); ἀφανισμός; ὁ λόγος τοῦ
θεοῦ "the word of God" (4:12, lowercase). Old Testament quotations are introduced as God, Christ or the Spirit speaking
("he says", "the Holy Spirit says"): keep the speaker the Greek gives.

**Sections and settings.** Hebrews is "a word of exhortation" (13:22): settings follow its alternation of exposition
and warning. 1:1-4 (the prologue, one Greek sentence: L); 1:5-14 (seven quotations: the Son above the angels); 2:1-4
(first warning); 2:5-18 (Psalm 8; the brothers; "a merciful and faithful high priest"); 3:1-6 (Moses and the Son);
3:7-4:13 (Psalm 95: "today", the rest); 4:14-5:10 (the high priest who sympathizes; Gethsemane in 5:7); 5:11-6:20
(the warning of 6:4-6, then hope as an anchor); 7:1-28 (Melchizedek); 8:1-13 (Jeremiah 31: the longest Old Testament
quotation in the New Testament: verify); 9:1-28 (the tent and the Day of Atonement); 10:1-18 (Psalm 40; once for all);
10:19-39 (draw near; the warning of 10:26-31; "my righteous one will live by faith"); 11:1-40 (the roll call of faith);
12:1-13 (the race, the discipline of sons); 12:14-29 (Esau; Sinai and Zion; the shaking, Haggai 2:6); 13:1-25 (the
ending: hospitality, "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever", "outside the camp", Timothy, "those from
Italy").

**H:** who the readers were (Jewish Christians tempted back to the synagogue or Temple; a mixed congregation; in Rome
("those from Italy" 13:24), Jerusalem, Alexandria or elsewhere: views); the date: before or after AD 70 (the present
tenses of the Temple service in 9:6-9; 10:1-2; 1 Clement's use of Hebrews (1 Clement 36: verify) as an upper limit);
their past sufferings (10:32-34; Claudius' expulsion of Jews from Rome, AD 49 (Suetonius, *Claudius* 25.4: verify);
Nero, AD 64). **C:** authorship (brief-general.md), the genre (homily), the structure (Vanhoye's chiastic outline,
Guthrie's discourse analysis: verify each before naming), Platonic or apocalyptic background (Philo's copy and pattern;
the heavenly sanctuary of Jewish apocalyptic: views), the Christology of 1:3 and 1:8 ("Your throne, O God": the
grammar). **L:** the alliteration of 1:1 (*polymerōs kai polytropōs*), the best Greek in the New Testament (say who
says so: verify), the a fortiori arguments ("how much more"), the inclusio of the roll call ("by faith"), the catena of
1:5-13, the rhetoric of the warnings. **J:** this is the most Jewish argument in the New Testament: the Day of Atonement
(Leviticus 16), the priesthood, Melchizedek in Second Temple Judaism (11QMelchizedek at Qumran; Philo; 2 Enoch's
Melchizedek story; the rabbinic identification with Shem: verify each), angels as givers of the Law (2:2; Acts 7:53;
Galatians 3:19; Jubilees 1:27-29: verify), the heavenly Temple, the martyrs of 11:35-38 (2 Maccabees 6-7; the sawing of
Isaiah in the Martyrdom and Ascension of Isaiah: verify; works.json names), Jewish readers' objection that Hebrews is
supersessionist (Jewish and Christian scholars on "obsolete" in 8:13: views, fairly). **T:** the fathers' use of 1:3
and 1:8 at Nicaea, Chrysostom's homilies on Hebrews, the Eastern church's early acceptance against the Latin West's
doubts (verify), Luther's preface, Calvin's commentary, the warnings in the Reformed-Arminian debate.
