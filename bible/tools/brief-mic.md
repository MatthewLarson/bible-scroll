# Micah: brief for the writers

Read tools/brief-twelve.md and tools/brief-twelve2.md first. Book id `Mic`, 7 chapters, 105 verses (16, 13, 12, 13, 15, 16, 20). Prophets lane. Two parts: `parts/01-04/` (54 verses; owns book.json) and `parts/05-07/` (51 verses). One review.md each.

**Numbering.** English 5:1 = Hebrew 4:14, and English 5:2-15 = Hebrew 5:1-14 (Hebrew chapter 5 has 14 verses). Verify in texts/orig/Mic.json; state it at 5:1 and in book.json `facts`. All Plain, notes and references use English numbers. The 01-04 writer states the rule; the 05-07 writer notes it at 5:1.

**Form.** Poetry throughout (` / `), with prose-like headings at 1:1 and the superscription; 6:1-8 is a lawsuit (*rib*), 7:1-7 a lament. Three speeches begin "Hear" (1:2; 3:1; 6:1) and the book moves from judgment to hope three times: keep that structure visible in the chapters' settings and the summary.

**Parts and owners.** 01-04: superscription, Samaria and Judah's fall with the place-name wordplay (1:10-16), the land-grabbers (2:1-11), the remnant (2:12-13), the rulers and prophets (3), the last days and Zion (4). 05-07: the ruler from Bethlehem (5:1-5), the remnant (5:7-9), the lawsuit (6), the lament, the creed and the closing praise (7). **The 01-04 writer owns the new topic cores `micah` and `swords-into-plowshares` (4:3); the 05-07 writer contributes to `bethlehem` (it exists), `messiah` and the others.** Check `ls content/proposals/prophets/topics/` right before writing a core.

**Lenses: H, C, J, X, F.**
- H: Micah of Moresheth in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah (Jotham and Ahaz are the model Plain of 2 Kings 15-16; Hezekiah's reform is 2 Kings 18; the reading of Micah 3:12 at Jeremiah 26:17-19 is the oldest quotation of a prophet in the Bible and Jeremiah's Plain is the model); Samaria's fall 722/721 (1:6), Sennacherib's campaign of 701 (1:10-16: the place names are in the Shephelah along the road to Lachish; Sennacherib's prism and the Lachish reliefs: cite only what you can name exactly), Assyria "the land of Nimrod" (5:5-6).
- C: the unity of the book (the three-cycle structure; the debate whether 4-5 and 7:8-20 are post-exilic: Wellhausen, Mays, Wolff, Hillers, Andersen-Freedman, Waltke, Allen, Smith; give the views), 1:10-16 as a text of hard Hebrew, the date of 4:1-5 and its relation to Isaiah 2:2-4 (same source, borrowing either way, or an old oracle; label), 5:2 and its context, 6:9-16.
- J: Micah 7:18-20 is the haftarah on the Sabbath after Rosh Hashanah (Shabbat Shuvah) in some rites and the *tashlich* custom (casting sins into the water) draws on 7:19; Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi, the Targum Jonathan (which interprets 5:2 as the Messiah: check the text), the Talmud on Micah 6:8 (Makkot 24a: "He has told you, O man, what is good... three things" and the 613 commandments reduced to three: verify and cite exactly), Sukkah 52b on the seven shepherds and eight princes (5:5: verify).
- X: Micah 5:2 (Matthew 2:5-6 follows a form that differs from the Hebrew and Greek; John 7:42), the fathers and Jerome's commentary on Micah, Origen on Micah 5:2, Augustine, Cyril of Alexandria, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Calvin on Micah, Luther; Micah 6:8 and the works debate; Micah 7:6 in Matthew 10:35-36 and Luke 12:53; Micah 4:1-4 and peace; Micah 7:18-20 and the Easter or Lent lectionary.
- F: 4:1-8 the last days and the nations at Zion; 5:2-5 the ruler; 7:11-20.

**Places for full treatment (from the 01-04 writer).**
- 1:1 "in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah"; "Moresheth" (*Moreshet-gat*, 1:14; Gath is a pun). 1:2-7 the LORD comes down (Judges 5:4-5; Psalm 18:7-15; Amos 1:2, Plain of Amos is the model); Samaria a heap, prostitutes' wages (1:7, Hosea's "prostitution" term).
- 1:8-16 the lament and the place-name puns (*Gath* "tell not", *Beth-leaphrah* "house of dust", *Shaphir* "pleasantness", *Zaanan* "going out", *Beth-ezel* "next-door", *Maroth* "bitterness", *Lachish* "horses", *Moresheth-gath* "a parting gift", *Achzib* "deception", *Mareshah* "heir", *Adullam*): keep the Hebrew names in the Plain, give the English in the glosses. Hard text: variants (the Greek differs greatly here, the Qumran pesher on 1:5-6; 1:12-13 at least).
- 2:1-11 the powerful take fields and houses (Exodus 20:17; Leviticus 25; 1 Kings 21: Naboth; Isaiah 5:8); "Do not preach" (2:6-7: the opponents' words); 2:12-13 the remnant gathered and the "breaker" (*happorets*): who speaks, who breaks? Verses 12-13 are read as salvation (the shepherd), as judgment (the breaker through the gate), or as the exiles' deliverance; label the views. 3:1-12 the rulers who "eat my people's flesh" (3:2-3), prophets who cry "peace" (3:5), "I am full of power... the Spirit of the LORD" (3:8), the summary of Zion plowed as a field (3:12) and its use in Jeremiah 26:18.
- 4:1-5 = Isaiah 2:2-4 (copy the Isaiah Plain exactly where the Hebrew is the same; say where it is not; add 4:4-5 which Isaiah lacks); 4:3 "plowshares"/"pruning hooks"; "in the last days" (*be'acharit hayyamim*, see the term in Isaiah); "everyone under their vine and under their fig tree" (1 Kings 4:25; Zechariah 3:10); the daughter "Zion" and "Daughter Zion" (4:8, 10, 13); "to Babylon you shall go" (4:10: the only mention of Babylon in the 8th-century prophet; the critical debate and the conservative reply that Isaiah 39 gives the same anticipation).

**Places for full treatment (from the 05-07 writer).**
- 5:1-5a "Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among the clans of Judah... from you one will come out to rule Israel... his origins are from of old, from the days of eternity" (*miqqedem mime 'olam*); 5:3 "until she who is in labor has given birth" (Isaiah 7:14: label the relation); 5:4 "he shall be their peace"; the shepherd (Ezekiel 34; 2 Samuel 7); **Matthew 2:5-6 quotes with differences from the Hebrew and the Greek: quote the Matthew Plain exactly, list the differences in a note and include a Where Christians differ card (how the churches read the prophecy: fulfilled in Christ at his birth, typological or double reference, Jewish readings with Hezekiah or an unnamed future king; Targum Jonathan, Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimhi; Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist, dispensational)**. 5:2 and the "clans" (*'alpe*, "thousands") versus *'allufe* "chiefs": variant entry. 5:5b-6 "the seven shepherds and eight princes", "Assyria... the land of Nimrod".
- 5:7-9 the remnant as dew and as a lion; 5:10-15 God's cleansing: horses, chariots, cities, sorcery and Asherah poles. Variants at 5:5, 5:7, 5:14.
- 6:1-8 the lawsuit (*rib*; Deuteronomy 32; Hosea 4:1; Isaiah 1:2): the mountains as jury; 6:3-5 "What have I done to you?... Balaam... Shittim to Gilgal"; 6:6-8 "Shall I come with burnt offerings... shall I give my firstborn for my rebellion" (child sacrifice: 2 Kings 3:27; 16:3; 21:6; Jeremiah 7:31, the Plain of Jeremiah is the model); **6:8 "to do justice, to love loyal love, and to walk humbly with your God" (*mishpat*, *hesed*, *hatsne'a lekhet*: "humbly" is rare: weigh "carefully"; the Greek reads "be ready to walk"): a full note, a variant, and a card (works, faith and grace: Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist; cite the church's own teachers) or `top: justice`. 6:9-16 the merchants' false measures (Leviticus 19:35-36; Deuteronomy 25:13-16; Amos 8:5), "the statutes of Omri and the works of the house of Ahab" (1 Kings 16; 21): very hard Hebrew; variants at 6:9, 10, 11, 14, 16.
- 7:1-7 the lament ("woe is me") and 7:6 "a man's enemies are the members of his own household" (Matthew 10:35-36 follows it; Luke 12:53; both Plain); 7:7 "I will look to the LORD". 7:8-13 the fallen enemy and the confession; 7:14-17 prayer for shepherding "as in the days of old"; **7:18-20 "Who is a God like you" (the pun on the name Micah, *Mikha-yahu* "who is like the LORD"; the creed of Exodus 34:6-7 and Jonah 4:2, the Plain of Exodus is the model; "you will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea"; "you will show faithfulness to Jacob, loyal love to Abraham"; Genesis 12, 22; Psalm 103:8-12; Shabbat Shuvah)**. A note at 7:18 on the Twelve's closing creed, and `book-of-the-twelve`.
